## 5. Measured-but-wrong — nine instruments

These are the broken instruments the record cannot flag for itself. Each returns `status: measured`.

### 5.1 `sessions.sessions_marker` — counts artifacts, not sessions
- **Reported:** 5,347. **Actually:** 20 `~/.dsh` entries + 5,327 wake-instance artifacts (dirs and files).
- **Evidence:** `extra: {"dsh_dir_entries": 20, "wake_instances": 5327}`; at 01:00, `785` dirs + `4,655` files.
- **Why it matters:** this single cell contributed 5,243 of the week's apparent movement. It is the largest number in the audit and the least meaningful.

### 5.2 `spend.spend` — counts cost rows, not dollars
- **Reported:** 410. **Actually:** 410 release cost records; **$29.0037** priced + 11 unsourced.
- **Evidence:** 399 numeric `cost_usd_or_unsourced` values summing to 29.0037; 11 rows literal `"unsourced"`, all failed releases on 2026-10-17 with no session mapping.
- **Why it matters:** the cell cannot test the **$61.20/day** cap. `wake.py stats` reports today's spend separately (`$1.6574/$61.20`), outside this cell.

### 5.3 `ledger.left_open` — counts `dropped` as open
- **Reported:** 170. **Actually:** 150 open (43 blocked + 3 running + 104 todo); 20 dropped.
- **Evidence:** `by_state: {"blocked":43,"done":476,"dropped":20,"running":3,"todo":104}`. The SQL is `state NOT IN done/cancelled/expired` — `dropped` is not in the exclusion list.
- **Why it matters:** the +159 delta overstates backlog growth by 20; the honest delta is +139.

### 5.4 `wake.stats` — counts lines
- **Reported:** 3. **Actually:** three lines of `wake.py stats`, nothing more.
- **Evidence:** `["wake rows: new=17 claimed=4 done=825 failed=116 unverified=6 cancelled=82 expired=0", "selftest rows: 157 ...", "released today: 6/150 ..."]`.
- **Why it matters:** it is flat at 3 every week regardless of estate activity.

### 5.5 `wake.releases` — counts claim events and mixes selftests
- **Reported:** 405 release/claim events. **Actually:** not units of work; one row can be released many times.
- **Evidence:** `by_status` at filing: `done 829, failed 269, cancelled 82, new 17, unverified 6, claimed 4, dropped 16`. `wake.py stats` reports `failed=116`, because it excludes **153 selftest failures** (157 selftest rows, 153 failed). A reader of the DB `failed=269` overstates real failures by 153.

### 5.6 `commits.commits_landed` — counts autocommits
- **Reported:** 701. **Actually:** 701 commits, **560 autocommit**, 141 other.
- **Evidence:** `git log --since=2026-10-12T00:07:28 --until=2026-10-19T00:07:28 --pretty=%s | grep -ci autocommit` → 560; total 701.
- **Why it matters:** +526 commits is not +526 units of engineering. The journal autocommit loop is most of it.

### 5.7 `capabilities.skills_added` — flow labelled like a stock
- **Reported:** 0 this week, −7 vs last week. **Actually:** no new paths matching `skill` in the window; the 7 from last week are not deleted.
- **Evidence:** `{"baseline": 7, "week": 0}`; the bucket is `"skill" in f.lower()`, not a skill registry count.
- **Why it matters:** a negative "added" count is a flow collapse, not a capability loss. The audit cannot say whether the estate *has* fewer skills.

### 5.8 `journal.open_pain` / `journal.by_kind` — two different windows
- **Reported:** 50 and 50. **Actually:** `open_pain` is all-time (50 open); `by_kind` is windowed (50 entries, but the mix is `handoff 28, lessons 13, pain 9`). Last week's mix was `handoff 18, lessons 25, pain 2, decisions 4, wins 1`.
- **Why it matters:** the totals coincide; the content does not. Pain entries rose 2→9 while lessons fell 25→13.

### 5.9 `ledger.closed_with_proof` — sound stock, misleading weekly flow
- **Reported:** 476. **Actually:** 476 done, all 476 with a non-empty `attempt.proof`. The cell is not windowed.
- **Evidence:** `done_at` min `2026-10-13T01:59:30Z`; by 01:00 the max was `2026-10-19T00:48:36Z`. The store's first item predates the baseline by 36 seconds.
- **Why it matters:** as a weekly delta, +475 reads as a throughput explosion. As a stock, it is a one-week-old ledger that closed 73.7% of everything ever filed in it. Both statements are true; only the second is useful.
