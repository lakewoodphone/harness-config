## P2932 — db_writable HIGH on the authority is writer queueing, not a stuck lock (2026-10-12): company-api holds WAL_WRITE_LOCK at shm byte 120 in multi-second bursts

Measured 2026-10-12 03:20-03:41Z on secratary-cloud (authority), while the kernel's
HIGH `db_writable` was firing and the brief's 4x 5.0s BEGIN IMMEDIATE reproduction
felt like a hung database.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS
The write lock is CONTENDED, not STUCK. `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` either returns in
0.0001s or burns the whole timeout; it never crawls. Over three windows:

 timeout=0.05s, 60s: ok=7 refused=225
 timeout=1.0s, 200 probes: ok=73 refused=126 (p50 = 1.0025s = the timeout)
 timeout=25s, 30 probes: ok=30/30, max wait 9.041s
 timeout=5.0s x3 and timeout=60s x1 (03:39:31-03:40:11): all "writable 0.00s"

So "locked for 5 s" is ordinary here; "locked forever" never happened.

WHO HOLDS IT -- named directly, not inferred
/proc/locks on the -shm inode showed, in 6 consecutive dumps taken the instant a
probe was refused:

 1: POSIX ADVISORY WRITE 332682 08:01:29756 120 120 <- /root/authority.db-shm

335682 is company-api (`/opt/company/.venv/bin/python -m uvicorn app.main:app`,
etime 02:16:38, 37 threads, RSS ~1.0GB). Byte 120 of the shm is SQLite's
WAL_WRITE_LOCK -- exactly what BEGIN IMMEDIATE needs. Read locks at 124/125 are
WAL_READ_LOCK marks held by normal readers (read-pages.py, test runners).

CAUSE: writer queueing under CPU starvation, not a long-lived transaction.
Load average was 4.4-7.2 on 2 vCPU across every sample; vmstat showed 0-1% idle,
~21k interrupts/s. The API's busy_timeout is 10000 ms (PS_SQLITE_BUSY_TIMEOUT_MS)
with an 8-attempt / 60s-budget retry, so its own writes ride it out and SUCCEED --
the journal shows full lifecycle sweeps completing at 03:23:37, 03:26:07, 03:28:34
and 03:39:20 with task_lifecycle_updated: 249-250. What fails is any writer with a
shorter patience: the kernel probe (6 s), `connect_db(db_url, busy_timeout_ms=750)`
(app/database.py:365), and the stdlib 5 s default.

REAL LOCK LOSSES ARE RARE: whole-box journal had exactly 1 "database is locked" in
30 min and 3 in 24 h (Oct 11 21:08:47, Oct 12 01:10:03, 03:18:27). The company
recorded work throughout.

CORRELATED LOAD (all on the 2 vCPU box at once):
 * pid 377480 gzip -6 -c, 54% CPU -- the streaming offsite backup (started 03:13:01)
 * pid 408930 pytest over 28 test files, 43% CPU, launched by an agent session
 (`bash /tmp/_dsh_run_.sh` <- sshd root@notty)
 * pid 409789 /root/.fsearch/comms-coverage.py --source /root/authority.db, 45% CPU
 * pid 332682 company-api, 36.6% CPU

NOT a repeat of 2026-09-25 (when the API held that same byte-120 lock for over an
hour). Here it is released continuously -- a 25 s wait always wins.

LESSON FOR THE PROBE: a 5-6 s BEGIN IMMEDIATE measures the box's load, not the
database's writability, on a 2 vCPU writer-shared store. Distinguish by re-probing
with a 60 s timeout before escalating to HIGH.
