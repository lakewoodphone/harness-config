**D3033 · 2026-10-08 · LPT comeback doctrine: inspect first, never argue - an unprovable claim by a real customer is settled on the bench.**

DECIDED 2026-10-08, on the Heber case (443) 610-7535 - a customer asking us to stand behind a
charging-port repair we cannot find in any record, where he holds a memory and we hold nothing.

CONTEXT
He is not a customer in either production store and never was; his three contacts (2026-05-05,
2026-05-27, 2026-07-29) exist only in Dialpad. So we cannot prove we did the work and we cannot
prove we did not. The owner declined to arbitrate when asked ("he claims we did what should we do"),
which makes it a standing doctrine question, not a one-off owner call.

THE RULE
1. Never tell the customer he is mistaken. Say we looked and cannot find the job on our side - true,
 and a statement about our records, not about him.
2. Inspect before quoting. The device comes in; the bench says what the fault is.
3. If the fault is in the part we would have worked on, redo it once at no charge and write the job
 up properly. A comeback is cheaper than the argument, and it is the only safe answer when the gap
 is in our own record.
4. A different fault, or no fault, is quoted normally - no blanket free repair.
5. Always write the job up this time, with device, fault, price and payment.

WHY, STATED HONESTLY
Not because it maximises this repair. Because the shop has no record of a paying customer who drives
in from Baltimore, the amount at stake is about one port repair, and the cost of being right is a
customer and a referral lane. This is a small economic choice made deliberately, not a principle
dressed up as one.

SCOPE
Applies wherever the customer's claim is real to him and unprovable from our record. It does not
apply where the record settles it: if the job IS written up, the written scope and price govern.
"Once" is part of the rule - the same claim a second time is a decision, not a doctrine.
