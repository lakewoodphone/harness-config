**D3053 · 2026-10-11 · The label is the instruction: a free walk-in repair is not texted to the customer (owner, 2026-10-11, Pinter battery).**

Owner, 2026-10-11, on the drafted arrival text for Yehuda Pinter (the laptop we sold him, back for the
battery): **"no, the label is enough."**

What that means, and it is a standing pattern and not just this ticket:
- The printed tape is the INSTRUCTION, and the shop reads it. The secretary does not need a text
 message to have been sent in order to act on the job.
- The customer is NOT contacted about a job that only needs him to walk in and pick up a free repair.
 The label carries "battery came in - tell him to come in" / "NO CHARGE - warranty"; the shop tells him.
- The drafted body ("Hi, your new battery came in. / Bring the laptop in whenever works for you and
 we'll swap it in for free...") was declined, never sent, and is recorded in
 lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/cases/laptop-yehuda-pinter-2026-08-11.md as considered-and-declined.
 **Do not re-draft it.** I asked for a per-message yes because that is the hard stop; the answer was no,
 and the correct next move is nothing, not a revision.
- What IS still owed on that case when the machine arrives: battery-report the new GJKNX cell BEFORE
 fitting it (under ~90% of the 67,997 mWh design -> free return, open to 2026-11-13), fit it, tick
 checklist line 8, tell him the real unplugged runtime.

Recorded in production too - note on WO26082712672 (2026-10-11, ledger seq 312) states the part is in
hand, the label is the instruction, no text was sent, and therefore to expect him to walk in without an
appointment. Status left at AWAITING_PARTS only because the status vocabulary has no "waiting on the
customer"; the part is in hand, not on order.
