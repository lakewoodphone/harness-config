**L3449 · The Dialpad call cache lags the call by ~15 min - 'no call today' from an early read is a false negative**

WHAT I LEARNED 2026-10-08, the hard way, on the Heber case.

MEASURED
At 16:19 UTC I read the operational store and the comms index for (443) 610-7535 and reported
'nothing from today'. A call existed: dialpad_call_full call_id 4935007478259712, started
2026-10-08 16:17:01 UTC, 116 s. It appeared in the local cache at fetched_at 16:32:15 UTC - about
15 minutes after it happened - and in the production communications table it has still not appeared.
So my 'no call today' was true of my source and false of the world.

THE RULE
'No record of X' from dialpad_call_full or ps_comms_search means 'not in the cache yet' for anything
within the last ~15-30 minutes. Before telling the owner a call or text does not exist TODAY:
 1. query dialpad_call_full directly and ORDER BY date_started_ms DESC, so you see the newest rows
 whatever they are - a WHERE on the number cannot find a call the cache has not written yet;
 2. check the max(fetched_at) in dialpad_call_full to see how fresh the cache is, and say the age;
 3. prefer the dialect that is not the cache where one exists: for calls the Dialpad API row itself
 lands in dialpad_call_full, so ordering by date_started is the cheapest freshness witness.
The same lag applies to dialpad_sms_cache (already a known pain).

ALSO
An owner's paraphrase of a customer call is not a transcript. The owner said 'he fixed a charging
port on a laptop'; the transcript says 'the recharger', and the customer never named a device. Read
the transcript before writing the customer's words into a record.
