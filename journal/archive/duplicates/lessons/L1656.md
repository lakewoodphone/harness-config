**L1656 · An alarm must separate the failure it names from a quiet success; where a central view cannot, say so**

**An alarm must be able to separate the failure it names from a quiet success -- and where a central
vantage point cannot, say so instead of guessing.**

Measured 2026-09-15, the fourth severity rule of mine to need this correction in three rounds:

1. `attention_debt` counted five-week-old briefings as live debt.
2. `check_delivery` measured a channel the owner had deliberately closed.
3. `tick_completion` shouted about a collapse window that had ended six weeks earlier.
4. `session_archive` alarmed on the one machine that had **nothing new to ship**, while three others had
 shipped minutes earlier.

The first three were "history versus live". The fourth is a different failure and worth its own rule:
**from the authority, "this machine had nothing to ship" and "this machine cannot reach me" look
identical.** The per-machine age cannot tell them apart, so alarming on it is a guess dressed as a
measurement -- and the honest fix is to alarm on the thing the vantage point *can* judge (no machine
shipping at all) and to name the ambiguous case as context.

Rule: before wiring a stale-ness threshold to a severity, ask whether the measurement can distinguish
the failure from the benign case that looks the same. If it cannot, report it; do not colour the badge
with it. The second half matters just as much: this check had also sent its cron output to `/dev/null`,
so the evidence that would have settled the question locally was being thrown away. **A monitor that
discards its own output cannot be diagnosed when it is wrong.**
