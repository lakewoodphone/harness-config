**L1968 · Family Link cannot block the Google app on Samsung, because system apps cannot be app-limited - the fix is ADB, and the reason is documented by Google**

VERIFIED 2026-09-17 against Google's own documentation, not inference.

The wall: a technician set Family Link up with every restriction it offers and still could not
block Google search on a Samsung Galaxy. This is NOT a misconfiguration.

TWO primary sources settle it:
1. support.google.com/families/answer/7103028 - "System apps can't have app limits placed on
 them." On a Galaxy the Google app (com.google.android.googlequicksearchbox) IS a system app,
 so it never appears in Family Link's Screen time -> App limits list. That absence IS the wall.
2. support.google.com/families/answer/7086922 - what the Family Link "Controls -> Google Search"
 screen actually offers is SafeSearch (Filter / Blur / Off) plus personalized recommendations.
 That is CONTENT FILTERING, not an off switch. The same page names the bypasses itself:
 browsers (Chrome, Firefox) and alternative search apps such as Google Go.

So "block Google search completely" is impossible from Family Link, BY DESIGN, and the workable
route is one level down: `adb shell pm suspend --user 0 com.google.android.googlequicksearchbox`
(most reversible; `pm unsuspend` reverses it), then `pm disable-user` if suspend is not resilient,
then close the browser/Assistant/Circle-to-Search/Google Go surfaces, then VERIFY BY REBOOTING.

Three related traps worth carrying:
- Family Link does NOT show system apps in the app list, so "I blocked everything it offers" is
 not the same claim as "I blocked the app".
- Blocking is not instant: Google documents ~5 minutes or until the device is next online, and
 apps required for supervision or device function cannot be blocked at all.
- Our own flip-phone lockdown lesson transfers and is the reason to do this ONE package at a time:
 on 2025-11-24 a `pm disable-user` on the Package Installer BOOT-LOOPED a device
 (legacy/kyocera-escalator-value/docs/archives/research/ADVANCED_LOCKDOWN_RESEARCH_DIRECTIVE.md:64).

The generalisable rule: when a parental-control product appears to have no off switch for a thing,
check whether the thing is a SYSTEM app before assuming a misconfiguration - platform vendors
routinely make system components unmanageable by their own parental tooling, and no amount of
poking at the parent app will change it.
