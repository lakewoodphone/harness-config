**D262 · 2026-09-17 · Shlock: the frame gets the brace-and-tie retrofit with the 40 mph furl rule, plus the owner's edge rings and two mid supports; house ties only as paired cables or rigid struts.**

**Owner's direction, 2026-09-17 (ZABZ-TECH), in his own words:** *"I'm assuming this is only when the tarp is out, and that's what creates the big sail. So in very strong winds we will retract it"* ... *"the concrete poles at midway are supposed to be strongly [connected] ... that must be strongly connected to the porch midway, and the three closest to the house should be connected with like steel wire to a pole that's actually screwed into the foundation."* He also described *"metal rings like very small ones that go along the two edges of the tarp, down the pole and on two poles in the middle to make sure that I can't extend too much and gather too much wind."*

**Decided:**
1. The frame gets the **brace-and-tie retrofit** — deck-height knee braces on all six posts **plus** positive bolted tension ties at every beam-to-post and rail-to-beam joint — and the **40 mph furled-above operating rule** stands.
2. His two additions are folded into the design, not treated as alternatives: **edge rings riding the leading poles** (anti-billow) and **two intermediate supports** under the 28.5 ft leading-edge pole (self-weight sag 7.98 in on one span, 0.50 in on two).
3. House-side ties to a foundation-anchored pole are accepted **only** as paired opposing cables with turnbuckles or a rigid compression strut — a single wire is slack and useless in half of all wind directions.

**Measured basis (this session, `2026-09-17-shlock-owner-ideas-check.py`):** a furled cover is not zero area — a tight roll is 0.4-0.5 ft dia and adds 254 lb of drag, a sloppy 12 in bundle 627 lb, on top of the 1,058 lb of pipe drag, so **furl means roll tight and lash, not merely bundle**. Billow is the largest single lever in the check: the tarp thrust is 573 lb at the as-built 4.37 deg slope and 1,949 lb at 15 deg, taking the unbraced post from 71 mph to 47 mph — which is what makes his rings a structural item rather than a nicety. A mid support for the leading pole is a **column** (1-3/8 in x 17 ga at 8.5 ft is KL/r = 218, ~820 lb service), so it needs a larger section and its own footing.

**Still open:** the backyard photo (beam-to-post and rail-to-beam joints, all 8 rails visible) — it decides whether those joints already carry tension (roof leaves ~76 mph) or merely sit in sockets (~40 mph), and it confirms the rail x-positions against the tarp's existing grommets.
