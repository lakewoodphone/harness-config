**D259 · 2026-09-17 · Goldstein microSD (848-210-4005): no in-house path to the content; cause NOT established; success-only if a lab is used.**

**D (2026-09-17) — Goldstein microSD (848-210-4005): the card is hardware-dead; no in-house path; and
four operations on it are now banned by name.** Recorded so this verdict is not relitigated by a future
session that sees only "card appears formatted" and reaches for the playbook.

**Decided (on evidence, not on authority):**
- **Not a formatting case.** The controller is in capacity-fallback: it cannot read its own configuration
 area. Evidence chain in the case file and `recovery-log`: one distinct 512-byte block across all
 248,320 LBAs, 0 signatures/strings in 127 MB, raw-SCSI LBA honouring proven, out-of-range properly
 rejected, and the arithmetic that the reported size is not expressible as a modern CSD geometry.
- **No in-house recovery exists for this state.** ddrescue, testdisk/FAT undelete, signature scanning
 and PhotoRec all require *different* data at *different* addresses. Imaging it produces a 127 MB file
 of one repeated block; running the playbook's carve chain on such a card is theatre, and this decision
 says so explicitly rather than leaving it to judgement on the day.
- **The only route is chip-off / monolith at a lab**, and it may still be refused (no pinout for a
 no-name controller: our own Beyda case), may still fail after a successful read (the LDPC wall:
 Desert's 1 TB SanDisk monolith), and costs **$500–$1,750** with a 4–8 week turnaround.

**Banned on this card, by name — every one of these destroys the FTL/translation area a lab needs:**
format · repartition · "resize"/"restore capacity" (MPTool-class vendor tools) · fsck on the device.
The mechanism, in Datarecovery.com's own words: *"The quick solution is to update the firmware and
reformat your memory card, but this permanently destroys all data on the device."*

**Also decided — the free action that actually resolves the case:** read the **printed capacity off the
card's label** before any money is discussed. 128 MB → almost certainly the pen's own bundled card and
the mother's photos were never on it (go and look at the phone/camera that took them). 32/64/128 GB →
fallback confirmed and a no-data-no-fee lab is a rational bet for irreplaceable photos.

**Rejected as recovery, kept as diagnostics (all cheap, none can damage the card):** a native SD host
(the Mac mini M4 has a built-in SDXC slot; phones and cameras also have native controllers) to get a real
SD init and, on Linux, CID/CSD via `sd_capture.sh`; a different reader/port; the freeze test (our own
`sd-card-ellman-2026-06-01.json` documents it as "low probability but zero-cost"); and swapping out any
microSD→SD adapter so the bare card is tested.

**Money:** $75 was quoted and approved, nothing collected. Recommendation to the owner: **no charge** —
nothing was recovered, and the owner's own words on the Newman call two days earlier were *"if we're not
able to do it, we don't charge."* Final call is the owner's; the question is queued, not assumed.
