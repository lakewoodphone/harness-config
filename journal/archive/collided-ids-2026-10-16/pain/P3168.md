## P3168 — P - her box reads as 'quiet' while her assistant is dead, and no alarm can say otherwise

MEASURED 2026-10-13 on DESKTOP-FGV6KMH, and still true after the repair.

## The gap

Her assistant can be completely dead while every existing alarm reads the box as healthy.

The two readings, taken the same minute:

| reading | value | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| shipper heartbeat `desktop-fgv6kmh:hb:...` | `received 2026-10-14T00:02:58Z`, `session_count 0` | the box is up and reaching the authority |
| `MAX(dsh_sessions.updated_at)` for `desktop-fgv6kmh` | `2026-10-05T20:19:31Z` (8.2 days) | no new work has happened in 8.2 days |

Every existing alarm reads that as **quiet**.

## Why nothing catches it

`check_session_archive` (`/root/ceo-kernel/ck/sentinel.py:2544`) separates CHANGE, CONTACT and
PRESENCE, and its HIGH branch requires `presence_age <= PRESENCE_WINDOW_SECONDS`. `PRESENCE_ALIASES`
(`:2650-2667`) has NO `desktop-fgv6kmh` entry, and `next_stage_node_reports` has no row for her box
either. So she cannot reach HIGH; she falls to `quiet or off: desktop-fgv6kmh 8.2d`,
`severity=INFO, ok=True`.

That is the same sentence that was true while her engine could not boot at all for ten days in
September (`journal/reference/yocheved-laptop.md` section 12).

Her box is also absent from every register a monitor reads: `transcript-archive-pull.py MACHINES`
(5 machines), the raw archive's own `machine_contact` (5 machines -- so her transcripts are in NO raw
archive, against AGENTS.md section 1), `next_stage_node_reports`, `next_stage_system_inventory`, and
`host-posture.tsv` (an unrecognised host is exit 4, not permission).

## The specific confusion to kill

"The shipper is alive" and "the engine is alive" are different facts, and only the first is monitored.
The shipper is a task that ships session files; it happily reports contact while the engine cannot
start. `journal/reference/yocheved-laptop.md` section 12.2 already records this for the September
outage -- `updated_at` age measures new rows, not contact -- and it was still the shape of the reading
on 2026-10-13.

## The second half: she closes the lid

Her box is a LAPTOP. On 2026-10-13 the whole remote surface returned Cloudflare **530** for about two
minutes while the lid was shut and recovered by itself; the exec service's own uptime counter ran
continuously through it (706258 -> 707223), so the machine did not even reboot. A laptop that sleeps
is not a broken laptop, and an alarm that cannot tell them apart will cry wolf. The GAP (a machine
holds data the archive lacks) versus SILENCE (cannot be judged from here) vocabulary in
`transcript-archive-server.py --check` is the right precedent, and a third state -- "reachable but its
engine is dead" -- is the one that was missing for ten days.

## What would close it

1. Give her box a `PRESENCE_ALIASES` entry and a `next_stage_node_reports` row, so the existing
 machinery can judge it at all.
2. Add `DESKTOP-FGV6KMH` to the transcript archive's `MACHINES`, so her sessions are archived.
3. An engine-level alarm that does not depend on her clicking. The four instruments installed
 2026-10-13 now log locally (`~/.dsh/metrics/engine-vitals.log`, `profile-gate.log`,
 `multi-window/health.log`) -- but a log with no READER is the failure P3004 already records.
 `estate-alerts-to-journal.py` is the existing reader pattern; nothing reads her box's logs.
4. Register the file's posture: her box has no `host-posture.tsv` row, so any agent that reaches it
 is operating on an unrecognised host.
