**L3939 · interval_sec=900 is NOT explained by the budget tier: both _budget_tier and get_budget_status report normal (day 68.5%, month 29.4%). Two hypotheses falsified; the 900s run_once backoff is the untested candidate**

Three hypotheses about one number, and the measurements have now falsified two of them outright and left a candidate I have not tested. This entry exists so the next attempt does not repeat my guesses.

**The observation, stable across all of it:** `data/autopilot_state.json` reports `interval_sec = 900` while `autopilot_interval_sec` is configured to **300**, and the observed tick gaps are **696 s and 900 s** — so the loop really is running at roughly a third of its intended rate. 900 = 300 × 3.

**Hypothesis 1 — "the value is stale and persisted across restarts" (`L3937`).** Falsified by the next measurement: I wrote **300**, restarted, read back **300**, and eight minutes later it was **900** again. **So something recomputes it.** `L3938` recorded that correction.

**Hypothesis 2 — "the stretch is choosing economy, and I measured the wrong tier function."** Falsified by the figures themselves. The stretch calls `_budget_tier(settings)` (`assistant_chat.py:1194`), which takes **the most restrictive of daily and monthly** from `get_budget_window_snapshot(settings.database_url, config=…)`. Printed directly:

 settings.database_url sqlite:////root/authority.db
 BudgetConfig.from_settings daily 37.50 monthly 750.00 dept 30.00 (the DB override wins, as designed)
 calendar_day_pct 68.5 calendar_month_pct 29.4
 _budget_tier(settings) -> normal
 get_budget_status(...) -> normal

`_tier_from_pct` needs **≥85 %** for economy. **68.5 % is not 85 %.** So the tier is `normal` by both functions, and the tier **cannot** be what set 900.

**So the honest state is: `interval_sec = 900` is unexplained by the budget path.** Two candidates remain and I have tested neither:
1. **The 900 s `run_once` backoff.** The log carries `run_once failed, backoff 900.0s (x1.0)` — **exactly 900**, and the loop assigns its interval from several places. If the backoff writes `interval_sec`, then every failed run ratchets the cadence down, and the "budget tier" story I built is a coincidence that fits because 900 happens to be reachable both ways.
2. **A second writer of `interval_sec`** that I have not looked for. I found one stretch site and assumed it was the only one, which is the same error as counting one marker for two failure classes (`L3935`) — **in the same file, one day apart.**

**What this costs and what it does not.** The company ticks at 696-900 s instead of 300 s, so it attempts roughly a third as much work per hour as configured — a real capability loss, and it is **invisible from every surface I habitually check**: `systemctl is-active` says `active`, `/health` says 200, `enabled` says `True`, `_budget_tier` says `normal`, and the only signal is a tick gap. **It is the fourth distinct cause of "the company looks idle" found today** — the cap pauses sessions, the disk wedges the scheduler, and now this — each owned by something different, each presenting identically.

**What I would do next, and it is cheap:** `grep -n "interval_sec" app/autopilot.py` and read **every** assignment rather than the one I found; then check whether the backoff writes it. **Two greps.** I am recording it unstarted rather than half-done, because a fourth guess would be worth less than the twelve lines above.
