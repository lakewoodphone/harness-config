**L3723 · A media price quoted before the data was measured is a guess, not a quote — measure the source volume before pricing the medium**

MEASURED 2026-10-13 on the Greenfeld Dell Latitude 3301 (case `dell-latitude-3301-hgbs463-greenfeld-2026-09-08`). Every entry in that case file from 2026-09-08 onwards, and the owner's own 2026-10-08 phone pricing, discussed "copy the data off the drive" — and **nothing anywhere had measured the drive**. The owner quoted the medium as *"75 plus a 10 USB"*. The drive actually holds **178.26 GB in 45,580 files**, of which **174.97 GB** is the customer's own data. Consequences of the missing measurement, all of which were latent in a live quoted price:

- The smallest medium that fits is a **256 GB** stick (238 GiB usable); a 128 GB stick does not fit and no 10-dollar stick is within a factor of four. The shop's own catalogue already priced the right medium at **22.46** — more than twice what the customer was told.
- **Two files exceed 4 GiB** (9.11 GB and 7.89 GB), so the medium has to be **exFAT or NTFS**. A 10-dollar stick is FAT32 by default and would have failed them, and it would have failed them *at the file level* — the copy would report an error on two files and look "nearly done".
- The customer's **Otzar HaChochma personal notes are OUTSIDE the Windows profile** (`F:\OTZAR_user_data`). A quote built on "copy the user's folder" silently excludes them.
- And the alternative delivery — **handing back his own healthy drive in the USB enclosure we read it in** — costs about ten minutes and delivers 100% of the data, against hours of copy plus hours of hash verification for a 175 GB stick. That comparison only exists once the size is known.

**RULE:** before any data-only or recovery price is given, measure the source and record four numbers with it — **total bytes, file count, largest single file, and the filesystem the intended medium will use**. It is cheap: a metadata walk of these 45,580 files took **7 seconds**. Quote the medium from that measurement, never from a guess about what a laptop like this "usually" holds.

**WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN IT LOOKS:** the customer is told the number, the number becomes the record's `usbCharge`, and the record then repeats it as though it had been derived. On this case a 10-dollar figure was carried in the case file and the sync record for five days with no data behind it, and only a scan performed for an entirely different reason exposed it.
