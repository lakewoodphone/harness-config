**L3680 · The business/personal Amazon split exists in a column that means something else elsewhere**

2026-10-13. The business/personal split the account-scrapers work existed to produce is in the ledger,
and the canonical store cannot see it - because the two stores use `account_label` to mean DIFFERENT
THINGS.

THE FINDING. `amazon_orders.account_label` carries the split the scraper was built to deliver:
 email 177 orders 7,815.10
 personal 145 orders 8,188.43
 business 65 orders 3,743.27
and the account-scrapers handoff names the target as "Amazon ~$2,442 on card 9650" - so **3,743.27 of
business Amazon spend** is the same order of magnitude as what the work was chasing, and 68 of the 69
business orders are present in the canonical store. None of them is flagged as business there.

MEANWHILE THE CANONICAL STORE'S `account_label` MEANS THE MAILBOX:
 lakewoodphoneandtech@gmail.com 608 | eliyahuzabrowsky@gmail.com 41 | ezabz68@gmail.com 18
**Two tables, one column name, two unrelated meanings.** `account_label` in the legacy table is a
spending class; in the canonical store it is a provenance record of which mailbox the message came
from. The sync's own docstring even explains that it deliberately preserved the legacy label when it
found one - "keeping whatever `account_label` it already had" - which means the meaning was already
mixed in that table before this round.

THE TRAP, stated so it is not walked into. The obvious move - merge the legacy label into the canonical
row the way I merged the items - would OVERWRITE a provenance value with a spending class in a column
that is unique-indexed and read by the matcher. The items merge was safe because the two tables agreed
on what the column meant; this one is not, and it needs a NEW field (`spending_class`, or an evidence
row) rather than a write into `account_label`.

WHY IT MATTERS TO THE OBJECTIVE. The CFO's whole job on card 9650 is to say whether a charge was shop
supplies or a household purchase - the account-scrapers README says exactly that - and the answer for
69 Amazon orders is sitting in a column nothing reads, under a name that means something else.

AND A UNITS ERROR, THE THIRD OF THIS PATTERN TODAY. I first read these sums as `sum(total_amount)/100`
and reported business = 37.43. `amazon_orders.total_amount` is REAL dollars, not cents - the same
mistake I made on `marketplace_orders` in round 52 and on the same shape of column. The cross-check that
caught it was implausibility, not the query: 69 business orders cannot total thirty-seven dollars.
**When a figure is absurd, suspect the units before the data - and print one raw row.**
