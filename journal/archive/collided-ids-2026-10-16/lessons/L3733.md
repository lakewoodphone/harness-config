**L3733 · A calendar cannot answer whether something was filed - and absence of a record is not evidence of absence**

## A deadline is a question about a calendar. Whether something was filed is a question about a document.

Measured 2026-10-13, in one session, on one document. Two owner-queue rows were raised as
emergencies about returns that had already been filed:

- **#475** — "the 2025 tax return extension runs out in TWO DAYS". The IRS had accepted the return
 five months earlier, on 2026-07-19. The root cause was recorded in the doc itself: a search of
 `email_triage_log` — an index that **cannot** contain the owner's personal Gmail — returned zero,
 and the zero was written down as evidence of absence.
- **#480** — "NJ ST-50 2026-07-20, 85 days PAST". The return was filed 2026-07-01, nineteen days
 *early*. The row had only ever seen a date.

Then I built the tool that would have prevented both, and **made the same category of mistake inside
it on the first attempt**: I checked the filing register *after* collapsing several quarters of one
form into a single question keyed on the earliest deadline, so a group whose first member was proven
filed was dropped whole and the genuinely open Q3 sales-tax return disappeared behind its four
already-filed predecessors. Settle each period on its own; only then group.

### The rule

**A calendar cannot answer "has this been filed?"** Neither can an index, a cache, a replica, a copy
of a portal page, or another summary. Only a document can. So:

1. Hold a **register** of what has been filed, one row per period, each carrying a confirmation
 number and a dated primary source. `scripts/tax/filing-register.json` on the authority is the
 instance.
2. Give the register **three verdicts that are not interchangeable**, and never let a reader
 collapse them:
 - `FILED` — a dated source exists.
 - `UNRECORDED` — **the fact is UNKNOWN.** Absence of a record is not evidence of absence. Go to a
 primary source; do not say "unfiled", and do not hand the question to the owner and call it a
 finding.
 - `N/A` — the register cannot speak to this kind of obligation at all. Not a soft `UNRECORDED`.
3. Let the register record a **determination that nothing is owed**, at the same evidence bar as a
 filing — a date, the arithmetic, and the source. Otherwise every future session re-argues it.
 `outcome: not_required` with `determined_on` + `reason` is that shape.
4. **A machine that turns a date into a question must be incapable of asserting the negative.** The
 feeder's old phrasing was "Has this been filed, and if not who is doing it?" — which is a
 conclusion wearing a question mark. It now says what is true: "I hold no filing record for X",
 which is a statement about the record, not about the world. It also stopped using `critical` for
 an unverified date, and routes its open questions to a file an agent reads *before* the owner.
5. **A stale reading must travel with its age and its supersession.** The same digest printed
 "ANNUAL REPORTS: MISSING" as today's finding from a capture two months old, four days after the
 report had been filed. Say where you read it and when it was written, or do not print it.

### The second, smaller lesson, and it cost real time

**The estate could read the owner's Gmail the whole time and did not know it.** `/root/bin/gmail-search.py`
searches all five authenticated accounts read-only — the exact source whose absence manufactured
#475. It **must** be run after `cd /home/zabz/personal-secretary-mvp`, because the token paths are
relative; without the `cd` it reports **zero authenticated accounts**, which is indistinguishable
from "this person has no mail". A tool that fails by reporting emptiness is worse than one that
fails loudly. Before concluding a source cannot answer, make it fail in a way you can tell apart
from a real negative.
