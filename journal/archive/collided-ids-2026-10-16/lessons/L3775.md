**L3775 · Two phones one SIM: a SIM-less phone can only MIRROR Google Messages, never receive on its own**

## The configuration (this is the case that actually walks in)

Phone A: **has the SIM**, has all the messages. Phone B: **no SIM at all**, Wi-Fi only, empty.
This is **not** the L3774 backlog problem — nothing was lost and there is nothing to "bring in". A
phone with no SIM has no line, so **nothing is ever delivered to it by itself**. It can only *mirror*
the phone that has the line.

## The free answer: pair phone B to phone A in Google Messages

Same flow as Google Messages for web. On phone B install Google Messages from Play, sign into the
same Google account, and pair with the QR/emoji flow. Phone B then shows the conversations and can
send and reply. Official doc: https://support.google.com/messages/answer/7611075 — "Check your
messages on your computer or Android tablet" — which states plainly that a Wi-Fi-only Android tablet
(or phone) can do this, and that only a tablet **with** a SIM works standalone.

**It is a RELAY, and this is the load-bearing consequence.** Google's own words: it *"sends SMS
messages using a connection from your computer to your phone"*, and it *"shows what's on your Google
Messages mobile app"*. So **phone A must be powered on and connected**. Phone B receives nothing
while A is off, dead, or offline. A customer who leaves phone A in a drawer will say "it stopped
working", and that is why.

**Two more limits, both of which bite this exact customer:**
1. **Partial history.** The linked device caches the *"latest conversation threads, contacts, and
 other settings"* — not a full archive. Do not promise that pairing alone restores years of history.
2. **Pairing expires.** *"If you don't use your Google Messages account for a few weeks, you'll be
 unpaired automatically for security reasons."* A phone that sits unused for months will need
 re-pairing — the same usage pattern as L3774.

## Full old history onto the SIM-less phone

**SMS Backup & Restore** works with no SIM: it writes into the local messages database. Back up on
phone A, restore on phone B. It is a **snapshot** — new messages still only arrive through the
pairing. Do the restore and the pairing in whatever order, but expect possible duplicates where both
supply the same thread.

## The only way phone B receives with phone A OFF

A real line. Either a SIM in phone B, or the carrier's multi-device service — T-Mobile **DIGITS**,
Verizon **Number Share**, AT&T **NumberSync**. Those are the carrier's own app, not Google Messages,
and they are usually a paid plan. There is no Google Messages path to an independent SIM-less inbox.

## Shop line for the customer

"The messages live on the phone that has the SIM. A phone with no SIM can't receive them on its own —
I can pair it so it shows them and can reply, but the phone with the SIM has to stay on."
