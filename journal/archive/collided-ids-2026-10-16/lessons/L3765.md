**L3765 · A MacBook screen complaint is three different jobs wearing one sentence - diagnose before quoting**

A MacBook screen complaint is three different jobs wearing one sentence. Never quote it from the model
number.

## The case that made this a rule (2026-10-13, LPT)

A customer asked what a MacBook Pro screen repair costs. He sent photographs of the machine. The
photographs were the **About panel and the System Report** — they showed no damage at all — and across
three calls he never said what the screen *did* wrong. The model was identified with certainty from those
photos (14-inch 2024, M4 Pro, 24 GB/512 GB, Mac16,8, MX2H3LL/A, chassis A3401) and the complete lid
assembly was priced across six suppliers. **All of that was correct and none of it answered his question**,
because a model number tells you *which* part, never *whether* that part is the fault.

## The procedure — Apple's own, not invented

Apple publishes a display troubleshooting procedure for 2024-and-later Apple-silicon Mac laptops at
`support.apple.com/en-us/117310`. It is unusually blunt: **Apple replaces exactly two parts — the display,
then the logic board.** Everything else is diagnosis. Three tests, in this order:

1. **Inspect with the machine OFF, then at full brightness.** A crack is visible with the backlight off.
 Apple raises brightness to maximum specifically to expose a crack, and gives its own marker —
 **vertical AND horizontal lines together are highly likely to be related to a crack.** Check for liquid
 first, every time; it invalidates every conclusion below.
2. **Connect an external display — the decisive test.** External fine + built-in broken = display or its
 cable. **Neither shows an image = logic board**, in Apple's own words, and no display price applies.
 Do not order anything before this test.
3. **Open and close the lid repeatedly while watching the fault.** If it changes, it is a cable pinched or
 shorted in the hinge; then open it and reseat the display cable at the logic board (Apple's internal
 inspection step 3).

Apple's fallback if nothing isolates it: replace the display, test, then replace the logic board.

## The two things that are NOT options, and get asked for anyway

- **No glass-only repair on Apple silicon.** The lid is one sealed assembly — panel, camera, ambient light
 sensor, antennas, hinge. One corner crack is therefore a whole-assembly price.
- **Flexgate and Staingate do not apply.** They are **Intel-era 2016–2019 defects** (fraying backlight
 cable; peeling anti-reflective coating). Customers read about them and ask for the cheap component-level
 fix on a 2024 machine. On Apple silicon the only cheap outcomes are the hinge cable and the connector,
 and both are **labour, not parts** — which is exactly why assuming the assembly is expensive.

## The economic frame to check before quoting

Two numbers decide whether to push the repair at all: what a working one sells for, and what a broker pays
for it **broken**. Read both. On this case (read 2026-10-13): same config used asks about **1,463** with the
512 GB M4 Pro floor near **1,100**; a buyback broker's defective tier pays about **465**. So a 595 repair
is roughly 43% of a working machine and beats the broken floor — worth doing, and the number the house rule
(`PRICING-RULE.md`: `(part + shipping) × 1.4 + 65`) already produced. **When a repair price approaches what
a broker pays for the machine broken, stop and say so.**
