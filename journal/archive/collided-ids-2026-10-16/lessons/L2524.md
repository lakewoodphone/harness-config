**L2524 · An LPT case file's phone can be one digit off from the number the customer actually calls from - read Dialpad, not the intake field**

Measured 2026-09-24 on LAKEWOOECHSMINI, answering "what do you know about 8456590807, two laptops".

The two Greenfeld case files and both sync records (and secretary contact 588, and production
customer 1295) carry `+18456598807` — i.e. 845-659-**8807**. The number the customer actually
calls and texts from is `+18456590807` — 845-659-**0807**. One transposed digit, 7th position.

What separates them, measurably, in `dialpad_call_full` / `dialpad_sms_cache` on secratary:

 8456590807 32 calls (2026-03-05 .. 2026-09-24), two-way SMS in March, Sept 8, 9, 24,
 Dialpad's own `contact_name` = "Y Greenfeld", transcripts naming the two laptops.
 8456598807 0 calls, 72 SMS, ALL outbound, never one inbound reply, 2026-09-08 .. 09-20.

So the number on the case file is the one the shop talks AT, and the number in Dialpad's call log
is the one the customer talks FROM. The two are not the same and only the call log is evidence.

Rule: an LPT case's phone comes from the Dialpad call/SMS record (`dialpad_call_full.contact_phone`
or `dialpad_sms_cache.phone_normalized`), not from an intake field and not from a contact record
that was themselves written from the intake. Before quoting or ordering against a phone number,
count the inbound rows under it: a thread with zero inbound rows in its whole life is a number
nobody answers, not a customer.

Second-order tell: `tools/case-index/lptcase.py find "<dialpad number>"` returned no match at all
for 8456590807 while `find greenfeld` matched both cases — the index only knows what the case files
say, so a phone lookup that finds nothing can mean the stored number is wrong, not that the case is
missing.
