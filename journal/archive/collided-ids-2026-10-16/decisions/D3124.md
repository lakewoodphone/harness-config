WHY IT IS A RISK JOB AND NOT A SWAP
The phone carries **two mics**, and which one has failed is not always determinable, so the repair
may be attempted on the wrong one. That - not the soldering - is why it fails about 30% of the time.
**Every mic is soldered**; that is ordinary and is not the reason this job is different. Do not tell
a customer that soldering is what makes it hard (owner correction, same session).

ALSO OWNER-STATED, AND IT CHANGES WHETHER TO TAKE THE JOB
*"unless the camera or screen aren't working well either"* - a further fault on the camera or the
screen changes whether a 70%-success mic repair is worth doing at all. **Ask about screen and camera
before quoting**, which is why that question is in the customer reply.

THE SHOPFILE IS WRONG IN THE SAME WAY THE LG PORT NUMBERS WERE
Production `repair_jobs` for device model "Exalt" assigns **Mike replacement = Tier 25** and
**Loud speaker fix = Tier 55**. A session that quotes from that table will say **25** for a job the
owner charges **110** for - under by 85, with no deposit and no failure terms. Measured 2026-10-12;
the tier table was read live through `lpt sql`. **Treat the Exalt "Mike replacement" tier as
superseded by this entry.** Same defect class as D3121 (LG Classic port: owner said 95, an August
recording said 85).

TERMINOLOGY, SO THE FAULT IS NOT MISREAD
"Speaker not working" as a customer describes it frequently means **the other party cannot hear
THEM** - the microphone - not that the phone is quiet to the customer (loud speaker) or that they
cannot hear the caller (earpiece). Ask which direction fails before naming a price. The three are
different jobs at different prices.

OPEN, AND IT IS MONEY
Owner has not yet stated what happens to the **remaining 60** when the repair fails. The drafted
customer reply says the customer is out **50** (deposit kept, balance never charged). This must be
confirmed before it goes in writing to anyone, and this entry updated with the answer.
