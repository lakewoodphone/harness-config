**D3134 · 2026-10-14 · M.2 customer handoff enclosure = UGREEN 10Gbps; Sabrent EC-SNVE stays a bench reader.**

Decided while comparing two Amazon pages the owner pasted (Sabrent EC-SNVE 24.89 vs UGREEN USB-C M.2 10Gbps 17.98), read 2026-10-14. What decides it is not chipset: BOTH are Realtek RTL9210, which the shop's own enclosure rule already accepts for M.2 (host-transfer-drive-standard.md, correction 2026-10-11), and neither is a banned JMS583. It is two shop rules. (1) Our own leave-the-shop check (Niasoff, 2026-10-11) requires the box to carry a USB-A option - UGREEN ships C-to-C AND C-to-A, Sabrent ships C-to-C only, so a customer with no USB-C port cannot plug in. (2) The drive we actually pull out of thin laptops is M.2 2230 (Greenfeld Dell part 81VX9 / 256G P30S3 BC501 = Toshiba-Kioxia BG4/BC501 class; case file records the part number, not the form factor - confirm with a ruler at the bench). UGREEN claims 2230 in its title and bullets; the Sabrent's title and bullet stop at 2242 and only its own spec table claims 2230, so the Sabrent is a coin flip on the common case. The Sabrent's real advantages - the clamshell retains the drive, and it reads M.2 SATA as well as NVMe - are bench advantages, so the two we own stay as bench readers; buy another Sabrent only when an M.2 SATA drive actually appears. Written into lpt-hub docs/customer-operations/host-transfer-drive-standard.md (new section, commit 70f71ed6f), gated on the shop's own buy-one-test-then-more rule: mount a real 2230, shake it, read a file over the C-to-A cable, and only then buy shelf quantity; fall back to the Sabrent if the 2230 does not seat or the latch lets it move.
