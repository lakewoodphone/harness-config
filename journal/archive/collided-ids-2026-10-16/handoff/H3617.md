*** THE ONE RULE THIS WHOLE EXERCISE PRODUCED ***
A BURST IS NOT A FLOOR. The shop's jobs are 100% write, so the only spec that decides anything is
sustained sequential write AFTER the SLC cache is exhausted, over a write of >=100GB.
CrystalDiskMark and AS SSD are 1GB tests. ssd-tester's 605-drive RANKING is AS SSD/CDM - and so are
MOST OF ITS PER-MODEL TABLES (verified against a real page: on the Corsair Voyager GTX page "HD Tune
writes a 1GB file" and there is no long-term test at all). Sorting that ranking by write and buying
the winner repeats the PNY mistake with a veneer of rigour.

*** THE META-FINDING - DO NOT LET A FUTURE SESSION RE-LEARN THIS THE HARD WAY ***
For this product class the evidence that DECIDES A PURCHASE IS NOT PUBLISHED AS TEXT. It is test
screenshots, charts, and reviewer prose that fetchers truncate. I hit that wall FOUR times trying to
verify the SanDisk Extreme PRO SDCZ880. Two prior research passes and a fleet of adversarial agents
all landed in the same place: able to rank bursts, unable to find a floor. Searching harder does not
work. The only routes that do are reading benchmark images by eye, or writing 100GB to a drive you own.

*** THE BEST EVIDENCE THE SHOP HAS IS ITS OWN BENCH ***
From research/handoff-media-2026-10-13/C-shop-corpus.md, and it is post-cache BY DEFINITION because
it is the real workload on the real media:
 * nobody has moved customer data onto a USB stick faster than about 20 MB/s; most of the time 9-11
 * USB-stick average 9.34 MB/s over 296.4GB in 31,741s; median of the four recorded jobs 10.05
 * SanDisk Extreme 55AE 2TB portable SSD: 285.8 MB/s (1,531 GiB in 1h36m) - the only fast one
 * so a MEDIAN 25.6 GiB job costs 49 MINUTES on the shop's default PNY stick vs ~1.6 minutes on a
 bare SSD. THIS IS A BENCH-HOURS DECISION, NOT A MEDIA-COST ONE
 * and 12 of 16 jobs recorded NO copy duration, so the shop does not know what its own media costs
 it. Recording the next copy's duration and rate is the cheapest improvement available.
