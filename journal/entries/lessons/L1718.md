**L1718 · An artifact that is not in the repo is a draft - 4.8 MB of this program's evidence sat in an unversioned directory on one laptop**

## The near-miss
This program produced four deep audits, six sets of measurements, two research documents and a
dozen reproduction tools — **4.8 MB of evidence — into `C:\Users\ezabz\code\_dsh-scale\`**, which
`git rev-parse` confirms is **not under version control**. It existed on one laptop. It could not
have survived a disk failure, a reinstall, or a session on ZABZ-TECH, and nothing in it would have
reached the second machine.

That is the exact failure this seat's own instructions warn about — *"Anything that must survive or
be seen from another machine goes to the authoritative store"* — committed by the program whose
job was to make the harness trustworthy. The work was real; its record was one laptop away from
being nothing.

## The fix (2026-09-16)
The durable parts moved into the repo at **`docs/dsh-at-scale/`**: the 8 documents, `tools/`
(22 measurement/reproduction tools), `bench/`, `research/` and `notes/` — 75 files, 652 KB — plus
a new `PROGRAM.md` as the spine (the programme's rule, the measured before/after table, every change
with the evidence behind it, what was *refused* and why, the per-host budgets, the open items and
who owns each, and the four verify commands). Committed as `20ce314`.

**Excluded deliberately, not by oversight:** `data/` (3.5 MB of raw provider usage exports — bulky,
and they carry account key *names*) and `src/` (656 KB of downloaded Node documentation anyone can
re-fetch). Both stay on ZABZ-YOGA, and the new `_dsh-scale/README.md` says exactly that and points
readers at the versioned copy.

**A secret scan ran before the commit** (`sk-`, `ghp_`, `gho_`, `xox[baprs]-`, `Bearer`, and the
three `*_API_KEY=` values): none found. 37 files mention an env-var *name*, which is harmless and
intentional — the launchers are documented by the variable they read.

## The transferable rule
**An artifact that is not in the repo is a draft, however finished it looks.** A document whose path
does not begin with the repo is one `rm -rf`, one reinstall, or one machine-change away from never
having existed — and the more valuable it is, the worse the loss. Check with
`git -C <dir> rev-parse --show-toplevel` before calling anything documentation; "I wrote it down" is
not the same as "it is kept".

Two smaller instances of the same class, both fixed in the same pass: the orchestration skill
pointed at the unversioned path (now points at the repo), and the verification script had to be
written as `harness-verify.ps1` inside the repo rather than beside the audits — because a check that
only exists on the machine that needed it is not a check.
