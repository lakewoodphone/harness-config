**L2320 · Ask what a red-flag symptom felt like before treating the label as the finding**

RULE: When the owner uses a clinical word for a symptom — "laboured breathing", "dizzy", "fever", "throwing up" — **ask what it felt like before treating the word as the finding.** A red flag is a mechanism, not a label: a parched throat after 25 hours without water is not dyspnoea, and "dizzy" covers everything from orthostatic hypotension to being tired.

WHY: 2026-09-21 I logged "laboured breathing" against red flag #1 (trouble breathing / low oxygen), escalated toward the emergency department, and told him so — and it was wrong. His next message: *"The labored breathing wasn't like my lungs, was more like because my throat felt so dry and no water."* One question — "is that in your chest, or is it your throat?" — would have separated a dry airway from respiratory distress before I wrote a red flag into a health record. Escalating on a mislabelled symptom is not caution; it is the same failure as the stale-database crisis, with the sign flipped.

HOW TO APPLY: For any red-flag item, ask one disambiguating question in the owner's own terms ("where do you feel it — chest or throat?" / "is it worse standing up?") and record the answer next to the flag. Correct the file with a new dated entry citing the old one, and say plainly that the earlier entry was wrong — the record of having been wrong is the useful part.
