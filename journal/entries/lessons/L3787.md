**L3787 · Never transcribe an exact value out of a photograph into a record - ask the person who was there; one digit cost a wrong phone PIN**

**Lesson, from a near-miss on 2026-10-13.** A customer's phone PIN was written on a photographed slip.
I transcribed the slip myself, published a low-confidence reading into the credentials file —
`475288` — plus a paragraph saying the slip might not even be his. **The real value was `147888`.** One
digit. That is a wrong phone PIN, written into a credential record with a caveat attached, which is the
worst possible shape: the caveat is read once and the number is read forever.

The owner read the slip out in ten seconds and both my readings were wrong (the other string I called
`12617538` was the password `Pinchas38` written sideways).

**The rule, and it generalises past handwriting:** *when a field has to be exact and the source is an
image, a voice, or a scan, the reading is a QUESTION for the person who was there — not an inference for
a tool.* Do not "transcribe with a caveat" into a field a later reader will act on. Ask, then write what
came back, quoting who supplied it and when. Image cropping and OCR are for deciding *what to ask*, not
for producing the value.

**Its twin, already on the record:** recording a plausible-looking value as though it were a reading
(the house-shape password rule in L3321). Both failures come from filling a field because it exists.
