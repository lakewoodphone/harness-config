**L3685 · Owner corrections 2026-10-12 (2nd pass): how to draft a customer text he will actually let go out**

## 2026-10-12, ZABZ-TECH. Nine corrections on ONE message. Read this before drafting any customer text.

He asked for it plainly: *"i really hope you are learning from all my corrections to you"*. The
first message of the session took one round; the second took nine. That ratio is the lesson.

### 1. Verify the day before you write ANY time claim
I wrote *"We are open today until 4"* into a draft on a **Monday**. The shop is open Sunday 12-4 and
Tue/Thu 10:30-5:30 - never Monday. His reply: *"we are not open today, what are you talking about"*.
I had the hours in hand from the records and had even printed *"Sunday 12-4, and Tuesday and
Thursday"* in my own earlier table. `Get-Date` is free. A time claim in a customer text is a
promise, and I made it without looking at a clock.

### 2. Never open with a commitment you spend the rest of the message walking back
*"Hi, we can do it, but I want to be straight with you about that phone first... it is not always a
fix... the deposit is not refundable"*. He cut the opener: *"don't start off with we can do it if we
are saying we may not succeed"*. The first five words of a customer text are the only ones some
customers read. Lead with the honest frame, then the price, then the terms.

### 3. Short beats complete. Say the reason, not the mechanism
*"the wording is too complex and long"*, and then *"silly most ports it's soldered, this is just
difficult to remove"*. I wrote "the port is soldered to the board" as if that were the
differentiator; every port is soldered. The customer needs *our price* and *one honest reason*.
Internal detail is not reassurance, it is length.

### 4. Never quote our own comparison pricing to a customer
Two corrections on one idea: *"don't write the usual just write the prices"* and *"i said you don't
need to mention how much regular is just say why this is more"*. Writing *"95 rather than 65 or
70"* tells the customer what everyone else is charged and invites an argument about which price
applies to them. State the price for THIS job. The comparison is ours to hold in the pricing table,
never to narrate.

### 5. One idea per sentence, and make the contrast explicit
*"and you can drop it any time at our secure drop-off - that isn't clear that he can drop it off
not during hours maybe write and also you can drop or something"*.
"We are open tomorrow 10:30 to 5:30" followed by "you can drop it any time" reads as *any time
Tuesday*, i.e. inside the hours I just gave. The drop-off is the shop's after-hours channel and
says nothing about hours - so give it its own sentence and say out loud that it beats the hours:
*"You can also drop it off any time, even when we are closed."*

### 6. Ask for the reference before you draft, not after
He clipped *"you can drop it any time"* when I had left the address out to save space, and the
address is the sentence customers ask for by name (*"Hi! What's your address?"* appeared twice in
24 h). I asked him one at a time, four times in a row, after the draft existed. It would have been
one question before it: *what do you want in every first-contact text?* The answers, now known:
price for this job, one-line honest framing, deposit terms if any, hours, and the 24/7 drop-off
with the address.

### 7. The gate exists; the runbook path is the way through
`ps_action send_sms` -> `Policy denied: requires owner approval - task #28371`. Correct guardrail,
closed in a session with approvals disabled. The working path is
`/personal-secretary-mvp/scripts/send-lpt-sms.py --to <E164> --message-file <file> [--send]
[--force]`, and running it **without** `--send` prints From / To / Body with the auto-appended
`- Daniel` signature - that preview IS how the body gets shown for approval. Its money-validator
false-positives on any price text (it flagged "95, 50, 45, 10, 30" as missing dollar signs, after
flagging "price" with no amount); read the reason, then `--force`. Never `--no-signature` on a
customer text.

### 8. The record beat my assumption, twice, and both times I had to be pushed
First I recommended three prices off a repair-type table before identifying which of them applied.
Then, on the LG Classic, the *real* answer was a price the owner had spoken into a recorded call
(*85, no-fix-no-charge*) and a discount into a work-order trail where **every LG Classic job is
UNFIXABLE** - none of which I had read when I first offered to quote. Search the comms corpus for
the device AND the job type before pricing anything. The owner's own words on a call are the
pricing policy more often than the tables are.

### 9. Deposit terms are the owner's, not derivable
I reconstructed "50 deposit, 45 on success" and he never corrected the split, but he *had* to tell
me the deposit existed at all. Never infer payment structure from a price. When a job takes money
up front, the number is his and the failure case is his.
