**L11455 · L11455 · I declared seven live children dead at 35 minutes; all seven reported — list_agents is empty for remote children by design**

**Corrects `H11625`.** That handoff records *"the seven-agent mesh fan-out returned nothing"* and treats
it as the third silent mesh loss. **It is wrong.** All seven children returned: the first at ~21:50Z,
the last at ~22:35Z, i.e. 45 minutes to 1h35m after dispatch, every one with a full report
(`MESH-HOST: zabz-tech`, exit 0). They were slow, not lost.

What actually happened: I checked `list_agents` at ~21:40, saw nothing, believed the children were
invisible/unmanaged, and wrote the conclusion into a handoff and a commit message. The remote backend
does not list remote children at all — `list_agents` returning empty is **not** evidence that a remote
child failed, and it never was. The completion notices arrived asynchronously, exactly as the tool
description says they will.

Cost of the error: two documents and a commit message now understate the mesh's reliability, and I
briefly performed the work twice (population and history) that two children were already doing.

RULES:
1. **A remote subagent has no observable state until it reports.** `list_agents`/`list_agents
 descendants` are empty for remote children by design; absence there is not failure, and must never be
 written down as one. If a deadline matters, check the clock, not the roster.
2. **Do not write "it failed" before the budget expired.** These were dispatched with a 45-minute
 ceiling per the transport metadata; I concluded they were dead at 35 minutes and committed the
 conclusion. The honest interim statement was "no report yet, budget not expired".
3. **A child that arrives late still carries the most valuable thing in the session** — here, the two
 reports that refuted my causal claim. Writing the fleet off early is how a session ships a wrong
 conclusion with a clean conscience.
