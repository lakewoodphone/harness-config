**L3672 · Counter checkout adds NJ tax to whatever you enter, so enter PRE-tax - quoting the customer a tax-inclusive figure and typing it in double-taxes at 6.625% (829.93 charged vs 778.36 owed, refunded)**

**Measured 2026-10-11/12 on the Kogel job (`WO2608060003`), from the Stripe receipt.**

## What happened

The owner's standing instruction is to quote the customer a **tax-inclusive** total and never write
"plus tax" (2026-10-09: *"include the tax, don't write plus tax"*). For this job that figure was
**778.36** — the 730 agreed, already multiplied by 1.06625.

That number was then entered at the counter, and **the checkout added New Jersey sales tax of
6.625% on top of it**:

```
items data recovery 778.36
sales tax NJ 6.625% 51.57
TOTAL 829.93 <- tax charged twice
```

The owner caught it and refunded exactly **51.57**, his own note on the transaction reading *"By
mistake charged a drop too much"*. Net paid **778.36** — correct to the cent, but only after a
refund, a Stripe fee on the larger amount, and a customer seeing a wrong total.

## The rule

**The order field and the counter checkout are PRE-tax. The customer quote is tax-inclusive. Those
are two different numbers and the gap between them is 6.625%.**

- quote the customer: **778.36** (tax included, as instructed)
- enter on the order / counter: **730.00** (pre-tax) — the system adds the 51.57 itself

This is confirmed by the shop's own data: order `WO2610090001` (3020) carries **total 149.00** and
**collected 155.34** — a collection larger than the total, which only makes sense if `total` is
pre-tax. (`lpt-write total set` also places the number in the same field the app validates.)

**Generalisation: never type a tax-inclusive figure into a field that will have tax added to it.**
Any place we hand a number from a customer-facing message into an internal system, check which side
of the tax line that system expects — the same "one number, two meanings" hazard as quoting a price
without its unit.

## And the guard that caught me

`lpt-write payment set --amount 778.36 --stripe-charge ch_…` **refused**:

> Stripe says ch_3UPRLmLnlgYj1gmT0M1RJUGu is for 829.93, and this command was asked to record
> 778.36. Refusing: a mismatch is either the wrong charge or the wrong order.

I had tried to record the *net* figure against a charge for the *gross*. The guard was right and my
arithmeetic was wrong: a refund is a separate event from the charge. **A tool that validates your
number against the source of truth is worth more than one that accepts it** — and this is the same
family as the estate's rule that money received must be found, not inferred.

## What this cost, and what it would have cost

One refund of 51.57 and a phone call. Applied silently to every counter sale at this price point it
is **6.625% of revenue overcharged**, which customers notice, dispute, or quietly leave over. The
reason it was caught is that one order's arithmetic was checked line by line against the receipt.
