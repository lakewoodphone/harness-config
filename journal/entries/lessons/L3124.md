**L3124 · A task was closed done because its own reminder fired - then a later session inverted the request it came from**

**L — A task was closed `done` because its own reminder fired, not because the work was done. Then a later session inverted the request.**

Measured 2026-09-30. The owner said on 2026-09-20, verbatim: *"now that i got verizon fios i need to
cancel my tmobile plan and service, remind me to do it one day"*. That was captured properly as decision
**D330**, with reminder **452** and task **26305** ("Cancel the T-Mobile plan and service (Verizon FiOS
is live)"). The reminder fired on 2026-09-21 as designed.

**Task 26305 was then marked `done` on 2026-09-21.** The cancellation never happened. The owner tried to
cancel by chat, the chat did not work, and he disputed the September charge instead — which the bank
records exactly: `TMOBILE*AUTO PAY` **85.00 charged 2026-09-21 and reversed 2026-09-22**, memo
`Reversal: TMOBILE*AUTO PAY WA 09/19 ClaimId: 295226282770001`.

**Then the inversion.** On 2026-09-28 a different session read the bank feed, saw a reversal against a
T-Mobile merchant, and filed owner-queue row **#176**: *"I need to pay the overdue T-Mobile bill."* From
the bank's point of view a reversed debit looks like an unpaid bill, and that is the only thing the
session looked at. Decision **D330**, seven days old and one `list` away, said the opposite in the
owner's own words.

**Two distinct failures, one root cause — an outcome was never required.**

1. **A task was closed by the passage of its own nudge.** "The reminder fired" and "the owner was
 reminded" are not evidence that the work happened. A task whose whole content is *the owner must do
 something* stays open until the **outcome** exists — here, a cancellation confirmation number.
2. **A later session re-framed an item without reading its decision entry.** The correct order when
 picking up any queue row or task is: read the decision that produced it *before* forming a view from
 the raw data. D330 carried the intent verbatim; the bank feed carried only a symptom.

**The cost.** An account that should have been cancelled on 21 September was still live and still
billing on 30 September, with an unresolved dispute behind it and roughly 85 a month continuing. Worse
than the money: the owner had to explain his own instruction back to the system that recorded it, and
the row that came back to him asked him to *pay* the thing he had asked to *close*.

**The rules, stated so they can be applied:**

- **Never close a task because its reminder fired.** Close it on evidence of the outcome, named in the
 task.
- **"Remind me" produces two things: a reminder and a task that stays open.** The reminder is the nudge;
 the task is the work.
- **Before re-framing any open item, read the decision record that produced it.** A bank feed tells you
 what a payment did, not what the owner wanted.
- **A reversal is not a debt.** A reversed debit means the money came back; *why* it came back is a
 question for the record, and it is often deliberate.

**Evidence.** Decision D330 and reminder 452 and task 26305 (all on the authority, read 2026-09-30);
task 26305 `status=done` with no cancellation; ledger row 2026-09-22, `Reversal: TMOBILE*AUTO PAY WA
09/19 ClaimId: 295226282770001`; owner-queue #176 withdrawn and replaced by #207; task 27215 opened with
a four-part definition of done.
