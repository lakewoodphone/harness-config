**L3804 · A text-only reading is not a reading of the conversation - he had been CALLED and quoted 442, and I reported 44 hours of silence; and the standard for presenting a text to him**

## 2026-10-14, ZABZ-TECH. His catch was the finding: ***"are you sure we didn't call him — I thought my wife called him."***

### What I had wrong

`+19725232905` asked in text for a price on an iPhone 16 Pro Max screen (2026-10-12 17:39Z), then
about an iPhone 12 camera (10-12 19:40Z), then *"The iPhone 12 is a pro"* and *"For a new camera"*
(10-13 18:32Z). The newest row in `dialpad_sms_cache` was **inbound**, so I read the thread as
*"nothing has been answered for 44 hours"* and drafted a text quoting 450/170 and asking him to bring
it in.

**The shop had called him.** `dialpad_call_full` on secratary-cloud, queried by `contact_phone`:

```
2026-10-13T18:26:40.410Z outbound 231 s contact=9725232905 ext=+19725232905 int=+17326552355
2026-10-13T18:26:39.511Z outbound 231 s contact=9725232905 ... (duplicate row)
```

And in that call, verbatim from the transcript:
- **the screen was quoted:** *"It's about 442 … That's for the sixteen pro Max screen. Write this down
 so you can compare."*
- **the camera was deferred, honestly:** *"We're going to have to get back to you about the camera …
 we're trying to source it, a genuine is coming up very expensive."*
- **the customer asked how to reach us:** *"can I text the number"* — answered yes.

So there was no silence. There was a live 231-second call with **exactly one open item: the camera
quote**, and I nearly sent a customer a screen price he had already been given, framed as an apology
for not answering.

### THE RULE

**Before drafting a text to a customer, read the CALLS as well as the texts.** A thread whose newest
row is inbound does not mean nobody answered — it means nobody answered *by text*. The phone is this
shop's primary channel and a call leaves no SMS row at all.

```sql
select date_started_ms, direction, coalesce(contact_phone,''), internal_number, external_number,
 length(coalesce(transcription_text,''))
from dialpad_call_full
where contact_phone like '%<phone10>%' or external_number like '%<phone10>%'
 or internal_number like '%<phone10>%'
order by date_started_ms desc;
```

A hit means **read the transcript before writing anything**. The same caution applies to
`ps_comms_waiting`, which is an index verdict and can name someone who has been spoken to.

**Corollary — a price spoken on a call is the price.** This one number carries **442** (call 10-13, his
own wife), **450 / 170 / 150** (a text to a *different* customer on 10-06) and **110** (an order text
07-22) for the same model. When they disagree, say so in one line and ask which stands instead of
picking the one that suits the draft.

### Second lesson from the same session: how he wants a text presented

His words, twice: ***"when you present a text you just give me the clear question and context and the
recommended draft"*** and ***"again too much evidence and random things you are showing me"***.

I had been sending him SQL column names, cache row ids, message ids, capture lags, my search
itinerary, three supplier prices and a landed-cost calculation. **He reads the first line and the
draft.** The standard is now a file —
`lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/sops/PRESENTING-CUSTOMER-TEXTS-TO-THE-OWNER.md` — with the four-part
shape (who · what they want · **what we already told them, dated** · the recommended draft), one
question if a fact is his, and a table of what not to put in front of him. `CUSTOMER-SMS-RUNBOOK.md`
§0 carries the call rule as **rule 5**.

**The general form, which is why this is a lesson and not a case note:** *a channel I did not read is
not evidence that nothing happened on it.* Two of this session's three worst statements came from
reading one surface and treating it as the whole record — this one, and the Pruzansky payment that was
sitting in the accounting store while I searched Stripe metadata (L3799). Both were cheap to check.
