**L12395 · Test a device credential where the error names the fault first, and stop after one failure on the device that can lock you out**

**What happened.** Handed one device credential (`admin` + a password) for the shop's cameras on 2026-10-27, I probed
to find the hardware, then tested it.

**The device map, which is the durable part** (read from Home Assistant's device registry via the websocket
`config/device_registry/list`, not guessed): the recorder is **`192.168.50.170`**, a `DHI-NVR4108-8P-4KS2-L`, serial
`8C03EA3PAZC4C2D`, firmware `4.003.0000000.1.R` build 2024-05-15, serving **HTTPS with a self-signed cert** and
redirecting HTTP to it. All camera channels are channels on that one box, so the cameras are **not individually
addressable on the LAN** — they sit behind its own 8-port PoE switch. The Reolink doorbell is separate at
`192.168.50.231` (`v3.0.0.4662_2508071283`). A 254-host sweep of ports 80/8000 found only four web devices on the
whole network: router `.1`, Shelly `.103`, Reolink `.231`, Dahua `.170` (and one more Dahua UI at `.95`).

**The credential failed, in two different ways.**
- Reolink: `{"code":1,"error":{"detail":"password wrong","rspCode":-502,"auth_alert_info":{"remain_times":10}}}` —
 its own words, one attempt consumed.
- Dahua NVR: `/RPC2_Login` is alive and issues `realm`/`random`/`qop`, but the challenge-response
 (`md5(user:random:md5(user:realm:pass))`, `loginType:Direct`, cookie carried) returned
 `{"error":{"code":268632079,"message":"Component error: login challenge!"},"result":false}` — the **same generic
 error it returns for any login it will not accept**, so it cannot distinguish a wrong password from a wrong
 handshake.

**Why I stopped after three attempts.** A Dahua NVR locks the account after a handful of failures. That recorder
holds the only copy of the video evidence and the owner is working in the shop; locking `admin` on it to save a few
minutes would be a self-inflicted outage on the one device that records. The correct stopping point was two attempts
on the NVR and one on the Reolink, then a human.

**The transferable rule.** Verify a credential first against the device whose error **names the fault** and which
reports remaining attempts — here the Reolink — and stop after one failure on the device that answers everything
with a generic error and can lock you out, here the Dahua. I tested in the order of value instead of the order of
safety. Also: when a vendor's login fails generically, a wrong *username* is as likely as a wrong password — Dahua
installers routinely rename the built-in `admin` account — so the second attempt should have been a question, not a
hash.

**Also, and separately:** a credential pasted into chat is in a transcript this estate archives permanently
(`AGENTS.md` §1). The fix is a dedicated, revocable account on the device rather than the shop's own admin login.
