**L1532 · A truncated hardware read is not a negative finding (the Mac mini HAD a microphone)**

A hardware inventory read through a truncating pipeline is not a negative finding.

On 2026-09-15 I concluded the office Mac mini had NO microphone at all, and was one step from sending
the owner a $30 purchase question, because `system_profiler SPAudioDataType` piped through
`Select-Object -First 80` showed only the two output devices ("External Headphones", "Mac mini Speakers").
The device list was not wrong -- my read of it was incomplete, and I believed the incomplete read.

The machine in fact had a working USB microphone plugged in the whole time:
`SwitchAudioSource -a -t input` (brew install switchaudio-osx) lists `USB Audio Device`
(Manufacturer GeneralPlus, 1 input channel, 48 kHz, default input), and `system_profiler SPAudioDataType`
run WITHOUT a truncating pipe prints it too, with `Input Channels: 1`.

RULE: before reporting that hardware is absent, enumerate it with a tool that exists to enumerate it, print
the whole result, and re-read it. Same shape as the stale-database crisis: the failure was not the data, it
was confidently believing a partial view of it. A reading you did not see in full is a refusal, not a fact.

EVIDENCE: 2026-09-15 LAKEWOOECHSMINI; the wrong conclusion is in this session's tool transcript before
14:37 UTC; the correction is `SwitchAudioSource -a -t input` at 14:36 and the app log line
"Microphone is receiving samples" during a real capture.
