**L12363 · Never prove absence from a listing that was piped through head - the disk was below the cut**

2026-10-25, case Werner (WO26102012764). I reported to the owner that her card was not on the bench and that the fix therefore could not be done. The card had been sitting in /dev/sde the whole time, unmounted, 119.1 G, exFAT.

The command was: lsblk -o NAME,SIZE,TYPE,RM,MODEL | head -20. Every one of the 20 lines was a snap loop device (/snap/firefox, /snap/gnome-42-2204, ...). The real disks were below the cut. The empty-looking answer was not an answer at all - it was 20 lines of noise and I read the silence under it as absence.

The rule: never put a device or process inventory through head, tail, or any window. A listing whose only job is to prove something is ABSENT must be complete, or it proves nothing. Ask for the specific answer instead of a slice of the output:
 lsblk -o NAME,SIZE,TYPE,RM,TRAN,SERIAL,MODEL -d | awk 'NR==1 || ==1' # removable only
 lsblk -b -o NAME,SIZE,TYPE,FSTYPE | awk '>100e9 && =="part"' # candidate 119 GiB volumes
 lsusb # full, no grep-in-the-same-command

Same family as a refusal read as a negative (searched:false, exit 4, did_not_answer). A pruned output is a refusal with the excuse built in, and it is worse than a refusal because it looks like a measurement.

The tell, and it is checkable: if a negative conclusion rests on output that also contained rows I did not need, the conclusion is unproven. Her card was identified three ways once I looked properly - 249,737,216 sectors, UUID 6EF7-F0FC matching our rebuild, and the six folders under DCIM/902___11 with the expected counts.
