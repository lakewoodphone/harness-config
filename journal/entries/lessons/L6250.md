## The contradiction worth resolving, because it will paralyse the next drafter

Runbook §9 says the accepted shape "opens with an apology that owns the fault" (his words, 2026-10-12: *"too much info, more concise, just apology and say we bonded the connector to ensure it doesn't come loose again"*). Journal **L328** (2026-09-15) records him rejecting an apology-first opener: *"you keep forgetting, we don't start off like that."* on a draft opening *"Hi Mrs. Weitman, Daniel from Lakewood Phone & Tech. Sorry for the slow reply, that's on me."*

**Both are right, and they are two different apologies.**
- **A late reply / our slowness → the apology goes AFTER the substance** (R26b N11). L328's own text says the only apology on file comes after it: *"Sorry about the miscommunication."*
- **A fault in the work, or a promise we broke → own it first.** That is the §9 shape, and it is what runbook §0.6 requires when the shop dropped the ball.

L328's actual complaint was the whole opener — the appositive `Hi <Name>, Daniel from …` **and** a slowness apology, in that order. So: do **not** read L328 as a ban on every apology opener, and do **not** read §9 as licence to apologise for being slow. A draft opening *"Hi, that's on us. We said we'd leave the laptop out and it wasn't there when you came."* is the §9 shape and is compliant; a draft opening *"Sorry for the slow reply, that's on me."* is the L328 failure regardless of what follows.

## A written rule that two approved sends falsify

The case-file rule *"Never open a customer message with 'Good news.'"* (Goldie Atlas case) is contradicted by two customer sends the owner **personally approved** afterwards: Weitman 2026-10-05, `dialpad_sms_cache` message `6651073537548288`, and Lenchitz 2026-10-07, message `5160029262225408` — both opening *"Hi, good news -"*.

The class of error is the same as L328: **a case-file rule is one instance, not a law, and an approved send outranks a written rule.** Before enforcing an old prohibition on a new draft, look for the newest approvals of the same shape. (Recorded rather than deleted: the record of having been wrong is the useful part.)

## Two operational facts from the same run

- **The runbook is not one file.** Measured 2026-10-15: ZABZ-TECH's `lpt-hub` working tree carries §3b and §9 (272 non-blank lines, HEAD `164be9302`) and `origin/main` carries both; a checkout on ZABZ-YOGA was reported to carry neither (193 lines, sections 0–8). **Which rules a drafter sees therefore depends on the machine.** Verify the section exists in the file you actually opened, and treat "the runbook says X" with no line number as unverified.
- **Transient output again.** The child wrote to `_scratch` on ZABZ-YOGA. `scp` failed with `subsystem request failed on channel 0` (the sftp subsystem); **`scp -O` (legacy protocol) worked.** Anything produced into another machine's `_scratch` must be copied into a repo the same day — see **P3805**.

## What this changes for drafting

Nothing in N1-N27 blocks the five replies prepared on 2026-10-15: all are name-free openers (N12), none states hours from memory (N15), none quotes a fee in a status text (N6), none invents a price (N27 keeps prices his), and the one apology-first draft owns a broken promise rather than a delay. The rule that decided the shape was read **before** the drafts were written, not after.
