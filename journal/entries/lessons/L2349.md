**L2349 · A case's front-matter price is the CASE total - a per-unit figure becomes the whole AR invoice**

A case file's front-matter `price` is the CASE total. When the body of the case describes a
per-unit price — "6 phones at $130 each", "per device", "each", "/phone", "per line" — a
front-matter `price` holding that unit figure is read by `tools/case-index/lptcase.py` as the
whole-case amount, lands in `lpt-hub/index/cases.db` as `money_owed` and `quoted_price`, and
from there becomes an AR invoice in `lpt-books` (`books/ingest/legacy.py` selects
`cases WHERE money_owed>0`). Nothing in the chain knows the number was a unit price.

Measured on `heichal-hatorah-order-2026-08-24`, 2026-09-22: the case carried `price: "130"`
and the index reported `money_owed = 130.0` for an order that was 6 units at $130 each plus
$10 setup each = **$840**, already delivered. The shop's own books would have shown $130 owed
on it. The written quote that settles it is the owner's own SMS: `dialpad_sms_cache` id
`763073`, 2026-08-24T16:54Z, "6 phones at $130 each, $10 setup per phone, and $11 a month per
phone for the service".

The rule: **a front-matter `price` on a multi-unit case must be the multiplied total, and the
per-unit figure plus the arithmetic belongs in the body.** `lptcase.py` exposes `prices` as a
list, so a case whose `prices` holds exactly one number while its body says "each" is the
signature to look for. When you write a bulk or multi-quantity case, put the total in
front-matter and spell out `N x unit = total` in the activity section.

Two corollaries worth keeping:
- Recurring charges have nowhere to live. There is no field for "$66/mo" in the case standard,
 so a monthly line item is at permanent risk of being billed to nobody. Measured here: 6
 lines x $11/mo = $66/mo, and production `subscriptions` for that customer holds 0 rows
 three weeks after delivery. A recurring figure must be written in the body and, if it is
 real, raised as its own item.
- The evidence for "how much do they owe" is never the case file alone. Here the case file's
 own index was wrong by 6.5x, and the three independent confirmations of $0 collected were
 `payment_transactions`, `orders.paymentStatus` and the bank feed. Check the money systems;
 the case file is a summary someone typed.

**L2349 · secratary has no photo or face identification; family_photo_context_resolver identifies nobody**

Searched 2026-09-22 while answering an owner image question.

FINDING: nothing in the company identifies a person from a photograph.
- 203-table DB: no photo/image/face/embedding table. Identity is phone-keyed only (person_canonical, person_phone, comms_identity_link, contacts). contacts holds name/relationship/phone - no image, no biometric.
- Host filesystem: only photos on secratary are network-install photos and plumbing photos.
- ~/personal-secretary-mvp/data/tools/family_photo_context_resolver.py (75 lines) is named as if it resolves who is in a family photo. It does not. It takes family_member as an INPUT param and echoes it back into notes/summary; zero lookup, zero matching, no ML, no image read. If called with no params it returns ok:false. Anything reading its name and believing it is a face-identification capability is being misled by the filename.
- app/services/board_photo_analyzer.py is circuit-board repair analysis, not people.
- No local vision/face-embedding deps in .venv (no face_recognition/dlib/insightface path found).

CONSEQUENCE: an owner request to identify a person from a photo cannot be served by lookup, and must not be answered by guessing. Correct response is to say so plainly and ask for the name.

LESSON: filenames and table names that assert an AI capability are not evidence of it. Verify by reading the body and running it.
