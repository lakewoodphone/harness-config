**L3758 · A rate is not a number until you read the whole pricing row: three confident wrong alarms in one session, all partial readings promoted to totals**

THIRD CORRECTION IN ONE NIGHT, AND THE PATTERN IS NOW THE FINDING.

WHAT I CLAIMED (to the owner, 2026-10-13 ~01:40Z): "each wake release costs about 2.5, not
0.036-0.068; the wake skill's figure was off by ~50x; your 25/day budget is about ten releases
a day." I then set WAKE_MAX_PER_DAY=12 on that basis.

WHAT IS TRUE, measured by me ten minutes later:
 * The harness's cache-hit rate is 97.0-97.8% on every one of today's releases
 (9,537,470 total -> 9,272,704 cache_read; 8,307,202 -> 8,094,976; 5,654,370 -> 5,527,936).
 * The app's own table says it at app/pricing.py:154 -- "The whole US business day is now
 OFF-PEAK: $0.15 in / $0.60 out / **$0.003 cache hit**".
 * I had bound cache_read_usd_per_mtok = 0.30, i.e. ONE HUNDRED TIMES the real cache-hit rate.
 * Re-priced with 0.003: per-release cost is 0.083-0.169, average about 0.13. Today's six
 priced releases total 0.78 -- NOT 12.11.
 * The original wake-skill figure (0.036-0.068) was in the right range. MY CORRECTION WAS THE
 ERROR, off by about 15x, in the direction of panic.

THE PATTERN, which is why this is one lesson and not three apologies. In one session I made
three confident wrong statements, all the same shape:
 (1) the transcript archive "OOM-killed hourly, falling behind" -- I read a kill line and did
 not ask the unit's Result/ExecMainStatus or the consumer's check. It was healthy. P3154
 retracted.
 (2) "21 OOM lines for company-api" -- `grep -i oom` matched the word "**r**oom" in a chat
 line, and all three real kills predated the memory resize.
 (3) "2.5 per release" -- I bound a rate without reading the whole pricing row, and the row
 had a cache-hit column 100x lower than the column I used.
Every one was a PARTIAL READING promoted to a TOTAL CLAIM. Every one was in the direction of
alarm, and every one was checkable in under a minute by reading one more line.

THE RULES I AM TAKING FROM IT, all three concrete:
 A. A KILL IS NOT AN OUTCOME. Pair any failure/kill reading with the unit's own Result and the
 consumer's own freshness check, in the same sentence, or do not report it.
 B. A COUNT IS NOT A TREND, AND A SUBSTRING IS NOT A MEANING. Print the matching lines before
 quoting a count. `grep -i oom` matching "room" is not a near miss, it is the whole error.
 C. A RATE IS NOT A NUMBER UNTIL YOU HAVE READ THE WHOLE ROW. For any billing or capacity
 figure: read every column of the pricing/limit table BEFORE binding a value, and print the
 tokens-per-category breakdown. A cache-heavy agent is priced by its cache-hit column, and
 using the input column overstates it by two orders of magnitude.

AND THE ONE THAT COST THE OWNER TIME, stated plainly: a confident wrong number spends the trust
a correct number needs. Three of them in one session, all in the same direction, is not bad luck
-- it is a habit of reporting the alarming reading before finishing the measurement. The fix is
cheap and mechanical: one more command before one more sentence.
