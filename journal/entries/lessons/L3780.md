## A total that mixes causes is not a finding - 6 damaged chats were 5 attributable plus 1 unattributed

Kramer case, 2026-10-13. I reported **6 conversations damaged by the allocator**. Five of them are; the sixth is
not, and the arithmetic that hid it is the point.

The allocator had reached `Z_MAX = 1519`. Its own victims are the rows at ids **1511-1519**:

 1512, 1513, 1515, 1516, 1517 REPLACED -> Felder Art x3, Facebook Business, SURI
 1514, 1519 DELETED -> Hadara Ceramics, Penina Sebag

Those five conversations are attributable and the mechanism is proven for each.

 Coco Bar: 1744, 1881, 2015 DELETED -> **all OUTSIDE 1511-1519**

I had counted the deletions alongside the replacements and reported one number for the pair. Three of those
deletions are at ids the allocator had not reached, so they cannot be its work — a different, unexplained loss
(most likely ordinary pruning of rows the app cannot map, but I have not established that either).

**The rule:** when two causes can produce the same symptom, count them separately and say which is proven. A
merged total reads as evidence for the cause you are already arguing for, and it borrows proof it has not earned.
The corpus row here is `Z_PK`, and the allocator's reach is a number I had measured — the check cost one
comparison.

Evidence: rows and ids in the audit `--compare` output; case file attribution note (2026-10-13 section).
