**L3376 · A region test disc is a hypothesis until one is proven locked - the cover B mark and the eBay Region Code field are not proof**

**L — A region-locked test disc must be proven locked, not assumed locked from the cover or the seller's field.**

Measured 2026-10-07 while running the four-disc RattleByte region test on the DMP-BD84. The
region-B disc in the kit — *The Apartment*, Arrow Academy UK — carries the hexagonal **B** region
mark on its cover, and its eBay listing's `Region Code` item specific read `B`. It **played on the
unit while the unit was set to region A** (the region-set sequence was confirmed to run: tray
closed, unit powered off and back on by itself).

That single result is ambiguous, and the ambiguity is the lesson: it is equally consistent with

- the disc having been pressed region-free (common — Arrow's UK output is not uniformly locked,
 and the cover symbol states the *intended* region, not what the disc refuses), or
- the modification not enforcing region at all on that unit.

Neither can be told from the cover, from the seller's item specific, or from a lookup: blu-ray.com
returns `error42` to a datacenter fetch and dvdcompare returned nothing usable the same day.

**Rule.** To prove a region test, use two discs and one setting: with the player set to region X,
the X disc must **play** and a disc **independently known to be locked to another region** must be
**refused**. Establish "known locked" from a stock, unmodified player of the other region, or from
the disc's own control data (a PC BD drive: MakeMKV/BDInfo report the region), never from the
cover art. A kit built from eBay item-specifics is a hypothesis until one disc has been shown to be
refused.
