**L3376 · GitHub's push protection caught a leak of my own: a 16MB GENERATED cache was versioned and carried Twilio identifiers into every push**

GOAL ROUND 22, second finding of the round and it was in my own repo. Setting up an offsite mirror for the journal, the push was REFUSED: 'GH013: Repository rule violations found ... Push cannot contain secrets - Twilio Account String Identifier, Twilio API Key', at journal/index/journal.db. THE DIAGNOSIS: push protection scans the COMMITS BEING PUSHED, not the whole tree - which is why the earlier pushes of the same repo succeeded while the entry files that quote those identifiers have been on the remote for weeks. What broke it was my new commit adding journal/index/journal.db: a 15.9 MB GENERATED cache (it rebuilds itself when stale) that nobody should be versioning at all, and which materialises the identifiers in binary form. THE VALUES: not authenticating secrets - Twilio AC account SIDs and one SK API-key ID, recorded by earlier sessions inside entries that were ABOUT a credential leak (D165 documents that the real secret, the auth token, was committed in plaintext elsewhere and rotated; P160 and H603 record the rest). Account and key IDs are identifiers: a SID alone cannot authenticate. They are nonetheless sensitive metadata in a durable record. WHAT I DID, in order: (1) wrote offsite copies for the two things that were single-copy - the company branch (30 unpushed commits of this session's resilience work, now off the laptop) and the journal (29 unpushed commits whose ONLY remote was secretary-ts, the office box that is dark tonight); (2) untracked the generated cache and gitignored it with a comment explaining exactly why it must not come back; (3) because the offending commit was MY OWN and unpushed, undid it with a SOFT reset (no force-push, nothing published was rewritten) so the cache never enters the pushed history, then recommitted without it; (4) pushed - offsite accepted, journal.py check still 0 errors. NOT DONE AND STATED PLAINLY: the identifiers remain in the PRIVATE repo's history from weeks ago and in the entry files; redacting now would not unpublish them, and rewriting published history to hide a fact is the thing the append-only rule exists to prevent. The precise remediation if that API key is still live is to delete it in the Twilio console - one click, and it makes the recorded ID worthless. RULES: (a) never version a generated cache - it is churn, bloat, and a carrier for whatever it happens to materialise; (b) push protection scans the commits being pushed, so 'it pushed fine last week' proves nothing about the string you are adding today; (c) when a scanner refuses your push, read WHAT and WHERE before deciding - the location told me the cause was the cache, not the entries, and that distinction decided whether I rewrote an append-only record (I did not); (d) check the durability of your own work, not just your subject's - the DR package was one laptop failure from gone and my memory's only mirror was the outage itself.
