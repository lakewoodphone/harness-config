**L2359 · A short bearing on a pipe self-locks under axial pull; fix it with LENGTH or a roller, never grease**

**A short bearing on a pipe self-locks under an axial pull. The cure is bearing LENGTH or a roller — never grease, never a plastic liner.**

**Rule.** A ring/collar/sleeve of bore D on a pipe of diameter d, with bearing length L, can only tilt until it touches
the pipe at both ends: `theta_max = atan((D-d)/L)`. It jams once `theta_max` exceeds the friction angle
`phi = atan(mu)`, because the load is then free to rotate the part to a wedging tilt.

**Measured on the Shlock sukkah rain cover, 2026-09-23.** A 2.000 in bore hitching ring on a 1.660 in galvanised rail
has 0.170 in of radial play on a bearing length of only its **0.262 in of wire** → `theta_max = 33 deg`, against a
steel-on-galvanised friction angle of 17-27 deg. The owner put one on a rail and reported "as soon as you pull on them
they get friction locked". Arithmetic predicted it; the site confirmed it.

**Same defect, thinner margin, same project:** the 3 in UHMW sleeve (2.000 in ID, same 0.170 in play) tilts
`atan(0.170/3) = 3.2 deg` against a UHMW-on-steel friction angle of 6-8.5 deg — half the margin, and it is the
mechanism the record already flagged as the most likely real-world failure. At 6 in long it is 1.6 deg and cannot cock.

**Why grease and liners do not work.** They lower `mu`, and therefore `phi`; they do not change `theta_max`, which is
set by the bearing's length. A ring free to tilt 33 deg will reach a locking tilt even on PTFE. Length is the only
geometric cure; a roller (trolley) is the only better one.

**The second lesson, about documents not arithmetic.** The Shlock rope package priced ring drag as "its own weight
(~0.15 lb) x mu ~ 0.3 → under 0.1 lb per ring" — i.e. it assumed the ring carries no axial load in travel, and that
metal-on-metal contact occurs only under lift. True only if something else drags the cover. The arithmetic in the
package was fine; **the assumption was never tested and it was wrong.** A number computed from an unstated assumption
is a claim, not a measurement — and this one was one site test away from being caught.
