**L3794 · CORRECTION to L3793: a dead automated notification path is not '273 customers were never told' - test the consequence, not just the count**

# CORRECTION to L3793 and H3659: I conflated a dead vestigial automation with the customer channel. The owner was right.

**L3793 and H3659 are wrong in their conclusion and must not be read alone.** I wrote that "273 real
customers were never told their repair was ready" and that this was "the biggest finding". The owner
corrected me on 2026-10-14: *"you are anyways not allowed to automatically send a text, and i tell you
explicitly when to send them through dialpad and they get sent out."* He is right. Measured, on the
same store:

**The customer channel works, and it is busy.**
- `DIALPAD_CUSTOMER_SMS_LIVE_SENDS_ENABLED=true`. Delivered OUTBOUND SMS: **60,434 all-time**;
 27,921 in 2026-07, 26,687 in 2026-08, 5,316 in 09, 810 so far in 10. Comms total 121,845 DELIVERED.
- **The decisive test I should have run first.** Of the **273** customers whose `REPAIR_COMPLETED`
 notification FAILED, **250 were subsequently reached by a DELIVERED outbound message** and **251 were
 reached at some point**. Only **22** were never reached by any delivered message. So the answer to
 "was the customer told?" is *yes, by the real channel, 251 of 273 times.*

**What the 300 FAILED rows actually are.** A **vestigial automated notification path** that writes
against Twilio (decommissioned) and lands a FAILED row in `communications`. Nobody relies on it for
contact — contact is the approved Dialpad path. So it is not "273 customers left in the dark"; it is one
dead automation and a **bookkeeping** defect: those FAILED rows are counted on the dashboard as
notifications, so an order can *read* as "customer told" when the automated attempt died. The correct
finding is small and internal, and it is worth retiring or repointing that path so the marker stops
lying — not because customers were missed.

**The residue that IS real, and it is small.** Of the 394 finished-and-never-released orders:
- **41 customers have never been sent a delivered outbound message at all** (earlier I said 33 for a
 narrower cut; 41 is the figure for the 394).
- The rest — about 353 — were contacted at some point; 303 of those last heard from us 30-90 days ago.
- And `pickupReleasedAt` is set on 3 orders of 649, so "uncollected" cannot be read from the store at
 all. It is a shelf question.

**The rule this violated.** I took a genuine, precise, verifiable fact (300 `REPAIR_COMPLETED` rows, all
FAILED, 84 % naming Twilio) and attached a causal claim to it that I never tested (therefore customers
were never told). I even wrote "I checked this specifically because I did not want to report 300
customers on the strength of one row" — I checked the *recipient count* and not the *consequence*,
which is the claim that mattered. **Recipients being real does not make "they were never told" true; the
only test for that is whether a delivered message reached them, and it takes one query.**

**How to avoid it: when a failure count is large, the next query is always "what happened to these
people NEXT, by another path?" — before any consequence is asserted at all.**

**Also recorded, because it bears on the rule that matters most:** `/opt/company/.env` carries
`SMS_SEND_EXTERNAL_REQUIRES_APPROVAL=false` while the standing operating rule is that every
customer-facing message needs an explicit per-message yes. The flag is the platform's; the discipline is
the owner's and mine. **Any automated path that can send externally without approval is a path where a
fabricated draft like Felder's could actually reach a customer** — which is the reason to keep the
discipline in the caller, not rely on the flag.

**Owner's rule, restated for the next session:** customer texts go out when HE says so, through Dialpad,
and they do go out. Do not build, fix or propose an automatic customer-notification path. Do not treat a
FAILED automated-notification row as a customer who was not contacted.
