**L217b · A question card on a phone must own the viewport: vh is a lie on iOS, and a tap test is the only honest check**

## Why the owner could not answer a question from his phone

He reported it twice in the same hour (2026-09-14, 15:40 and 15:50 UTC): the `ask_user_question`
card on his phone showed only part of itself, would not scroll, and would not click — so he
could not answer from the phone at all. His instruction: *"don't use that tool; if you want to
use it you have to seriously fix it up for the UI so I can actually see the questions/options
and click on them."*

**What was wrong, measured.** The card is `QuestionComposer` in
`@deepseek-ai/dsh-client-ui-user-questions`. It caps itself with `max-height: min(60vh, 520px)`
and scrolls its own body. Reproduced at four phone heights with the app's own CSSOM and the real
class map (`scripts/question-card-check.py`):

 viewport card Submit composer overlap
 393x852 y=194 h=511 OK no
 393x700 y=194 h=420 OK no
 393x640 y=194 h=384 OK YES
 393x560 y=194 h=336 UNREACHABLE tap landed on the composer's own row

Two causes, both of them phone-specific:

1. **`vh` is the LARGE viewport.** With iOS Safari's bars up, and again with the keyboard open,
 the real visible height is far less than `100vh`, so `60vh` can be larger than the space that
 exists. The card's footer — which is where Submit lives — is pushed out of the visible area,
 and the page behind has nothing left to scroll.
2. **The card is in the stream and the composer sits under it.** Once the card is taller than the
 gap above the composer, the composer's surface covers the footer, and a tap at Submit's centre
 lands on the composer instead. That is "I can't click it", exactly.

**The fix** (`assets/question-card.css`, served in the phone layer): on phones the question is a
sheet that owns the viewport — `position: fixed; inset: 0`, card `height: 100%` then `100dvh`,
safe-area padding top and bottom, header/footer/strip `flex-shrink: 0`, only the option list
scrolling, `-webkit-overflow-scrolling: touch`, and `z-index: 2147483600` so it outranks the
attention badge (below). Verified after the change at 393x852/700/640/560 and 430x852/700/640/560:
the card fits the viewport, Submit and every option are inside it, and `document.elementFromPoint`
at each control's centre returns that control.

**The lesson that generalises past this card.**
- `max-height: X vh` is wrong on any phone. `dvh`, or owning the viewport, is right.
- **A layout claim needs a TAP test, not a geometry test.** "The button is inside the viewport"
 was true at 393x640 while the composer was covering it. `document.elementFromPoint` at the
 control's centre is what actually models a thumb, and it is one line.
- **A rendered card cannot be provoked on demand, so reproduce it from the app's own pieces.**
 The host refuses `ask_user_question` when the asking agent is owned by another live agent
 (measured: a subagent's call failed with exactly that message), so no fixture can be driven
 through the UI. What works is lifting the theme CSSOM from the running page, the card's real
 stylesheet out of the bundle, and rebuilding its element tree from the shipped class map.
- **A fix that changes nothing is the default failure.** The first version of this fix used
 `:has(> [class*="_card"] > [class*="_options"])`, but the shipped tree is
 frame > card > **body** > options, so the selector never matched and the numbers were identical
 before and after. Identical numbers after a change are the signal to check the selector, not to
 re-run it.

**Two adjacent findings, recorded rather than fixed.**
- The "needing attention" badge (`#dsh-attention-badge`, `z-index: 2147483000`, `.pill` carries
 `pointer-events: auto`) sits in the bottom-right 34px of the screen. In a session with messages
 it does not touch the composer, but on a short viewport it landed on Submit's centre, and the
 same zone is where a hero-state Send sits. The question card now outranks it.
- The mobile layer's `button[aria-label] { min-width: 44px }` is not the effective value for the
 composer's Send button, which measures 34px wide — a shipped rule with equal-or-higher
 precedence wins. Recorded in P66's file rather than papered over.
