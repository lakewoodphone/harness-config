**L3390 · A truncated scan is not a negative; and cold cache is not scope**

**A scan cut off by a timeout was reported as a clean negative. And the slow case was cold cache,
not scope - which I got wrong first.**

The live ripgrep branch gave each root `timeout 25`. A root killed at 25 s produced no output, no
error, and `rc=1` - indistinguishable from "this tree does not contain it".

Reachable in normal use, measured on the authority: **`~/code` takes 29.8 s cold and 0.205 s warm**
(2 rounds interleaved, same command). So the *first* search after a cache eviction is the one that
gets truncated. `fa code` measured 0.94 s on a hit against 25.9-34.9 s on a miss.

**I nearly reported the wrong cause.** My first measurement said `~/code` = 34.8 s but
`~/code` minus `_worktrees` = 0.205 s, and `_worktrees` alone = 0.105 s. Two disjoint halves cannot
sum to 34.8 s - that contradiction is what exposed the confound. Re-running interleaved showed
`~/code` at 29.79 s then 0.205 s. **It was the page cache, not `_worktrees`.** When a decomposition
does not add up, the measurement is wrong, not the world.

Fixed: rg writes to a file so its exit status is visible, a killed root sets `truncated`, says so on
stderr, and a miss with any truncated root exits **4** ("nothing searched in full") instead of 1.
Budget is now `FIND_ANYTHING_ROOT_TIMEOUT` (default 25) so the path is testable - proved with a
forced 1 s budget over the whole home: `rc=4` with the stderr line.
