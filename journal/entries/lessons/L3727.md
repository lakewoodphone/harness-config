**L3727 · A 300 MiB write measures the SLC cache, not the stick - I repeated L3512's own trap and got three different speeds from one device**

I read L3512 - which says in its own words that "a 200 MB write only measures the write cache; a 1.5 GB sustained write came in at 5.71 MB/s" - and then measured a USB stick with a 300 MiB write and concluded "17 MB/s, the stick is fine".

MEASURED 2026-10-13, Weinreb stick (PNY USB 3.2.1 FD, 128 GB nominal, formatted exFAT). Three numbers, one device, each with a plausible claim to be "the speed":
- **1.3 MB/s** from a 300 MiB `dd` - taken while three of my own `rsync` children (a `pkill` killed the wrapper, not the workers) and a stray `dd` read-back were still on the device. Contaminated. See L3724.
- **17.0 MB/s** from the SAME kind of 300 MiB `dd` after clearing the device - which is **the SLC cache**, not the medium. This is the mistake L3512 documents, repeated by the session that had just read it.
- **3 MB/s** - the truth. Measured with the device provably idle (`ps aux`, `fuser -v`), from **the kernel's own counter** (`/sys/block/sda/stat`, field 7) over a 60-second window: 198 MB written = 3 MB/s, while the source `/dev/sdc` read 206 MB in the same window. Both sides equal, so the stick paces the pipeline.

THE RULE: **a short write measures a cache, and a measurement taken while anything else is on the device measures the contention.** To get a sustained rate you need (a) a write larger than the cache - multiple GB, not hundreds of MB, (b) the kernel's /sys/block counter rather than the copier's own progress line, which averages in the initial burst, and (c) `ps aux` + `fuser -v <mount>` showing the device idle. Any one of those missing has now produced a wrong number in this job three times.

Corollary for planning: state the regime AND the file size behind any throughput figure, or the estimate built on it is fiction. The ETA from 17 MB/s was 1h20m; the truth is 11 hours.
