**L8127 · A CFO question to her is approval-free by name, not because she was the owner**

OWNER RULE, 2026-10-18: "Send SMS from the cfo to her does not require approval. The cfo
was allowed to ask her questions about charges that are clearly from her."

This corrects a sentence I wrote an hour earlier -- "a send_sms aimed at her now requires
approval" -- which was wrong twice over:

 * `send_sms` (the Twilio action) never reaches an approval decision for her at all.
 chat_action_runtime refuses a non-owner target first, under twilio_sms_owner_only=True:
 "Twilio SMS is owner-only. Customer/prospect SMS must use dialpad_send_sms". It is a
 REFUSAL, not a hold, and it happens before the approval branch.
 * the approval gate itself is `dialpad_send_sms` + sms_send_external_requires_approval.
 That is the branch where my change had re-armed approval on her.

WHY IT HAD BEEN WORKING, AND WHY THAT WAS NOT A GOOD REASON
 policy._needs_approval exempts a target that `is_owner_number()` calls the owner
 (owner_target_param="to" on both send_sms and dialpad_send_sms). She sat in
 OWNER_ALT_PHONE_NUMBERS, so her messages inherited the owner's exemption. Correct
 outcome, wrong reason -- and the same wrong reason was arming his 20-minute floor on a
 text to her and letting a send to her skip the external gate. Fixing the identity
 removed the exemption as a side effect. That is the trap: a correct fix to identity
 silently changed a send policy nobody had stated in code.

WHAT NOW CARRIES THE RULE
 app/config.py aitext_private_ask_allow <- AITEXT_PRIVATE_ASK_ALLOW
 owner_notifications.py is_private_ask_number() next to is_owner_number, so each
 predicate has exactly one definition
 policy.py a target on that list is not an external-approval case,
 with the owner's own sentence in the comment

MEASURED ON THE LIVE SETTINGS (PolicyEnforcer.check on the real Settings object)
 aitext_private_ask_allow '+18482245096'
 is_private_ask_number(HER) True is_owner_number(HER) False
 dialpad_send_sms -> her requires_approval=False
 dialpad_send_sms -> you requires_approval=False
 dialpad_send_sms -> stranger requires_approval=True, allowed=False
 company-api restarted 04:58:23Z; commit 22bf850b5.

SO THE THREE CLAIMS THAT ARE NOW TRUE AT ONCE, and must stay true:
 1. she is NOT the owner -- no floor, no owner turn, no approval bypass by identity
 2. a CFO question to her about her own charge needs NO approval -- by NAME, not by
 inheriting his identity
 3. everyone else still needs approval -- a stranger is allowed=False, requires=True

THE GENERAL LESSON: when an identity bug is fixed, search every decision that USED the
wrong identity as a proxy for a policy. Here one wrong membership was simultaneously
(a) a bug and (b) the accidental carrier of a permission the owner wanted. Give the
permission its own name before removing the proxy, or the fix takes something with it.
