**L3876 · A push policy written for documents silently excluded every chat, mail and comms document**

**A push policy written for documents silently excluded every chat, mail and comms document.**

`config/sources.json → push_policy.allow_ext` exists to keep a machine's operational debris out of
the central index: `.log`, binaries, decompiled APKs, no-extension blobs. It judges a document by the
extension of its `path`. For a **file** document that is exactly right — the path is a file on disk.

For every other corpus the path is the *artefact the document was parsed from*:

 chat:vscode C:\...\chatSessions\<id>.jsonl
 chat:copilot-cli ~/.copilot/session-state/<id>/events.jsonl
 mail:* /root/mail-archive/<box>/raw/<xx>/<id>.json.gz
 comms dialpad rows, path empty

None of those extensions is on an allow-list of documents, so **every one of them was dropped at push
time**. Measured 2026-10-19: the chats adapter indexed 1,101 Copilot CLI sessions and the run printed
`1/1 source(s) ok, 1,101 document(s) written` followed by `pushed 0 document(s)` — success, twice, in
the same breath.

The rule now: the policy applies to sources whose id starts with `files:` or `docs:`. Anything else is
knowledge by construction and crosses unfiltered. `scripts/backfill.py` already worked this way, which
is why the backfill could put 118,617 emails in the centre while the hourly sync was still "sending"
them and delivering nothing.

**What to take from it:** a filter defined on a field must be checked against every producer of that
field. `path` means "the file" for one corpus and "the parser's input" for another, and the second
reading turned a debris filter into a total block. The tell was in the log the whole time — "written"
and "pushed" are two different numbers and nobody had compared them.

Refs: `unified-search` commit `3b24084`, `scripts/push.py` `_wanted`, `docs/SEARCH-AUDIT-2026-10-19.md`
§3.7.
