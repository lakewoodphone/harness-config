**L3473 · Before writing a total on an N9 finding, read the order's own notes - the price is often already there**

**L3471 · Before writing a total on an N9 finding, read the order's own notes — the price is often already there, and the gate cannot see it**

**The rule.** `bin/lpt-price` (check N9) decides "a zero nobody decided" by reading exactly two fields: `orders.chargeDescription` and `orders.adjustmentNote`. It does **not** read `customer_notes`, and that is deliberate and documented. The consequence for anyone acting on its findings — which is what L3358 built `lpt-write total set` for — is that **two very different problems are printed in one indistinguishable list**:

1. an order where nobody ever decided a price, and
2. an order where somebody decided the price, told the customer, and **the field was simply never filled**.

For class 2, the answer is already in the record, and the fix is one command. For class 1, it is a real decision.

**Measured 2026-10-09 (ZABZ-TECH).** `lpt-price --json` returned **258 findings**, verdict *"297 open work order(s) cannot be charged and cannot be released: 58 with no total at all and 239 at zero with nothing written down. 48 of them are READY_FOR_PICKUP."* One of them, finding **N9-199** severity HIGH:

> *"WO2608040007 is [READY_FOR_PICKUP], open 66 day(s), and its total is 0.00 with neither chargeDescription nor adjustmentNote filled in - a zero-dollar repair with no record that anybody decided it was free."*

`lpt ticket WO2608040007` showed the price **twice**, in notes: 2026-08-04 17:28 *"Customer quoted $135 total installed"*, and 2026-08-11 15:58 *"paid $135 via card"*. `lpt-write total set --amount 135 --apply` set it (ledger seq 302) and a correction note citing both was added (note 3037, ledger seq 303). The order then disappeared from the findings; the other 258 did not move.

**Transferable:** a checker that reads one field will report a class of records that a person can fix from another field in seconds. **Reading the finding's own record before acting on it is not optional diligence — on this list it is the difference between a fix and a decision.** And the same reading produced something the finding did not: the payment row for that order is still missing (`paymentStatus` PENDING, SUCCEEDED transactions 0.00) while a note says the customer paid by card. Setting the price made that visible; it did not settle it. **A price and a payment are two facts, and one zero hides both.**
