**L11582 · L11519 · A disabled enrollment looks identical to a sleeping phone — and 'no result' can mean undeliverable**

**Corrects `H11689` and the reply that followed it.** I said the re-lock of DRN 36 was "queued, not
applied … the phone is off or asleep", and earlier that the enrollment-profile removal "was never
delivered". Both were wrong, and the truth was one query away:

`enrollments` for 36: `enabled = false`, `updated_at = last_seen_at = 20:46:54`. The unenroll **did**
land in that check-in; the phone removed its management profile, sent `CheckOut`, and NanoMDM disabled
the enrollment. No `command_results` row exists because the device left before one could be written —
which is why the queue looked stuck and the device looked asleep. Exactly two enrollments in the fleet
have `enabled=false`: 25 and 36, the two we unlocked.

RULES:
1. **"No result" has two meanings with opposite fixes: not yet delivered, and not deliverable.** Before
 reporting a queue as waiting, read `enrollments.enabled` and `updated_at`. A disabled enrollment
 means every queued row is permanently undeliverable — waking the phone cannot help, only re-enrolling
 it can.
2. **Absence of a result is not absence of an event.** The device acted, and the evidence of the action
 is the state change (`enabled=false`, a check-out timestamp), not a result row. Ask what WRITES the
 field: the result table is written by the device's reply; the enrollment flag is written by the
 check-out handler. When the device leaves, only the second is written.
3. **A slow APNs push looks exactly like a dead device.** Three pushes were accepted by Apple and
 nothing moved, which I read as "asleep". The phone was awake and answering the whole time — to a
 different question (it had left).
