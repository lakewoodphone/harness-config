**L2097 · L2097 - CORRECTION: the 65 percent failure rate is a completion predicate, not a capability loss - the 9/12 decline was an accounting fix**

I reported the wrong root cause for the 65 % work-session failure rate in the AI company on
secratary, and H615 carries the wrong reading. This entry corrects it. The wrong claim stands above
as the record of having been wrong.

**WHAT I CLAIMED (wrong).** That 2026-09-12..14 was a three-day *collapse* - a capability loss, with
success falling from ~95 % to 3-4 % - and that it was caused by Home Assistant being unreachable plus
a dead-model fallback, i.e. the worker being handed tasks it had no credentials for. I wrote that
into the audit document twice and into the owner's queue row #126 as a supporting measurement.

**WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE.** At **2026-09-11T22:31** commit `53739081`
("fix(autopilot): stop recording unfinished work as completed") redirected four code paths that wrote
`status="completed", progress_pct=100` for sessions that had merely exhausted their step budget, hit
a hard ceiling, or tripped the repetition guard, into a helper that writes `status="failed"` with the
reason. **The sessions did not begin failing on 9/12. They had always failed to produce `[WORK_DONE]`
and were being counted as successes.** The 9/06-9/11 "~95 % success" WAS the flattering metric that
commit removed.

Evidence, all from `subagent-15` (mesh child on zabz-tech), which did the work I should have done
before publishing:

- All 1,146 failures in 14 days carry exactly one of three loop-owned markers, partitioning them with
 **zero unmarked**: `STEP_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` 804, `STALLED_REPEATING_OUTPUT` 297,
 `COMPLETION BLOCKED` 45.
- `output_log` holds **0 tracebacks, 0 permission-denied, 1 timeout**, mean length 1,519 chars. The
 failure vocabulary is the loop's own, not a crashing dependency's.
- Sessions on 9/12-9/14 ran the **same** steps (3/4/4 vs 3) and the **same** duration (140/147/163 s
 vs 143-163 s) as the "95 % success" days.
- The same failure string sits on **112 failed AND 65 completed** jobs in the same window - the
 deploy landed mid-window, so both code paths are visible.
- `tick_telemetry`, `error_log` and `work_order_executions` show no outage; `database is locked` was
 *lower* during the "collapse" (47) than in the baseline (82); `settings_overrides` has no row
 updated on 9/11 or 9/12.
- Onset is sharp to the hour: completions 18h:0, 19h:4, 20h:5, 21h:13, 22h:4, **23h:0**.

**WHY I GOT IT WRONG, and why that matters more than the error.** I had two hard data points - "95 %
before" and "3-4 % after" - and reached for the story that made them a story: something broke. That
is the same failure mode as revision 1 of the 2026-09-11 autopsy, which invented a six-week outage
from a stale database copy. **A step change in a metric is not evidence of an event.** I never asked
the obvious question - *did the work change, or did the measurement change?* - which one query over
`steps_completed`, `total_steps` and duration would have answered, and which I had the data to ask. I
also published into the owner's queue before the triage child I had dispatched returned. The child
was dispatched precisely because I did not know; I should have waited for it.

**WHAT SURVIVES, and is worse than what I claimed.** `[WORK_DONE]` appears in only **6,506 sessions,
ever, against 76,165 total** - twelve of every thirteen work sessions the company has ever run did
not finish. And recurrence is fragmented across task ids: normalising the `Task #<id>: ` prefix,
**202 logical tasks failed >=3x in 14 days and 168 of them (83 %) have NEVER succeeded once**, with
"Daily Android Filter AI product push" at **3 successes in 829 lifetime runs (0.4 %)** still being
scheduled daily.

**One number I also overstated:** "3,728 corrupt task titles (14 %)". The defensible tight class is
**1,650-1,660 rows whose title is literally agent JSON** (`title like '%"actions"%'`), 6.3 % - my
wider heuristic's "ends mid-sentence" clause fires on legitimate long descriptive titles.

**Lesson, stated so it can be acted on:** before reporting a change in a metric, measure whether the
WORK changed or the MEASUREMENT changed. Compare the population's shape (steps, duration, size)
across the boundary, and look for a code or config change at the exact hour. A metric that moves is a
question, not a conclusion.
