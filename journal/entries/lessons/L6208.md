**L6208 · A verification can lie in three ways and each looks like a catastrophe: wrong device (hardcoded /dev/sda1 verified a 2 TB lab-return drive), metadata counted as content (888 'sha256 differences' that were FAT32 two-second mtimes), and mis-paired nested sets**

**MEASURED 2026-10-14, verifying a completed 118 GB customer delivery onto a USB stick. All three failures happened on the same verification, in sequence, and each one produced an output indistinguishable from a destroyed copy. None of them was a problem with the copy.**

**1. The verification verified the WRONG DEVICE.** The script did `umount /mnt/stick` then `mount -o ro /dev/sda1 /mnt/stick` - a hardcoded device node. The stick had been swapped during the job and was now `/dev/sdd1`; `/dev/sda1` was a 2 TB lab-return drive. Output: `FAIL 0 of 5`, `files 1812 vs 0`, every byte total `vs 0`, and a `df` line reporting a **2 TB** filesystem on a 128 GB stick. The mount was read-only so nothing was harmed - but the only reason it was caught is that the `df` line was read. **Fix: resolve the medium by LABEL, and re-prove the label after the remount.**

**2. The verification counted METADATA as CONTENT.** It ran `rsync -rtl --checksum --dry-run --itemize-changes` and counted *every* itemized change, then printed that count as `sha256 differences: 888`. The 888 were **mtimes**: FAT32/exFAT store timestamps at two-second granularity, and the verify compared without the two-second window the copy itself had used. Contents were identical. **Fix: compare content only (`rsync -rlc`, checksums, NO `-t`), and report timestamp-only differences separately and never as failures.** A difference count that lumps metadata in with content is a wrong number wearing the right units.

**3. The verification compared the WRONG PAIR.** The destination deliberately nested one set inside another (`OTZAR_user_data` inside `Otzar-HaChochma-library`), while the comparison treated them as siblings - so it reported the child as **40 extra files and 529 MB of extra data**. **Fix: exclude the nested child from the parent's comparison (repeatable `--exclude`).**

**The count that was actually right:** file counts and byte totals matched EXACTLY on four of five sets (1812 = 1812, 805 = 805, 79 = 79, 40 = 40) and the stick flushed in 3 seconds, which is what a committed write looks like.

**The general shape, which is why this is a lesson and not an incident:** a verification is itself a program, and an unverified verification is worse than no verification, because it produces a **confident verdict** where an honest gap would have produced caution. So: name the thing you are checking (label, not node), compare the property you care about (content, not metadata), and pair the two sides deliberately. Every one of the three defects here would have been invisible in a PASS - they only showed up as a FAIL, which is the lucky direction.

Written into `lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/linux-usb-copy.md` (the three ways a verification can lie) and into `scripts/customer-operations/copy-to-stick-linux.sh verify`.
