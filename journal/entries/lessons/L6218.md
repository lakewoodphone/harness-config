**L6218 · L — A child's test total is a claim about its machine; ask for the delta against baseline**

**A mesh child's test numbers are about the child's machine, not about the code.** On 2026-10-14 a
child reported the fleet-api suite as **203 passed, 0 failed** and, separately, **191 passed** on the
baseline. On the authority, the same branch gave **148 passed / 55 failed**, and untouched
`origin/master` gave **136 passed / 55 failed**. The 55 are the same on both sides: they are
environment failures on a Linux node (the child ran on Windows, where they do not occur).

**Net effect of the change: +12 green tests, zero new failures** — which is what the child's
`191 → 203` also says, and neither number is the one a reader would take away from "203 passed".

**So state the delta, not the total.** "203 passed" reads as a healthy suite; the true, portable
statement is *"the same 55 failures before and after, +12 passing"*, and it is only obtainable by
running the suite twice — once on the change and once on untouched `origin/master`. That second run
is the whole point: **a green count without a baseline is a claim about a machine.**

What this cost: one extra worktree and two `pytest` invocations (`git worktree add --detach
/root/_worktrees/<name> origin/master`, run the same command, compare). What it bought: the ability to
say "no new failures" without guessing, on a suite where the local failure count is nowhere near zero.

Corollary for the briefs: when a child is asked to report the suite total, ask it for **before and
after on the same machine**, or treat the number as unusable.
