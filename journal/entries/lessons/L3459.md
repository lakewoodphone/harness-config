**L3459 · A stalled job is usually STOPPED: check ps state, /proc/<pid>/io and PPid before blaming I/O or disk**

A job that "stalls" is often STOPPED, not slow. On secratary-cloud 2026-10-11 the offsite backup's first run was reported as stalled after the copy, "no gzip child exists". The process list had been read for `gzip` only. What was actually there was `sqlite3 ... PRAGMA integrity_check` (stage 1, which is why no gzip existed), and BOTH processes were in state **T (stopped)** with **PPid 1**: the launching shell had died, they were reparented to init, and job control left them stopped. Nothing would ever have resumed them.

The readings that settle "stopped vs slow" in under a minute, all cheap:
* `ps -o pid,ppid,stat,wchan -p <pids>` -> `T` with wchan `do_signal_stop`; `grep PPid /proc/<pid>/status` -> 1
* `head -1 /proc/<pid>/io` twice, 8 s apart -> `rchar` IDENTICAL means zero progress; a slow job moves
* `ps -o etime,time` -> elapsed 10:47 but CPU 00:00:00 is not a busy process
* `dmesg | tail -30` and `journalctl -k --since` -> no OOM, no I/O error, no ENOSPC, i.e. the disk-full story is not supported

Consequence for design: a long job started in the foreground of an ssh session can be orphan-stopped and sit there forever looking like work. Long jobs get a systemd unit (TimeoutStartSec=infinity), not an ssh session.

Cost of not knowing this: the report that triggered the work named the wrong stage and the wrong mechanism, and a "fix" for the wrong cause was already being written.
