**L3372 · The recovery agent must live where the surviving host is, not where the monitoring is - and the way in was closed by design, not by accident**

GOAL ROUND 18, and the most useful thing this round was a NEGATIVE result that took nine checks to establish. During a 7-hour office outage I asked whether the office could be brought back remotely at all. Every path was examined and every one is closed: Tailscale to the four machines is offline (the whole LAN is gone), ha.abletelsolutions.com returns 302 to a CLOUDFLARE ACCESS sign-in (the HA panel is Access-gated), the live homeassistant tunnel exposes exactly ONE hostname (ha -> homeassistant:8123) plus a 404 catch-all, the home-services and personal-secretary tunnels are down with ZERO connectors, and the account token has no Access scope (403 on /access/apps) so no service token can be minted. That gate is CORRECT and must stay - it keeps a panel that controls the shop's doors, power and cameras off the public internet. The lesson is not to weaken it: it is that a RECOVERY agent must not need inbound reachability at all, and must run on the host that SURVIVES the failure. AND WE HAVE ONE: the four wired machines died within 40 seconds of each other (22:57:19-22:57:50Z) while the Home Assistant host's tunnel stayed healthy with a live connector for the whole outage, because HA has PERMANENT POWER on a different circuit (annex A - the Dahua cameras are the things on the Shelly). So the office is not a single failure domain; it contains one host that outlives the fault, on the shop LAN, wired to every machine. Built: deploy/home-assistant/company-recovery.yaml - HA pings the four machines every 30s, and when ALL FOUR have been unreachable for 15 minutes it sends Wake-on-LAN and then REPORTS WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED rather than that it tried. Guardrails chosen deliberately: it NEVER turns anything off (a recovery agent that can cut power is a new way to cause an outage, and the owner's Shabbat design exists because power cuts are the dangerous direction), it does nothing on Shabbat or Yom Tov, it fires at most once an hour so it cannot become its own outage, and it does NOT touch the Shelly plug because annex A says the cameras are alone on it and guessing at a power action in a shop is worse than not acting. RULES: (a) when you cannot reach something, enumerate the paths you tried and record WHY each is closed - 'unreachable' and 'closed by design' are different facts and only the second one tells you where to put the fix; (b) put the recovery where the surviving host is, not where the monitoring is; (c) a recovery action that can also destroy is a new failure mode wearing the costume of a fix - power-on only, never off.
