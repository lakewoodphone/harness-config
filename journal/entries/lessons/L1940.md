**L1940 · Read the printed label BEFORE the analysis: one free fact reversed a struck conclusion - and a correction can itself be corrected**

**Lesson (2026-09-17, Goldstein microSD): the label is a cheap experiment that can reverse a conclusion in
both directions — run it BEFORE the analysis, not after. And when a fault has several live explanations, the
record must say which evidence would kill each one.**

Sequence, in order, from one physical card:
1. I concluded "controller capacity fallback".
2. An adversarial pass struck that clause: `121.25 MiB = 485 × 256 KiB` is a clean **SDSC CSD decode** and is
 **exactly what a genuine 128 MB-class card reports**, and documented fallback capacities are 512 KB / 2 GB /
 30–32 MB / 46 MB — nowhere near it. On the evidence then, **either** a genuine tiny card **or** a fallback
 was possible, and I was wrong to state one as fact. The strike was correct.
3. The owner read the **label: `onn` 32 GB** — and the fallback reading was reinstated as arithmetic: a 32 GB
 card is SDHC/SDXC, a valid CSD v2.00 capacity is always an integer multiple of 512 KiB, and
 `127,139,840 / 524,288 = 242.5` is **not** an integer. The presented geometry therefore cannot be the
 card's own, at ~1/264th of its labeled size.

**The transferable rules:**
- **The printed label is the cheapest and most decisive evidence on any weird-capacity card. Read it first.**
 It would have made step 2 unnecessary, and it is free. A card's label converts "which of these three
 explanations is true?" into arithmetic.
- **A wrong-capacity finding needs a *stated* discriminator per alternative**, not one confident mechanism.
 The two candidates here (genuine-tiny vs fallback) are distinguished by exactly one fact — the labeled
 capacity — and the honest write-up names that fact instead of picking a winner.
- **A correction can itself be corrected, and both must stay in the record.** The strike was right when made;
 the fallback reading is right now. Deleting either would destroy the only thing that makes the record
 trustworthy: the visible path, including the wrong turns. Append-only is not bureaucracy here — it is what
 let the label land as evidence instead of as a contradiction.
- **Do not let the label close the file either.** The `32 GB` label killed the "genuine tiny card" escape but
 **not** the others: the **NAND is still untested** (so a lab quote must be success-only) and "unaddressable"
 still means only **this host and this reader** — the bridge has its own CPU and can fabricate a response.
 One fact resolving one ambiguity is not the same as the case being closed.
