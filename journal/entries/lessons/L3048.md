Last round I reported that task completion (93.4 %) is "a far better denominator than sessions" and
that "the company is above the 85 % target" on the measure the objective names. I attached the
caveat that `done` is self-reported. **Then I sampled it, which is what I said I would do, and the
caveat is worse than I stated.**

Sample: 20 tasks marked `done` today. Result:

```
corroborated (all goal steps completed) : 0
unverifiable (no goal plan at all) : 20
contradicted (task done, steps NOT) : 0
```

**Twenty of twenty have no goal plan whatsoever.** There is nothing behind the `done` field to
corroborate it -- not a failing plan, not a partial one, nothing. So 93.4 % is the company's
*assertion* about its own work with no supporting structure, and it cannot be used as evidence that
the work is done. The honest statement is: **the session figure (52 %) is measured and pessimistic;
the task figure (93.4 %) is unverified and optimistic. Neither one is the answer yet.**

**And two of the twenty are not tasks at all:**

```
#27061 ** #2 (quote conversion) is the fastest path to revenue wi

```

Those are **model reply fragments stored as task titles** -- the defect the 2026-09-20 audit
measured at 6.3 % of the task table (1,650-1,660 rows) and attributed to a `create_task` whose `args`
were never parsed as JSON. **Still live, nine days later**, and this sample puts it at 10 %.

**And `#27068` is marked `done`** -- the very task whose session FAILED at 19:05 and 19:14, and whose
"work" was *creating a follow-up task* rather than doing it. It was closed at 19:37 by something. A
task closed 32 minutes after the session that worked on it failed, with no goal plan, is very
plausibly closed for the wrong reason.
