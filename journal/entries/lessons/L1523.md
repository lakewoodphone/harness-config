**L1523 · Fixing a suite's pre-existing failures does not make it a gate: the remaining errors are environmental, not attributable**

**Fixing a suite's pre-existing failures does not make it a gate -- measure what is left.**

Measured 2026-09-15. The nine named pre-existing failures are now zero, and the full suite still
cannot be green: **6 failed, 74 errors, 2,635 passed** (from 21 failed / 153 errors / 2,123 passed).
That is real progress and it is not a gate, and the difference is the point of this lesson:

- the 9 were **deterministic and attributable** -- each one named a component, and each fix was a
 real defect (a fail-open guard, a Windows-only path decoder, a contract that ignored aliases, three
 tests that lied about their environment);
- the 74 errors are **environmental**, an `OSError` that appears under whole-suite load and not in
 isolation -- P139 measured `test_work_generation.py` at 94/94 alone.

So "the suite has N pre-existing failures" and "the suite is not a gate" are two different facts, and
fixing the first does not fix the second. The next step is to characterise the `OSError` (likely a
resource limit -- file descriptors or threads -- hit by a 2,600-test single process), because that is
the thing standing between this repo and a gate that can be trusted.

Rule worth keeping: when a long-standing "known-bad" number finally drops, re-measure it
immediately and report the *new* number, rather than letting the old reputation stand in either
direction. P139 said 21/153; it now says 6/74, and only the measurement changed anyone's mind.
