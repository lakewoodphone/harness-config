**L10818 · On a mixed card, 'it is already attributed' is usually a dead twin talking**

**L10818 · On a mixed card the answer "it is already attributed" is usually a dead twin talking**

Measured on the CFO book, 2026-10-20, cards ending 4258 and 2005.

WHAT I ASSERTED, and why it was wrong. Asked whether card 2005's business spend was on the record,
I queried `select account_id, transaction_date, description, amount_cents where account_id='personal-amex-22005' and entity_id='lpt'`
and got three rows worth 8,641.34, all Alibaba parts. I wrote "2005 is handled: business spend is
attributed to LPT" and nearly moved on. All three of those rows were `duplicate_of_transaction_id
IS NOT NULL` -- rows a later de-duplication had deactivated -- and their live twins were
`entity_id='personal'`. On the live card, not one business charge reached LPT. The proportion of
active, non-duplicate rows on 22005 with entity='lpt' was **zero**.

HOW IT SURFACED. Not by re-reading the query but by running the consumer and reading its own
output: `owner-loan-balance.py` printed `A3_amex_biz $0.00`. A component that should have held
8,641.34 holding zero is a louder signal than any amount that merely looks plausible.

THE RULE. Any "is this row attributed / categorised / counted?" question must exclude
`duplicate_of_transaction_id IS NOT NULL` **and** say so in the query, because a duplicate keeps the
labels its twin lost. A dead twin is not an absence of evidence -- it is evidence pointing the wrong
way, which is worse. Two ways to catch it: count `ingestion_status` and duplicate status next to
every entity total, and run the downstream consumer rather than the upstream query.

THE SECOND HALF, same card. Two charges (632.00, 299.00) sat `ingestion_status='archived'` with
`duplicate_of_transaction_id` NULL, no `audit_log` row, and an exact amount+date twin search across
every account returning zero rows. The balance the bank reports (4,557.92) equalled the sum of all
68 rows **including** those two, so the debt carried them and no expense line did. The statement's
own closure check passed, because it sums *active* rows and never compares them to a balance. An
archived row with no twin is not a settled row: it is a row removed from one side of the books and
left on the other. The test that finds it is arithmetic, not belief: sum all rows, compare to the
balance snapshot, and if they differ, list what is not active.
