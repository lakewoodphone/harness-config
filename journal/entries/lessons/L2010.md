**L2010 · A1980 port job: the risk is the opening, not the port - screen crack economics**

Owner follow-up 2026: 'the port is visibly cracked - how hard is it and how likely is the screen to crack?' Physical port damage on an iPad Pro 11 1st gen (A1980). Answer: the port itself is the easy part; the DISPLAY OPENING is the entire risk and the entire difficulty.

HARD PART. iFixit's own iPad Pro 11-inch teardown, on opening it: 'Heat, carve, hold your breath, and hope nothing breaks.' Same teardown: 'the case is a little thicker around the Pencil charging area - we started prying there and quickly regretted it' (do NOT start on the right/Pencil-magnet side), and the display's ribbon cables are 'spread out in a way that makes display removal really awkward' - iFixit was 'forced to lay the display at a weird angle and hopefully not damage anything while we disconnect them'. iFixit scored the 2018 Pro 3/10.

FACE ID. The Face ID module sits at the top of the display (IR cameras + dot projector). Damage there permanently kills Face ID - the tech guides carry this as a specific warning. Adds an uninsurable-looking failure to the opening step.

ONCE OPEN. The port is two screws and a plug - the swap is minutes (confirmed on A1980). Display is the only way in, so display removal is mandatory regardless.

CRACK ECONOMICS. Display is a laminated glass+LCD assembly, so any crack = whole assembly, no glass-only fix in shop. MobileSentrix aftermarket LCD assembly with digitizer for iPad Pro 11 1st gen (2018) = ~\.29. Shop retail for a 1st-gen iPad Pro 11 screen job was quoted ~\ / 2-3 hours (Yelp shop answer, Mar 2024); Apple charges \. So one crack eats the entire margin of a port swap and adds hours.

LIKELIHOOD. No shop publishes a crack rate and I did not find one - do not invent a percentage. Mechanism-based read: this is one of the riskier iPads to open (iFixit's language is the industry consensus), NOT a low-risk base-iPad opening. Risk multiplies sharply with: any existing crack, chip or dent at the frame edge; a previous repair re-glued with hard adhesive; insufficient heat; prying at the Pencil side; and levering against the glass instead of sliding cards through softened adhesive. A clean, never-opened unit with proper heat usually survives - the failure mode is real and common enough to price for, not rare.

HANDLING RULE for this class of job: price the opening as the risk it is, or get the customer to pre-agree in writing that a display crack means they pay the display part at cost. Otherwise the shop eats ~\ + labour on a job worth a fraction of that.
