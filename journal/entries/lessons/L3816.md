**L3816 · The zero-tick hours were the BUDGET CAP, not a stall - and the afternoon's completion rate measures the cap, not the work**

I attributed two hour-long gaps in the company's ticks to a stall. They were the budget cap, and the correction matters because the mis-read could have sent someone hunting a control-flow bug that does not exist.

**The measurement that exposed it.** After deploying the repetition and timestamp fixes I looked for the failure mix and found **7 sessions since the deploys, every one `paused`, all at `steps_completed = 0`, each created and paused inside 0.2 s** — while today's totals read 48 failed / 36 completed / **17 paused**, i.e. **10 paused in thirteen hours and 7 in eleven minutes**. Seven zero-step sessions immediately after four changes to the loop is the shape of a regression, and I went looking for mine.

**It is not mine, and the sessions said so themselves.** Each paused session's own `output_log` carries its reason:

 id 78711 [BUDGET-PAUSED: agent_soft_cap $6.6694/$6.67]
 id 78710 [BUDGET-PAUSED: dept_pool_exhausted $20.0051/$20.00]
 id 78705 [BUDGET-PAUSED: agent_soft_cap $6.6694/$6.67]
 id 78694 [BUDGET-PAUSED: agent_soft_cap $6.6694/$6.67] <- 09:24Z
 id 78685 [BUDGET-PAUSED: agent_soft_cap $6.6694/$6.67] <- 08:54Z
 id 78684 [BUDGET-PAUSED: agent_soft_cap $6.6694/$6.67] <- 08:53Z

**The earliest is 08:53Z — more than four hours before my first company change.** The cap has been binding all morning: `agent_budgets` is keyed `(agent_id, date_id)` with `base_spend`, `pool_spend` and `is_exhausted`, and the orchestrator's soft cap is 6.67 while a department pool is 20.00. Spend today is **23.0726 USD across 2,393 calls**.

**So two earlier records of mine are corrected:**
1. In this session's round-2 entry I wrote that hours 09 and 11 had *"ZERO ticks"* and concluded *"the tick loop stalls for whole hours as a pattern."* **That was wrong.** The loop was doing exactly what it was told: refusing to start work it was not allowed to pay for. **A zero that comes from a guard is not a stall**, and the two look identical in a per-hour tick count.
2. Today's headline completion rate — 36.0 % against yesterday's 32.3 % — **is not a fair comparison.** The company has been capped since 08:53, so the afternoon is not a period of work that failed; it is a period of work that did not run. A rate computed across a capped afternoon measures the cap, not the work.

**And it changes what the deployed fixes can be judged on.** The post-deploy window contains **no completed and no failed sessions** — they cannot run — so the failure mix is not merely unmeasured, it is currently *unmeasurable*. Any claim about the repetition or timestamp fixes moving the mix has to wait for a window in which the company is actually spending.

**The condition itself is not a defect: it is the governor working.** A per-agent soft cap and a per-department pool stopped the spend. **My recommendation, and it is a decision I can defend without him:** leave both caps where they are. The company was failing roughly two thirds of what it attempted, and the fixes that address that — the budget extension, the redirect, the fails-closed gate — landed in the last two hours and are unmeasured. Raising a cap on unmeasured work is how a 20-dollar day becomes a 40-dollar day with the same outcome. Revisit when a spending window shows the failure rate down. **What is genuinely his is the number itself, and it does not need deciding today.**
