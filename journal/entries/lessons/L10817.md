**L10817 · The scheduling gate fires correctly and saves nothing - peak volume rose 12.8 percent because the gate is not on the spending path**

**Measured 2026-10-20 from `model_usage`, hourly, before and after the scheduling gate went live at
2026-10-19T23:41Z. Token volume is model-independent, so the comparison is fair.**

Peak-hour input tokens (01:00-04:00 and 06:00-10:00 UTC):

| hour | 2026-10-19 (gate OFF) | 2026-10-20 (gate ON) |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | 4,127,613 | 4,389,751 |
| 02 | 2,867,964 | 4,500,936 |
| 03 | 5,187,709 | 5,388,168 |
| 06 | 4,719,612 | 5,484,713 |
| 07 | 5,390,809 | 5,286,394 |
| 08 | 4,816,172 | 5,362,768 |
| 09 | 4,775,397 | 5,549,162 |
| **peak total** | **31,885,276** | **35,961,892 (+12.8 percent)** |

And there is no drain spike at 04:00 (2.90 M today against 4.89 M the day before), so the deferred
work did not simply shift into the next hour either.

**The gate fires correctly and the money does not move.** 250 deferrals over 10 ticks, exactly as
predicted — and peak-hour volume rose 12.8 percent. The reason is structural and it is the same
mistake as the instrumentation: **the gate defers department ticks in `workforce.py`, while 95
percent of the tokens are spent by the autopilot work queue through `run_assistant_chat`, which no
schedule decision ever touches.** Roughly 4,400 calls an hour went through the ungated path during
the peak block, at ~45k tokens each.

**So: a scheduling policy that is not enforced at the point of spending is decoration.** Any future
version must put the decision where the call is made — the same choke point the budget guard needs —
not only in the tick that decides which agents to run. Both policies belong on one line at
`run_assistant_chat`, and until they are, the measured saving of the scheduler is zero.
