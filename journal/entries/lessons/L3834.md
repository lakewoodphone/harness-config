**L3683 · A vendor ticket is not the whole case - a state line about a CUSTOMER must be read out of the customer's own traffic, not the vendor's mailbox. Read one channel and the board is confidently wrong.**

**Measured 2026-10-12, on my own work, minutes after I had diagnosed the same defect in the other direction.**

I built a 300DDR lab board by sweeping the lab's **email** in full and filing six messages into four case files. The board then said twice that Weissman *'has not replied'* to the premium-lab offer. **He had replied at 13:47:42Z that afternoon** — *'Ok thanks for trying. Gzl't'*, \dialpad_sms_cache\ id \1125224\ — three and a half hours before the board was assembled. A parallel session caught it.

**The shape, which is why this is a lesson and not a slip:** the board's own trap list warned that *'a lab message reached no case file'* — the vendor channel being unread — and I fixed that, then wrote a customer-state line out of the one channel that cannot speak for the customer.

**Rule.** A vendor/lab ticket is a **per-device handle**, not the case. For any statement about what a *customer* has said, done, or is waiting on, read the customer's own comms:
- \ps_comms_waiting\ — who is waiting on a reply from us, across every channel. One call, answers exactly this.
- \ps_comms_person <name|number>\ — the whole timeline across texts, calls, voicemails and email.
- \ps_comms_thread\ — one party, oldest first.

**And a cheaper tell: if a document names a customer as unanswered, the claim is only true as of the moment someone read that channel.** Write the read time next to it, or do not write it.
