**L2518 · Cheap rungs resolve charge complaints - and 'it's charging' off a weak source is not a result**

Part of the Goldberg A2602 case (`ipad-goldberg-7325697189-2026-09-24`).

## What I learned

The case was recorded and updated for a *board-soldered Lightning port* repair path — and then rung 1
of the ladder, the free rung, resolved it in one step. The owner plugged the same iPad into his Mac
with an **Apple USB-C-to-Lightning cable** and it charges. Nothing was opened, nothing was ordered.

The transferable instruction is about **order of record-keeping, not about this iPad**: when a repair
case's symptom admits an expensive hardware cause *and* a free source/cable cause, write the cheap
rung's expected reading into the record **before** the expensive path is described, because the
expensive prose is what a later session will act on. My own first pass on this case ended its case
file with "the port is soldered, so this is board-level work" — true, and irrelevant to what the case
turned out to be. The case survived because the ladder was written into the same document as the
warning, so rung 1 sat directly under the board-level paragraph.

Also: "it's charging" off an on-screen indicator is **not** a result. A Mac's USB-C port is a weak
source and the bolt can appear on a source that is barely feeding the device. The finding is only
worth recording as a finding once the **percentage is seen climbing** and the charge holds on a wall
adapter. In this case the cheap rung produced a *candidate* explanation — the customer's cable or
adapter — and the customer's own cable and adapter are not at the shop, so the actual failed item is
still unidentified. Do not report "it's the cable" from this evidence.
