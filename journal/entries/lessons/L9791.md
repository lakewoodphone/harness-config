## Read the invoice, not the price list — and check what your own ledger is pricing (2026-10-19)

Three separate errors were live in this estate's understanding of its own spend, and all three were
found in one afternoon by reading primary records instead of reasoning from them.

**1. A list price is not a bill, and one account is not all the accounts.**
The 2026-09-16 audit (`docs/mesh/79-elastic-cloud.md` §1.2) correctly reported **COULD NOT VERIFY**
what the owner pays, and substituted Hetzner's list price for the SKUs the measured hardware matched.
That inference was reasonable and, for two of four machines, exactly right — but the estate's *total*
was never the sum of four list prices. The invoice (`086001226723`, read out of the owner's own mail
through the company's Gmail integration) itemises **two** servers, `CPX11 $6.99 + CX33 $9.99 + 2 IPv4
$1.20 = $18.27/month`, and those two are `waze-mdm-01` and `lpt-apps-01`, confirmed by their measured
cores/RAM/disk. **The other two machines — including the $165.99 CCX33 that is the company's
authority — are billed to a second Hetzner account** whose invoice reaches no mailbox we can read
(instance ids 169556315 / 169027743; `ls /root/.hcloud*` ENOENT on all four hosts; the token one child
found answers `HTTP 401`). The lesson is not "the earlier audit was wrong"; it is that **a bill has
accounts, and an estate has more accounts than anyone remembers**. Read the invoice first, then find
out which invoices are missing.

**2. Never trust a cost column whose rate card you have not read.**
`model_usage.estimated_cost_usd` reported **$237.94 for seven days** of DeepSeek — a $1,030/month run
rate, the largest line in the company. It was computed from `app/pricing.py:150-172`, which prices
`deepseek-v4-flash` at **(0.44, 1.32) $/M** — the entry the comment immediately above labels
*"legacy V4-Flash official peak (superseded by deepseek-flash)"*, whose current card in the same
dictionary is **(0.15, 0.60)**. Recomputed on the measured tokens (534,474,098 in − 4,590,592 cached,
3,803,697 out): **$238.17 on the old card** — matching the ledger to the cent, which is how we know
which card it used — and **$81.76 on the current one**. The recorded top-ups of $53 / $79.50 / $106 in
that week could not have funded $238 of usage, so the lower number is the true one and **the ledger
overstates AI spend by 2.9×**. A cost column is a *model of a price*, and prices change under it. When
a number is about to drive a decision, recompute it from tokens and a dated rate; if the two rate
cards disagree, say so and price both.

**3. A control-plane probe that answers 403 is not "the node is down".**
The broker read the authority as `unreachable` while `curl http://127.0.0.1:3086/mesh/capacity`
answered 200 in the same minute. The cause was `phone-gate.py`'s device allow-list: the file lists
every peer on the tailnet **except the authority itself**, so a request from the node to its own
tailnet name is refused with *"This node does not sign in this device"*. The obvious fix — add the
authority's address to the list — would have handed a 30-day engine cookie to any process on the box
that holds the database. The correct fix was to notice that **the broker runs on that same host**, and
point the roster row at loopback. When a probe fails, read the body of the refusal: `403` and
`unreachable` are different sentences, and the difference here was the difference between adding a
security hole and adding a roster row.

**Applies to:** any future "what do we pay" question (start from invoices; enumerate accounts first),
any cost figure taken from `model_usage` (check the card), and any mesh node whose gate answers 403
(read whether the refusal is about *identity* before concluding anything about *health*).
