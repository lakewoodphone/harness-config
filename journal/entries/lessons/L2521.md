**L2521 · Customer comms: never ask for their charger back, and never explain that their accessory was inferior**

Owner corrections on customer communication, 2026-09-24 (Goldberg iPad case). Both are his words and
both are cheap to obey — record them so the next draft does not need re-cutting.

1. **Do not ask a customer to bring their own charger or cable in.** He said plainly he does not want
 to see it. So the diagnostic instruction "get her hardware so we can identify which part failed"
 is off the table regardless of how much better the diagnosis would be. If the item cannot be
 identified without her hardware, the shop accepts the unidentified cause and moves on.

2. **"Off-brand cables get rejected because they are not certified" is condescending — and possibly
 false.** His objection was exactly right: the failed cable may have been a genuine one that simply
 broke, which is ordinary and blameless. The replacement wording, which he accepted, was *"Cables do
 fail over time, and that is what happened here."* **The rule generalises: when the cause is a
 customer's own accessory failing, say the thing failed — do not explain why their choice was
 inferior.** Blaming the customer's judgement is both rude and a claim we usually cannot support.

Also from the same case, for the record: he is willing to send a marketplace link to a customer when
he decides to — that is his business and he owns the trade-off. He also had me say the **port was
examined and shows no damage**, which is his first-hand observation and the honest thing to tell her
given the original complaint named the port.
