**L2386 · Used-phone sourcing: price eBay's Refurbished condition tier, and never assume grade tracks price**

**Measured 2026-09-22 (ZABZ-YOGA), two source sweeps had missed this twice.**

- **eBay's own condition tiers are a separate, cheaper ladder and were not priced by the 2026-09-14 or 2026-09-22 Waze sourcing sweeps.** Live on 2026-09-22: eBay item 27837790428 -> see doc. Concretely: **iPhone 11 64GB unlocked, "Excellent - Refurbished" = \.57 free shipping, 10+ available** (Soonersoft Electronics, 99.4%/870K, Top Rated Plus) while a "Grade B+ Good" 24-lot cost **\/unit with NO returns** (ZDIC). **Excellent-Refurbished was CHEAPER than Good.**
- **Therefore: grade and price are NOT monotonic in the used-phone market.** Any premium table that assumes grade and price rise together produces the wrong recommendation. Two sweeps did exactly that.
- **The tier that guarantees what a QC gate tests beats the tier that merely permits it.** "Excellent-Refurbished" guarantees battery >85% and "no scratching on the screen"; ZDIC's "Good" and even "A+ Excellent" both say "Non-OEM parts may be present; batteries are typically around 80% ... not guaranteed" - which collides with any gate that requires battery >=80%.
- **Returns terms decide which dead-rate model you may use.** With a seller-paid return window a QC reject costs a cycle, not dollars (do NOT multiply the device price by 1/(1-d)); with "Seller does not accept returns" it is a total loss. ZDIC's \ lot clears the \ margin floor only at a **zero** reject rate; its \ A+ lot fails the floor outright (\.60).
- **Method that worked:** Firecrawl with proxy:stealth renders eBay SEARCH and ITEM pages reliably (2026-09-22); eBay SOLD/completed is login-gated and remains unreadable. Firecrawl's JSON extraction mode fails on eBay search pages (context limit) - use markdown.
- **iPhone 11 parts are cheap and this changes risk maths:** aftermarket LCD assembly \.29 (MobileSentrix) / \.25 (Injured Gadgets); battery \.23-\.69. Retail screen job \-\, Apple \. Guide figures of "\-\ for a screen" are DIY KITS, not the bare part.
- **No published failure rate exists for refurbished or used phones.** The only real claims data is SquareTrade 2010 (n=28,729, brand-new handsets, month-8 cumulative 7.7% of which 0.9pp malfunction; 76% of accidents are drops). Do not invent a refurb failure rate.
