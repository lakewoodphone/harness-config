**L2132 · L2141 - four times in one session I read a rolling window as a live rate; the rule is to split every total at the moment of the change**

This is the single most repeated error of the session, and it is worth writing down as a procedure
because repetition is the point.

Four separate times I took a rolling total - "the last 7 days", "the last 90 minutes" - and reported
it as though it described the present, when it actually described a period that mostly predated the
fix I was evaluating:

1. **Lock errors**: reported "124 in the last hour" when every one of those timestamps was before the
 mashgiach deploy. After the deploy: 0.
2. **Missing paths**: reported "129 blocked" when 50 predated the repo-path fix and the rest were
 older. After the fix: 2.
3. **Corrupt task titles**: reported "escalating, Sep 507" when the producer had died on 09-11 and the
 507 were all from the first eleven days of the month.
4. **The gate fix**: reported "the gate is not working" from 13 sessions that all predated the gate
 deploy. Split at the deploy time: 3 of 3 closed-task sessions completed, 0 failures with a closed
 task.

**The procedure, now mandatory before reporting any defect total or any fix's effect:**

 SELECT ... WHERE started_at > <DEPLOY_TIME> -- the "after"
 SELECT ... WHERE started_at BETWEEN <T0> AND <DEPLOY_TIME> -- the "before"

Two queries, and the deploy timestamp taken from `systemctl show <svc> -p ActiveEnterTimestamp` or the
commit time - never "the last N hours/minutes", because that phrase silently includes the regime you
are trying to leave behind.

**Why I kept making it.** A rolling window is the natural thing to reach for, and it LOOKS like the
stronger evidence - more rows, a bigger number, a more alarming finding. The bug is that it answers a
different question: "was this a problem recently?" instead of "is this a problem now?". Those differ
enormously on the day you fix something.

**Guard against it structurally:** print the window bounds and the deploy time next to the number. If
the number was computed over a period spanning the fix, it cannot be cited as the fix's effect, and
seeing the two timestamps side by side makes that obvious before the claim is made rather than after.
