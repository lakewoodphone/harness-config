**L7550 · 2026-10-16 measure the PREMISE of a row before working it: row 312 asked about the hours of an employee who had left three weeks earlier, and our own queue recorded that six times**

The owner, flatly: 'don't you know yisroel doesn't work for us anymore.' He left 2026-09-14, and the queue recorded it six times over - rows 163 (why he left), 175/182/183 (the NJ DOL BC28 separation filing), 196 (his last day worked) and 131/132 (his own texts to the AI line). Row 312 was raised 2026-10-07, three weeks AFTER the departure, asking whether his working hours had changed with the shop's new week, because a September profitability document computes his hours from the old week and that figure feeds payroll. The premise was dead on arrival and the row sat open nine days. Resolved MOOT, not answered. THE RULE: before working a row, measure whether its premise still holds - and use the instrument the queue already ships and the row itself cited: python3 /root/bin/premise-check.py check <id>, or sweep --apply for every due row. A row about a person, a price or a system that no longer exists is a cleanup, not a question, and the difference is one command. The same class of failure as the two the owner caught today (a price inferred from a marketing page, and a negative asserted more strongly than the search behind it): the answer was already in our records and I never asked the records the question.
