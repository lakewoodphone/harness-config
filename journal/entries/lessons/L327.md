A backlog of unsent drafts is a generator defect, not a to-do list for the owner

WHAT I FOUND (secratary, 2026-09-14, email_drafts on data/secretary.db):
64 drafts sit in status=pending_review — not the 36 the owner queue claimed, and
not "customer replies". Read the senders before believing a count:
 ~54 reply to AUTOMATED senders: Fulton Bank, AliExpress, Amazon, PayPal, Amex,
 Telnyx, Netlify, Bank of America alerts, Groq, US Mobile, and Walmart
 marketplace notices relayed via Bison Commerce.
 id=258 is not a reply at all: the body is the internal annotation

 id=264 stored original_draft_body is an ACTION ENVELOPE,
 {"reply": "", "actions": [{"name": "get_current_datetime", ...}]} — the same
 defect class as the briefings bug fixed earlier the same night.
 <=10 involve a real person, and they are OLD (2026-06-07 to 2026-09-14). The
 one that mattered, Michelle Wasserlauf at Heichal HaTorah (priority 0.9,
 "Dumb phones for Heichal", promising a callback and phones for the first day

 would have reopened a closed order with a promise that is no longer true.
 The customer email itself was filed by owner_message_queue #1335 as
 routed_to_engineering — a customer enquiry routed to engineering rather than
 answered.

MY ERROR, RECORDED BECAUSE IT IS THE POINT: the owner queue presented this as
"36 customer replies waiting, walk them one at a time", I repeated that framing as
my recommendation, and he picked it. It would have burned an hour of his evening
sending replies to bank notifications. The queue gave a count; I did not check who
the senders were before putting it in front of him.

RULE: before presenting a backlog as the owner work, classify it. Distinct
senders, human versus automated, age, and whether each item is still true. A pile
of drafts that exists because the drafter replies to machines is a bug to fix, not
a queue to work. Age matters as much as content: an honest draft becomes a wrong
one once its promise has expired.
