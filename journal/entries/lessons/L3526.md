**L3526 · Assessing a pulled drive non-invasively: the five read-only commands, what each number means, and the -FVE-FS- test that settles encryption**

A pulled customer drive can be fully assessed without mounting it, and the two questions that decide a data job - is it healthy, is it encrypted - are answered by five read-only commands. Measured 2026-10-11 on the Weinreb job (Seagate ST1000DM003-1SB102, s/n Z9AGMN33, 1 TB, attached to zabz-tech-linux as /dev/sdb over USB).

WHICH DRIVE, AND WHAT LAYOUT. `lsblk -o NAME,SIZE,TYPE,TRAN,ROTA,MODEL,SERIAL,FSTYPE,LABEL,MOUNTPOINT`. ROTA=1 is a spinning disk; the OEM Windows 10 layout is EFI 260M vfat + MSR 16M + Windows NTFS + WinRE ~1G + RECOVERY ~1G. Confirm nothing is already mounted before you touch it.

HEALTH. `sudo smartctl -a /dev/sdb`. The headline "PASSED" is nearly worthless on its own - it only means the drive has not tripped its own thresholds. What decides it:
 * `Current_Pending_Sector` and `Offline_Uncorrectable` - the live "is it dying right now" numbers. 0 and 0 means it is not.
 * `Reallocated_Sector_Ct` - low single digits is normal and stable; hundreds climbing between reads is not.
 * `UDMA_CRC_Error_Count` - 0 means the link/cable is clean, which matters doubly inside a USB enclosure.
 * `smartctl -l error` - "No Errors Logged" means it has never recorded an uncorrectable event. This is the single most reassuring line.
 * `Power_On_Hours` - wear, not fault. 54,082 hours is ~6.2 years: an elderly drive is still a healthy drive, but it is a reason to read it once, not to run experiments on it.
 * `smartctl -l selftest` - a short test recorded at hour 48 tells you nobody has actually tested this drive in years.

ENCRYPTION, THE DECISIVE TEST - read the volume boot sector, do not infer from a label or a tool's guess:
 `sudo xxd -l 512 /dev/sdb3`
 Offset 3 holds the OEM filesystem id. `NTFS ` means an ordinary NTFS volume. **`-FVE-FS-` means BitLocker** (including Windows "device encryption"). `LUKS\xba\xbe` at offset 0 means LUKS. `blkid` agrees and is a good second read - a BitLocker volume reports TYPE="BitLocker", not TYPE="ntfs". This test is what closed the encryption caveat on the Weinreb job: plain NTFS, no BitLocker signature, no LUKS header, so no key was needed and pulling the drive gained the data rather than ciphertext.

READABLE WITHOUT MOUNTING. `sudo ntfsinfo -m /dev/sdb3` prints version, sector/cluster sizes, volume size and `Volume Flags` - **0x0000 means clean, so no journal replay is owed** and a read-only mount is safe. `sudo ntfsls /dev/sdb3` lists a directory from the raw volume. Both read without opening the filesystem for writing, which is what "non-invasive" has to mean when the drive is the only copy of somebody's data.

TWO THINGS TO BE CAREFUL ABOUT
- 'Write Protect is off' in `dmesg` means writes ARE possible: nothing here is protected by hardware, only by your choice of command. Never run `fsck`/`ntfsfix` (writes), never run a self-test on a suspect drive to "check" it (it exercises the media), and never let a mount default to read-write.
- A read-only ntfs-3g mount (`-o ro,noatime`) writes nothing to a CLEAN volume. That is why `Volume Flags 0x0000` is checked first - on a dirty volume, replaying the journal is a write.
