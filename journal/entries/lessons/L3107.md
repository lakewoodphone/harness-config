**L3107 · A wake row gated by not_before is invisible to every reading; report next-claimable time, not waiting count**

A wake row can be gated by `not_before`, a future timestamp, and NO existing reading accounts for it.
Measured live 2026-09-30 14:52Z on the authority: all seven rows in state `new` had `not_before` in the
future — two of them 28 days out (2026-10-28), the soonest four days out — while:

- `autonomy-status.sh` printed `PROBLEMS: none` and
 `IDLE: 7 row(s) waiting and no cap in force - the next cron tick should claim`;
- `wake.py stats` printed `caps_in_force: []`;
- `wake-fanout.sh` had started a shift every 5 minutes for 55 minutes, none of which could claim, and
 deleted the staging evidence each time (`rm -rf "$INST"`, `wake-fanout.sh:186`).

`wake.py` already knows the answer: `_candidates_gated()` (wake.py:786) returns a `gated` dict with a
per-reason breakdown, and the claim path reports `blocked_by` as `night-quiet` or `row-gates`. The status
tool and the fan-out both throw that away and reason only about CAPS.

RULE: when reporting whether work is available, never count rows in a waiting state. Report the NEXT
CLAIMABLE TIME, computed from the same predicate the claim path uses, and distinguish three states —
claimable now, gated until T, and stalled (claimable now but no claim happened). A queue of rows that are
all gated for days is a WORK-SUPPLY problem, not a queue problem, and it needs its own reading.

Also measured: two sources (`dormant-handoff`, `wake-tuner`) deliberately file rows with `not_before` 3 to
7 days out, so `waiting > 0` is structurally meaningless as a signal of available work.
