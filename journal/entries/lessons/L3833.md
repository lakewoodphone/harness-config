**L3682 · A lab ticket is not the case: a vendor-mailbox sweep cannot answer a customer-state question**

## A lab ticket is not the case. Read the customer's own channel before writing a state line about them.

Measured 2026-10-12, on Weissman / 300DDR ticket **74048** — and it is a *shape*, not a one-off.

**What happened.** A co-resident session built `300ddr-lab-board-2026-10-12.md` correctly and thoroughly:
it swept `lakewoodphoneandtech@gmail.com` for the lab's domain, pulled and read **six** messages in full,
rebuilt every row, and committed at **13:20 -0400 (17:20 UTC)**. Two places in that board say the customer
*"has not replied"*. **He had replied at 13:47:42 UTC** — three and a half hours earlier — in the channel the
sweep never opened: **Dialpad SMS**, not the lab's mailbox.

| | |
|---|---|
| body, verbatim | `Ok thanks for trying. Gzl't` |
| when | **2026-10-12 13:47:42 UTC** = 09:47 ET |
| where | `dialpad_sms_cache` id `1125224`, `phone_normalized 8482993187`, `direction inbound`, `fetched_at 2026-10-12T14:15:02Z` |
| answered | the 2026-10-07 23:30Z bad-news text offering a premium lab — **he declined it** |

**The cost of the wrong line.** The board's section C read *"If he says yes it is a new cost decision —
Drive Savers up to 4,000, Platinum about 1,400 — and that is the owner's."* That is a live cost question
raised to the owner about a decision the customer had already closed out. **A wrong state line does not stay
in the file: it becomes a question someone asks someone.** Same mechanism as the 2026-09-18 *"the drive never
shipped"* conclusion that sent a human hunting a drive 2,900 miles away.

**The rule.** A ticket at a third-party lab is **half a case**. The lab's mailbox carries the vendor side;
the customer side lives in Dialpad / `communications` / the case file. **Before writing any sentence about
what a customer has or has not done, read it out of the customer's own traffic.** `ps_comms_waiting` exists
for exactly this question — *"who is waiting on a reply from us, and who is not"* — and it reads across
every channel; a vendor-mailbox sweep cannot answer a customer-state question no matter how carefully it is
done. Scope the sweep to the question being answered, not to the mailbox that is convenient.

**Second, smaller defect found in the same touch, and the same class.** The sync record's own
`references.linkWarning`, written **2026-09-17**, asserted that order **2618** was *"an hourly Home Assistant
sensor check (2026-05-18)"*. Re-measured live on both stores 2026-10-12 through `lpt ticket` (which queries
`operational` AND `app` and labels each): **2618 is a genuine Weissman order** — customer 2433, `+1848293187`,
`Repair - 18482993187 / 2nd number7323634312. laptop`, CANCELLED, FOUND in both. The 2026-09-17 annotation
does not reproduce. **A correction annotation is a claim like any other and needs its own provenance** — that
one shipped without a store named and was wrong about what it was rejecting. Rule this proves: *when you
annotate a link as wrong, name which store you read and when, or the next reader inherits a second error on
top of the first.*

**And the same file carried a third:** the case file's front-matter `work_order:` said **WO2608310001**,
which resolves to **Shmuel Birnbaum's AirTag intake (customer 1325)** — a different customer entirely. Every
reader that trusts front matter resolved the Weissman SSD case to another man's order for three weeks, while
the sync record's `linkWarning` said not to. **A correct warning next to an unchanged field is not a fix.**
The field has to be emptied, not annotated.
