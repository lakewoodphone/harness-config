**L3791 · A ceiling that refuses is a tripwire, not a safety property**

# A ceiling that refuses is a tripwire, not a safety property

**Rule.** A policy rule whose condition can only be satisfied by *every* caller being perfect is not a
safety property. Measured the same evening it shipped: the relay's new `TOO_LONG` refused any owner body
over 320 chars, on the theory that a door which fits its own body can never reach it. It did reach it
immediately — `textsend.send()` posts its transport-fitted body (up to `SOFT_LIMIT` 930) while
`_relay_gate` was being handed the RAW body, so **every owner text between 321 and 930 characters would
have been refused**, i.e. a message a door had already committed to, silently dropped. That is the exact
failure the relay was built to remove, reintroduced by its own guard.

**What replaced it.** Over-ceiling is now *measured, recorded and visible* (`verdict_reason='too-long'`,
counted by `status()`), and each **door** enforces the ceiling by fitting: the app door calls
`relay.fit()` before it submits; `textsend` reports the bytes it will post. Nothing is lost and the fact
is not hidden.

**Apply this.** Before shipping a refusal, ask: *can any current caller reach this condition?* and
*what does the caller do with the refusal?* If the answer is "it drops the message", the guard is worse
than the thing it guards against. Prefer **record + let the door enforce** over **refuse**. Related
measurement from the same session: the first real submission through the relay was a **766-character**
wall of text delivered to the owner at 01:54:51Z, because the relay had no length policy and the app
door's ceiling was roughly three times `textsend`'s.
