**L3204 · Our own origins proxy held the EA App's IPC port 3216 - check who owns a port before theorising about the app**

WHAT HAPPENED: the EA App showed "Communication error - The app couldn't connect to port 3216
... Error code EC:10701", warning it might block game launches. The cause was OUR OWN harness.

MEASURED: 127.0.0.1 ports 3200..3223 were ALL held by node running
`dshw-proxy.mjs --base 3200 --count 24 --target 3099`. The EA App's in-game-overlay IPC server
binds port 3216 - inside that block - so it could never bind, and EA's own log had been saying
`IGOIPCClient ... Origin not found IGOIPC Server3216` since 2026-10-01. I had seen that line at
01:46 last night and written it off as "hunting a client retired in 2022". It was our proxy
sitting on the port.

FIX: basePort 3200 -> 3300 in windows.json (the live value) and in the two dshw.ps1 fallbacks.
Old proxy stopped, new one started on 3300-3323 and verified (HTTP 200 on /__dshw/stats). EA app
restarted; EADesktop now owns 127.0.0.1:3216 Listen. Committed and pushed to harness-config.

THE DURABLE LESSON: a harness that grabs a contiguous block of low loopback ports is competing
for a shared namespace with every other application on the machine, and third-party apps do bind
fixed low ports - EA uses 3216, 5249 and 7544. Two rules follow:
1. Keep the harness's port block documented with WHY it sits where it does, so a future session
 cannot renumber it back into a contested range (windows.json now carries _basePortWhy).
2. When a third-party app reports a port or IPC failure, CHECK WHO OWNS THE PORT before theorising
 about the app. `Get-NetTCPConnection -LocalPort N` is one call and it would have saved hours.
 I spent that time building overlay theories while our own process held the answer.

CAVEAT, and it is a real one: I do NOT yet know that this port conflict caused the EA App's
freezing. It is now testable - if the app stops freezing, this was it. Do not record it as solved
until that is observed.
