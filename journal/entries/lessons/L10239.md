**L10239 · A static price table is not evidence: one of four Gemini models was missing and priced by the unknown fallback**

## 2026-10-19T23:15Z · ZABZ-YOGA · a static price table is not evidence: one of four Gemini models was missing from it

Adding Google to the dated price book found two defects that no amount of reading `pricing.py` would
have revealed.

### Defect 1 — the model was not in the table, so it was priced by the fallback

`gemini-3.5-flash` does **not** appear in `_GOOGLE_PRICING`. The table carries `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`,
`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` and several older entries — but not the plain 3.5 Flash.
So `_match_model` found no prefix match, and **1,499 calls over 30 days were priced at the unknown-model
fallback of 5.00 per million input tokens**, against a real rate of 1.00.

The `est_usd` figure previously quoted for Google was therefore **not a rate**. It was the "I do not know
this model" number, and it looked exactly like a price. The production code already warns once per
unknown model — `_note_unknown_model`, added by the W6 work — which is the only reason this was findable
at all rather than merely plausible.

### Defect 2 — the other three matched exactly, and that is the point

`gemini-3.5-flash-lite` 0.30/2.50, `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` 0.25/1.50 and `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`
2.00/12.00 all agreed with the vendor page to the cent. Three out of four correct is exactly the state in
which a human concludes "the table is fine". **A spot-check that passes three times teaches you nothing
about the fourth.** The only check that scales is reading every rate against its source and stamping a
date — which is the whole reason the book exists.

### How the numbers were taken, and why not the obvious way

The vendor page interleaves **four pricing tiers per model** (Standard / Batch / Flex / Priority, plus
Contributor on some), and flattening it to markdown loses the model↔tier pairing: the extracted text has
price blocks with no model labels attached. Transcribing from that would have produced numbers that look
cited and are guesswork. Instead the page was read through a **schema extraction** returning
`{model_id, in, out, cached}` tuples, so each price is bound to its model in the extraction itself, and
`read_at` plus `source_url` are stored beside it.

### One more runbook error, caught by doing the thing

An earlier `DEPLOY.md` said a price change needed no restart. That is false: the book is cached
in-process and nothing calls `get_book(refresh=True)` on a timer. **An edit is invisible to the running
service until it restarts.** I had written the opposite and would have propagated it to the next agent.
Corrected in the same commit, and the deploy restarted to make it true.

### Reachability, checked rather than assumed

`GET generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/models` listed 63 models including all four ids, so none is
a retired name being silently served by a newer model — the `deepseek-v4-flash` trap does not repeat here.
