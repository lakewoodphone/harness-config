**L2099 · A text assistant dies of silence: the AI line was healthy and unused because the only outcome for a real person was nothing**

**Measured 2026-09-20 on ZABZ-YOGA, against the live store and Twilio's own API.**

The AI text line `+1 732-444-7361` looked healthy: sensor green, 334 inbound rows, 15-row
permission ledger, cron running every 5 minutes. It had also been effectively dead for months.

- Newest real inbound (excluding my own test texts): **2026-09-18 03:29**. Undecided: **0**.
- `sms-responder.py run` printed `0 to decide` on 40+ consecutive cron ticks.
- **Chips Zebrowski** — the line's heaviest user at **239 in / 232 out** — last texted **2026-06-29**.
- The owner himself texted this line **11 times ever**, most recently **2026-05-11**, and every one
 was recorded `seen` or `ignored`. His own ledger row is `allow=queue`, so the system's only
 possible response to its owner was "raise an owner-queue row about yourself" or silence.

**The mechanism, and it generalises.** `allow=queue` was implemented as *send nothing, raise a
queue row*. For a personal line, where 12 of 17 correspondents are friends and family, that made
silence the default outcome for almost everyone — and the queue row it raised was not a decision
for the owner, it was the assistant asking permission to be given a job it should have done.
13 inbound texts still have no reply after them, including a door-code question from Bachrach
unanswered for 11 days and his father's *"I believe you sent this message to the wrong number"*.

**The rule.** A permission model whose *deny* branch is silence is not a safety feature — it is an
outage with a green dashboard. Every branch of a decision must end in something a human can
observe: an answer, a tracked piece of work, one real question for the owner, or an explicit
`ignored` with a reason. "Nobody answered" must be a value the system can count, not an absence.
