**L3719 · A counter visit and a part changing hands leave no trace in the shop's records - the owner's account is the only source, so write it and say so**

A customer standing at the counter and a part changing hands leave **no trace anywhere** in this
shop's records. Measured 2026-10-12 on WO2607280003 (Mrs. Vahab, LG Gram 16T90P).

The owner, in conversation: *"she came yesterday and i gave the ssd to get the data from"* and
*"we just have to confirm with her we can throw out the laptop."* Nothing corroborated it:

- `communications` for customer 2214: her last inbound is 2026-10-08, her 2026-10-11 request for
 the address is answered the same evening, and **nothing after that** - no text, no call, no
 voicemail.
- `customer_notes` on the order: the newest was the 08-06 bench chain; the 2026-10-12 notes are mine.
- `order_status_history`: no row between 2026-10-08 and my own writes.
- `lptcase pickup` / the case index: nothing recorded.

So the shop's record knew the address had been texted and nothing else, while in the real world the
SSD had left the building and the laptop had become scrap-pending. Had the label not been asked for,
the next staff member to open this order would have concluded the laptop was still waiting to be
collected.

**Rules.**
1. **A counter visit is a recordable event with no automatic recorder.** When the owner says a
 customer came, took something, or left something, write it: a production note with his words as
 the source, and the case file. Do not wait for a comms row - there will not be one.
2. **Date it as his account when nothing else supports it, and say so in the note.** "By the owner's
 account, 2026-10-11" is honest; a bare date invents a corroboration that does not exist.
3. **When a part leaves an item the shop still holds, the item's own label is the only reader who
 sees it.** The label is where "SSD IS OUT" and "DO NOT SCRAP YET" belong, because the machine is
 what someone picks up.
4. **A status has to describe what is actually pending.** After the visit the honest status was
 AWAITING_APPROVAL - waiting on the customer's yes to scrap - not READY_FOR_PICKUP, which says the
 machine is waiting to be collected. Both are valid vocabulary; only one is true.
