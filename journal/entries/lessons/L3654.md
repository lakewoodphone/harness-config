**L3654 · I marked a queue row answered when the owner had not spoken**

2026-10-13. I recorded the owner's answer to a queue row when he had not spoken.

WHAT I DID. `owner-queue.py add --from-json` was the safe path for writing a row, and I had used it
correctly six times. To correct that row's recommendation I reached for `owner-queue.py answer 470`,
whose help text reads `answer ID --answer "his words"`, and put MY OWN corrected finding into it.
The row flipped to `status=answered` with `answered_at` set, which is the queue's record that the
man himself decided something. He had not; at that point in the session he had not replied to
anything since 03:30.

WHY IT IS WORSE THAN A TYPO. This queue exists because 595 messages once reached him as "waiting for
the owner" when 383 of them were engineering faults that were never his. Its whole value is that a
row marked answered means HE answered. A false `answered` is the same defect the queue was built to
remove, written by the hand that built it - and it would have made the next session skip a live
question while believing it was settled.

HOW I FIXED IT, and why not by editing. The queue has no un-answer, and the DB is not mine to hand-
edit for cosmetics. So #470 was RESOLVED with a resolution that says, in the row itself, that it was
marked answered in error, that the `answer` field holds an agent correction and not his words, and
that the question he still owes is unchanged. The corrected row is #471. The erroneous state is
left visible on purpose: the queue should carry the evidence that this happened, the same way a
correction in the journal is a new entry citing the old one rather than a quiet rewrite.

THE RULE. **`answer` means the owner spoke; `resolve` means the work is done; a correction is
neither.** When I have new information about a row and the owner has said nothing, the tools that
fit are `resolve` (with a `--how` that says so) plus a new row - never `answer`. Read the flag's
help text as a description of what gets recorded, not as a menu of ways to update a row.
