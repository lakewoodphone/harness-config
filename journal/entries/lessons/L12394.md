**L12394 · A bookkeeping row with a null status resets every staleness clock in the LPT store - 12 devices became 20**

Order status history in the LPT store carries rows whose fromStatus AND toStatus are both NULL. They are bookkeeping, not activity: on 2026-10-21 a backfill wrote 51 of them reading "Backfill: order total set from its recorded successful payments", and the 2026-10-21 rows land on orders that are otherwise 50+ days stale.

Any staleness or age clock built on max(order_status_history.changedAt) therefore silently resets itself for every backfilled row. Measured 2026-10-27 while applying the owner's 50-day uncollected-device rule: including null-status rows gave 12 devices past the line, excluding them gave 20. The 8 missing ones included mrs gross (57 days, paid in full) and mrs meyer (50 days, paid in full) - devices that would have stayed on the shelf while the report said nothing was old.

The fix is not "filter where toStatus is not null" alone, because that still treats a backfill of any kind as a move. The general rule: an age clock must ask what WRITES the field it reads, and a field written by more than one process needs the writer named per row. The policy doc's clock uses the newest of five specific sources (pickup_notified_at, the READY_FOR_PICKUP/UNFIXABLE transition, the outbound ready-for-pickup message, the newest real transition, createdAt) precisely so that one noisy writer cannot reset it.

Companion trap from the same hour: orders.pickup_notified_at is null on all 60 collectable rows, so a query that leads with it returns "nobody is overdue" - a refusal wearing the costume of good news.
