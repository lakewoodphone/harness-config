**L2514 · iPad 9's Lightning port is soldered to the board - a 3-second charge is a refusal, not a port fault**

## What I learned

The iPad 9th generation's Lightning port — the dock connector — is **soldered directly to the logic
board**. It is not a plug-in flex. Parts for this generation are sold explicitly labelled "Charging
Port Only — Soldering Required", and two board-level service shops describe the iPad 9 dock as
board-mounted. On the iPad 7 and 8 the connector **is** a flex, which is where the wrong belief comes
from: the part is even sold as a "Charging Port Flex Cable for iPad 7/8/9".

Why this was worth writing down: this repo already contained the wrong conclusion, stated confidently.
`docs/customer-operations/cases/wajsfeld-charging-port-7322283232-2026-09-09.md` says, of an iPad 9
(A2603): *"the Lightning port is a flex cable assembly, not soldered - it snaps onto the board via a
connector (no microsoldering)"*, and a $1.68 Mobilesentrix iPad 7/8/9 port flex was ordered on that
basis (order #108608845). An agent quoting the next iPad 9 port case from that case file orders the
wrong part and promises a job the bench cannot do without board-level work. The Wajsfeld case now
carries a dated correction section pointing at the evidence.

Also learned on the same case, and true of charge faults generally: a charge that **starts and is then
cut off after ~3 seconds** is a *negotiated* charge being refused. The port cannot time itself out, so
"the port looks fine" and "it stops after 3 seconds" are not in conflict — and a visual inspection
cannot settle the case. The split is *measurement*: (1) known-good MFi cable and 20 W adapter, free —
a non-MFi or damaged cable produces exactly this 3-second signature; (2) clean the port under
magnification, free — lint compacted against the back wall looks clean from outside and is the most
common single cause; (3) a USB power meter inline for 60 seconds without touching the cable, which
distinguishes repeating 2–5 s current bursts (battery: high ESR or a failed gas gauge) from 5 V and
0 A with no 9 V negotiation (board/charging IC) from current that restarts when the plug is wiggled
(port contact). Battery OCV 3.6–4.2 V normal, < 3.3 V deeply discharged, < 2.5 V replace; more than
0.3–0.5 V of sag under a ~1 A load means high ESR. Those thresholds are shop criteria derived from the
3.73 V / 4.3 V cell spec, not Apple service limits.
