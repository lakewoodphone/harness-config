**L1560 · When an operation keeps losing something, fix the LAYER it lives in, not the step that loses it - and verify durability across TWO repetitions of the breaking operation**

**Evidence, 2026-09-15, round 10.** Last round I documented a fragility and it bit four hours later. This round I closed the *recurrence* of it, and the method is worth recording separately from the fix.

## What worked

The guards were dying because they lived in the container's **writable layer** — a place a recreate replaces. The fix was not to guard the file better; it was to **move the guard to a layer a recreate cannot touch**: extract the six patched files, bake them into a derived image with a 7-line Dockerfile, and pin the compose service to that image.

**Verified in a specific order, and the order is the point:**

1. Check `bare=0` **inside the new image**, before using it — not in the running container, which is the thing that had already proven untrustworthy.
2. Check the posture on the old container (baseline).
3. **Recreate** — the exact operation that had wiped the guards.
4. Check the posture **after**.
5. **Recreate again, with `--force-recreate`**, and check a third time.

**Step 5 is the one worth keeping.** A mechanism that failed once should not be believed on one success. Two independent recreates cost about two minutes and turn "it seems to work" into "it survives the operation that broke it, twice".

## The general rule

**When something is lost by an operation, look for the *layer* it lives in, not the step that loses it.** The instinct is to make the losing step safer — add a warning, add a checklist item, remember to re-apply. All of those are habits, and habits fail at exactly the moment they are needed. The durable question is *"which storage class does this thing live in, and does the operation that hurts me preserve that class?"* Container writable layer: no. Image layer: yes.

**Corollary for anyone reading the record later:** the fix protects **six files only**. The session-preservation change and everything else committed after the 2026-09-10 build are still undeployed, and the build context is still an unsourced tree. **A narrow fix that closes a recurrence must say what it does not cover**, or a future session will read "guards are durable now" and stop looking at the rest.

## And the habit that found the bug

I opened this round by running the posture check **before touching anything**. Last round's lesson was that my own four-hour-old "all nine still 401" had aged into a false statement — a measurement that was true when taken. The only defence against a record that ages badly is to **re-measure at the start**, not to trust the note.
