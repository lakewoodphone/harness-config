## The mechanism — a grep reads the checkout, not the branch you are describing

The working tree was on `lpt/intake-cancel-control-20261018`, a PR branch twelve days stale, with 62 uncommitted files. I ran `grep runs-on .github/workflows` **in that tree** and reported the result as the state of the repo's CI. Both statements were individually "measured" and the conclusion was still wrong, because I never asked which commit I was measuring.

**Rule: to describe what CI does, read the branch that runs it — `git show origin/test:<path>`, never a working-tree grep.** `git status` tells you which branch a checkout is on; a grep does not. Before quoting any file as evidence about a branch, state the branch and the revision in the same breath.

This is the same failure family as L3425's stale "private + free plan" claim, which I caught and corrected hours earlier — and then committed in a new place. Being on guard against a stale claim does not protect a *freshly measured* claim about the wrong object.

## The CI cost arithmetic, measured properly

**Anchor trap.** The literal 20 most recent completed runs are useless as a cost basis when the account is refusing jobs: 86 jobs, 84 of them never started, **4 billed minutes total**. A job that does not start is not billable, so a cost estimate anchored on an outage reports near-zero spend. Price only runs that executed steps.

**Per-job rounding is the whole story.** Billing rounds each job up to the whole minute. On a cheap sample, 3.8 exact minutes became **40 billed minutes — 10.4x**. A 12-job workflow is never priced at its wall clock.

**Correct basis** (20 most recent runs that ran steps, 2026-10-20T03:25Z):

| workflow | n | mean billed/run | median | range | jobs/run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PR Pre-Check | 21 | **24.8** | 30 | 4-58 | 14.1 |
| Phantom Dependency Check | 25 | 1.5 | 2 | 1-2 | 1.0 |
| Validate Backend Image | 23 | 2.0 | 1 | 1-6 | 1.0 |
| Deploy environment | 5 | 14.8 | 14 | 11-21 | 3.0 |

One PR push is about **28.3 billed minutes**. Daily: **1,953 billed minutes on 2026-10-19** (76 PR events) rising to **4,876 on 2026-10-20** (177 PR events).

**So a spending limit is a fuse, not a fix.** At 0.008/minute, 60 buys 7,500 minutes; plus a 2,000-minute allotment that is 9,500 min/month = 317 min/day. The shop is pushing 76-177 times a day, which is **8-19x over**. A 60 limit lasts roughly two to four days and then jobs are refused again — with a *different* annotation the next session will misdiagnose.

Cheap mitigations, measured, both too small to matter: `cancel-in-progress` on phantom saves 6% (most pushes are distinct SHAs on distinct branches, so supersession is rare); skipping PR workflows on markdown-only pushes saves 15% (9 of 60 sampled PR heads). Together they leave 16x over. **The lever is the trigger policy — 167 pushes a day — not the cap and not the workflow internals.**
