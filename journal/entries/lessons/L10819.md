**L10819 · The exempt set is the spending set - a cheap-hour gate can only save what the deferrable departments are worth**

**Measured 2026-10-20.** The live gate exempts seven agents so that no customer or owner path waits:
`orchestrator`, `dept_business_ops`, `dept_comms_relationships`, `dept_scheduling`, `dept_home_life`,
`dept_research`, `dept_devices`. That is the right safety call.

**But those seven are the spenders.** Measured over 3 hours, on `deepseek-flash` at ~45k tokens a call:
dept_business_ops 213 calls, dept_comms_relationships 173, dept_ai_platform 221 (deferred),
orchestrator 179. The 25 deferred departments are the *light* users. So the gate holds back the cheap
minority and runs the expensive majority at double price.

That is why peak-hour volume rose 12.8 percent on the day the gate went live instead of falling: the
saving was structurally near zero before a single line was written.

**The lesson is not "exempt fewer departments".** Exempting them is correct — a shop that delays a
customer reply for three hours to save half a cent on tokens has misunderstood the business. The
lesson is that **a price/hour policy can only ever save what the deferrable set is worth, so measure
that set's spend before building the policy.** Here it was measured afterwards and it was ~zero.

The money is in the prompt, not the clock: 90 percent of input tokens are carried by calls over 30k
tokens, and cutting them to 12k removes 73 percent of the input bill.
