**L12266 · The disk fills in 1-3 days no matter the size - every fill on record, and the lesson that did not fire**

The fleet closed the case on "why every week you make me resize", and the answer is that the disk size was never in the equation.

EVIDENCE, from the journal, dates only:
2026-09-27/28 office 100% / 17 MB free (345 GB of snapshots) -> 09-30 99% / 7.6 GB (298 GB snapshots; prune-backups.sh had been deleted by a deploy, so retention had silently stopped) -> 10-05 99% / 6.4 GB -> cutover 10-11 to a fresh 75 GB -> 10-13 97%, then 98.7% / 0.0 GB free -> 10-14 ZERO bytes free three times in one day, filesystem doubled 76 -> 152 GB at 01:15Z and it still filled twice more that day -> 10-16 100% / 1.0 GB (.cfo/backups 33 GB incl. one 28.72 GB VACUUM INTO) -> 10-18/19 96% -> 100% / 1.41 GiB (five parallel audit agents spilling unindexed aggregates into /var/tmp) -> 10-19 rescale to 225 GiB with 99 GB free -> 10-22 90% / 23.9 GB free, three days later.

THE INTERVAL: 2 days, 5, 8, then 1, 2, 2, 3 - flat at 1-3 days since 2026-10-13, unchanged by 76 -> 152 -> 225 GiB. What fills it is discrete 18-29 GB writes and producers with no ceiling (child copies, VACUUM INTO, agent worktrees, snapshot dirs that lose their pruner). Steady database growth at ~1 GB/day cannot fill a 225 GB disk in three days and never did.

THE LESSON WAS ALREADY WRITTEN: L3932 (2026-10-14) - a host at 97% turned SQLite writes into waits and wedged the running loop; the fix named there was a disk budget in every child brief and a disk reading before and after every fleet run. It was written on 10-14 and the same failure recurred on 10-18 with five parallel agents. Lessons that are not installed in a script do not fire.

A second thing this session learned the hard way: **two of five research children died at the 30-minute transport ceiling because their own read-only measurements saturated the box** - a recursive du over 194 GB on a shared 8-vCPU host starved sshd for everything else, including the children. The three that finished used bounded queries (dbstat where aggregate=1; a single streaming pass instead of GROUP BY). Reading this host heavily is itself an outage risk: bound it, ionice it, time-limit it.

STATE AFTER TODAY: df 194G used / 23G free / 90% -> 155G / 62G / 72%; 39 GB reclaimed with archive-before-remove; nightly reaper and hourly 88%/25GB journal alarm installed and proved. The remaining unbounded producers are the CI loop image at /srv/ci, the search index, and the session-event table - and only the last one needs the owner's decision.
