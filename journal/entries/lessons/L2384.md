**L2384 · A stored conclusion is a claim, not a measurement - and per-unit price is not cost per customer-ready unit**

**What happened (2026-09-22):** asked to source iPhones for the Waze device launch, I found the 2026-09-14/15 sweep, read its verdict ("settled 2026-09-15: buy the $135.80 five-lot, stop optimising"), and adopted it as my own. I proposed that lot, the owner rejected it, I proposed it again, and he pushed back a second time: *"the 5 unit lot is not so great a price and doesn't promise so great a condition, i don't know why you are so enamored by it"*.

**He was right, and I had already been given the evidence against my own position.** My own 40-unit hunt, run hours earlier, reported verbatim that *"the prior sweep's error was treating a low unit price as the objective... $135.80 is a fine price for a Fair unit and a bad deal for THIS product"* - and I still led with $135.80.

**Two distinct errors, both previously recorded in this journal and both repeated:**
1. **Believing a stored number.** A prior session's conclusion is a claim, not a measurement. Its age and its inputs both matter. The sweep was 8 days old and the market moves within the hour - the sweep itself documents two of its own figures going stale the same day.
2. **Optimising the wrong variable.** Sticker price per unit is not the objective. The objective is **cost per customer-ready unit a customer keeps**, which includes the dead-unit rate and the expected warranty cost. Grade "Fair" explicitly allows minor screen flaws and third-party parts; on a navigation device the screen IS the product, and we carry an 8-month warranty, so Fair shifts cost from the purchase order into the warranty line where it is invisible until it bites.

**The signal I should have weighted:** the owner runs a phone business and flips phones for a living. When he says *"i can find better deals on ebay for phones in better conditions"*, that is domain expertise, not a preference. I had a research document; he has a thousand transactions.

**Rule:** when a stored conclusion and the owner's direct market intuition disagree, treat the stored conclusion as the thing to re-test, not the thing to defend. And never let a unit price stand in for the cost of a unit the customer keeps.
