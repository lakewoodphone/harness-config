## A four-minute window is not a maintenance window: what two refused database swaps taught (2026-10-19)

Compacting the company database (`authority.db`, 32 GiB with 13 GiB of free pages) needs every writer
gone for the length of a `VACUUM INTO` rebuild. Measured rebuild: **4 min 4 s**. Both attempts to swap
the result in on a live afternoon were refused by my own verification, and the refusal is the finding.

| attempt | what was stopped | what refused it |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 18:08:41Z | `company-api`, `esearch` (the only two processes holding the file) | `dsh_sessions` live 8926 vs copy 8925 — **a systemd timer fired mid-rebuild and wrote one row** |
| 2, 18:17:52Z | the two above **plus** `cron`, `lpt-owner-queue.timer`, `dsh-archive-local.timer`, `transcript-archive-pull.timer` | `model_usage` live 276902 vs copy 276897 — **five telemetry rows from a writer that is none of those** (a cron line already running when cron stopped, `atd`, or a shell left open) |

Neither attempt changed anything: the script restarted the writers, `/health` came back `ok`, the
database stayed at 32 G, zero failed units. Nine minutes of company-API downtime bought two facts.

**The transferable rules.**

1. **On a host with 74 cron lines and 20 timers, "stop the processes that hold the file" is not the
 same as "nothing will write".** Stopping `cron` does not stop a cron job that started before you
 stopped it. If the operation needs exclusivity, it needs a *quiet hour*, not a clever stop list.
2. **Verification must distinguish business data from telemetry.** A blanket "any row-count difference
 aborts" is unachievable on a live box and, worse, it is the wrong shape: five missing cost rows and
 one missing customer task are not the same event. The rule is now exact match on
 `dsh_sessions`/`tasks`/`conversations`/`memories`, and a bounded 0–500 behind on
 `model_usage`/`dsh_session_events`.
3. **A pre-built compacted copy cannot be swapped in later.** It is a snapshot; twelve minutes on it
 was already 5,549 session events behind. The rebuild belongs inside the window, always.
4. **`authority.db-wal` belongs to the old inode.** Rename the database and leave the WAL, and the next
 process applies a foreign WAL to the new file — corruption of a database that passed
 `integrity_check` a second earlier. `-wal` and `-shm` move with the original or nothing moves.
5. **When two attempts have cost the afternoon and delivered nothing, stop.** The reclaim is 13 GiB on
 a disk with 97 GiB free. That is an optimisation, not an emergency, and a third interruption during
 business hours would be a worse decision than deferring to the window.

**Where it stands:** `/root/bin/compact-authority-db.sh` — `--check` and `--apply`, automatic rollback,
refuses on stale backup / unaccounted holder / business-table mismatch / telemetry drift beyond bound.
Preflighted green at 18:1xZ. It runs after 21:00 UTC, once.
