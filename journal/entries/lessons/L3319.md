**L3319 · L: Never address the customer by name in an SMS body**

OWNER CORRECTION, 2026-10-05, verbatim: "you forgot that we don''t say the customers name".

WHAT I DID WRONG
I drafted: "Hi Mrs. Whiteman, good news - your laptop is in. ..."
He removed the name and kept the greeting: "Hi, good news - your laptop is in. ..."
Similarity of the corrected text to mine: 0.9504 - a one-token edit, and the whole
point of the exercise is that the one token matters.

THE RULE
Never put the customer''s name in the message body. Not "Hi Mrs. Whiteman", not
"Hi John", not "Hi Meir Shmuel" mid-message either. Open with a bare greeting
("Hi,") or no greeting at all. The shop knows who it is texting; the phone number is
the address.

style_rules row 1 in ~/secretary-drafts.db on secratary, active. The corrected text

with source='owner_correction'.

NOTE ON THE LEDGER: the corrected text is still NOT sent. final_text means "the
agreed wording", not "delivered". A separate 'sent' write happens only after the
message actually goes out, so the two states never get confused.

WHY I GOT IT WRONG: the record does contain a few "Hi <name>" openers (36 duplications
of the canned laptop-options message, one "Yes Bezh"). I generalised from those. The
distinction he is making is that canned multi-line quotes may name the person, while
conversational replies never do.
