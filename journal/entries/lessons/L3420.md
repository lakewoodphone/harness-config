**L3420 · Scrapping dead shop laptops: SSD first, RAM second, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth never**

Owner is working through a box of dead shop laptops, removing valuable parts before binning. He asked whether the Bluetooth module is worth pulling. It is not, and the pull-list is now measured rather than guessed.

PART VALUES (all captured 2026-10-11 unless noted)
- Used M.2 NVMe, eBay sold via getpcparts.com, tracker refreshed 2026-10-10: 128GB 15, 240GB 20, 256GB 25, 500GB 63, 512GB 70, 960GB 108, 1TB 119, 2TB 234.
- 2.5" SATA SSD new, Newegg live scrape 2026-10-11: 512GB 61-99. Used SATA sits near the NVMe numbers.
- DDR4-3200 SODIMM new (Newegg live): 8GB 54-78, 16GB 87-129, 32GB single 180-333 (median 243).
- DDR3L-1600 SODIMM new (Newegg live): 8GB 22-33. DDR3 still has value in 2026 because the whole DRAM market is in a record surge.
- Intel AX200 M.2 2230 Wi-Fi 6 card new: 13.88-22.99. Older 7260/3160/Realtek/Qualcomm: effectively zero.
- Bluetooth in a laptop is NOT a separate chip. It is a radio on the Wi-Fi card (M.2 2230 or half-size mini PCIe). Pulling "the Bluetooth" means pulling the Wi-Fi card, for about 10 of value and a fight with the antenna wires through the hinge.

RULES
1. Pull SSDs first - an M.2 or mSATA drive in a machine being binned is worth more than the RAM stick in it (512GB NVMe 70 used vs 8GB DDR4 SODIMM roughly 25-40).
2. Then DDR4 SODIMM (8GB and up), then DDR3/DDR3L SODIMM 4GB and up. DDR2 and 2GB-and-under DDR3 are skip.
3. Skip batteries - no value and a fire hazard, especially swollen ones. Skip optical drives, webcams, speakers, keyboards, DDR2, and every Wi-Fi card older than AX200.
4. Do NOT bin hard drives. They are worth nothing and they carry customer data; the shop has lost customer data twice. Pull, keep, wipe or physically destroy.
5. Exception worth stopping for: a dead MacBook is worth far more in parts than a dead Windows laptop (logic board, Retina panel, top case, charger). Same for old ThinkPads.
6. Chargers and power bricks are real money (15-40, Apple MagSafe more) and are needed for bench testing.
