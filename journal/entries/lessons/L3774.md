**L3774 · I committed the #1 ranked failure while writing the audit that ranked it: absence at a guessed path is not absence**

THE SINGLE MOST USEFUL PIECE OF EVIDENCE THIS AUDIT PRODUCED, AND IT IS AGAINST ME.

On 2026-10-13 I measured 6,927 DSH sessions, classified 945 of the owner's, and ranked the
failure modes. The top one, at 11.4% of his sessions, was `wrong-data` -- "acted on, or
reported, something that was not true". I wrote a whole section of the new persona about it,
echoing the predecessor audit's worst incident ("a crisis reported that did not exist, because
a stale database copy was read and believed").

**Within the hour I did exactly that.** I reported to the owner, in words, that "the transcript
archive has been dead since the cutover" and that four days of his sessions existed in one
place only. I filed it as a data-loss emergency (`P3162`), wrote a dated "CORRECTED, measured"
block into `AGENTS.md` section 1, added a caveat to the `yocheved` preset, and dispatched a
repair workstream and a second one for the disk arithmetic I had built on top of it.

NONE OF IT WAS TRUE.

 what I did : checked /root/lpt-transcripts and /home/zabz/lpt-transcripts on the
 authority, found neither, concluded "the authority has no archive"
 the truth : it is /opt/company/archive/lpt-transcripts -- 6.6 GB, manifest.sqlite
 52 MB, 258 object dirs, re-hosted on 2026-10-12
 how it was found: `systemctl list-timers | grep transcript` -- one command, on the box I
 had already ssh'd into twice
 the last pass : 2026-10-13T13:39:56Z, exit 0, ingested from five machines, twenty minutes
 BEFORE I declared it dead

The archive was not dead. It is ALIVE AND FLAPPING -- the 13:23Z pass failed exit 3 with the
message `TRANSCRIPT ARCHIVE PULL FAILED -- cannot reach , which this archive has pulled before`,
with an EMPTY machine name, and the 12:28Z pass failed the same way at a 6 GB memory peak. That
is a real, smaller, different defect: an alarm that cannot name the machine it could not reach.

WHY IT HAPPENED, in the mechanism and not the apology:

 1. **I checked two guessed paths and treated their absence as the state of the world.** The
 store had been MOVED eleven days earlier by a migration, and I knew a migration had
 happened -- I had cited the cutover in the same document. I did not look in /opt, and I did
 not list the filesystem. Absence at a guessed path is not absence.
 2. **I did not check the thing that would have answered it in one command.** `systemctl
 list-units`, `list-timers`, `crontab -l` -- the estate's own AGENTS.md section 0 says to do
 precisely this BEFORE DESIGNING ANYTHING, and I skipped it for a system I was about to
 declare broken.
 3. **A stale artifact corroborated me.** `secratary:/home/zabz/.transcript-archive/status.json`
 is the OLD store's status file, frozen at 2026-10-11T20:23Z, still sitting on the frozen box.
 It was exactly the "last ingest" I expected to see, so it read as confirmation instead of as
 the stale copy it was. A stale reading that agrees with your hypothesis is the most
 dangerous kind.
 4. **The claim travelled further than the evidence.** Within one turn it was in a journal pain
 entry, in the always-on instruction surface (AGENTS.md), in a second preset, in a subagent
 brief, and in my report to the owner. A wrong reading of one path became standing policy in
 four places, and the two that will be read by agents who were not here are exactly the two
 that will be believed.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD -- and these are now the rules, not the lessons:

 * **Before declaring a service dead, ask the SERVICE.** `systemctl status/list-timers`,
 `crontab -l`, `docker ps`, a status endpoint. A filesystem path is one of many possible
 locations for a system's state; the service's own scheduler is the authority on whether it
 runs.
 * **"Not at the path I guessed" is a refusal, not a negative.** Say "I could not find it at X"
 and then go and find where it actually is. This estate moves things by migration, and the
 standing rule is already that an empty result is a refusal.
 * **When a reading confirms what you expected, that is when to check its age.** A status file
 whose timestamp matches your hypothesis perfectly is a candidate for being the stale copy,
 not the proof.
 * **Do not let one unverified reading reach the always-on surface.** A journal pain entry is
 cheap to retract. `AGENTS.md` is read by every agent on every machine, and a false
 "CORRECTED, measured" block there is worse than no block: it carries the authority of the
 file it sits in. Instruction-surface edits need the same evidentiary bar as a claim to the
 owner -- and I applied a LOWER bar there than to a journal entry, which is backwards.

THE MEASUREMENT THAT MAKES THIS EVIDENCE RATHER THAN ANECDOTE: I committed the #1 ranked
failure, in the same session, while writing the audit that ranked it. The failure mode is not
theoretical, it is not fixed by knowing about it, and it is not specific to a model reading a
stale database -- it is what happens when absence is inferred from a guessed location. That is
now the centre of `docs/dsh-evolution/05-instruction-redesign.md` and the reason the new persona
requires a command and its output line for every claim.
