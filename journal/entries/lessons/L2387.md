**L2387 · The case corpus cannot answer an 8-month return rate - check a corpus's SPAN and population before computing any rate from it**

**What I tried to measure:** the 8-month return/failure rate on used iPhones the shop SELLS. This is the number that settles Fair-vs-Excellent-Refurbished sourcing (below ~14% return rate the $135.80 Fair unit wins; above ~25% the refurbished unit is unambiguous), and I told the owner it was mine to measure rather than his to guess.

**Result: the accessible records CANNOT answer it, and I am recording that rather than substituting a proxy.**

Evidence (measured 2026-09-22): `lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/cases/` holds **199 case files spanning 2026-07-22 to 2026-09-19** - about **two months**, against a question that needs eight. Only **7 case FILES mention iPhone at all**, and every one is a *repair* intake (deleted threads, a 14 Pro, a 16 Pro with no charging, an iPhone 11 for Mrs Mizrahi, an 13 Pro, an iPhone 8 WhatsApp job, a backup job) - **not one is a warranty return on a used device we sold**. Corpus-wide string counts for "warranty" (137) and "return" (398) are boilerplate and case-template language, not events, and must not be divided into anything. A count of 514 for "RMA" across 199 files is self-evidently a false positive, which is a good illustration of why counts before inspection are not evidence.

**The correct source, for whoever picks this up:** the production repair/warranty records in the website's Postgres (work orders and their status history over 8+ months), not the case corpus. That needs database access this session did not have, not more searching of the markdown corpus.

**The generalisation:** a corpus with a two-month span cannot answer an eight-month-rate question, and no amount of grep fixes that. Check the SPAN and the RELEVANT POPULATION before computing any rate from documents - and when it cannot answer, say so instead of producing a number that looks like an answer.
