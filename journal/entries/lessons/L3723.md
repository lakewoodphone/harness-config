**L3723 · Never text 'out for pickup' on an intention - the message states a physical fact, and Pinter drove over to an empty shelf**

## 2026-10-12, ZABZ-TECH. Cost: a customer stood at the pick-up, said "I don''t see it there?" twice,
and got an opening-hours robot for 51 minutes.

### WHAT HAPPENED
Owner, 2026-10-12: *"i''m putting it out for pickup now"*. I drafted and sent, on that word,
**"Hi, your laptop is out for pickup"** (Dialpad `6161332475568128`, 19:51 local). The laptop was
**still inside the office**. The owner later: *"i forgot to put it out and i think he came and just
picked up his plug"*.

Sequence, live from `dialpad_call_full` + `dialpad_sms_cache`:
- 17:27 he calls the shop, 28.9 s, **never connected** - he was trying to reach us before the text;
- 19:51 **we say the laptop is out for pickup**;
- 21:25:44 **"I don''t see it there?"**;
- 21:25:45 auto-reply: **hours and the drop-off address**;
- 21:25:47 **"I don''t see it there?"** again;
- 21:25:48 the same auto-reply;
- 22:16 our apology (Dialpad `6362478980931584`), 51 minutes after he first said he could not find it.

### RULE 1 - do not send a location claim on an intention
"Out for pickup" is not a courtesy, it is a **statement about where a physical object is**. The owner
saying *"i''m putting it out now"* authorises the message but does not make it true. **Before sending
any ready/out-for-pickup text, the device must already be where the message says it is** - and if I
cannot verify that from the bench, the message does not go, or it is worded as *"ready - come by and
we will bring it out"*, which cannot be false.

### RULE 2 - the auto-reply is actively harmful in an emergency, and nobody notices
The closed-hours auto-responder fires on ANY inbound while we are shut, with our opening hours. To a
customer at an empty pick-up saying *"I don''t see it there?"* that is the worst possible answer, and
it fired **twice**. It also cannot distinguish a routine after-hours question from a customer standing
in our hallway. **This is the second customer-side harm from the same robot in one week** (the other:
the address-less template that sent hours to people asking for the address).
**The fix is not a better template.** It is that a second inbound from the same number inside a few
minutes, at an hour the shop is shut, is a signal that wants a human - and today there is nothing that
turns it into one. Raise it as a real piece of work, not a wording tweak.

### RULE 3 - when the shop has caused the problem, say so in the first clause
The owner''s wording, and it is the right instinct: *"it was mistakenly left inside the office
instead of out, not it was left out"*. Admitting the real mistake beats describing the state we
announced and failed to deliver. Same family as the runbook''s rule that a stale job is the shop''s
failure before it is the customer''s.

### ALSO RECORDED
The customer also has a **charging wire with us** that nobody had tracked - the owner had to ask
whether he collected it. Every case that holds a customer''s loose accessory needs it written down,
because "did you get your wire?" is not a question we should be asking after the fact.
