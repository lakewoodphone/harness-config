**L2612 · Establish the interface before recommending anything that has to fit**

2026-09-27, household bathroom drains. I recommended a TubShroom before knowing what kind of tub stopper he had. The stopper type was the single fact that decided whether the product works at all: a **trip-lever** tub (lever on the overflow plate, plunger hidden inside the pipe) takes an in-drain catcher "without any modification"; **lift-and-turn, push-pull, pop-up, toe-touch and flip-it** all put the plug in the drain hole, so the catcher competes with it and the plug must be removed first — which destroys the reason for buying it.

His tub is a **lift-and-turn knob**. My recommendation was wrong for it, and I only found out because he answered a question I should have asked before recommending anything.

**Rule: for any product that must physically interface with something the owner already has — a fixture, a port, a mount, a case, a connector — establish the interface type first, not after the recommendation.** One question up front costs a turn; a wrong cart costs money and confidence. This generalises past plumbing: it is the same failure as specifying a part without the model number.

**Evidence:** tubshroom.com/blogs/tips/types-of-bathtub-drains-a-complete-identification-guide, read 2026-09-28.
