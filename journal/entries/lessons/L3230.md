**L3230 · CORRECTION to L3229/P2824: the 14 ps_* tools vanished because a reboot exhausted the MCP reconnect budget, and the live connector I measured belonged to a dying engine**

The earlier diagnosis in this session asserted that "the harness holds a live, abandoned
MCP session" from ssh.exe pid 23784 (child of engine pid 11176). That reading was wrong
in one material way and the correction matters.

WHAT I HAD: ssh.exe 23784 alive with parent 11176, and a matching sshd-session +
ps_mcp_server on secratary still open - read as "the harness has an abandoned live session".

WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS: pid 11176 was an engine in its death throes. The laptop had been
restarted at 10:52 (shutdown.exe, Event 1074) and again at 11:05:50 (StartMenuExperienceHost,
"Other (Unplanned)", Event 1074), with LastBootUpTime 11:06:25. The old engine and its
connector were on their way out; the connector's liveness was evidence of nothing about
the supervisor's state. The current engine is pid 25228 (started 11:07:28 by the watchdog),
and its connector is ssh.exe 18100 (11:09:09) - a DIFFERENT process on a DIFFERENT engine.

LESSON, general: an ssh/MCP child process that is still alive tells you the transport
exists, NOT that the client considers it registered. When judging whether a connector is
healthy, read the ENGINE's identity (pid + start time) first and bind the connector to it
by ParentProcessId, and compare that engine start time with the failure window. A connector
whose parent engine predates a restart is a zombie of the previous generation.

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED, end to end (all times 2026-10-02 local, ZABZ-YOGA):
10:52:01 deliberate reboot -> 10:53:00 boot -> 10:54:16 engine pid 11176 up (dshw),
duplicate boot at 10:54:52 dies on EADDRINUSE 127.0.0.1:3099, connector spawned 10:55:37,
the connection closed almost immediately, the supervisor's bounded budget (10 attempts,
delays 0.5/1/2/4/8/16 + 30 s cap = ~121 s) expired and it disposed all 14 tools; the
owner saw "Tools updated / 14 removed" at 10:56:38. The engine itself stayed up (it was
still serving at 10:59:59), then the 11:05:50 reboot killed it, and the 1-minute watchdog
ensure brought 25228 up at 11:07:28 - which re-registered the tools. Verified with a real
call: mcp__secretary__ps_db_query SELECT COUNT(*) FROM tasks -> 27346.

SO: a machine restart is what caused the tool loss AND a machine restart is what cured it.
Bounded give-up means "shut the laptop for lunch, come back to an engine whose company
interface is quietly gone" is normal behaviour, not an edge case.

Corollary for future journal entries: my first pain entry P2824 and lesson L3229 were
written from live measurements that were true but interpreted against the wrong engine.
Correct them by citing this entry; never rewrite them.
