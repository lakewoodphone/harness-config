**L1753 · Leaving a VoIP provider: read the DESTINATION carrier's porting guide, and test DTMF not audio in the money-back window**

**Evidence (2026-09-16, verified for the owner's mother's line +1 732-363-1752):**

- **Port-out from magicJack is a formality, not a trap** - and the proof is that four unrelated carriers
 publish the exact fields in their own porting guides: Mint Mobile, Visible, Ultra Mobile and Straight
 Talk all document account number = magicJack online account, and PIN = **your magicJack account
 password, which must be 6 digits** (change it in the portal first if it is not), plus magicJack's
 porting line **844-866-2442** for "problems releasing your number". Carriers do not publish the steps
 out of a provider that never releases numbers. The FCC states it plainly: *"Once you request service
 from a new company, your old company cannot refuse to port your number, even if you owe money for an
 outstanding balance or termination fee"* (fcc.gov porting guide, read 2026-09-16), and simple ports
 are due in one business day. Practice reports for VoIP-to-wireless run ~10 days; one documented
 refusal exists (an AT&T landline port) and is the exception.

- **The recurring real-world magicJack complaint is DTMF, not audio quality.** Voice quality reads as
 fine (WhichVoIP: no worse than a typical phone), but pressing digits into an automated menu -
 "press 1 for English", a doctor's office, a bank IVR - is a multi-year, router-dependent complaint
 across magicJack forums. For any VoIP home line, DTMF-in-IVR is the test to run inside the money-back
 window, not audio quality. Generalise this: **the failure mode of cheap VoIP is not the sound of the
 voice, it is the digits not arriving.**

- **VoIP providers differ enormously in port-out friction, and it is worth pricing before choosing.**
 Callcentric: **$5 Number Unlock Fee** on their Dirt Cheap DIDs. Ooma: no published port-out
 instructions at all (only port-in), which is its own signal. VoIP.ms: port-in free, port-out usually
 clean. magicJack: documented by third parties as above. When a decision is "cheap line for a
 non-technical person", port-out friction belongs in the comparison table next to the price.

**Research-hygiene note:** the decisive evidence came from *third-party* carriers' porting guides, not
from magicJack's own documentation - vendor help pages describe porting IN, never OUT. When a question
is "can I leave", read the destination's paperwork, not the incumbent's.
