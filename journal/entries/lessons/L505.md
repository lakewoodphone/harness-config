**L505 · A short health-probe timeout is a kill decision; require two patient probes before taking a process away**

A health probe's timeout is a KILL DECISION, not a log line. `dshw.ps1` decided "the engine is
dead" with a 1500 ms connect and a 2500 ms read, and then acted on it destructively.

The numbers: the same probe against a HEALTHY engine on this machine returns in 49-431 ms
(measured 2026-09-14 18:37, probe isolation test). But the budget that decides life or death was
2500 ms, and this box runs 10 windows / ~97 Edge processes / 379 processes at 84% CPU with
24.1 GB committed of 31.6 GB. A busy Node event loop behind a loopback GET can exceed that, and
when it did, `Ensure-Engine` reclaimed (KILLED) the port holder on the strength of ONE probe.

Worse, the destructive path was wired to the wrong command: `Ensure-Engine` was called only by
`dshw new` and `dshw restore` -- commands whose entire job is to OPEN A WINDOW. So "open another
window" was the machine's only engine-recovery path, and it fired against a LIVE engine.

The rule from here: a probe that can cost a process must (a) be patient, and (b) require two
independent failures, five seconds apart, before anything is taken away. See `Test-EngineWedged`.

Evidence: `dshw.ps1` `Test-EngineAlive` (was `Wait(1500)` / `ReadTimeout 2500`); the file's own
comments already record the same wound twice -- "restarted 3099 as pid 32044" at 15:05 on
2026-09-14, and 2026-09-13 -- which is how a fixed bug gets re-fixed instead of never happening.
