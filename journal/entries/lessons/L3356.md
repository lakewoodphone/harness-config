**L3356 · The duplicate comms row is more optimistic than the real one: the copy says DELIVERED where the true message says SENT**

**Recorded 2026-10-08 (ZABZ-YOGA), extending L3352 from noise to a wrong answer. Found on the
Heichal Hatorah order while checking my own claim.**

I wrote a note saying the owner had sent the receipts twice, at 19:53Z and again at 20:09Z. It was
one send. Production held two rows for it:

| communications.id | at | orderId | twilioSid | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 139780 | 2026-10-07 19:53:53Z | 2789 | `dialpad:sms:5335420786483200` | **SENT** |
| 139786 | 2026-10-07 20:09:13Z | *(null)* | *(null)* | **DELIVERED** |

Byte-identical content (md5 `131b7ac8d467eaf8e2a60525a8b290c2`). The second is the thread-ingest
writer's copy (L3352). Every genuine row on that customer carries a provider id; every row without one
is a copy of a row that does.

**The dangerous part is not that the copy exists — it is that the copy disagrees.** The duplicate says
`DELIVERED`, the real message says `SENT`. So the duplicate lineage is not merely redundant: **it is
more optimistic than the truth**, and any delivery claim read from it is unearned. That matters most
exactly where it bit here — a customer whose entire complaint was that he had received nothing, on an
order where the correct answer was "Dialpad accepted it and we cannot prove you got it".

**The rule to carry forward.** For any claim about whether a message reached a person, use the row that
carries a provider id (`twilioSid` / Dialpad message id). Rows with a null provider id are ingest
copies, their status field is not evidence of anything, and on this database they are in the majority —
`lpt ticket` folds 9 duplicates on this customer alone, 101 on klugman, 258 on bornstein. A session that
reads `status = 'DELIVERED'` without checking for a provider id will confidently assert deliveries that
never happened, which is the failure mode this whole system has been burned by before.

**And the correction discipline that caught it:** I wrote the note claiming two sends and checked the
supporting rows immediately afterwards, because the claim was about a customer and money. The check
took one query. Catching your own wrong sentence in the same session costs minutes; leaving it in
production costs a future reader's trust in the whole table.
