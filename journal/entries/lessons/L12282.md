**L12282 · A listing is not verified until you open its photos - and a stock render is not evidence of condition**

The owner caught something on a laptop listing in one line: "it has massive black dots on it". I had ranked
that listing FIRST on price and spec, twice, and never opened its photograph. The photo showed two large
black patches across the lid - sticker residue or eaten coating - on a machine destined for a customer.

THE RULE: a marketplace listing is not verified until its PHOTOS have been looked at, not just its price, spec
and seller feedback. Reading the description is not enough because sellers describe the model, not the unit.
On this same listing the describe-and-price reading produced "i7-1265U, 16GB, 512GB, 1920x1080, charger
included, 30-day seller-paid return, 299" - every fact correct, and the wrong buy.

THE SECOND HALF, and it is the part that makes photo-checking useless if you skip it: A MANUFACTURER STOCK
RENDER IS NOT EVIDENCE OF CONDITION. The next candidate up (333.83) had three images and all three were Dell
marketing renders - clean because they are a render, not because the unit is clean. So the three states are:
 * real photos of the actual unit, flaws visible -> you know what you are buying (this is the one to buy)
 * seller stock images, no unit photos -> condition UNKNOWN, not "fine"
 * the same stock render as every other seller -> condition UNKNOWN and the seller wrote nothing
Do not upgrade "no photos" to "looks good". It reads as neutral and it is not: it is a blind buy.

HOW TO DO IT, cheaply: eBay image URLs sit in the listing as i.ebayimg.com/images/g/<id>/s-l1600.webp, and
swapping s-l500 for s-l1600 gives the large version. firecrawl_scrape with formats ["images"] returns the full
image list for a listing in one call; the seller's own unit photos cluster on one id suffix and the marketing
templates are the s3.amazonaws.com/iwm-ebay/template/*.png ones. Download two or three at 1600 and look at
them - the lid edge-on, the palm rest, and the worst-looking close-up.

A listing whose seller photographs the actual flaws is worth MORE than a cheaper one with clean stock art,
because on a resale the flaw you can see is priced and the flaw you cannot see is the bench's problem.
