**L9278 · Read the motivation clause, not the topic: he asked for jiu-jitsu, he wants to be worked hard with his wife**

2026-10-19, immediately after L9150. I asked whether the first block should be escapes or attacks; he did not answer the question, he corrected the brief: "The primary motivation and the main thing I want to do is do very wild exercise with my wife… working hard, and the motivation for that, instead of just jumping up and down, is to do it through jiu-jitsu. I want the jiu-jitsu there. I'm learning jiu-jitsu as I go along but it's very exercise oriented."

So the objective function is WORK OUTPUT, and jiu-jitsu is the delivery vehicle — not the reverse. L9150 got the format right (10 min video, three 20-min blocks) and the objective wrong (skill acquisition). Two lessons:

1. When he states a goal, the MOTIVATION clause is the requirement and the topic is only the substrate. 'Exercise with my wife, through jiu-jitsu' means: maximise effort, time together, and sweat; treat technique as the thing that makes the effort positional rather than random. A perfect syllabus that leaves him unexhausted misses the point entirely.
2. An offers a binary ('escapes or attacks?') and he answers with a premise change means the binary was mine, not his. Do not re-ask it in different words — rebuild around the premise.

Bonus, and it is load-bearing for the design: the hardest metabolic work in grappling is escaping from underneath, which is also exactly his white-belt weakness. Exercise-first and skill-first point the same way for him — he should spend his rounds under her. Her blue belt gets the top game, which she needs anyway.
