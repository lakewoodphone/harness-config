# The mechanism behind the owner's 13.5-hour wait, and two rows that died of measurement

**Correction and detail for pain P4252**, measured 2026-10-15T15:0xZ.

## The mechanism: an error message counts as an answer

`owner_text_turn` id 109, sent **2026-10-15T01:35:21Z**, reason `owner_sms_reply`:

> *"Sorry - all providers are currently unavailable. I tried every option. Try again…"*

The owner replied **one second later** (01:35:22), *"Didn't we already go through that it's my wife's
professional liability insurance…"*. His inbound is recorded in `/root/.sms-inbox/inbox.db` with
**`state='answered'`** — because a reply did go out. Nothing has gone out to him since, in either
lineage (`inbox.db` newest outbound to him: 2026-09-30; `dialpad_sms_cache` newest outbound: 2026-10-12).

So `sms-responder.py` reports **"0 to decide"** on every five-minute run, correctly, on a worklist that
has been emptied by an apology. The state field cannot tell "I answered his question" from "I told him I
was broken", and the consequence is that he is the longest-waiting party on his own line.

**The generalisable defect: a failure notice must not close a work item.** Whatever marks an inbound
`answered` needs the reply's *outcome*, not its existence. A reply that says "I could not do this" has
to leave the row open or re-open it.

**And the outage was not systemic.** That text was sent **once**, ever (`COUNT(*) = 1` for
`body LIKE '%providers are currently unavailable%'`). By 15:08:31Z `wake-balance.py --json` reports
`balance 261.0, state ok`, `paused=false`, no `WAKE_PAUSED` file, and `GET /v1/models` answers **200 in
0.004 s**. I nearly wrote the opposite — that the AI line "cannot think" — from one row and a stale
balance row. Both were wrong.

## Two rows that died of measurement, within an hour of my handing them over

- **#449** (fund Telnyx) — the account reads balance 10597 cents, 6 active SIMs, 0 blocked. Resolved by a
 waze-fleet session at 14:54. #519 replaces it.
- **#514** (top up the prepaid AI balance) — balance 261.0, state ok. I resolved it myself at 15:08.

**The lesson is not "measure before filing" — I did measure, on 2026-10-13 and 2026-10-14, and both rows
were true then.** It is that **a queue row decays**, and a row whose premise is a live number has a shelf
life measured in hours, not days. A decision queue that holds such rows needs the premise re-read at
delivery time, or it hands the owner work that has already been done — which is the same "wrong-data"
failure as reporting a stale number, aimed at the person who has least time to check it.

**What to do instead:** when a row's question is "should I spend on X" and X's problem is a number that
moves, the row should carry the measurement timestamp of its premise, and anything older than a few hours
should be re-measured before it is put in front of him.
