**L3247 · For this household, check kashrus before proposing a dish: the US recipe corpus is built on meat+dairy, and stripping the meat strips the protein with it**

Asked for a supper idea on 2026-10-04, my first pass at a lasagna dip would have put Italian sausage
or ground beef in a cheese base - which is how every published version of that recipe is written,
and which is meat and dairy in one pan. This house keeps kosher. The constraint was sitting in this
own repository the whole time: ~/code/home/trips/ checks hashgacha before it will recommend a
restaurant, across eight files and 152 matches.

The rule: for any food recommendation touching this household, establish the kashrus posture BEFORE
proposing a dish, and filter the source material by it. The American recipe corpus in particular is
built on meat+dairy combinations - sausage in a cheese dip, bacon in a cream sauce, chicken in a
cream sauce - so a recipe surfaced by a search is more likely than not to be unusable as written,
and it will usually be unusable in the one place the recipe gets its protein.

The second-order effect is the one worth remembering: removing the meat removes the protein, so a
kashrus-legal version of these dishes is a LOWER-protein dish than the original unless the dairy
base is chosen deliberately. Cream cheese (6 g protein, 34 g fat per 100 g) is the trap; cottage
cheese (11-12 g protein, 2.3 g fat) and part-skim ricotta/mozzarella are the protein. Recorded here
because the same trap will appear in every cream-cheese-and-marinara dish, not just this one.
