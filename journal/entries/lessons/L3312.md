**L3312 · A focus ring cannot be measured on an unfocused element - and a 100% failure rate means suspect the instrument**

## 2026-10-05 · ZABZ-TECH · A focus ring cannot be measured on an element that does not have focus

WHAT I DID. Auditing accessibility, I wrote a check that walked every focusable element and, for each,
read `getComputedStyle(el).outlineStyle` and `boxShadow` and counted the ones where both were "none".
It reported **9 of 9** controls on /post and **31 of 31** on the signed-in form as having no focus
indicator, which reads as a serious accessibility failure.

WHY IT WAS WRONG. A focus ring exists only in the `:focus` / `:focus-visible` state. `getComputedStyle`
on an element that is NOT focused returns its resting style, where `outline: none` is the normal,
correct value. The check could therefore never pass for any element, on any page, in any app — it was
measuring its own mistake, not the product. I nearly filed a defect against working CSS.

THE CORRECT METHOD, now in `data/browser/mcp-output/focus-audit.js`. For each visible focusable
element: snapshot a tuple of the properties a ring can be drawn with (outline-style/width/colour/offset,
box-shadow, border colour+width, background, text-decoration, filter), call `el.focus()`, confirm
`document.activeElement === el`, snapshot again, and treat a changed tuple as a visible indicator. Also
walk up to three ancestors for an outline, because `:focus-within` rings are real. Result on the same
page: **0 of 29** without an indicator.

THE TRANSFERABLE RULE. When a check reports a uniform, total failure across every element of a class
(9/9, 31/31), suspect the instrument before the product. A real defect is usually uneven; a 100% failure
rate is the signature of a test that cannot pass. Before reporting, make the check FAIL ON PURPOSE: if
you cannot make it pass by any legitimate means, it is not measuring what you think.
