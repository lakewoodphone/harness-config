**L3664 · Clause 3's recovery target: 45,740.87 of movement between two authoritative readings that the ledger cannot explain**

2026-10-13. Clause 3's recovery half now has a number and a cause, taken from the tool's own output
rather than from a re-derivation.

THE CHAIN RULE, read rather than guessed. `cfo-balances.py` reports a disagreement where the chain from
one ANCHORED month meets the next:
 v2 - v1 - flow(d1..d2] != 0 "either a missing transaction or a bad balance"
and `flows.between(account_id, d1, d2)` is a SIGNED flow over the half-open window `(d1, d2]`, where
`signed = account_flow_cents(cents, direction, account_type)` - the sign depends on whether the account
is an asset or a liability. That is why my first ad-hoc attempt returned 9,976.05 where the tool implies
8,878.77: I summed raw `amount_cents` over an inclusive window with no sign convention. **Re-deriving a
tool's number without its rule produces a number that does not reconcile, which is worse than no number.**

WHY "BOTH ENDS AUTHORITATIVE" IS THE DECIDING SPLIT. A statement balance and a Plaid-reported balance
are both READINGS OF THE ACCOUNT, not derivations of it. When two trusted readings of one account do
not agree with the transactions recorded between them, the readings are not the suspect part - the FLOW
is. So of the 55,319.21, the 45,740.87 sitting between two authoritative readings is balance movement
the ledger's transactions cannot explain, and that is the recovery target rather than the list of 69.
Only 2 of the 69 involve an anchor the tool already warns about, so "the bad anchor" is not the general
answer here even though it is the tool's own first hypothesis.

THE CAVEAT THAT KEEPS THIS HONEST. Unexplained movement has TWO causes: rows MISSING from the ledger,
or rows PRESENT TWICE (a duplicate inflates the flow and produces the same signature in the opposite
direction). This measurement cannot tell them apart, and it does not try. The estate has a known
collision problem on personal-chase-9650 and lpt-chase-5578 - the two largest contributors here at
14,425.02 and 3,750.38 - so duplicates are a live hypothesis for at least part of it, and the per-case
work is to distinguish the two rather than to assume missing rows.

WHAT WOULD CLOSE IT. For each of the 66: pull the statement for the two months, list what the bank says
happened, and diff against the ledger's rows for that window. The estate already reads statements out of
Gmail through the application's own reader (`charge_msg.email_match`), which is how the 119 anchors
exist at all, so the inputs are reachable. That is a bounded, per-case task with a checkable end, not a
research question.
