**L3440 · Check the figure before arguing about the answer - and say a thing once**

On 2026-10-09 I argued with the owner three times about answers on his SNAP application. The thing that actually decided his case was a figure I had not checked.

WHAT HAPPENED
- He answered "Utilities: No". I told him to change it to Yes because the utility allowance was "the largest legitimate deduction". I was wrong twice: the accounts are in the landlord's name, so the household incurs no utility cost at all, AND because his countable income is already zero, the allowance could not have raised his allotment anyway - a deduction against zero income changes nothing. I had to retract it.
- He answered "Help received: No" after I pushed Yes. He was right: no money passes through the household, and a third-party payment made straight to a vendor is not income (7 CFR 273.9(c)(1)). He told me to stop, in these words: "will you stop harassing, i can just write we don't get help instead of the strange justifications."
- Meanwhile the figure that moved his case was the Chase checking balance: the form said 2,000, he actually had 72. Corrected to 72 plus 20 and 10 at Citizens, total 102, and the expedited test flipped from No to Yes - benefits in 7 days instead of 30.

THE RULES THIS PRODUCES
1. Before debating an answer, check the number the answer rests on. An answer that looks wrong is often a figure that is wrong.
2. Say it once. A point repeated after the owner has heard it is not diligence, it is noise, and it costs his attention - the resource this seat exists to protect.
3. For a household with zero countable income, deductions are irrelevant to the allotment: allotment = maximum minus 30 percent of net income, and net income cannot fall below zero. Check whether a deduction can move the result at all before recommending it.
4. State the consequence once, then follow the owner's decision and record it as his. His file, his signature, his call.
