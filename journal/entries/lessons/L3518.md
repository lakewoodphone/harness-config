**L3518 · lpt-label on the 29mm roll: characters per line IS the type size - 12 short lines print larger than 9 long ones**

MEASURED 2026-10-11 on the shop's Brother QL-820NWB, 29 mm roll, via `lpt-label` on the
authority (NOT the older `python -m lpt_ops.label_printer`, which is what the 2026-10-08
Heber case used - `lpt-label print` with stdin `-` is the current path, and passing a
FILE PATH positionally prints the path as the heading).

THE RULE: on this roll, characters per line is the type size. `lpt-label` sizes each
line independently to the tape width, so
 * the longest line on the tape comes out SMALLEST, and
 * a short line ("laptopscreen.com", 16 chars) comes out as a headline next to it.

Measured on this job:
 attempt 1 - heading `CALL TO ORDER A SCREEN` (22 chars) + body lines up to 28 chars
 -> the HEADING WAS TRUNCATED at the tape edge and `Part B140HAN04.0 / 412487`,
 the most important detail on the tape, printed smallest. 28.5 mm of tape.
 attempt 2 - heading `CALL TO ORDER` (13) + all 12 body lines at <= 22 chars
 -> every line rendered at the SAME large size, heading fits, 44.2 mm of tape.
So the readable label is the LONGER one: 12 short lines beat 9 long ones, and it used
less tape than the seven-line label the owner accepted on 2026-10-08 (48 mm).

DO THIS: keep the heading at ~15 characters, keep every body line under ~22 characters
on the 29 mm roll, and dry-run to a PNG and LOOK at it before spending tape
(`--dry-run --out /tmp/x.png`, then read the image). Never print the first render of a
label whose wording has not been looked at.
