**L1838 · The NVR records H.265 and pcm_alaw audio - transcode or the clips will not play**

Facts measured 2026-09-15 on the shop NVR, all of which break a naive capture pipeline:

1. **The main streams are H.265/HEVC at 2560x1440/30.** ffprobe via RTSP: `codec_name=hevc width=2560 height=1440 avg_frame_rate=30/1`. So `-c:v copy` — the free, lossless, obvious choice — produces MP4s most browsers refuse to play. That is a direct cause of "i don't see ... full playable clips". Anything destined for a browser must be transcoded to H.264; `-c:v libx264 -preset ultrafast -crf 26` at 1440p costs roughly a minute of Raspberry Pi 4 CPU per 20 s of footage.

2. **The NVR also records an audio track, `pcm_alaw`.** MP4 cannot hold it: `-c copy` fails with `Could not find tag for codec pcm_alaw in stream #1` and `Could not write header`. Transcode audio (`-c:a aac`) or drop it. Consequence worth knowing: the "NVR channels have no audio" comment in the old capture script was wrong — every channel has an audio track.

3. **DAV durations are unreliable but the MP4's is not.** ffprobe reports `duration=N/A` on the DAV; the MP4 muxer derives duration from packet timestamps and comes out correct (20.000 s request -> 20.520 s file). Gate on the output, never on the DAV.

4. **`-f mp4` is mandatory when writing to `<name>.part`.** The atomic-write pattern writes a `.part` file and ffmpeg infers the muxer from the extension, so it refuses with `Unable to choose an output format ... use a standard extension`. Cost an entire first deploy.

5. **DAV ptp/frame accounting can look absurd and still be correct.** A verified-correct 40.9 s clip reported `nb_frames=4085, r_frame_rate=100/1`. The clip was real-time (OSD at clip 2 s = 14:15:49, at clip 38 s = 14:16:25) — 36 s of scene across 36 s of clip. Do not "fix" a clip because ffprobe's frame rate looks wrong; check the burned-in clock instead.

Fetching an exact past window in one GET, which is what makes pre-event footage possible:

 GET https://<nvr>/cgi-bin/loadfile.cgi?action=startLoad&channel=<1-based>&startTime=YYYY-MM-DD%20HH:MM:SS&endTime=...&subtype=0

Returned 5.4 MB of DAV in 1.9 s for a 20 s window, 11 MB for 38 s. Digest auth only. Window times are the NVR's **local** time — read the NVR's own clock first (`global.cgi?action=getCurrentTime`) rather than the caller's, and wait until the whole window is in the past. Fallback: `rtsp://<u>:<p>@<nvr>:554/cam/playback?channel=<N>&subtype=0&starttime=YYYY_MM_DD_HH_MM_SS&endtime=...`.
