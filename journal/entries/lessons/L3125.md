**L3125 · An actor that acts without asking, and one number in two places: the same defect class, twice in one system**

ROUND 2, 2026-09-30, both measured on the authority and both fixed.

(1) AN ACTOR THAT ACTS WITHOUT ASKING. `wake-fanout.sh` started a dispatcher every five minutes through a
55-minute stretch in which nothing was claimable, and the dispatcher correctly exited having done nothing.
Nobody could see this, because the log line read like a failure and the reason was deleted by `rm -rf`. The
preserved evidence from the 15:40Z tick - now kept by this session's earlier patch - states it exactly:

 {"ok": true, "row": null, "blocked_by": "row-gates",
 "gated": {"not_before": 10, "night_quiet": 0, "source_hourly_cap": 4, "new_rows": 14}}

Fourteen rows waiting, ten gated by a date, four by a source cap. The fan-out now asks
`wake-nextclaim.py` first and says `nothing claimable now - starting nothing this tick`.

(2) ONE NUMBER IN TWO PLACES. `autonomy-status.sh` printed `source_cap: project-keepalive 3/3` while the
sources timer carried `WAKE_MAX_PER_SOURCE_PER_HOUR=12` in its crontab line: two processes, two answers to
one question, and a reader that contradicts the thing it reports on. The number now lives in wake.py's own
default (12), the cron override is REMOVED, and every reader and writer agrees:
`effective per-source cap: 12 | cooldown: 1800` from `wake.py stats --json`.

RULE TO CARRY: when you add a guard, check every actor that can act on it; when you add a reading, check
every process that can override the number it reports. An override buried in a crontab line is invisible to
the process that prints the cap, which is how a reader ends up confidently describing the wrong system.
