**L3824 · A default-deny DNS filter breaks features by refusing their asset host, silently**

**L · A default-deny DNS filter breaks features by refusing their asset host, and the feature then looks broken rather than filtered**

WHAT HAPPENED, measured 2026-10-14 on the Waze kiosk estate. Working out why voice input is off, the decisive
finding was not the restriction key at all. Apple's on-device dictation models are MobileAsset downloads, and
Apple's own signed catalogue for `com.apple.MobileAsset.EmbeddedSpeech`
(`https://mesu.apple.com/assets/com_apple_MobileAsset_EmbeddedSpeech/com_apple_MobileAsset_EmbeddedSpeech.xml`)
puts every model file's `__BaseURL` on `updates.cdn-apple.com`, `updates-http.cdn-apple.com` or
`appldnld.apple.com`. `mesu.apple.com` and `gdmf.apple.com` serve only the catalogue. The English (US) entry is a
**307 MB** download.

The DoH filter is default-deny (`handle_dns_query()` falls through to `default-blocked`), and none of those three
hosts is on its 58-name allowlist:

- Queried live: `updates.cdn-apple.com` → **NXDOMAIN**.
- The filter's own 24-hour log: **15 requests for `updates.cdn-apple.com` from the fleet, every one
 `default-blocked`** — plus 36 for `app-site-association.cdn-apple.com`.

So a device can discover the dictation model and is refused the model. Flipping the feature's restriction key would
have produced a microphone key that does nothing, and the cause would have been our filter, not Apple.

RULES:
1. **When a feature is configured on but does nothing, check whether its *asset* host is reachable before
 re-reading the feature's own switch.** Default-deny filters fail closed on assets exactly as they do on
 endpoints, and the failure is silent — no error reaches the app, the log line says only `default-blocked`.
2. **For a default-deny filter, the allowlist is the configuration.** Absence from the blocklist means nothing; a
 name either is on the list or does not resolve. Reasoning from a blocklist about a default-deny filter produces
 confident wrong answers (L3822's sibling mistake).
3. **The cheap fix is provisioning order, not a filter change.** Download the asset on an unfiltered network before
 locking the device down. Write it into the bench procedure; the alternative (allowlisting an asset CDN) is a
 deliberate, recordable widening.
4. The general shape: this is the third instance in one day of "a device asked for something legitimate and got
 silence" — the CNAME targets of allowed hosts (4,459 blocked and ~14,000 default-blocked requests in 24 h) are
 the same failure. Any allowlist built on resolved names alone has this property.
