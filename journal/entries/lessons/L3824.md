**L3673 · A PAID item goes to the shop's 24/6 outside dropoff/pickup and can be collected any hour - do NOT write a pickup message around the staffed hours (Sun 12-4, Tue/Thu 10:30-5:30)**

**Owner, 2026-10-12:** *"when something is paid for we can put it out in the 24/6 dropoff and pickup location even if we aren't there."*

## The rule

**A PAID item goes out to the shop's 24/6 dropoff and pickup location, and the customer can collect it at any hour, staffed or not.** So a pickup message must NOT be written around the staffed hours.

The staffed hours are documented and I found them — **Sunday 12:00-16:00, Tuesday & Thursday 10:30-17:30** (`docs/operations/lpt-pivot-2026-10-05-program.md:17`). I then wrote a pickup text offering *those* hours, which is wrong for a paid item and would have told a customer he could only come three half-days.

## Where the 24/6 access is actually documented

`docs/customer-operations/cases/data-recovery-347-534-6208-2026-09-01.md` — the Fishman case, 2026-09-01:

> Customer asked about a **lockbox / anytime dropoff**. Clarified: no lockbox - walk into the outside doors (open), leave it in a labeled bin with your name and number.

**So the mechanism is: outside doors that stay open, and a bin.** It is filed under *dropoff*, which is exactly why a search for *hours* missed it.

## The lesson about the search, not just the fact

**I searched for the shop's opening hours and found them, and that made me confident the question was answered.** The question was "when can he collect it", and collecting is not the same activity as being open. **A search that finds *an* answer is not a search that finds *the* answer** — the same shape as the earlier trap in this estate where an empty result was read as health. When the question is "how does X work here", search the *thing being asked about*, not the nearest word that returns hits.

Recorded in the Kogel case file too, so a pickup text for this job does not repeat it.
