**L3697 · A hedged sentence about money is a lean, not a decision - 'maybe forget it' cost a production write, a printed label and a correction**

An owner sentence about money containing a hedge is **not** a decision, and writing it into a field as one costs a production write, a printed label and a correction note.

Measured 2026-10-12 on WO26081312596 (Chezky Shwartz). The owner said *"owes 50 but took us a while so maybe forget it"*. I read that as "waive it": set `orders.total` to 0.00 through lpt-write and printed a label reading `NO CHARGE`. His next message was *"don't write no charge, right unsure if we will charge type of thing"* — he had decided nothing, and "maybe" was the whole signal. Undoing it took a second production write (total back to 50.00), a third label, and a correction note (3145 superseding 3143).

**The rule.** A hedge ("maybe", "I think", "probably", "we'll see") about money is a LEAN. The committed figure stays in the field; the lean goes in the note, attributed, with the words that carry it. A field is a decision; a note can hold an inclination. The counter decides the rest.

**The second half, which is the same mistake wearing different clothes:** an artefact a customer can read must not assert anything the record has not decided. Labels are customer-visible even when they are written for staff. I printed `BALANCE 50` and then `NO CHARGE` inside an hour, and either one would have told the customer something the shop had not settled. If a number is undecided, the honest label carries no number.

**Cheap way to avoid both:** before writing a figure or printing a claim, ask "did he decide, or did he lean?" — and if the answer is lean, the write is a note and the label stays silent.
