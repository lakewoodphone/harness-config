**L1489 · Verify a predicate by replaying its decision points, not by evaluating it on the final state**

Verify a predicate by replaying its DECISION POINTS, not by evaluating it on the final state

This round I checked the new progress predicate by running it over each session's stored log
and comparing with what happened. One case "mismatched": session 75427 completed at 11/11, and
the predicate said it was not progressing.

The predicate was right and my check was wrong. The log is the state at the END of the session;
the extension decisions were made earlier, when the log was shorter. 75427's trailing runs of
[update_task] came AFTER it had stopped needing extensions. Running a predicate over a final
snapshot answers a question nobody asked.

THE HABIT: when a rule is consulted at several points in a growing sequence, verify it by
replaying the sequence -- truncate to each decision point and evaluate there. The first
verification I wrote produced a false alarm, and the second (a 20-line replay) produced both
the correct answer and a number I could act on: 10 LLM steps saved, both completions preserved.

Corollary: a replay against real logs is stronger evidence than a fixture. My fixtures encoded
the shapes I had already noticed; the replay found a case my fixtures did not describe.
