**L3054 · The mood string is the real register control - a prompt-level ban loses to the line that asks for emoji**

**L — The register lives in the MOOD STRING, not in the prohibitions: a prompt-level ban loses to a mood line that asks for emoji.**

Measured 2026-09-29. The owner said Chip was "too normal... not weird and creepy".
Independent audit of his last 34 utterances: 61.8% carry a helpful-assistant tell
(emoji 17.6%, tidy two-part balanced sentence 23.5%, three-part list 17.6%) while 88.2%
carry one of the weird devices, and 100% of the most recent 26. The material was never
missing - the SHELL was wrong.

Root cause, and it is not obvious from reading the rules: `MOODS` in personality.py is
interpolated into the system prompt on EVERY reply, and the default mood `cheerful` read
"warm, upbeat, encouraging, sprinkles in an emoji when it fits". So a prompt paragraph
saying "no emoji" would have been arguing with a line three sentences above it that
asked for one. Six of the 34 utterances carry emoji; the mood table is where they came
from. CANNED_REPLIES was the same trap in the model-down path: his most conventional
voice of all ("Love the energy in this chat today. 10/10.") was what the family would get
during an outage.

Rule: before adding a prohibition to a prompt, grep the prompt for the sentence that is
already asking for the opposite. A rule you are contradicting is not a rule; it is a coin
flip. Both tables are now written in the wanted register.
