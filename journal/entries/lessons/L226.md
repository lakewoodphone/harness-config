**L226 · A price page can carry TWO series; a truncated extraction silently hands you the wrong one**

EVIDENCE: while building a bulk-iPhone price report (2026-09-14), I built a margin model on
Swappa "/en/prices/<model>" figures read from the carrier table's "Avg Price" column. An
adversarial verifier pass reported that Swappa ALSO publishes a "Storage Prices" table with
"Avg list price" AND "Avg sale price" (actual completed transactions). My readability
extraction had truncated that second table, so I had only ever seen the asking series -- and I
had labelled it "average sale price" in a report whose whole section 0 promises no number is
invented. I then CHALLENGED the finding, arguing the 136/149 figures were trend-chart monthly
points. I was WRONG: fetching raw HTML with start_index into the late page body showed a table
captioned "Storage Prices for iPhone 12, Updated September 14, 2026" with headers literally
"Avg list price | Avg sale price".

IMPACT: the asking series overstated the resale ceiling by 11-33% on every row. Correcting it
moved the report's headline margin from "+16/unit, iPhone 12 is the sweet spot" to "-63/unit,
no profitable wholesale channel exists". The recommendation inverted.

RULE:
(1) A price page can publish TWO different series under similar labels. A truncated extraction
 silently hands you the wrong one and no error surfaces. On a long page, fetch raw HTML in
 start_index slices until the whole body is seen, or grep the raw bytes for markers like
 "Avg sale", "list price", "Updated" -- do NOT trust a readability extraction's tail.
(2) When someone challenges a number you read, re-fetch the SOURCE BYTES before defending it.
 My defence was plausible and wrong; the raw HTML settled it in one call.
(3) Distinguish asking from achieved EVERY time. A benchmark labelled "average price" is
 asking; only "sale" / "sold" / "completed" is achieved.
(4) 13% marketplace fees are charged on SALE PRICE, not on cost. My own report's prose said
 "sale" while its table computed on cost -- a label bug that would mislead anyone re-running
 it.
(5) A single wholesale quote is not a cost basis. The same SKU appeared at two very different
 prices on one platform on one day; quote twice, and record the SPREAD across sellers, not
 just a point value.
