**L1994 · A demo published to real customers still looks like a demo - render it at phone size and look, and search before building**

# L-new — A demo that ships to real customers still looks like a demo, and I cannot see that from the code

The owner looked at the live page we published for a customer and said it *"looks a little bit
strange"*. He was right. Nothing was broken; it worked, it was tested, every link was correct — and it
looked wrong, because **it had been designed as a demonstration instance and was published unchanged
to real customers.**

What the customer actually saw: four cards of explanation — *"Choose a message"*, *"What it looks
like"* (which repeated message #1 verbatim), *"Step 2 — save our number"*, *"How it works"* — 1190px of
scrolling on a phone. Instructions about how to sign up, instead of a business. Plus retail copy on a
food business (*"I'd like to see your new stock"*) and a favicon 404.

**The rule: render the thing and look at it, at the size the customer uses it.** Every check I had run
was a *mechanical* check — the right number in the CONFIG, the right labels, HTTP 200, the vcf serving.
All of them passed on a page that a human would call strange. A correctness suite cannot see design.
`browser_resize` to a phone viewport, screenshot, and actually look at the image, before anything
reaches a customer.

**Second rule: a demo is not a template.** The page began as an instance published so the owner could
tap through before the real details existed. When the real details arrived, the fix was to fill in two
values and republish — which carried the demo's *shape* along with its content. If something is built
as a demo, decide explicitly what has to change before it becomes the real thing, and check that list.

**Third, asked for directly:** *"see what other people do [and] piggyback off them instead of trying to
make this on your own."* The research found that **Meta Business Manager → WhatsApp Manager → the
number → Settings → Message links already generates both the QR code and the pre-filled message link**
([360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/guides/driving-users-to-your-whatsapp-account)). We had
hand-built a QR generator and a bespoke page around a mechanism WhatsApp ships in its own dashboard.
The `wa.me` link we used is the standard and was correct; the bespoke wrapper was the part that looked
odd. **Before building a thing, spend the search that tells you whether it already exists.**

**Also, a small honest error inside the same fix:** the "save our number" card was toggled with the
`hidden` attribute, which did nothing at all — `.save{display:flex}` beats the browser's
`[hidden]{display:none}` — so it behaved as if it were always visible while the code claimed otherwise.
An attribute-based toggle on an element with a CSS display rule is not a toggle.

**Evidence:** the redesign and the verification (rendered at 390×844, read, hrefs checked, served page
re-checked after Pages rebuilt) — lpt-hub commit `fbec95deb`; live page
<https://lakewoodphone.github.io/yos-munchies/>.
