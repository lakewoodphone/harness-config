**L3655 · No payment row is not missing money: this shop takes cash and cheques**

2026-10-13. This corrects what I told the owner in W2752 and it is the more important half of that
finding.

WHAT W2752 SAID. "268 of 326 SUCCEEDED payments worth 28,142.70 carry no work order, so the shop has
the money recorded but cannot say which repair it paid it." Every measurement there is right and the
framing was too narrow in a way that made the shop look worse than it is.

WHAT I FOUND NEXT. Pulling the full case for a queued order (WO2605280002, total 175.00,
paymentStatus PENDING, zero payment rows) returned ONE note, written by the status change itself:
 "Status changed from CHECKED_IN to COMPLETED Note: paid $50 via card number"
So the record DOES say what was taken and how. It says it in a free-text note rather than a
structured field, which is why the payment table has nothing. Measured across the store: **375 orders
carry a note that states a payment amount and have no SUCCEEDED payment row.**

AND THEN THE CORRECTION THAT MATTERS. The same notes name the METHOD - cash, check, card. **A cash or
check job SHOULD have no row in `payment_transactions`**, because no card was involved. So the
absence of a payment row is not evidence that money went unrecorded; for a large part of this shop it
is the expected, correct state. I had been reading "no payment row" as "no money accounted for", and
that reading is wrong.

WHY I AM NOT PUBLISHING A NUMBER FOR THE REMAINDER. I tried to split the 375 by method and produced
two answers from the same data - 137 check / 95 cash / 83 unstated / 43 card under an exclusive rule,
and 171 card under an inclusive one - because a note like "paid 40 card and $190 cash" is genuinely
both, and my first pass quietly forced it into one bucket. The unstated bucket also summed to
129,335.79, which is impossible as payments and means the expression is matching non-payment figures
such as the "quoted $1,500 and money owed $1,500" on a USB-drive case. **A parse that returns a
different plausible number each time it is tightened is not measuring anything**, so there is no
honest figure to give for "card jobs with no card row" yet.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE AND USABLE:
 * The shop's payment record exists in two places with different guarantees - structured rows in
 `payment_transactions`, and free text in `customer_notes.content` written by the status change.
 Neither alone is trustworthy: the rows miss the notes, and the notes cannot be aggregated.
 * 268 terminal payments worth 28,142.70 are real, received, and carry no work order (W2752). That
 stands - and it is a LINKING failure, not a missing-money one.
 * 375 orders state an amount in a note with no payment row, and for the cash and cheque ones that
 is correct behaviour, not a defect.
 * 456 of 607 finished orders (75 per cent) have no `orders.total` at all, which is a third and
 separate gap: no quote, no payment row, and a note.

LESSON, and it is the third time in this session that tightening a query changed the answer.
**Before reporting an absence, check whether the absence is the expected state.** "No payment row"
means "no card payment row"; this shop also takes cash and cheques, so for a large share of its work
the correct record IS the absence. I reported a defect that was partly a business model, and I would
have sent the owner looking for money that was never missing.
