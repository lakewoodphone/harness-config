**L · Absence of a notification is not evidence that an action did not happen - and this session raised a high-severity money alarm on exactly that error.**

Measured 2026-09-20, ZABZ-YOGA. The payroll close-out had one item with a hard deadline: the employee's
dismissal had to be entered in Gusto before AutoPilot generated the Sep 14-20 week (run Tue, debit Wed
2026-09-23, ~$1,472.21, against $1,269.01 in the operating account). Gusto is unreadable by this seat, so
the only window was outside evidence. The reasoning written into the queue row was:

> *"THE EVIDENCE THAT THE DISMISSAL WAS NEVER ENTERED: (a) no Gusto termination, dismissal or final-pay
> email has EVER arrived at lakewoodphoneandtech@gmail.com (searched from:gusto.com with
> dismiss/termination/terminated/'final pay'/off-cycle/'last day'/severance across the whole mailbox - the
> only match is a 2026-05-27 marketing email); (b) no off-cycle payroll has ever debited the account; (c)
> the $2,208.32 is untouched 35 days after Gusto first priced it."*

It was raised as a **high-severity** row telling the owner to act within three days. The owner answered in
one line - *"he was already dismissed"* - and sent a screenshot of the Gusto app showing the dismissal
record, `Last working date Sep 14, 2026`, `Pay through date Sep 14, 2026`, and the Rehire link that only
appears on a dismissed employee.

**(a) was worth nothing.** Gusto does not email on a dismissal. (b) and (c) are true but they are evidence
about *the arrears*, not about the dismissal - they were folded into a claim they do not support. The
inference was built entirely on the fact that a notification which was never going to arrive had not
arrived.

**Why this is the most expensive error class in this system, and not merely a false alarm:** the seat's
worst recorded failure is reporting a crisis that did not exist because a stale copy of a database was
read and believed. That one was a stale *number*. This one is a stale *argument from silence* - same
shape, one level up: it produced an urgent instruction and spent the owner's attention on a problem that
did not exist. **He is time-poor by design; a false alarm costs him the same as a missed one.**

**The transferable rules:**

1. **Absence of evidence is only evidence when the evidence would certainly have existed.** Before
 treating "no notification" as a fact, ask whether the notification is guaranteed: does that vendor
 email about this event at all? Gusto emails about runs, blocks and cancellations - it does not email
 about dismissals. A searched mailbox proves what was *sent*, never what was *done*.
2. **Keep an unverifiable premise labelled as unverifiable.** The row said "unverified" in one clause and
 then argued for three paragraphs as if it were near-certain, with a severity that said the opposite.
 Severity must follow the *weakest* link in the chain, not the strongest.
3. **When a question has a cheap answer the owner can give in one line, ask it - don't build a case
 around its absence.** The whole alarm would have been avoided by asking "was he dismissed in Gusto?"
 as the question, rather than assembling outside evidence to *infer* the answer and then asking him to
 fix a problem.
4. **Say the error out loud and write it down.** The correction is recorded in the queue resolution, in
 the obligation's evidence field, in `LPT-BOOKS.md` and in the position document - not quietly patched.
 A seat that hides its wrong inferences cannot be trusted with a confident one.
