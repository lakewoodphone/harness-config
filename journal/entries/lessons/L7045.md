**L7045 · My thrash baseline was one day's number, not the loop's habit - the same loop spent 9.88 attempts per cluster the day before**

Measured 2026-10-16 by a new scheduled tool, cfo-thrash-report.py, on its first run. In round 24 I measured thrash from a single day's shift log and reported 413 attempts over 237 distinct clusters, 176 repeats, 0.74 repeats per distinct cluster - and installed a daily per-cluster attempt cap on that basis. The new tool, pointed at a full PRE-CAP day, reports 2026-10-15: 20 shifts, 653 attempts, 60 distinct clusters, 593 repeats, 9.883 repeats per distinct cluster, with five clusters attempted 18-20 times each - desc:autopay payment thank you 20x, desc:shenduqrwea 19x, moshe bachrach 19x, american express 18x, avi shapiro 18x. So clusters were being re-researched ONCE PER SHIFT, all day long, and my baseline understated the loop's habit by a factor of thirteen because the day I measured was dominated by first-time work. The lesson is not 'measure more' but 'measure the steady state, not the day you happen to look': a repeat RATE is a property of the loop and needs a period in which the loop was doing its normal job, and a day full of new clusters will always flatter it. The tool also refuses to let a half-empty split pass as a result: each half names its own turnover, and a half with no work in it is printed as 'NO WORK IN THIS HALF: a gap, not a null result'. Two of my own bugs were found by running it rather than reading it: attempted is a list per shift but an int inside rate(), and the first cron payload carried %%F where date needs %F, so the job asked for shifts on the day named '%F' and returned rc=1 - treated as an open item and fixed, then re-verified by running the literal crontab line under a cron-like environment until it returned rc=0 with a real report.
