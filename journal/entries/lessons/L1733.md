**L1733 · A helpdesk portal stored a 122-char stub of a 2,900-char message and still reported success - read the body's END, keep posts short, attach long content**

**L · 2026-09-16 · A helpdesk portal can accept a message, report success, reopen the ticket — and store a 122-character stub of it. Verify the END of the body in a fresh fetch, and keep portal messages short and plain.**

Corrects and extends **L1732** (same session, same portal): that entry said to verify a portal post by re-fetching the ticket
and looking for the attachment. Doing exactly that is what caught this — but it was caught only because the check read the
*whole* stored body, and the first attempt at verification read the message's opening, where the stub and the real message are
identical for their first 120 characters.

Measured on RattleByte ticket #188880 (osTicket), 2026-09-16:

| Message | Size sent | Size stored | Portal's report |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reply 1 — several paragraphs, list markers, quotation marks, `>` characters | 2,900 chars | **122 chars** (first sentence and a half) | *"Message Posted Successfully"*, ticket reopened |
| Reply 2 — one paragraph, ~430 chars | 431 chars | 431 chars | success |
| Reply 3 — one paragraph, plain ASCII, 1,125 chars | 1,125 chars | 1,125 chars (verified to its last line) | success |
| Attachment uploaded via `ajax.php/form/upload/ticket/attach` | 2,392-byte .txt | stored whole, listed in the thread | success |

There is **no error channel**: the post response, the ticket status change and the thread's own entry all look correct. The
portal's `a=print` view (a PDF) reproduced the same 122-character stub, so the truncation is in storage, not in the HTML
rendering — the client-side thread body and the printed ticket agree.

Rules that follow:
1. **Read the end of the stored body**, in a fresh `GET tickets.php?id=…`, before believing a portal post landed. Compare the
 last line, not the first.
2. **Keep portal messages short, single-paragraph and plain** (no `>` characters, no list markers, no multi-paragraph
 structure) — 1,125 characters stored fine; 2,900 did not.
3. **Carry long content as an attached file** rather than a long body: an uploaded `.txt` of the entire reply stored verbatim
 and gives the counterparty the detail without depending on the body path.
4. When a message is truncated, **say so in the repair** ("my previous message was cut off by the helpdesk") rather than
 silently reposting — the counterparty can see the stub, and a re-post that pretends the first one said it all reads as a
 non-answer.

Why it matters beyond this vendor: the same class of platform (osTicket, Zendesk, Freshdesk) is where vendor support, RMAs and
warranty claims live, and an outbound message that looks sent and is not is the same failure mode as a task closed on a
notification (L1512).
