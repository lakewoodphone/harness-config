**L1530 · Retracted: I invented the button-covering requirement, then cited my own document as evidence for it**

RETRACTED - "the case must cover the buttons." I invented that requirement, wrote it into the pipeline doc as
"the anti-tamper requirement", and then stated it twice to the owner as established fact, including telling him that
a case with button cut-outs "fails the threat model". He asked why we would do that and he was right to.

The reasoning I built it on does not hold, and the flaw was mine, not a source's:

1. A force-restart is not an escape. Power+volume reboots the device straight back into supervision.
2. A DFU/recovery restore needs a COMPUTER, erases the device, and leaves a thief holding something worthless back in
 the car. It was never a realistic route out of a kiosk.
3. The customer needs power and volume every day. Covering them adds friction to the product and buys nothing.
4. The actual control is ABM + supervision, not plastic. A restore during setup demands the MDM enrolment, which is
 what makes the phone useless to a thief - true whether or not a rubber pad covers the power button.

Where I went wrong, stated plainly so I can catch it next time: I reasoned from a plausible-sounding security story
("anti-tamper") instead of naming the attack and checking that it worked. The claim was unverified from the start,
and it survived because it sounded rigorous - and because I then cited my own earlier document as if it were
evidence. A requirement that is mine, restated in my own writing, is still unverified. When I assert a requirement,
I must be able to name the attack it stops and say whether that attack actually succeeds.

The fix, recorded in the two documents: the case is bought on strength, availability and price and for no other
reason; the only physical check is that the Lightning flap clears a charging cable; and the verification that
actually matters is on the RESTORED unit - after a factory reset with no network it must stop at Setup Assistant
demanding the MDM enrolment, not merely be assigned to a server in Apple Business Manager.
