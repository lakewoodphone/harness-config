**L12222 · Offsite for the music ledger: the path existed, my claim did not**

Correcting H12265 and D9217, which both said "nothing ships /opt/company/music offsite" and "there is no rclone and no restic on that box". The first half was wrong, and the second was beside the point.

WHAT I DID WRONG
I grepped crontab and /root/bin, found no offsite script, and concluded absence. I never listed the
systemd timers. Two of them do this work:
 authority-data-offsite.timer -> archives /opt/company/data to R2 bucket lpt-authority-backups,
 daily ~06:37 UTC, boto3, creds /root/.lpt-r2.env (600).
 Measured 2026-10-20T06:50Z: 4,051,091,403 bytes, 112,159 entries, rc=0.
 authority-offsite-backup.timer -> ships /root/authority.db itself, daily ~05:28 UTC.
"rclone/restic are absent" was true and irrelevant: the estate uploads with boto3 directly.

THE FIX, VERIFIED NOT ASSUMED
1. snapshot.sh now writes its verified sqlite copy to TWO places: /var/lib/authority-snapshots/music
 (local) and /opt/company/data/music (rides the archive). Both pruned at 14 days.
 Ran it: taste-20261021.db 204800 bytes, plays=50, staged 2026-10-21T03:23:22Z.
2. authority-data-offsite.sh now asserts 'music' among the files it requires to be present in the
 archive, so every daily run prints "present: music" or a warning. Published by atomic rename
 (timer may be mid-run) - bash -n clean, mv, new inode.
3. The token file is NOT staged. A credential inside a tar on R2 is a leaked credential; auth.py is
 the recovery path.

WHAT THE NEXT RUN PROVES
The archive at 2026-10-21T06:37Z is the first that can contain the ledger. Confirm with:
 grep "present: music" /var/log/authority-data-offsite.log
Until that line appears, the claim is "staged", not "shipped" - and I should say which.

LESSON
"Absence of evidence in one surface is not evidence of absence" applies to infrastructure exactly as
it does to data. crontab is not the scheduler on this estate; systemd timers run a large share of it.
Before ever writing "nothing does X" about a host, run `systemctl list-timers` and read the units that
matter. Also: --dry-run on the data archiver still builds the full 4 GB tar; I killed one at 60s and
left a 213 MB partial in /var/tmp, which I then had to clear by hand.
