**L2134 · L2147 - a long-running job launched from a shell inherits the service cgroup, so a service restart kills it**

Measured 2026-09-20 on the authority. I launched the repo's full test suite with
`nohup ... pytest tests/ ... &` over SSH, watched it reach 53%, and then saw it stop advancing with no
summary line. I was one step from recording "the test suite hangs at 53%" as a finding.

**It had not hung. It had been killed.** The log stopped at exactly `17:54:06`, which is the timestamp
`secretary-api` restarted, and `systemctl show secretary-api -p KillMode` is `control-group`. A process
started from an SSH shell as the same user lands in a cgroup that systemd is entitled to reap when the
service is restarted - so every restart of the API killed my suite mid-run.

Two separate things worth keeping:

1. **Do not run a long job inside a service's cgroup if anything might restart that service.** Use
 `systemd-run --unit=<name> --collect --property=Type=oneshot --working-directory=... <cmd>`, which
 puts the job in its own transient unit and survives service restarts. That is how the suite was
 relaunched, and it completed the second time.

2. **A stalled log is not evidence of a hang.** The discriminating check is the process state:
 `etimes=0` with `wchan=do_wait` meant the process I was looking at was a *wrapper waiting on a dead
 child*, not a worker stuck in a test. Reading `/proc/<pid>/stat` (utime/stime not advancing) and
 `wchan` took one command and would have said "killed", not "hung" - and I nearly wrote the wrong
 finding anyway, because "it stopped at 53% and never finished" is a very convincing story.

**The generalisable rule.** Before calling something hung, look for a killer: a restart, an OOM, a
cgroup reap, a supervisor. A process that vanished and a process that is stuck look identical from the
outside if you only look at the output.
