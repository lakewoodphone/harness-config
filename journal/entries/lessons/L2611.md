**L2611 · Score a recommendation by friction per use, not quality per fix**

2026-09-27, household bathroom drains. I recommended a TubShroom and then wrote out what it took to use: pull the catcher out, fit the stopper, bathe, pull the stopper, refit the catcher, drain. He stopped it in one line — *"hold on, i take baths, it can't be a 27 step process."*

The product was correct and the recommendation was wrong. He bathes daily; my fix added two hand motions to something he does every day. The alternative I had dismissed as cruder — 60 seconds with a Zip-It every 2-4 weeks — is strictly better, and costs nothing extra.

**Rule: when the thing being recommended sits in the path of a routine the owner performs daily, weigh actions-per-use above quality-per-fix.** A superior product that adds a daily chore is a worse product. Ask "how many times a day does he have to touch this" before "which one catches more hair."

**Second-order failure worth naming:** I had every fact needed to see this and still wrote the procedure out, because I was optimising the product instead of his day. The giveaway was that I wrote "step 4 is the one that matters" — the moment a recommendation needs a note about which step is the one that matters, it has too many steps.
