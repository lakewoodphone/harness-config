**L12276 · Ultrasonic: the dose is a ratio not a volume, the tank needs two-thirds, and a board bigger than the tank is a coverage problem not a flex problem**

Three corrections that came out of running the Duestch Latitude 5520 through the CREWORKS 10L on 2026-10-22, all now written into `lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/sops/CREWORKS-10L-ULTRASONIC-CLEANER-SOP.md` and committed (9f807a041).

1. THE DOSE IS A RATIO, NOT A VOLUME. The SOP said "~120mL Liquinox (roughly half cup, ~3% solution)". 120 mL is 3% of a 4 L fill - a phone-sized bath - not a fixed dose. Poured into the 2-gallon fill a laptop board needs, it is 1.6%, under-dosed; and anyone who scales the volume rather than the ratio gets a different bath every time. Alconox publishes 0.5-2% as the effective range for Liquinox, so the SOP's 3% was already above the manufacturer's own range. Working dose is 2% = 20 mL/L. 2 US gallons (7.6 L) takes 150 mL. A stronger mix does not clean corrosion better, it only loads the rinse, and the rinse is where a liquid-damage job fails.

2. THE TANK MUST STAY AT LEAST TWO-THIRDS FULL whenever the ultrasonics run, and no document said so. The transducers are bonded under the tank floor and rely on the water above them to carry heat away; running shallow is the commonest way this class of machine dies. For this machine: floor 300 x 235 mm = 0.0705 m2, so 14.2 mm of depth per litre; two-thirds = 7.0 L = 100 mm; 2 US gallons = 7.6 L = 107 mm = 71%.

3. "CLEAN IN HALVES IS NOT SAFE (BOARD FLEX)" WAS WRONG, and it was in a case file as guidance. Measured and reasoned through on this job: a 140 mm overhang of 1 mm FR4 carrying 50 g sags about a third of a millimetre, less than seating a RAM stick. The only real effect of partial immersion is a free-surface pressure release - acoustic pressure falls to zero at the liquid/air boundary, so cavitation tapers in roughly the last 10 mm below the waterline - and overlapping passes cover it, because whatever sat at the waterline on one pass is submerged on the next. What actually matters is coverage: every square mm under the surface at least once on both faces, four dips with a 55-60% overlap, component side down first so the populated face is not shadowed by the board from the transducers on the floor, never let it dry between dips, and rinse coverage matching clean coverage. Two reasons it is better than it sounds: cavitation is strongest near the transducers so every region gets one pass down low, and the overlap band gets 8 minutes instead of 4, inside the 5-8 this SOP already allows for heavy corrosion.

THE REASONING FAILURE IS THE ACTUAL LESSON. The owner asked twice whether half at a time was a real problem, and both times I produced a list of warnings rather than an analysis: I led with flex, then kept flex in the list after conceding it was secondary, and I asserted re-contamination from settling without checking that it needs still liquid - which an active 40 kHz bath is not. His words: "It almost seems like you're following manual warnings from some manual... you're not understanding or thinking about it." He was right. A documented warning is evidence about someone else's situation; before repeating it, check the mechanism actually applies to the one on the bench. When the answer is "the objection does not survive", say that first and say which objection died.

Working practice that came out of it: give one number, with the arithmetic shown and the source named, not a menu. He asked for solutions twice before the numbers arrived.
