**L3602 · Permission denied (publickey) from TWO unrelated source addresses with TCP reachable is a server-side key change, not a fail2ban ban - and check whether the 'the key is fine' reading predates the failure**

WHAT. `root@178.156.194.1: Permission denied (publickey)` — is that a rate-limit ban that will clear, or
has the key been removed? The two have completely different recovery actions and they look nearly
identical from one machine. Here is the discriminating test set, measured 2026-10-11.

THE SET, in order of strength. Do (1) first — it is one command from a second host and it settles the
"per-source" question by itself.

1. **Present the SAME key from a SECOND, unrelated source address.** Measured here: the office box
 `secratary-ts` holds the identical keypair (`ssh-keygen -lf ~/.ssh/id_ed25519_hetzner.pub` →
 `SHA256:91iMQc2AFg+sSA1oscDHF9pz7jClBMynfHtRL4rmAKg mesh-hetzner-2026-08-31`), and its attempt
 `ssh -i ~/.ssh/id_ed25519_hetzner root@178.156.194.1 hostname` returned the SAME
 `Permission denied (publickey)` — from the **office** WAN, while the laptop is on the **home** WAN
 (`tailscale status` shows the office nodes sharing `direct 71.104.140.242`). A ban on one source
 address cannot refuse a key presented from a second one that has made exactly one connection.
2. **Is TCP/22 actually reachable, and does the handshake complete?** `Test-NetConnection
 178.156.194.1 -Port 22 -InformationLevel Quiet` → `True`, and `ssh -vv` shows kex, then
 `SSH2_MSG_SERVICE_ACCEPT`, then `Authentications that can continue: publickey`, then the key
 offered and refused. fail2ban's `sshd` jail acts with a packet **DROP**: you get timeouts or
 `Connection closed`, never a completed handshake followed by a key rejection. `kex_exchange_
 identification: read: Unknown error` and `Connection closed` DO indicate a drop — so a mix of both
 symptoms means two problems, not one.
3. **Did the SAME client work inside the window the ban is claimed to cover?** Here the sibling
 workstream dated the block to 06:34Z; this session's commands from the same laptop with the same key
 succeeded at 06:34:35, 06:36:02 and 06:40:03Z. If the source were banned, those would have failed
 too.
4. **Is the cited proof of "the key is fine" PREDATING the failure?** `135-PARITY-AND-ROBUSTNESS.md`
 §4.6 recorded `ssh-keygen -l -f /root/.ssh/authorized_keys` → one key at ~06:20Z, and that reading is
 cited as proof of the present. It is evidence only of the past: it says what the file held BEFORE the
 failure, so it cannot discriminate between "still there" and "replaced". **Always check whether a
 quoted measurement was taken before or after the event it is offered to explain.**

THE RULE. A completed handshake plus a refused key from two unrelated networks is a **userauth-layer
rejection**: `authorized_keys` changed, an `sshd` config or `Match` changed, or `StrictModes` is refusing
on ownership/mode. None of those clear themselves. The correct response is to stop retrying (every
denied connection restarts a real ban window and creates a second, genuine problem) and to go
out-of-band. And **do not name the mechanism you could not read** — say the three candidates and put the
read on the first successful reconnect.
