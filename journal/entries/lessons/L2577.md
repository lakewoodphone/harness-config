### Reading paper off a phone photo: never trust the first pass, and re-read at 2x or more

Measured this session (2026-09-25, desk-paper intake, `secratary`). The harness
hands every uploaded image to the model as a ~692x923 thumbnail. On that thumbnail
a phone number or an account digit is *plausible* rather than legible, and the
plausible reading is the one that gets written down.

Three wrong readings from thumbnails in one afternoon:

| Document | Thumbnail said | Truth (3x read) | Consequence if kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rider insurance ID card (item 10) | plate `111T1Z6` | **the card has no plate field at all** | a plate number in the record that exists nowhere |
| Tolls by Mail invoice (item 15) | account `773867772` | **773667772** | paying against the wrong account, or a failed payment on a bill already 8 weeks late |
| NJ Taxpayer Portal passcode (item 19) | `2M7HE5` | **2MZHE5** | an administrator credential that does not work |

The rule that follows: **for any document whose value will be acted on, crop the
region with PIL, upscale 2x–3.6x, and read the crop** — never the full frame. A
full-frame read is fine for "what is this document", never for "what does it say".

Cost of getting it right: two extra image reads, seconds. Cost of getting it
wrong: a wrong number inside a system that reports it with full confidence, which
is the failure mode this seat exists to avoid.

Corollary: **do not stop at a second reading either.** These three survived a first
pass that felt certain and were caught only because an independent reader went
over the same images and reported what it saw rather than agreeing.
