**L3773 · A sample read in waves is not the population: shop went from fifth to first between 495 and 935 sessions**

MEASURED 2026-10-13, against myself, in the same session.

The 18-corpus-reading fleet ran in two waves because of a cap of 10 background jobs. Shards are
ordered by (day, machine, session). I aggregated the first 9 shards when they landed and reported
the result as if it were the census:

 first 9 shards (495 sessions): company=79 research=79 build=74 shop=69 personal=58
 17 of 18 (935 sessions): shop=207 build=162 harness=118 personal=111 research=103

`shop` moved from fifth to first. The early shards were a biased slice of the date range, and I
reported a partial sample in the same register as a complete one. Nothing about the analysis was
wrong except the scope I attached to it.

Two rules fall out, and both are now in the persona:

 1. **A stratified sample read in waves is not the population.** If shards are partitioned by date
 or by machine, any prefix of them is a biased slice. Aggregate only when every shard is
 present, or label the number with the coverage -- "495 of 935 sessions" -- in the same sentence.
 2. **A rank order can invert on the last third.** It was not a small difference; the top domain
 changed identity. Rank statements are the most fragile thing to publish early.

WHAT TO DO INSTEAD: make the aggregator report `reports_present / reports_expected` and
`sessions_classified` in its FIRST lines, and paste those two numbers next to any count taken from
it. `aggregate_readers.py` does exactly this, and it also reports `missing` and
`suspiciously_short` so a thin report cannot pass as a complete one.
