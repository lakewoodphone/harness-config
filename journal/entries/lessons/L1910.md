**L1910 · Ask what failed, not what the customer felt - the two can point at opposite ends of your capability**

**A customer's description of a symptom names what he experiences, not what failed - and on some hardware classes the two are on opposite ends of our ability.**

Measured 2026-09-17, Levi's two Sony 8mm Video8/Hi8 players. He said "tape deck and playback issues", which reads as a mechanical fault. On 1988-1998 Sony 8mm/Hi8 the **signature** failure is leaking surface-mount electrolytics, and it presents as exactly that: "won't load the tape", no playback, dead drum. So the sentence a customer uses to describe the fault pointed at the one subsystem we had no experience of, while the actual likelihood sat in the subsystem we are strongest at - and the second one is the *worst-value* job of the two (4-10 h/unit, unguaranteeable).

RULE: before pricing any repair, establish the **failure class**, not the symptom class. For any hardware more than ~20 years old, ask "what is the signature failure of this model family, and does it present as what he described?" Two searches answered it here. I had already reached a recommendation before I asked, and the answer reversed it.

COROLLARY, and the more expensive half: **a real capability is not automatically a business.** The bench genuinely can do board-level work - hot air, microscope, trace repair already sold at $125 - and that made "yes" feel obvious. But the market rate for a full mechanical repair on these models is ~$150 (TapePlayers, parts+labor+shipping), so the 4-10 h cap job is bad work even when it succeeds. Check what the *market* pays for the job before deciding our capability makes it worth doing.
