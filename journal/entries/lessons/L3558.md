**L3558 · A wrong packing-plan line became three wrong conclusions: the Kogel transfer drive is OURS - hardware in front of you outranks a document about it**

**Corrects H3461, item 4(b), and the case file's money sections. Owner, 2026-10-09:** *"it's our 2tb, we offer to sell him a drive or he brings his own, that's how we always do it."*

## What I got wrong

The Kogel case file's packing plan said, of the 2026-08-12 mail-in shipment, **"we are NOT providing a transfer drive for this case — 300DDR will supply the recovered-data medium."** I read that as fact and derived three conclusions from it:

1. the transfer medium might still be **unbilled** ("do not assume 520 was the last money out");
2. the recovered set **arrives on the lab's medium**, so it is not ours;
3. therefore **300DDR's 2 TB drive may need returning**, and its property was in play.

**All three are wrong, and the truth was on the bench the entire time.** The drive is **ours** — a 2 TB Crucial X9, serial `2540EA947389`, volume label **`73459 - Transfer`**. That is precisely why the 520.01 invoice carried no medium charge: we supplied the medium. The 520.01 paid 2026-10-02 was the **last** money out to the lab.

## The lesson, which is bigger than this case

**One unverified line in a document propagated into three separate wrong conclusions, and each one read as evidence for the next.** The packing plan said no drive; the invoice had no medium line, which "confirmed" it; and the absence of a charge then became a live liability I told the owner to plan around. **A conclusion derived from a document is only as good as that document — and hardware in front of you outranks it.** The drive was plugged into this machine, wearing a label with the ticket number on it, for the whole session in which I reasoned about whose it was.

**The rule to keep:** when a claim is about *a physical thing in the room* — a drive, a device, a serial, a label — **go and look at it before deriving anything from a document about it.** Reading the volume label costs one command. Deriving a liability from a packing note cost a wrong paragraph in a customer record, a wrong line in the sync record, a wrong line in the recovery index, and a wrong recommendation to the owner.

**And the thing the derivation destroyed:** the case file's own `transferDrive` field said the medium was the lab's, which contradicted a *different* line in the same file (a 2 TB Crucial listed among our hardware). **Two fields in one record disagreeing is the cheapest possible signal that one of them was never checked**, and nothing in the session noticed it.

## Also settled by this: the standard delivery model, which I had turned into an owner question
**The customer buys a drive from us, or brings his own.** That is the shop's standing flow and it was never a decision to escalate. I had queued it as one because I believed the media belonged to the lab. **The corpus did carry the model all along** — Golembick 2026-10-07 (our USB drive 10, or walk-in with her own), Newman 2026-09-15 (no charge, went back on their own drive). **Before escalating a question as the owner's, check whether the shop's own recorded pattern already answers it.**
