**L2376 · Yeast infection vs jock itch vs intertrigo in a skin fold: what differs, and which products cover which**

Asked 2026-09-24 ('what is a yeast infection and what does it feel like'), immediately after the Aygestin thread; the owner has a recurrent groin rash on file (docs/personal/health/groin-skin-condition.md) already running a miconazole 2% course since 2026-09-18.

- Candidiasis = overgrowth of Candida, which already lives on skin/gut/vagina. NHS states it is NOT an STI (sex can trigger it). Triggers: damaged/irritated skin, antibiotics, poorly controlled diabetes, immunosuppression, HRT, pregnancy, hormonal change.
- Vaginal thrush: white discharge 'often like cottage cheese, which does not usually smell', vulvar/vaginal itching and irritation, stinging with sex or urination. THE smell is the discriminator: fishy = bacterial vaginosis; frothy yellow-green + odour = trichomonas - neither responds to an antifungal.
- Skin folds (groin, armpit, between fingers): red, itchy or PAINFUL rash scaling over with white/yellow discharge. In the groin, candida burns more than it itches, is glazed/macerated in the fold, and shows SATELLITE spots beyond the main red area, and it often involves the scrotum; tinea cruris itches, has a sharp raised scaly advancing edge with central clearing, and usually spares the scrotum.
- Product coverage, which is the actionable part: miconazole 2% covers Candida + dermatophytes + Malassezia (so a jock-itch powder treats yeast too, which is why the owner's unknown diagnosis has never mattered); tolnaftate 1% does NOT cover yeast; Calmoseptine has no antifungal at all - barrier-only on a fungal/yeast rash relieves symptoms and it returns.
- Clinician before self-treating: first episode, >4 episodes/12 months, pregnancy/breastfeeding, immunosuppression, treatment failure. Antifungal creams damage condoms/diaphragms. Never put skin-fold antifungal powder inside the vagina.
- Cross-file interaction: azoles (miconazole, clotrimazole, fluconazole) are CYP3A4 inhibitors and the norethisterone SmPC lists azoles among substances that INCREASE progestin plasma levels - relevant because the wife takes 10 mg/day norethindrone acetate (docs/wedding/aygestin-duration-safety.md). Not a contraindication; something the pharmacist should know.
