**L4129 · CORRECTS L4128 — iOS shows the current carrier while Network Selection is Automatic; 'AT&T' was never a pin, and the fix is a Telnyx blocklist**

**L — CORRECTS L4128. iOS shows the current carrier in "Network Selection" while Automatic is on — and I built a diagnosis on reading it as a pin**

**What I got wrong, on 2026-10-14.** L4128 says the LPT devices were "pinned by hand" to AT&T and
that following Telnyx's advice to "scan networks and manually select one" is how the pin gets set.
**That is false.** The owner replied: *"It already was automatic and the phone itself is picking
AT&T"* — and *"if you tell me to always just switch it back to T-Mobile that's ridiculous... part of
our pitch for our device is the fact that it switches networks automatically."*

The UI reading was mine, from a photograph: the row read `AT&T`. **iOS displays the current
carrier's name in that row even while Automatic is on.** A carrier name there is not a pin, and
there is no way to tell a pin from the row at all.

**Why it mattered more than a wrong fact.** I turned it into an instruction — "tap this, change
that" — and into a runbook rule ("never scan networks"). Hand-fixing a device's network selection
*is* the failure mode for a product whose whole claim is that it chooses correctly. A wrong
diagnosis that costs a session is survivable; a wrong diagnosis that becomes a bench procedure
ships the fault to customers.

**The rule.** A claim that is going to become an *instruction to a person* or a *procedure in a
runbook* must rest on a measurement, not on an inference from a screenshot, a label, or a UI
convention I half-remember. I gave the UI reading as roughly 90% confident and used it anyway,
because the *network-side* evidence (the SIM camped on 310/410, `live_data_session: connected`,
internet-APN sessions created, 50 KB consumed) agreed with it. Two agreeing inferences are still
two inferences. The measurement I did not have, and could have got in one call, was **whether the
device's choice was automatic** — and the honest form of that statement was "the device is on AT&T
and AT&T carries no payload", which was true and sufficient.

**The real fault, and it is not device-side.** The devices select AT&T (`310410`) on their own. On
that attach the SIM registers, opens an LTE data session including on the internet APN, and moves
**no payload** — 0.010 / 0.050 / 0.058 MB across DRN 2005/2007/2008, against 94.96 MB on the SIM
camped on T-Mobile in the same fleet. AT&T is not one of the five IMSIs on the Telnyx profile
(Telnyx, Sparkle, BICs, T-Mobile, US Cellular,
<https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/iot-sim/imsi-selection>).

**The fix is account-level and I should have reached for it first: Telnyx wireless blocklists.**
`POST /v2/wireless_blocklists` with `type: plmn`, then
`POST /v2/sim_card_groups/{id}/actions/set_wireless_blocklist`. Blocking `310410` on the `lpt`
group makes every SIM — including ones bought later — refuse AT&T and re-select by itself.
**The device keeps choosing its own network, which is the entire product.**
(<https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/iot-sim/wireless-blocklists>)

**Generalised for this estate:** when a fleet misbehaves on one carrier, look for the *policy
object* on the platform that owns the SIM — group, blocklist, network preference, traffic policy —
before looking for a setting on the device. A device-side fix is a per-device cost forever, and on
a product that advertises automatic behaviour it is a contradiction.
