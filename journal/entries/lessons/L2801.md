**L2801 · Score every text-extraction rule against the labelled subset before trusting it**

When you have to extract structured values out of free text at scale, do not write rules and
apply them. Write the rules, then SCORE every one of them against the subset of the data where
the value is already recorded, and quote the per-rule agreement in the code.

Measured 2026-09-28 (`lpt-more-apps`, the Work Orders Device cell): 351 of 595 orders already
carry `orders.deviceInfo.brand`/`.model`. Scoring the extractor on those 351 turned a guess into
a number — `lg-exalt` 19/19, `kyocera-cadence` 22/22, `qin-brand` 24/26, `dell-brand` 4/4 — and
it also KILLED a rule that looked safe: a bare `4610` in the operator text agreed with the
recorded device only 2 of 3 times, because operators concatenate two jobs into one note
("data retrieval from exalt for $75 4610 sale for $215"). Real fix: make the bare-number rules
last-resort — they fire only when no named brand or model appears anywhere in the note, and are
suppressed entirely when two different bare model numbers appear. Same session, overall
agreement across all rules: 199/203 decisive = 98.0 %.

Two corollaries:
- A source can look attractive and be worthless. "The same customer's other orders" covered 20
 of the 173 missing rows — and the measured agreement between consecutive same-customer orders
 was 21.4 % (56 pairs, 12 shared a device). A customer owns several devices. Reject such a
 source with the number, not with a hunch.
- Before claiming a source has nothing, check the structural alternatives in the same database:
 here `selectedRepairPartIds` (0 non-empty), `repair_parts` snapshots (empty), `order_items`
 (0 rows), `device_services` (table empty), `communications` (118 linked, 8 naming a device).
 "There is no independent record" is a finding only after you have looked.
