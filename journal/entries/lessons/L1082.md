## Dialpad interleaves its own AI labels into transcripts — 38.6% of every line

Measured over all 5,710 transcripts on 2026-09-14: 148,419 lines, of which **57,221 (38.6%)** are
standalone `Speaker: label` lines from a closed 30-token vocabulary (`question`, `ner`,
`whole_call_summary`, `action_item_v2`, `ai_csat_reboot_ineligible`, `monologuing`, `currency`, …).
They fragment phrase searches and make every call result unreadable — a search for "screen" returned
`whole_call_summary_fragment` as the top hit.

The strip rule is *provable* rather than tuned: every payload-only line that is real speech is
capitalised and punctuated (`'Oh.'`, `'No.'`, `'Hi.'`), so matching only lowercase
`^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{2,40}$` payloads drops all 57,221 labels and, of those, exactly **2** payloads were
seen fewer than three times in the whole corpus — and both are labels too (`case_number`,
`postcode`). Deriving the vocabulary from the corpus and then testing the rule against the corpus is
what made it safe to strip at 38.6% coverage of all text.

Same shape of lesson as the artifact-marker strip the previous session did: **treat transcript text
as data that needs cleaning before it is indexed, and prove the cleaning rule against the corpus
instead of eyeballing examples.**
