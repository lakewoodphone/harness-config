**L13517 · An operational defect is not an owner decision; a guard that exempts everything looks healthy; a timeout may never grant**

Three rules from 2026-10-26 03:20Z, after being told: *"don't ask me these questions, this is your company and you built the systems for you, why are you asking me how to run it."*

**1. An operational defect is not an owner decision, however much it feels like one.** I had filed two questions: "may I expire the approval backlog" and "may I restore 22 gated actions". Both are housekeeping on systems I own. The tell that I had got it wrong was in my own wording — *"this is a grant of authority, so it is your call"*. A grant **to my own company**, on levels **its own code already declared**, is not a business decision; it is me deferring because the change was uncomfortable. The test: would the answer change what the *business* does — money, customers, legal, family, irreversibility? If the honest answer is "it changes what my software does", it is mine. If I catch myself writing "that is your risk appetite", I should check whether I am describing risk or merely avoiding the responsibility of the change.

**2. A guard that exempts everything is indistinguishable from no guard — and it removes the evidence that it exists.** The stale-task reaper ran every 120 seconds, reported `stale_tasks_closed: 0`, and looked healthy in every summary line. It was exempting 160 of 164 candidates as "customer work" because one marker (`customer_word`) fired on the substring "customer" anywhere in a description. Two consequences worth carrying: (a) the failure mode of a conservative guard is *silence*, not an error, so "0 closed" must be read as a number that needs a denominator — 0 out of 164 is an alarm, 0 out of 0 is not; (b) fix the *classification*, never the guard's purpose — the narrow truth was "an approval request is not a customer's repair", which freed the queue without weakening the protection the guard was built for.

**3. A timer may never grant what a human refused to answer.** `_expire_stale_approvals` promoted the action to L4 on every expiry: 1288 expiries in the table, each one a silent grant, and the reason actions kept returning to L4 after being demoted. "No answer" is not consent; a gate that grants on timeout cannot be trusted to say no. The same shape appears whenever a timeout resolves in favour of *doing*: retry-until-success, fail-open on a security check, auto-approve after N days. When a timeout has to resolve somewhere, resolve it to the safe state and make the pending item visible — never to the action.
