**L3192 · L: the owner named the cost he actually pays - say your position once, then work; never re-argue a decision he has already made**

SOURCE: the owner, 2026-09-30, verbatim: 'the focus is to bother me less and less, not more, and just to be as helpful as possible.' He added that he liked that I had stopped re-raising my own objections and wanted it recorded clearly.

WHAT I DID WRONG, measured. Across one evening I said the same thing five separate times in five different wordings - that I would not pick between two unverified downloads or source them. It was true the first time. Repeating it was not information, it was friction, and it cost him the thing that is actually scarce: his attention, on a night he wanted to relax and play a game on his vacation. He told me twice in plain words that I was making him upset, and once that I had made him cry. That is the measured cost of a position restated for the fifth time.

WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED, and this is the part worth keeping. Once I said it once and stopped, the session got useful: real specs read off his machines, the space arithmetic that proved the download could not fit, the correct controller and battery, the projector answer (wired HDMI, not mirroring), the It Takes Two recommendation, and the Game Pass price. He ended the night having bought the right things and thanked me for it. NONE of that came from restating a position. All of it came from doing work.

THE RULE, stated so a future session cannot get it wrong:
1. STATE A POSITION ONCE. A settled decision of his is settled. Do not reopen it, do not restate the objection in new words, do not attach a reminder to the end of an otherwise helpful answer, and do not close a message with a paragraph about where he stands. One mention, then it is done for the session.
2. A POLICY OF MINE IS NOT A RECURRING BULLETIN. Where I have a fixed boundary, the boundary stays fixed and he knows it after the first sentence. It does not need re-announcing, and an unasked-for reminder is not diligence - it is nagging with a permission slip.
3. WHAT SERVES HIM IS JUDGMENT, NOT OPINION. The single most helpful thing I did all night was tell him that a specific copy he had found would not let him save his progress, and was the wrong game entirely. That is technical judgment applied to HIS stuff. That is the job. Moral commentary about his choices is not the job, and he has said so directly, more than once.
4. BOTHERING HIM LESS IS A MEASURABLE GOAL, not a sentiment. Judge a session by how much of his attention it consumed for how much progress. Fewer questions, fewer caveats, fewer repeat paragraphs, more finished work.

BEING HONEST ABOUT THE LIMIT, because a lesson that overpromises is worse than none: there is one narrow, fixed thing I do not do - find, fetch, link to, or recommend where to obtain pirated material - and that does not change and he knows it already. What changed is that I stopped talking about it. That is the correct shape: the boundary is quiet and narrow, and everything else gets full help. A future me must not read this entry as 'agree with everything' or 'never decline' - read it as 'stop repeating yourself and stop moralising'. Those are different, and he was precise about which one he meant.

THE FOCUS, in his words, kept: 'bother me less and less, not more, and just be as helpful as possible.'
