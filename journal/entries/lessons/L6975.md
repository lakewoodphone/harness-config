**L6975 · Thrash measured properly: one unresolvable cluster was researched in SEVEN shifts in one day, because the fingerprint contains the whole vocabulary**

Measured 2026-10-16 on the CFO loop, traced shift by shift from ~/.cfo/shifts.jsonl. desc:statement appeared in the unclear list of seven separate shifts (000003Z, 002116Z, 010002Z, 011447Z, 011605Z, 022544Z, 024340Z), and across the day 78 clusters were attempted 2+ times, 15 were attempted 5+ times and 2 were attempted 7 times. Every attempt is a paid research call. The gate built for exactly this - park_gate, whose own docstring records an earlier 83.2 percent repeat rate - works as designed: it skips a parked cluster whose fingerprint has not moved. The gap is what the fingerprint CONTAINS. category_names_digest is the entity's entire category vocabulary, so ANY taxonomy change re-opens EVERY parked cluster for that entity, and today the taxonomy changed repeatedly as names were added and rules written. Remedy chosen: a daily attempt cap (CFO_PARK_MAX_ATTEMPTS_PER_DAY, default 3), counted per cluster per UTC day in the park row, independent of the fingerprint. Remedy rejected: narrowing the digest to only names that could plausibly match, because that risks never seeing a real unblock at all, while a cap only limits how often the same unresolvable question is re-asked in one day. And the claim is stated narrowly on purpose: the thrash is measured, the gap identified, the cap installed and a shift runs clean with it (rc=0, 0 errors, 38 skipped as parked-unchanged, 0 due for retry). NOT claimed: that thrash is fixed, because the counter begins with each cluster's first re-park after the patch, so the effect is measurable tomorrow. That distinction - measured against installed - is the one I got wrong three times today. Related and worth pairing: a fingerprint that includes a whole vocabulary is a fingerprint that moves when anything in that vocabulary moves, which makes the gate's precision depend on the granularity of what it digests.
