**L1756 · Bad website is a weak signal; support model and the vendor's own self-disclaimer are the strong ones (Ooma vs magicJack)**

**Evidence (2026-09-16):** the owner, shown the magicJackHOME order page, said *"I think the whole site
is bad vibes and looks old - is there another company that's better?"*

**The instinct is correct and it is not about web design.** The objective form of "bad vibes" for a
budget VoIP vendor is three things, and all three are checkable: (1) what its own terms say about
itself, (2) whether a human answers the phone, (3) whether the company will exist in five years.
magicJack: its subscriber agreement says *"magicJack is not a substitute for traditional landline
service"*; support is **chat and a knowledge base with no phone support**; terms allow termination with
no refund for "excessive use"; BBB not accredited; WhichVoIP 3.4. Ooma: NYSE: **OOMA**, **$306.6M**
trailing revenue, **$83.2M** in the quarter reported 2026-08-26 (up 25% y/y), serving consumers since
**2009**, live phone support.

**Cost of the reputation, measured:** Ooma Telo is **$79.99 on the Best Buy shelf** (or $99.99 direct),
Basic is **$0/mo + $6-9/mo mandatory taxes and fees**, port $39.99. Year one **~$192-228** vs
magicJack **~$70-75**; after, **~$72-108/yr** vs **~$43-50/yr**. Over three years that is only
**~$175-250 more, about $5-7/month** - cheap enough that "confidence in the company" is a reasonable
thing to buy, which is the owner's own framing.

**Two transferable rules:**

- **A vendor's own website quality is a weak signal; the support model and the self-disclaimer clauses
 are strong ones.** Ask "does a human answer, and does the company's own agreement say it is not a
 substitute for the thing I am replacing?" Both are answerable in ten minutes and both predicted the
 owner's reaction before he had it.
- **"Free plan" means taxes are the price.** Ooma Basic is $0/mo plus **$6-9/mo** in mandatory taxes and
 fees - so the honest running cost is ~2x the headline comparison, and any "Ooma is nearly free"
 summary is wrong by that amount.

**Also checked and rejected:** Voiply (~$89.50/yr) prices between the two but is a small reseller with no
longer track record and self-reported disconnections - a nicer site, not a stronger company. Verizon Fios
Digital Voice is the same VoIP product at $25-35/mo (~$300-420/yr) with identical failure modes: the
price ceiling, not a contender.
