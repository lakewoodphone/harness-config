**L1516 · Wiring a channel is not surfacing it: verify the signal at the reader, not at the writer**

**Wiring a channel is not surfacing it. Check the new signal at the reader, not at the writer.**

Measured 2026-09-15, in this order, on a reminder the owner had asked for:

1. The in-app reminder channel did not exist. `delivery_channel` defaulted to `dashboard` in the
 schema, the create API and chat scheduling; three writers, no reader; `process_due_reminders`
 skipped everything that was not sms/both. A due reminder was marked `delivered` and reached
 nobody while the row said `sent`. Fixed: in-app reminders enqueue into the owner-message queue.
2. I verified the fix at the writer: the poll returned `{checked 1, queued 1, sms_sent 0}`, and one
 `held` row existed with `reason=reminder:398`. **The job looked done.**
3. The badge still showed the same two six-day-old Google security alerts. The queue row was real
 and invisible, because `check_attention_debt` samples four held rows and orders them
 `reason, urgency, created_at DESC` -- urgency outranks everything, so two stale `critical`
 emails owned both headline slots for ever and nothing live could ever appear.

So the fix needed a second change (freshness first, using the same 48-hour window and observation
clock the severity rule already uses), and without it the reminder channel would have been
*technically wired and practically invisible* -- the same silent-drop disease one level down, with
a green test suite proving the part that worked.

Rule: a signal is surfaced only if it appears at the **reader**. Verify at the smallest unit
(the writer, the queue row, the query) and you prove the mechanism, not the outcome -- and for
anything owner-facing, the outcome is the only thing that counts. This is a sharper form of
L1515 ("a representation is not the value"): there, a proxy lied about the number; here, the
number was right and the last step in the pipeline dropped it.
