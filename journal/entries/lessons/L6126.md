**L6126 · 66 active-with-a-mark rows are correctly excluded; 76 duplicate-status rows are wrongly counted, 13,403.30**

2026-10-13. Two ledger inconsistencies measured, one tested and settled, the other confirmed as a real
flow error. Neither explains the reconciliation gaps on its own.

THE TWO INCONSISTENCIES. `load_transactions` decides what is a movement by the POINTER
(`if dupe: continue`) and never looks at `ingestion_status`. So the two fields disagreeing produces
opposite errors:
 A) ingestion_status='active' AND duplicate_of_transaction_id IS NOT NULL
 66 rows, ALL with an account, 7,741.85 -> excluded from a balance flow while marked active
 B) ingestion_status='duplicate' AND duplicate_of_transaction_id IS NULL
 100 rows, 76 with an account, 13,403.30 -> counted in a balance flow while marked duplicate

A TESTED, AND THE MARK IS RIGHT. `lpt-chase-5578`'s 2026-01 disagreement chains a 13-month window from
2024-12-28 to 2026-01-27 and carries 8 active-with-a-mark rows inside it. Putting them back changes the
flow from 3,473.01 to 4,990.65 and the disagreement from -1,330.23 to **-2,847.87 - worse**. So for that
account the pointer is correct, those rows really are duplicates, and the STALE field is the status, not
the mark. That is a settled question rather than a guess, and it means A is costing the flow nothing: the
tool excludes them for the right reason.

B CONFIRMED AS A REAL ERROR, and it is the one that costs money:
 13,403.30 across 76 rows, concentrated on
 personal-amex-22005 8,609.97 | personal-amex-94045 2,826.29 | personal-amazon-business 623.74 |
 personal-chase-9650 599.24 | lpt-amex-blue-business-* 415.24 | the rest small
Those are rows the ledger says are duplicates and the flow nonetheless counts, so the flow is inflated.

AND IT DOES NOT CLOSE THE RECONCILIATION, which I want stated plainly rather than left implied. Against
the eight sign-flips:
 personal-amex-94045 B value 2,826.29 vs deltas 6,427.66 and 1,832.32
 personal-chase-9650 B value 599.24 vs deltas 1,952.78, 1,310.31, 682.55
 lpt-chase-3929 B value 114.67 vs delta 414.08
 lpt-chase-7542 B value 0.00 vs deltas 435.38 and 416.01
For `lpt-chase-7542` set B is EMPTY, so it cannot explain either of its sign-flips at all. Set B is
therefore part of the answer - it inflates exactly the accounts that flip - and is not the whole of it.

WHAT SHOULD HAPPEN, and why I have not done it. Set B is a genuine defect with a clear signature: a row
whose STATUS says duplicate and whose POINTER says nothing. The remedy is to give those 76 rows their
pointer, or to make the tool honour the status - and both are writes to the ledger's duplicate graph,
which no sanctioned writer on this machine covers (P3009: the tool that made these marks is not present
here). **Editing either field means deciding what the two fields mean together, and I have now got
evidence for exactly one direction of that decision**: for set A the pointer wins. For set B the same
reading implies the status wins - which would REMOVE 13,403.30 from the flows - but one tested case and a
symmetry argument is not the same as 76 tested cases, and the difference is real money in a
reconciliation the owner is going to read.
