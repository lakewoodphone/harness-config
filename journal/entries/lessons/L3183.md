**L3183 · Check who can actually take the money before calling a fee owed - and size risk on the cheapest branch**

**Before calling a payment "owed", check who can actually take the money - and size the risk on the cheapest branch, not the most expensive one.**

Measured 2026-09-30. I recommended paying 300DDR on two tickets (74115 Fishman, 73459 Kogel) and justified
it with *"at 99% or better the fee is owed either way"*. Two things were wrong with that.

1. **The claim itself was unverified.** It came from our own paraphrase of the lab's terms page. The lab's
 own letter on 74115, written the same week, offers a decline branch verbatim: *"We charge a $100 labor
 fee if you don't want the data we recovered."* So the "no decline" rule I leaned on does not hold
 across their own tickets.
2. **The payment was never forced.** **300DDR has no card on file and no authority to debit us.** Every
 charge - deposit, open-cover fee, success fee, return shipping - arrives as a **JotForm link we choose
 to open**. Nothing is committed by approving in their portal; everything is committed by paying. **If
 the money cannot leave without your click, then "owed" is a claim about the relationship, not about the
 bank - and the click is a lever, not a formality.**

Then the arithmetic that decides it: paying first risks the **success fee** (500); showing the customer
the listing first risks the **decline fee** (100). A 400 swing, spent on a guess about whether the
customer will like what came back - when the listing itself already said 2,239 files and 89.17 GB did not
come back. And on the other ticket the customer had paid **zero**: the shop would have bought a
non-refundable asset to sell to a payer who had never been asked. The owner's test is the right one:
*"would this get a bookkeeper or cfo fired?"*

Rule for next time, in order: **(1) can they take the money without me? (2) what is the cheapest branch?
(3) has the payer agreed?** Only then decide. An uncollected deposit is not a deposit, and an approval is
not a payment.
