**L3260 · Never ship a heuristic justified by n=1: measure the population first, and delete a wrong feature rather than tuning it**

﻿MEASURED 2026-10-05. I built a cross-reference that annotated an obligation with
any closed owner task whose title looked similar. First real run produced a FALSE
CLAIM in front of the owner: the "Lakewood Phone & Tech - NJ annual report
filing" obligation was annotated "task 18754 (done) already covers this: SITE
DOWN: lakewoodphone.com", because both contain the company's own name.

I then tried a frequency filter, and the numbers killed it. Across all 27,567
task titles: `abletel` df 0.01%, `escape` 0.03%, `weinberg` 0.05%, but the FALSE
pair shares `lakewood` 0.68% and `phone` 5.12%. There is no threshold that drops
`phone` while keeping `abletel` -- any cut that works here is fitted to one
example.

So I REMOVED the feature rather than tuning it a third time. It was also not
load-bearing: every genuine already-asked case was caught by the dedup union and
by the queue CLI's own guard (12 suppressions on the first real run). Trading a
wrong sentence in front of the owner for no extra coverage is a bad trade.

Then I nearly did it again: I planned a filter to stop the sweep asking about a
15-day-old dated self-reminder task, and measured first -- exactly 1 of 7 open
owner tasks is one. n=1. No filter was added.

THE RULE: measure the population before adding a heuristic. If the justification
is a single instance, you are fitting, not engineering. Deleting a wrong feature
costs one paragraph; keeping it costs the owner's trust in every other line.
