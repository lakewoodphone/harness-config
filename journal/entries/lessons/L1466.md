**L1466 · The Google Voice home-phone trick is dead twice over, and magicJack's cheapest price is not the one on the front page**

**Evidence (2026-09-15, research for the owner's home phone, filed in
`personal-secretary-mvp/docs/network/home-phone-2026-09-15/`):**

- **Google Voice cannot be a phone line, and no current ATA can use a personal GV number.** GV has no
 SIP credentials — it can only ring a *linked number*. Obihai's official GV support ended in 2014, then
 OBiTALK itself was decommissioned **2024-11-01** and PDMS-SP on **2025-05-31**, so no OBi device can
 even be activated; the reverse-engineered bridges (Simonics GVSIP, Asterisk `chan_motif`) rode
 protocols Google closed. **What GV can still do is forward to a real number** (up to 6 linked
 numbers), which is how it stays useful: it becomes the household's *public* number that rings the
 bought line, with GV voicemail and transcription on top. Do not spend time re-litigating this.
- **magicJack's real price is the device page, not the banner.** The homepage advertises "$119 for a
 3-year plan", but the store lists the **device at $44.99 including the first 12 months** (reg. $59.99),
 with renewal $43/yr. Year one is cheaper than the "3-year plan" headline suggests, and the headline
 number alone would have made magicJack look *more* expensive than it is.
- **There is no free dial tone in a Verizon Fios router.** Its phone jacks need a paid Fios Digital
 Voice subscription, and that is itself VoIP with the same failure modes as every third-party box.
- **A subagent's report beat my own first answer on two facts** (the Fios dial-tone question and
 magicJack's device-inclusive pricing) in the same session. Parallel deep research on a buying
 decision is worth the wall-clock time even when a quick answer already exists — but only if the
 report is explicit about what it could not verify.

**Research-hygiene rule this produced:** instruct subagents to tag every price with how it was obtained
(`[direct]` page fetched, `[wayback]` dated archive snapshot, `[snippet]` search text only,
`[UNVERIFIED]`) and to **name the sites that blocked them** rather than silently guessing. Cloudflare
403s (callcentric.com, voip.ms, camelcamelcamel), an Amazon 503/captcha and a 404 pricing path are real
findings about the quality of a number, not obstacles to route around quietly.
