**L425 · 2026-09-14 · Do not judge a price model against a market the business does not buy in**

I put a pricing question to the owner — laptops are priced at 1.45x source cost, so a machine sells at
about — and laid out the sourcing analysis behind it: *"the cheapest NEW laptop worth owning is
.49, which is through us."* He did not answer the question. He corrected the premise:
***"that's why we mostly source refurbished for our customers."*** The analysis had priced **new retail**
while LPT mostly sells **refurbished**, so the yardstick was for a market the business is not in.
*Rules:* (a) before quantifying a business question, state **how the business actually buys and sells** and
check the data against that, not against the obvious public price; (b) when an owner answer does not match
any option offered, treat it as a **correction to the frame**, not as a choice — the options were wrong, and
re-offering them is how a question gets asked three times; (c) a correction does not automatically close the
row: record it, then re-run the numbers under the corrected premise before claiming the question is
settled. Here the same analysis had already found refurbished costs about the same as new for that class
(only worn machines were cheaper, and he had rejected those) — so the answer **re-framed** the question
rather than answering it, and saying so is the honest outcome. → HANDOFF.md 18:20 entry, queue #30.
