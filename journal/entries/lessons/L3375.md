**L3375 · The disaster-recovery work was itself a single point of failure: 30 commits of it existed only on one laptop, and the journal's only remote was the dark office**

GOAL ROUND 22. I went looking at whether a fresh survivor could obtain the CODE and the CREDENTIALS while the office is down, and the answer exposed something about my own work. GOOD NEWS FIRST: the code's remote is GitHub (lakewoodphone/personal-secretary-mvp, PRIVATE), so a fresh host can clone; and the offsite runtime tarball DOES include .env, web/.env.local, gmail/calendar tokens and browser sessions (scripts/server/backup-data.sh line 68 onward), so credentials are recoverable from the encrypted offsite copy. THE BAD NEWS: (1) 30 commits of THIS SESSION'S work - the whole survivor package, the preflight, the heartbeat hardening, 20 annexes - existed ONLY on this laptop, never pushed; (2) ~/code/harness-config, which is my memory (the journal), had exactly ONE remote - secretary-ts, the office box that has been dark for seven hours - with 29 unpushed commits including every entry from tonight. So the work that exists to make the company survive any single failure was itself one laptop failure away from gone, and my continuity record was one laptop failure away from gone WITH ITS ONLY MIRROR ON THE THING THAT WAS DOWN. FIXED: pushed the company branch (3ee3bfe45..731870458, fast-forward, no force - 30 commits now off this laptop and clonable by the survivor); discovered that lakewoodphone/harness-config ALREADY existed as a private repo, added it as an 'offsite' remote, pushed the journal branch, and then configured origin with TWO pushurls (the office AND the offsite) so a plain 'git push' reaches both without anyone remembering to. RULES: (a) the recovery machinery needs the same recovery as the thing it protects - check the durability of the DR work itself, not just its targets; (b) a remote inside your failure domain is not an offsite copy, and my journal's only remote was inside the failure domain that tonight is entirely defined by; (c) make the safe path the DEFAULT path (two pushurls) rather than a habit somebody has to remember.
