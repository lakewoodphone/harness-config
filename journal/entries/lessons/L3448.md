## L — A public hostname is not the machine you think it is: read the provenance, not the uptime

MEASURED 2026-10-09. I reported "secratary has been up 9.5 hours, so Wake-on-LAN
cannot apply" and told the owner not to bother. He drove to the office and found
the machine POWERED OFF. He was right; I was wrong, and the error was avoidable.

WHAT I ACTUALLY READ:
 curl https://api.lakewoodphoneandtech.com/health
 -> {"status":"healthy","uptime":38301,...}
 Resolve-DnsName api.lakewoodphoneandtech.com -> 2.28.33.58
And 2.28.33.58 is the HETZNER lpt-apps-01 box. That uptime was a VPS's.

THE MACHINE'S OWN HEALTH SHAPE IS DIFFERENT, and that is the tell I skipped:
 ssh secratary-ts "curl -s http://127.0.0.1:8002/health"
 -> {"ok":true,"time":...,"database":{"ok":true},...} <-- no "uptime" field at all

RULE: before reporting a service's health as a machine's health, resolve the
hostname and match the response shape to the machine. Two APIs on one estate with
different shapes is the cheap, five-second check. And an externally-served API
proves the NEXT hop is up, never the machine.

COST: a wasted trip to the office for the owner. This is pain P3 ("reading the
wrong data and believing it") in a new costume, and "a reading without its
provenance is not a reading" is the rule that was already on the wall.
