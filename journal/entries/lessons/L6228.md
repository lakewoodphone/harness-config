**L6228 · An ownership test is only as good as the media you test with: ask about a person's OWN recordings - a device's own folder, a personal name, a numbered set, a duration - never about songs, which prove nothing either way**

**An ownership test is only as good as the media you choose to test with — and "recordings" is not one thing.**

MEASURED 2026-10-14, the Fisher SD card (WO26101112743). I put ten names from a recovered card in front of
the owner and asked whether the customer would recognise them. He rejected the whole approach in one
line: *"some of those are songs, he would not remember songs."* He was right, and the error was mine.

WHY THE TEST WAS WORTHLESS AS WRITTEN
- Five of the ten I picked were plainly music (`גן עדן`, `ניגון המגיד`, `אודך ב צ שנקר`, `בצאת ישרא ב צ
 שנקר`, `שמחה`) and the entire `MUSIC/` folder is albums (`'85`, `chosonem`, `BRAVERMAN R' MOSHE`).
- **A card full of music says nothing about who owns it.** Anybody's card can hold a song; nobody
 remembers owning a song they did not make. So the question "do you recognise these files?" produces a
 no from the true owner just as readily as from a stranger — it cannot distinguish them.
- I picked names for looking exotic and unpronounceable to me, which is precisely the wrong signal: an
 unusual name is unusual to *me*, and tells me nothing about whether it is *personal* to him.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY RECOGNISABLE, AND I FOUND IT BY LOOKING IN A FOLDER NOBODY HAD OPENED
- `PRIVATE/SONY/REC_FILE/FOLDER01/` is the voice recorder's OWN recording folder, and it holds **exactly
 one file**: named for a person, three hours long (`ffprobe`: 181m42s). That is the lead — it had been
 on the card all along and in none of my lists, because I had listed the folders I expected data in.
- The other genuinely recognisable material is the shape of someone's own set: **ten files numbered 1 to
 10** (`ר פכ 1`…`ר פכ 10`), and two files numbered 1 and 2 for an occasion. Numbering, plus a device's
 own folder, plus a personal name, is what ownership evidence looks like.

THE RULE, GENERALISED
1. **Sort the media into "his own" and "acquired" before asking anything.** Recordings a device made for
 its owner, files carrying a personal name, numbered sequences, dated memos and voice notes: ask about
 those. Music, films, downloaded albums, other people's material: never ask about those, they prove
 nothing either way.
2. **Look inside the device's own folders first** — a recorder's `REC_FILE`, a phone's own voice-note
 folder, an app's private directory. That is where a person's own output lives, and it is the part most
 often skipped because the folder name looks like plumbing.
3. **A duration or a size is a recognition handle.** "A three-hour recording" is something a man knows
 about himself; a filename he may not have read in years is not.
4. **When the owner rejects a selection, treat the rejection as the finding.** His one sentence changed
 the test, found the card's best lead, and stopped a bad question being put to a customer — the sheet
 with songs on it was on the roll already, and it was binned.
5. **Do not let a label printer decide what evidence you can show.** Hebrew cannot go on this roll (it
 prints reversed, silently), so the labels carry transliterations for the person asking and the script
 goes on paper for the person recognising. Two artefacts, one question.
