**L3360 · Prove a degraded mode is inert by measuring what it DID, table by table - not by listing the flags you set**

GOAL ROUND 6. The survivor's claim is 'captures inbound, queues outbound, no autonomous sending or spending'. A config diff cannot support that claim (round 5 proved the flag/env default was overridden by persisted state). So I booted the configuration against a FIXTURE copy of the dev database (620 MB, quick_check ok, 182 tables; never the authority, which is on the disconnected office box; MOCK_OUTBOUND_COMMS=True as a safety belt because this laptop holds live credentials) and took row counts of 15 work tables before and after several minutes of runtime. RESULT: 14 of 15 unchanged - job_queue, tasks, tick_telemetry, MODEL_USAGE (no LLM spend), outbound_buffer, sms_log, email_drafts, owner_message_queue, delegations, reminders, action_approval_queue, events, memory_entries all flat - and the single write was activity_log +1, which on inspection is the app recording action='startup', detail='Secretary API started'. Meanwhile /health answered 200 with mode=survivor, ok=true, autopilot_enabled=false, database.ok=true. So: it served, and it did nothing. THE GENERAL RULE: for any reduced, standby, dry-run or degraded mode, the acceptance test is (a) boot it, (b) read its own health surface, (c) measure the SIDE EFFECTS it claims not to have, by counting rows/dollars/messages before and after. A list of flags you set is a description of your intent; a table of unchanged counters is evidence about the world. LIMITS STATED SO THEY ARE NOT OVERREAD: this does not prove inbound is captured or outbound is queued in practice (no real inbound arrived; that half is unit-tested), nor behaviour under load, nor with the live 203-table database.
