**L3793 · Fleet reading a remote corpus: copy it once, do not loop ssh**

Evidence: 2026-10-14, a 14-analyst fan-out over a 10 MB email corpus on secratary-cloud. Four of ten
children died with "the remote one-shot exited 255 and produced no final message" - an ssh transport
drop from the dispatcher to ZABZ-TECH, not a task failure. Their scratch files on the node prove they
had started working; only the report was lost.

Cause: the brief told each child to read its slice by looping `ssh secratary-cloud '...slice.py...'`.
A child reading 250 KB in repeated ssh calls holds a connection open for many minutes, and the link
drops. Nothing on either host was under pressure - the authority sat at load 0.89 with 11 GB free.

Fix: the whole corpus was 9.9 MB. The brief now says copy it ONCE (`scp -r`) and read locally. Short
ssh, no long-lived session, and a dropped connection costs nothing because the report is already on
local disk and the scp is simply retried.

RULE for any fleet reading a remote corpus: measure it first - if it is small enough to copy, it
usually is (this one was 10 MB), so copy it once and analyse locally. Do not build a read loop on a
long-lived ssh session. Reach for remote reads only when the corpus genuinely cannot be copied, and
then make every call one-shot and expect drops.
