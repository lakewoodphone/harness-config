**L3362 · Do not fire the thing that pages the family to test a guard - read the call chain, then test the exact path**

GOAL ROUND 8. The survivor's core promise is that it holds outbound instead of sending it, and the sharpest untested case was the Home Assistant security-alert webhook, which pages the owner PLUS HA_SECURITY_EXTRA_RECIPIENTS (mom and wife) and whose sender deliberately BYPASSES the owner's routine SMS kill switch ('an intrusion must page the owner even while routine owner SMS are switched off'). The obvious test was to boot the survivor and fire the webhook. I did not, and the reasoning is the lesson: if the survivor gate AND the MOCK_OUTBOUND_COMMS belt both failed, the blast radius is a false intrusion text to three people including family, and no argument about which flags were set makes that an acceptable way to learn a fact. INSTEAD: (1) read the call chain - main.py's webhook calls send_security_alert_mms(), which loops the roster calling send_message(..., bypass_owner_kill_switch=True), which IS the gated function, so the bypass flag is about the owner's kill switch and cannot exempt a standby; (2) proved it on that exact path with a unit test that asserts all three recipients are held, queued as pending rows, and that the call reports ok=False rather than claiming the family was paged. THREE GENERAL RULES FROM THIS: (a) choose the verification whose FAILURE MODE you can accept - a unit test that fails is a red line of text, a live webhook that fails is a text message to your mother; (b) a standby must gate at the TRANSPORT ENTRY POINT, never at a feature's own switch, because kill switches, approval flags and bypass flags are written for the primary's operational needs and say nothing about whether THIS host may transmit; (c) when you decline the obvious test, write down that you declined it and why - otherwise a future session reads the missing test as an oversight and fires it.
