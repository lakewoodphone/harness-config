**L1953 · SD reader audit: our bench could manufacture a dead-card verdict - the known-good-control gate we already require on HDD was never run on a card**

**Lesson (2026-09-17, the SD reader audit): our bench could manufacture a dead-card verdict, and nobody had
ever checked — the same known-good-control step we already require on the HDD side had never once been run
on a card.**

The owner asked the right question — *"we always use these readers and this computer, so is our process
wrong?"* — and the audit of all 11 SD/microSD dead-or-unfixable verdicts on record says: **yes, systematically.**

**The four findings that matter:**
1. **Exactly ONE case in the whole record documents a card failing in one reader and reading fine in
 another** (`sd-card-7326746333-2026-07-09`: *"First reader produced I/O errors — couldn't read the first
 sector. Second reader: first sector readable — confirmed FAT32"*). **A reader swap is what rescued that
 card** — so the bench demonstrably can produce a false dead verdict.
2. **No other case ever ran a known-good card through the reader that condemned a card.**
3. **The `14cd:1212` unit (serial `121220160204`) has never been recorded reading one sector of real data,
 ever** — and it is a party to three dead-card verdicts (Wilner 08-31, Tabak 09-09, Beyda 03-04). Its
 Wilner clearance was *"reader verified working"* **by argument**. On 2026-09-17 it reported a 128 GB
 card's capacity perfectly and failed **every** data read.
4. **Ellman** is a single-reader measurement **mislabelled in our own brief as a "Multi-reader test"** — so
 it reads as cross-reader corroboration and is not.

**And the discipline already existed in our own repo — on the HDD side only:** in the Fahan case a known-good
HGST 1 TB control drive was read through the same Sabrent dock before the patient drive was condemned. The
SD playbook now carries a row saying exactly this, and **it had never been executed on a card.** A rule that
lives in a document and is never enforced is not a control.

**Worse, we had been warned in our own research and did not act:** the 2026-02-27 capacity-collapse report
lists *"Card reader SDXC incompatibility (confounding factor) … 'Generic MassStorageClass' readers may lack
SDXC support; **SDXC → SDSC fallback is a documented SD protocol behavior**"*. That is precisely the symptom
shape the Goldstein card produced (a modern card presenting a legacy-SDSC geometry) — and seven months later
it was diagnosed as a card fault.

**The transferable rules:**
- **A verdict is only as good as the weakest component that produced it, and an unvalidated reader is a
 component nobody has characterised.** Before condemning any media: put a device you have **just watched
 read** through the identical path — same reader, same slot, same port, same sitting. On the HDD side we
 already do this; on the SD side we never had.
- **A verdict that names two readers is not two-reader evidence unless both readers have a positive control.**
 "Two independent readers + two ports, identical failure signature" was, in our own Tabak case, one unnamed
 reader plus one that has never read a sector.
- **Write the gate as a precondition, not as advice.** The old row said the right thing ("exonerate the
 reader with a known-good card, never by argument") and was ignored for months because it was a lesson
 among lessons instead of a step you cannot proceed without. It is now the first block of the playbook.
- **When a measurement is surprising, first check the instrument against something that varies.** The reason
 I could trust "one identical block at every LBA" was that the same code found **175 and 95 distinct
 blocks** on the bench's own SSDs and exactly **1** on a file that genuinely is one block. Validate the
 code against a known-varying input before you conclude anything about a patient device — the same rule as
 the control card, applied to software.

**Left open, deliberately, for the owner:** whether the Wilner, Tabak and Ellman customers need to hear
anything. Their physical evidence (Wilner's worn pads, Tabak's `Aborted Command` / 0 B on the first reader)
is independent of the reader question, so the verdicts are probably right — but "probably right" is not what
we told them, and that is a customer decision, not an engineering one.
