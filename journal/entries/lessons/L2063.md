**L2063 · pkill -f matches its own shell; use a pidfile or a DSH background job**

## What happened
`pkill -f "node src/server.js"` matched the bash process running the pkill itself, because
the pattern appears in that process's own command line. The shell died with SIGTERM and the
command produced no output at all - it looked like a silent no-op failure.

## Two fixes, both better than pkill
1. Use a pidfile: `echo $! > /tmp/x.pid`, then `kill "$(cat /tmp/x.pid)"`.
2. Or exclude self: `for p in $(pgrep -f 'server\.js' | grep -v $$); do kill "$p"; done`

Best of all on this host: start long-lived servers as a DSH background job (`run_in_background`),
which gives a job id, survives, and produces readable output via job_output. A nohup'd child
of a `bash -c` call dies with that call.

Signal to recognise: a bash tool call returning NO output and `[killed by signal: SIGTERM]`.
