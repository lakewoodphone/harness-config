**L1958 · A 2000 px derivative cannot answer a hardware question - crop the original native and the answer appears; ask for a re-shoot only after that**

**What happened.** Seven vision agents read 44 site frames at the harness's ~950 px preview resolution and returned, honestly, that they could not name a single fitting: bolt threads, nut sizes, grommets and printed labels were all "not resolvable". The framing was that the owner needed to re-shoot.

**It was my mistake, not the photograph's.** The originals are 3024x4032. A single agent was sent back with one instruction — crop `../original/*.HEIC` at native resolution with `pillow_heif` and read the crop — and the set went from "no fitting identifiable anywhere" to:

- **A positively bolted crossing**: a formed galvanised saddle plate with TWO U-bolts, TWO hex nuts and countable threads clamping two crossing pipes (`IMG_0896`), corroborated at three more frames.
- **The vendor identity of an enclosure** four agents had logged as an unresolvable tan panel: `SUKKAHOUTLET` / `(973) 778-5624`, read character for character off a plate.
- Pipe stencils: `ASTM A5[?]` + `PIPE`, and `SCH 40` fragments.

**The geometry of the lever**, worth keeping: the reader's long edge is ~950 px, so a crop of W native pixels reads at best 4152/W magnification. A 150-250 px crop therefore reaches **20-32x**; a 1530 px crop cannot exceed 2.7x no matter how much you upscale it. **Small crop + big zoom is the whole technique.** Nearest-neighbour, and state plainly that upscaling invents no detail.

**The rule.** Before accepting "not resolvable" from any image analysis — and before asking a human to re-shoot — crop the ORIGINAL at native resolution over the specific feature. Re-shooting costs the owner a trip; a crop costs one script. Reserve "needs a re-shoot" for a genuine pixel-count limit, and say how many pixels the feature occupies: a ~40 px feature under hot-dip spangle is a real limit, and no amount of interpolation fixes it.
