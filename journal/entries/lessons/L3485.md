**L3485 · A failed match is not a negative result: three duplicate labels because a working printer read as silent, and an observer validated on one rendering of a varying input**

**Measured 2026-10-09.** Three duplicate customer labels came out of the shop's Brother QL-820NWB because **a working printer was read as silent and the job was re-sent twice.** The tool reported `sent to 192.168.50.60 … 23.3mm of tape` every time — it was right every time. The verification was wrong.

## The bug, in one line

`Device&#32;Status READY` — the separator was a **numeric HTML entity**. The parser replaced tags and `&nbsp;` but not `&#32;`, so `Device\s*Status` never matched and the sample came back `nomatch`.

**The same page renders that separator three ways** — a real space, `&nbsp;`, `&#32;` — served from the same printer minutes apart with no configuration change. Fix: `[System.Net.WebUtility]::HtmlDecode($html)` before matching, never a hand-rolled entity replace. Full detail in `_labelprinter/journal/l4.md`.

## The two general rules, and neither is about labels

**1. A failed match is not a negative result.** `_labelprinter/journal/l1.md` already said a blank sample *"is NOT evidence of a fault and must not be read as 'not printing'"*. A `nomatch` is the same class of nothing. I read it as "the print did not happen" and re-sent. **When a check cannot answer, the correct output is `unknown` — and `unknown` must never trigger the action.** Rule form: **never act on a null result; only act on a positive.**

**2. Validating a check once does not validate it against a varying input.** I tested the parser against the live page immediately before printing and got **8/8 READY**, then trusted its silence for the next 87 samples. It was not validated — it was validated *on one rendering*. **A check is only as good as the set of inputs it has been shown, so either enumerate the forms the input takes or make the parse total.** This is the same shape as the estate's worst historical failure (a stale read believed because it was the only read) and it deserves the same weight.

## And: do not over-poll the instrument

Hitting the status page at ~5 requests/second for 50 s (250+ requests) stopped the server serving the status block at all. **Over-polling manufactures the very blank that rule (1) then misreads.** Sample about once a second, for the duration of the job only.

## The cost, stated plainly
Three labels, one customer, and the owner had to notice it. On a label that is tape; on anything with a real side effect — a payment, an outbound message, a destructive job — **the same `nomatch`-read-as-negative would have repeated the action, and repeated actions are how the estate has lost customer data.**
