**L7046 · One attempt is one model call and one web search, so attempts ARE the spend - and the cap binds only where the thrash is**

Measured 2026-10-16 on the CFO loop. The unit of spend is not an assumption: merchant_resolve.py line 92 sets RESEARCH_MAX_PER_RUN from MERCHANT_RESOLVE_RESEARCH_MAX with a DEFAULT OF 1, counted per resolver PROCESS, and the shift spawns one resolver process per cluster - which the resolver own comment confirms: without that line a shift resolving 40 clusters would make 40 model calls, which is exactly the runaway the budget exists for. So one attempt costs at most one model call plus one web search, and the attempt count IS the spend. Measured: 2026-10-15 had 653 attempts of which 523 were research-shaped, 7,062 seconds of shift wall-clock, about 10.8 seconds per attempt, and the repeats alone accounted for roughly 6,413 seconds, one hour forty-seven minutes of machine time. 2026-10-16 had 413 attempts, 204 research-shaped, 6,338 seconds, about 15.3 seconds each. Then the arithmetic that makes the daily cap judgeable rather than merely installed: on 2026-10-15 the observed 653 attempts over 60 distinct clusters against a cap of 3 allows at most 180, so the cap implies a 72.4 percent reduction when it bites; on 2026-10-16 the observed 413 over 237 distinct clusters against a ceiling of 711 implies 0.0 percent, because the ceiling sits ABOVE the work. So the cap binds only where the thrash is - it removes three quarters of the attempts on a day of sixty clusters re-researched all day long and constrains nothing on a day of useful first-time work. That is the right shape for a guard and worth stating as a property rather than discovering later. And a bug of mine inside this very edit: the units string was left unterminated and py_compile caught it, the third consecutive round in which running the thing found what reading it had not. The general rules: to put a price on a loop, find the guard that bounds its spend per unit of work and read its default - the default IS the policy; and an instrument that only bites on one shape of day should be described by the shapes it bites on, not by its presence.
