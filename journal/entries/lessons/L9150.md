**L9150 · How he wants to be taught: 10 minutes of video, three 20-minute fight blocks — never a lecture course**

2026-10-19, after he tried Gracie Combatives several times on my recommendation: he rejected it for PACING, not quality — "their videos are an hour and a half long and they sit there explaining stuff. I need it for exercise and for action… a 10 minute video at most, three parts, and each part you fight and try to do a move for 20 minutes."

RULE, and it generalises past jiujitsu: when he asks to learn a skill, do not hand him a course. Hand him (1) at most 10 minutes of watchable material split into 3 short pieces, (2) a 20-minute block of doing per piece with a win condition, (3) 60-70 minutes total of which 50+ is movement. Explanation goes at the point of failure, over the mat, not in a chair. Anything with a monologue front end and a 90-minute runtime is a reference document, not a session — labelling it 'the curriculum' wastes his evening and my credibility.

The August jujitsu doc recommended Gracie Combatives on exactly the grounds (structured, linear, spouse-friendly) that a person who wants to MOVE does not care about. Structure was the right axis and pacing was the missing one. Check runtime and talk-time of any recommended material before recommending it.
