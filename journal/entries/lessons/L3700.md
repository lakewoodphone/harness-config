**L3700 · Owner corrections 2026-10-12 (3rd pass): nine process failures, and the evidence source I never checked**

## 2026-10-12, ZABZ-TECH. The same session, after the two earlier lesson entries. These are PROCESS
failures - the first two entries covered drafting. This one covers how I read the record and how I
present it.

### 1. `ps_comms_waiting` CAN REPORT A CUSTOMER WHO WAS ALREADY ANSWERED
It listed (845) 825-6431 as waiting. **The shop had answered them at 21:10 on 2026-10-11** - the
full six-item price list, Dialpad message `6090214251208704`. The tool builds its verdict from the
**comms index**, and the index was last rebuilt at **16:37 UTC**, hours before that reply. An
outbound answer newer than the index is invisible, so the thread's last *indexed* message stays
inbound and the customer is reported as waiting.
**RULE: never take `ps_comms_waiting` as the verdict on one customer.** For any customer I am about
to act on, read `dialpad_sms_cache` for that number in BOTH directions and check the newest row's
`direction`. Cheap, and it is the only way to see an answer that post-dates the index.

### 2. I CONCLUDED FROM THE CORPUS INSTEAD OF OPENING THE CASE FILE
For Golembick I searched the comms corpus and the production database, found an order showing 660
unpaid and pending, and brought the owner an alarm about a customer asking for a receipt on an
unpaid job. **One command existed for this and I did not run it:**
`python tools/case-index/lptcase.py find "<name|phone|case id>"` (lpt-hub, ~40 ms warm).
It returned `status: closed`, `$660`, **`payment: paid`**, and the case file itself said
**"Owner, 2026-10-08, verbatim: 'she took it paid and left'"** with **703.79 collected, balance
0.00** and a **receipt already emailed**. His reply: *"did you pull up her case file and info
before bringing this to me or you are being lazy"*. He was right.
**RULE: for ANY customer, device, phone or ticket, `lptcase.py` comes FIRST. The database and the
comms corpus are for the parts the case file does not carry.** `lpt-hub/AGENTS.md` section 0 says
this in those words and I had read it.

### 3. THE PRODUCTION DATABASE AND THE CASE FILE DISAGREE, AND THE CASE FILE WINS
The work order said `paymentStatus = PENDING` and carried a 660 balance with no payment rows at
all; the case file recorded 703.79 collected, balance 0.00, card ending 7324, Stripe intent
`pi_3UOObZLnlgYj1gmT06aUwfD5`. **Cause recorded in the case: the 160 deposit was taken off-system
and never written to the order**, and two payment tables hold zero rows for this order.
**RULE: an order's `paymentStatus` is not the money truth. `lptcase.py show` is.** Never present an
order balance to the owner or a customer without checking the case file.

### 4. A HARDCODED `To` IS WHY THE CUSTOMER NEVER GOT HER RECEIPT
`scripts/send_golembick_receipt.py` hardcodes `TO = "golombeck1@gmail.com"`. The receipt was sent
there on 2026-10-08 14:26. **At 21:15 that same evening she texted asking for it at
`shanagolombeck1@gmail.com`** - a different mailbox - and nobody replied for four days.
**RULE: before telling the owner a document was "sent", check WHO it was addressed to against who
asked for it.** Same family as the outbound-comms rule: `SENT` describes what we did, not who
received it. A one-line `To` read-back would have caught this on 8 October.

### 5. VERIFY THE LINE CAN RECEIVE A TEXT BEFORE DRAFTING ONE
I drafted a text to 848-352-3618 and was one call away from sending it. Her number had answered an
outbound text on 2026-10-06 with **"This phone does not receive text messages"**, twice. She had
called five times across four days and left two voicemails; SMS was never a route to her.
**RULE: search the number's own thread for that auto-response before drafting.** A customer who says
their phone cannot receive texts has told us the channel, and the record already held it.

### 6. READ THE CURRENT STATE, NOT A STALE BACKGROUND JOB
I waited on a subagent for over an hour, then led my report with its findings - a discussion of
which auto-reply template was saved - when the owner had solved that himself hours earlier and was
mid-decision on a live customer. He had to ask twice: *"didn't i already change the auto reply and
you said it was good?"*
**RULE: a background job that outlives its relevance is noise. When it lands, ask "is this still a
live question" BEFORE putting it in front of him.** Never lead with a side quest while a customer is
unanswered.

### 7. A JOB THAT REPORTED NO RESULT IS NOT EVIDENCE
A subagent was SIGKILLed after 679 s having printed only its transport preamble. I then treated the
question it was meant to answer as still live, and worse, let it consume the owner''s window. When a
delegation dies, the honest statement is "it produced nothing" and the work gets redone or dropped -
never presented as a finding.

### 8. TIME CLAIMS NEED A CLOCK, TWICE OVER
`Get-Date` said `2026-10-12` was a **Monday**, and the shop is closed Mondays. Separately I told him
a customer had waited "16.8 hours" from a tool rather than computing it. Both were free to check.
**RULE: any hour, day or elapsed-time figure in a customer message or in a report to him is computed
in the session, from the row timestamps, immediately before writing it.**

### 9. THE ADDRESS IS CASE-DEPENDENT, NOT MANDATORY
Lesson L3685 said the address belongs in every first-contact text. **On 2026-10-12 he removed it for
Esther Levi: "it's a fast job leave out the drop off anytime and don't include the address she
already knows".** So: include the address and the 24/7 drop-off for a customer who does NOT know the
shop (a first-time remote caller), drop both for an established local customer, and for a fast
in-bench job. Ask, do not assume.
