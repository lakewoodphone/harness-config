**L3802 · Entries, files and distinct names are three numbers - reconcile a folder count against the total the filesystem prints, or you will write 23, then 29, then 30 to production for one unchanged folder**

**"Entries", "files" and "distinct names" are three different numbers, and choosing the wrong one produced a wrong count three times in one day.**

MEASURED 2026-10-14, the Fisher SD card (WO26101112743). The same folder's size was recorded as 23, then
29, then 30 — and only the last one is right. Nothing about the card changed between those readings.

WHY IT KEPT MOVING
- **23** was written on 2026-10-13 from a listing that had only shown the `.mp3` and `.wav` files. It was
 never a count of the folder; it was a count of what one tool happened to display.
- **29** is what a quick reconciliation produced: `31 audio-looking lines minus 1 duplicate = 30`, and
 then a subtraction error of one produced 29. I wrote 29 to production (note 4087) before the
 arithmetic was settled.
- **30** is the only figure that survives contact with a check. The card's own directory says
 **31 file entries**; one of them (`ר פכ 3[א].mp3`) is listed twice at the same size, so there are
 **30 distinct files**; and the 30 sizes sum to **509,488,212 bytes**, which is byte-for-byte the
 folder total `mdir` prints. Three independent readings agree.

THE RULE THAT WOULD HAVE PREVENTED ALL OF IT
1. **Never quote a directory's contents from a listing whose filter you cannot state.** "grep '\.mp3'"
 is not a count of the folder; it is a count of mp3s. If the claim is "the folder holds N", the
 filter has to include everything the folder holds, or the sentence must name the filter.
2. **Reconcile against a total the filesystem itself printed.** `mdir` gives a file count AND a byte
 total for the folder. Sum the sizes you are about to publish and compare. If the two do not match,
 you are missing entries or counting them twice - and you find that in ten seconds instead of after
 a production note.
3. **Say which of the three numbers you mean, every time:** entries on the disk, distinct files, or
 distinct names. A duplicate directory entry is ordinary on FAT (damaged, interrupted formats leave
 them) and it is not a second file.
4. **When a count goes to production, put the reconciliation in the note.** Note 4087 got the number
 wrong; note 4088 corrected it and said why, quoting the sum that proves it. The correction cost one
 write and no credibility *because* the arithmetic was shown. A bare "30, not 29" would not have.
