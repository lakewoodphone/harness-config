# L — A row in the owner-text ledger is not a message: suppressed is not delivered, and two rows can be one send

**Rule.** On the owner text line (`+17324447361` -> `+18483897895`, table `authority.db`
`sms_log`), a row is **not** a message. Three traps, all measured 2026-10-15, each of which produced
a confidently wrong sentence in an audit before it was caught:

| trap | the wrong reading | the measurement that corrects it |
|---|---|---|
| `status='suppressed'` | "he received 500 texts in 7 days" | 414 of the last 500 rows by id were **gate refusals he never saw**. 2,023 suppressed + 985 failed against 458 delivered/sent, all time. The largest single cluster in the "last 500" is **375 copies of one reminder**, 488 of 489 rows with **no Twilio sid**, one delivered. |
| two rows, one send | "every reply goes out twice, he is being double-texted" | both rows carry the **same `twilio_sid`** (`agent_id` differs: `sms_service.audit` and `orchestrator`, ~1.5 ms apart). One send, two log lines; 36 bodies have both rows. A row count overstates volume ~2x; `COUNT(DISTINCT twilio_sid)` does not. |
| `status` as delivery truth | "the digest was delivered" | the post-instruction digest is `status='queued'` with `updated_at = created_at`, while the relay says `status=sent` and Twilio returned `SM433eb01717ad795a7523621ad0f289b9`. **Transmission is not arrival**, and `reconcile-sent-status.py` structurally could not see this class of row. |

**Apply this.** Before writing any number about the owner channel: filter `status IN
('delivered','sent','undelivered') OR direction='inbound'`, count `DISTINCT twilio_sid` rather than
rows, and read the relay's `attempt` table for what actually left the building. An empty or huge
result here is almost always a **filter** talking, not the channel. Related: L3790 (judged vs posted
vs recorded), the suppressed-vs-empty distinction in the `fast-search` skill, and `wrong-data`,
the estate's number-one measured failure.

**Corollary for any "he asked us to stop" mechanism.** `owner_text_suppression` matched a lowercase
**substring** of `f"{reason} {body}"`, and its single row's `topic` was the owner's own complaint
sentence (`"I have no idea what it means, it's too much information, and it's weird"`) — extracted by
the stop-ack at `app/main.py:2926`. A whole English sentence can never appear in a later body, so the
row is structurally inert: the mechanism that records a standing instruction has never once had an
effect on this channel. If you build a suppression store, the key must be a **class the producer can
name**, not a quotation of the complaint.
