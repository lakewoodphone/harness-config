**L3646 · I diagnosed the reconciliation gap twice and was wrong twice; the third reading held**

CORRECTS `P3001` (2026-10-13), whose split of the 21 disagreements into "small timing deltas" and
"a zero balance reading poisoning a chain" was wrong on the second half. Recorded rather than
edited, because the wrong reasoning is the useful part.

WHAT I ASSERTED, and why it was wrong. `personal-amex-22005` reads 0 on 2026-08-31, and the
September chain appeared to inherit it, so I proposed a new suspect rule: "a zero balance reading
is a defect." I nearly shipped it. Then I looked at the readings around the zero instead of at the
disagreement:

 2026-08-28 186458 (3 readings)
 2026-08-30 0
 2026-08-31 0
 2026-09-01 0
 2026-09-02 0
 2026-09-04 2900 <- resumes, and CLIMBS (4494, 6092, ...)

That is a card being PAID OFF and then used again. **The zero is real.** A rule that discarded zero
readings would have thrown away a true balance on a real account, and the estate would have had a
reconciliation tool that hides rather than finds. The same check also killed a blanket rule: one
Amex account holds 110 zero readings against 2 non-zero - a dormant card whose true state IS zero.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE, after fixing the flag. I then made `cfo-balances.py` name the foundation of
each chain, because each comparison uses the REAL reading at both ends (`expected = v1 + flow(...)`,
where both `v1` and `v2` are anchored readings). So a bad anchor taints only its own step:

 disagreements: 21
 disagreements_from_warned_anchor: 3 <- personal-amex-22005 2026-08,
 personal-amex-94045 2026-08,
 personal-amex-hysa-3181 2026-08
 the other 18 are standalone.

All three tainted ones open on the 2026-07-01 `manual` batch, whose own month note reads "of which 7
are demonstrably defective; the value that survived is not by that fact proven good".

I ALSO SAID "8 of 21" IN THE ROUND BEFORE CHECKING. That came from counting warned MONTHS per
ACCOUNT (3 accounts, 8 disagreements between them) and calling it a disagreement count. Two
different units, one number each, and I used the wrong one for a sentence. The tool now emits the
count so the sentence cannot be assembled by hand again.

RULES.
1. **Look at the data before writing the rule.** My hypothesis survived exactly as long as it took
 to print the eight readings around the zero. A suspect rule is a claim about the world, and a
 wrong one silently deletes a true value - the failure mode `cfo-balances.py`'s own R1/R2/R3
 headers say they exist to prevent.
2. **Name the unit in the sentence.** "3 disagreements" and "3 accounts holding 8 disagreements"
 are not interchangeable, and a rounded claim like "8 of 21" reads as precision while being the
 wrong quantity.
3. **A disagreement is only as good as the reading it opens on.** The tool now says so in the note
 itself, so the next reader does not have to reconstruct the chain by hand to know whether they
 are looking for a missing row or at a number nobody can vouch for.
