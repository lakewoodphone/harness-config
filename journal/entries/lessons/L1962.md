**L1962 · PowerShell silently eats a dollar sign in a double-quoted argument, and the damage to a customer record was a split between two stores**

## The trap, measured 2026-09-17 on the Rosenberg case (`zabz-tech`)

Every argument I passed to `tools/customer/cli.py edit` as a **double-quoted
PowerShell string** had its `$` silently eaten. PowerShell interpolates inside
`"..."`, and `$23` is not a valid variable name, so `$23.88` vanished:

 --activity-title "... adaper, \$23.88" -> "... adapter, \.88"

No error. No warning. The value was written to a customer record that way.

## The damage was worse than a typo, because it split the record

The mangled `--next` value was rendered into the section's `**NEXT:**` line but
was **not** applied to the case header - so the case file kept its old next
action while the sync record got a new one. **Two stores, two answers, which is
exactly the drift the case standard exists to prevent.** It was caught by
printing the case header's line and the record's `operational.nextAction`
side by side and comparing them, and repaired the same session.

## The rule

- **A literal that contains `$`, backtick, or `"` goes in a SINGLE-quoted
 PowerShell string, or a single-quoted here-string (`@'...'@`).** Only the
 here-string body was safe; the two double-quoted arguments were the ones that
 broke.
- **Never trust an argument you did not see echoed back.** After any
 `cli.py edit` on a record that carries a price, read the resulting line.
- **Detection, not hope:** grep the file for the residue. A swallowed `$` leaves
 `\.` or `\$` behind:

 Select-String -Path <case>.md -Pattern '\\\.|\\\$'

- **Verification that actually proves it:** compare the case header's
 `**Next action:**` against the record's `operational.nextAction` and require
 them to be identical. `cli.py edit` has no such assertion, and
 `store.diff_record` cannot supply one (see P259 - it never compares anything).

## The better fix, applied here

`_scratch/report-rosenberg-part.py` now **reads `nextAction` out of the case
header** instead of hardcoding it, so the mirror cannot disagree with the source
of truth on that field no matter how the value was typed. Prefer deriving over
duplicating whenever a field exists in two stores.
