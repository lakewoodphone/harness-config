**L3887 · The counter page prices PRE-TAX and charges whatever is typed: typing the same number on both under-collects the tax**

Measured 2026-10-20 on the operational store (dsn d5oq83veii4rlb, via `ssh secratary-cloud`), from the
Stripe receipt the owner pasted for payment pi_3USfQsLnlgYj1gmT0CWNsv3o.

**The case.** WO2610200003 (order id 3144, canon sx740, customer 2980):

```
adjustmentAmount 79.97 (adjustmentNote: "Price set on the counter page")
orders.total 85.27 (tax-inclusive: 79.97 x 1.06625, pricing_config."taxRate" = 6.625)
payment_transactions 3408 SUCCEEDED 79.97 orderId 3144
paymentStatus CAPTURED
```

79.97 against a total of 85.27 is **5.30 short**, and the order reads `CAPTURED`, so nothing on the board
looks wrong. The only way it shows is total-minus-SUCCEEDED.

**The control, same day, same counter page.** Order 3138: `adjustmentAmount` null, `total` 159.94, two
SUCCEEDED rows 53.31 + 106.63 = **159.94 exactly**. So the charge field is normally handed the
tax-inclusive figure. 3144 is the case where the pre-tax figure was typed into it.

**What this is not.** It is not evidence of a mistake: a 5.30 concession is a thing a shop gives. What it
is evidence of is that **the two numbers are typed separately and nothing forces them to agree**:
`lpt-write total set` and `order create --total` both take PRE-TAX and store tax-inclusive
(`lpt-ops/docs/writes.md`, "`total set` takes a PRE-TAX amount"), while the card amount on the counter
page is whatever the operator types. Type the same number in both and the tax is never collected.

**So:** before calling a CAPTURED order paid, compare `orders.total` with the sum of its SUCCEEDED
`payment_transactions`. If they differ by 6.625%, the pre-tax figure was charged. Ask the owner whether
the difference was a break before writing anything down as an error - and never "fix" the total to match
the card, because the total is the figure the customer was told.

other way: the $75 cleaning was described as paid, and no payment row of any amount exists for that
customer - so the note, not the ledger, is the record of it.
