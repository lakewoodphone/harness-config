**L3470 · A Gmail message with no body is not broken — it can be six photographs, and the read API makes it look deleted**

**Rule.** When `GET /gmail/messages/<id>?account=<mailbox>` returns an **empty `body` and an empty `snippet`**, do not conclude the message is gone, malformed, or that you asked for it wrong. Pull the MIME tree — `users().messages().get(userId="me", id=<id>, format="full")` — and look for `parts` with an `attachmentId`. An attachment-only mail (iPhone photos sent with no text) has a `text/plain` and a `text/html` part that are each **one newline** and nothing else.

**Evidence (measured 2026-10-09, ZABZ-TECH).** Gmail message `1a1223182acc32e0` on `eliyahuzabrowsky@gmail.com`, from `moishe bachrach <moshbachrach@gmail.com>`, `Fri, 9 Oct 2026 15:43:37 -0400`. The app's `/gmail/messages/<id>` returned `body: ""` and `snippet: ""` with `sizeEstimate` **34,285,243**. The MIME tree was `multipart/mixed` → `multipart/alternative` (text/plain 2 bytes, text/html 2 bytes) → **six `image/jpeg` parts**, 3,483,673–4,666,010 bytes each. The entire content of the message was the pictures; the whole case turned on them.

**Two more traps in the same call, both cost me a round trip:**

- `svc.users().messages().get(...)` on the installed `google-api-python-client` returns an **`HttpRequest`**, not a dict — you must call **`.execute()`**. `.json()` raises `AttributeError: 'HttpRequest' object has no attribute 'json'`. There is no `.json()`.
- Searching by the name a human uses finds nothing. `from:bacharach` over `all_accounts=true` returned **0** for a sender the mailbox spells `moishe bachrach <moshbachrach@gmail.com>`, addressed by the owner as "Moshe Bacharach". **Search `newer_than:1d` on the specific account and read the list** instead of guessing the display name. The owner's spelling of a name is not the header's spelling.

**Also, for the record:** iPhone photos carry **EXIF orientation 6** with a landscape raw buffer (5712x4284) and display portrait (4284x5712). Any crop computed in raw pixel coordinates will land somewhere else entirely. Apply the rotation first, then crop — a `System.Drawing` `RotateFlip(Rotate90FlipNone)` on orientation 6.
