**L1477 · A substring match on a historical payload is not a live signal - my watchdog reported a device that never answered**

**A substring match against a payload that describes HISTORY is not a live signal. My watchdog announced "DEVICE RESPONDED" because the word it was looking for appeared in a 40 KB JSON blob listing every device's interview state from three days earlier.**

Measured 2026-09-14, on the live Zigbee estate.

I armed a watcher for a door sensor to rejoin, grepping the zigbee2mqtt log for:

 announce|Received Zigbee message|Interview - start|SUCCESSFUL|rejoined

At 20:06:22 it fired: **"DEVICE RESPONDED"**. The device had not responded. The match was the
`bridge/devices` MQTT message from `2026-09-13 21:51:06` -- a single ~40 KB line containing every
device's full descriptor including `"interview_state":"SUCCESSFUL"`. It is republished periodically,
so it appears early in any tail.

The next step my watcher automatically took exposed it: it re-ran `device/configure`, which failed
with the identical error it had failed with before -- `Bind ... failed (bindRsp after 10000ms)`, the
device not answering at all. Had the job not *acted* on its own false detection, I would have
reported a working sensor to the owner.

**Two rules.**
1. **Detect on structure, not on substrings, when the thing you are watching broadcasts its own
 state.** Match only lines a device itself produces. Exclude the bridge's own summary payloads --
 they contain the words you are looking for, from before you started looking.
2. **Watch by offset, not by tail.** Record the log's line count when you arm, then read only lines
 written after it (`wc -l` once, then `tail -n +N+1`). Tail-based watching cannot distinguish
 "this just happened" from "this is the newest matching thing that has ever happened".

**Why the false positive was caught:** the watcher was built to *act* on its detection, not just
report it, so the action failed loudly and visibly. A detector with a consequence attached tests
itself; a detector that only prints does not.
