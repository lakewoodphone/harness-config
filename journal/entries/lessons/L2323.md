**L2323 · A quote is perishable: re-read the source listing live before a sourcing number leaves the shop**

A quote's price and availability are **perishable facts**. On 2026-09-22 the Weitman laptop decision
(queue row #100, written 2026-09-18) was re-checked live before it was raised again, and the machine at
the centre of it no longer existed:

- the **$390** option — Newegg item `9SIAANWKNV0712`, Dell Latitude 5400 touchscreen, seller
 RenewTronics — read **"Out of stock — this item is currently out of stock and it may or may not be
 restocked"**;
- the **$265** option's source — eBay `168558807514` — had moved from **$130.00 to $169.00** in the same
 four-day window, which turns the case file's $188.51 ledger lane into a $19.51 gross instead of a
 $74.51 one.

Both numbers were read off live pages when they were recorded and both were wrong when they were acted
on. The customer had already chosen the machine that had gone out of stock.

**Rule.** On any customer sourcing quote, re-read the source listing live at the moment the decision is
made, *before* the number leaves the shop, and record price **and** availability **and** unit count with
the read time. A sourcing decision is not a decision until the listing is re-checked live.

**Second rule, from the same session — how to read a marketplace page.** Three reads of the same eBay
item page in one session produced three different prices (`$169`, `$179.99`, and the page's own
structured `"price":"169.0"`), because these pages are script-rendered and different extractors latch
onto different elements — `$179.99` was almost certainly a "similar items" tile. Only two things count
as evidence of a marketplace price: the page's **own structured data** (the `"price"` value in its JSON
blob — read by fetching the raw HTML and extracting it, not by asking a model to summarise the page), or
a direct-quote extraction. A schema-mode extraction of a dynamic marketplace page is a guess and must
not be reported as a price.
