**L1944 · Open the product page before quoting a price: read the stock line, the seller, and the delivery date - and search a product's failure record before recommending it**

Rule: never recommend a product from a search-result snippet. Open the product page and read three things before quoting a price - the stock line, the seller, and the delivery date. Then verify the seller is the retailer itself or a named authorized dealer, because on this class of equipment the warranty IS the product.

Evidence, 2026-09-17, one purchasing session, four snippet-driven errors caught only by opening pages:
1. "Crucial MX500 2TB, ~$259.99 Best Buy" - the Best Buy product page reads "This item is no longer available in new condition" (SKU 6321004). I recommended a dead link. The page's own embedded metadata still carried a stale $199.99, which is exactly the ghost price that fooled me.
2. "WD Red SA500 2TB, $410 Newegg" - real price, but the seller is "Global Electronic Treasure Store" with 3 seller ratings, shipping from Hong Kong in 9-19 days, and the warranty line says only "contact the Seller directly for warranty information." The drive's entire advantage over the cheaper Samsung was a real warranty and real vendor support.
3. Best Buy's WD Red SA500 listing - $604.03 and "Sold & shipped by Beach Audio Inc" - a marketplace middleman, not Best Buy, at $194 over the list price.
4. "Crucial P5 Plus 2TB, ~$240" - a 2022 article's price; current listing is $599 third-party and out of stock at Amazon.

The same session produced two more errors of a different kind, both corrected:
5. I published the 870 EVO 2TB endurance as 2,400 TBW. That is the 4TB model. The 2TB is 1,200 TBW. Both B&H's page and Samsung's own spec page say so. The "up to 2,400 TBW" line sits in a marketing block immediately above a footnote that lists per-capacity values - and I read the headline instead of the footnote.
6. I recommended the SanDisk Extreme Portable at $289.99 as the shop-grade pick, then found the failure evidence: patched AND replacement units still failing, sudden total data loss with no warning, active class action. Recommending a product before checking its failure history is the worst version of this mistake, because the price was right and the specs were right.

How to apply:
- Open the page. Read the seller name, the stock status, and the delivery date. Quote those, not the snippet.
- Prefer a retailer selling its own stock, or a brand-marked "Authorized Dealer". Treat marketplace "sold by" names as a different product.
- For any spec that drives a money or capacity decision, find the manufacturer's own value, and check the footnote rather than the headline. Marketing blocks aggregate across capacities; footnotes are per-capacity.
- Before recommending a product, search for its failures specifically ("<product> failure", "<product> lawsuit", "<product> warranty nightmare"). A good price on a drive with a known failure mode is not a good buy, and the failure record rarely appears in a price search.
- Delivery date is a spec. Same price, eight days apart, is the difference between a job shipping and a job sitting.
