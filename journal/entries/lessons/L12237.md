**L12237 · A dead-looking 5V reading is usually the cable: compute series resistance, and >500mA proves the data lines work**

Measured 2026-10-20 on mrs woldiger S20 FE (WO2608190005) after the charging port board was replaced. Two 5 V readings, same phone, only the cable changed:

 old cable: 4.75 V at 0.29 A = 1.4 W
 new cable: 4.80 V at 1.30 A = 6.2 W

Work the series resistance out instead of guessing. Take the source as 5.0 V nominal: R = (5.0 - V) / I.

 old: 0.25 V / 0.29 A = 0.86 ohm
 new: 0.20 V / 1.30 A = 0.15 ohm

0.86 ohm is the finding. That cable could not pass the same phone's 1.6 A at 9 V - it would drop 1.38 V and the phone would abandon the PD contract - so "the phone refuses to charge on 5 V" was never the phone.

THE RULE: on a 5 V reading that looks dead, compute R = (5.0 - V) / I BEFORE suspecting the device. Roughly >0.5 ohm means the cable or the connector is the suspect, not the phone. Below ~0.2 ohm the cable is fine and the current limit is the source or the phone.

SECOND USE, and it answers a different question: a phone drawing MORE than 500 mA on 5 V has decided its source is a dedicated charger. On an A-to-C cable that decision is made on the D+/D- pair (BC1.2 shorts them), so >500 mA is also a live data-line health signal. 1.3 A there is evidence the D+/D- path through a newly fitted port board works, which is worth more than the watts. On a C-to-C PD cable it says nothing about D+/D-, because current comes from CC negotiation instead - so always say which cable type the number came from.

Corollary for reporting: quote the cable type with every charge measurement. Without it, the same phone looks broken on one bench and perfect on another.
