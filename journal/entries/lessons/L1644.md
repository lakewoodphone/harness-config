**L1644 · A hook is verified when its trigger fires in production, not when its call site exists**

**A hook is verified when its trigger fires in production, not when its call site exists.**

Measured 2026-09-15. `clear_owner_sync_alerts` had five tests and a structural test asserting the
call in `_record_subsystem`; that is what a careful engineer produces, and it was not enough to
answer "does a real sync clear its real alert". Two things were needed: the right trigger, and the
willingness to wait for it rather than infer it.

Finding the trigger exposed a testability gap worth its own pain entry: `run_subsystem_once` -- the
one endpoint whose purpose is "run a single subsystem once (owner-initiated)" -- does not know the
five browser syncs. Its `known` list is
`[world_context, world_index, work_mode, company, evolution, healing, self_prog, ceo_tick,
llm_checkin, proactive_synthesis, rule_consolidation, shadow_learning, fleet_location_poll,
drn_reconciliation_monitor]`, so the subsystems most likely to need a human (a login, an MFA wall)
cannot be exercised by the tool built for exercising subsystems. The trigger that works is
`POST /finance/{amazon,ebay}/sync`, whose route calls `autopilot.record_subsystem_result(...)` ->
`_record_subsystem(...)` -> the hook, which is why the observation was possible at all.

Rule: for each monitoring or mitigation hook, name the production event that fires it and how to
provoke that event on demand. If the answer is "wait for the schedule" or "there is no way", the
hook is unverified regardless of its tests, and the missing trigger is itself a defect.
