**L2401 · A document written in observation grammar becomes a fake history - I invented a viewing log the owner never had**

MEASURED 2026-09-23, ZABZ-YOGA. Same session, second and worse failure of the same kind. The owner asked for the next watch suggestion. I led with a paragraph of reasoning built on the profile's "Known Anti-Patterns" section -- a list of shows with "Why it failed" lines -- and presented it as his viewing history. His reply: "i never watched reacher, or upgrade or into the badlands or limitletss. you make it sound like i watched those."

He was right on all four. THE ACTUAL COUNT OF TITLES HE HAS EVER TOLD US HE WATCHED AND DROPPED IS ONE: Person of Interest, stated earlier in the same conversation. Everything else in that section was analysis, written in the grammar of observation.

THE MECHANISM, which is the transferable part and is not specific to TV:

 A document is written in the CONFIDENT DECLARATIVE -- "### Into the Badlands / Why it failed: action without enough reason to care" -- with no marker distinguishing OBSERVED from INFERRED. It carries a "Confidence Levels" preamble at the top saying the doc mixes confirmed preferences and reasonable inferences, and that preamble is 300 lines away from the section that gets quoted. Every later reader -- a recommendation agent in January, the Copilot memory cache in May, me twice today -- absorbs the section as a record of events. The document does not lie; it just never says which sentences are memories and which are predictions.

 Second-order multiplier: one false record became FOUR false records in under two hours, because I WROTE THE ERROR FORWARD. I put it in lpt-hub (anti-patterns entry + version history v2.2), the Copilot memory file, the authority DB (memory 21145), and journal L2400. Each write made the next reader more confident, and the correction now had to chase the error through four stores. That is the real hazard: not the bad claim, but the bad claim's rate of propagation through a system whose whole job is to trust what it finds.

 Third-order, and this is the one that costs the business: a false anti-pattern BURNS SUPPLY. I had written off Reacher, Upgrade and Into the Badlands as exhausted. He has never seen any of them. My error was shrinking the space of things I could usefully recommend, and I would have gone on doing it because a written anti-pattern reads as settled.

THE RULES:
1. Every claim about WHAT THE OWNER DID lives at an explicit evidence level, stated inline next to the claim, not in a preamble: CONFIRMED (he said it) / ASSUMED (inferred, never observed) / UNKNOWN. If a section cannot carry a level, it must not use observation grammar. "Why it failed" is observation grammar. For an unobserved title, write "why this class of thing tends to fail this profile" instead.
2. A DOCUMENT IS NOT A DATABASE. A profile that reasons about taste is not a log of what happened. Where the two are stored in one file, the file must say so at the point of use -- I have now done exactly that: the anti-patterns section opens with the evidence key, Person of Interest is marked CONFIRMED with his verbatim words, and Andor/Limitless/The Boys S2/Into the Badlands are marked "ASSUMED, owner viewing status NOT confirmed" with a line stating his contradiction directly.
3. When a recommendation depends on whether he has seen something, the cost of asking is one question; the cost of assuming is recommending a show he has never heard of as if he rejected it, or recommending one he already dropped. Ask.
4. THE STRUCTURAL FIX, still unbuilt and correctly not built without his consent: a real watch log. Authority DB `watch_history` = 0 rows (checked 2026-09-23); no per-title record exists in ANY store. Until one exists, "what has he watched" is answerable only by asking him, and every recommendation rests on a taste profile that is inference all the way down. He was asked once and did not answer; do not build it unasked, and do not pretend the record is better than it is.

WHERE IT IS NOW RECORDED (four stores, all corrected today): lpt-hub research/miscellaneous/tv-movie-taste-profile.md (evidence key + per-title markers + title-status rule); Copilot memory movie-preferences.md; authority memories 21146 (supersedes 21143/21145, which are left in place as the record of having been wrong); and this entry, which supersedes nothing but cites L2400 -- L2400 is about ranking sources by freshness instead of authority, this is about mistaking inference for observation. Different failure, same session.

COST OF NOT HAVING THIS RULE: the previous seat's audit found "stale or wrong copy believed" in 769 sessions with 11,964 events and 159 explicit retractions in seven days (L1868). This session produced two more retractions, one per hour, and both were the same mistake in different clothing.
