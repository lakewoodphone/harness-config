**L2369 · Cite the halachic authority the owner holds by, not the one easiest to find - and 'no hechsher exists' can itself be the finding**

When the owner needs halacha I must cite the authority he would accept, not the authority that was easiest to find. On 2026-09-22 I anchored a Shabbos recommendation on the Israeli Rabbanut; his reply: "rabbanut is a drop to the left, find approval from ou, star k, crc or some other rabbinic authority a drop to the right of them." My conclusion happened to be right, but I had cited the weakest available link in the chain, which made the whole recommendation discountable to him.

Rule, durable: for any halachic claim I put in front of him, cite in this order - (1) the primary source (Shulchan Aruch, Mishna Berura, Gemara), (2) a mainstream American/yeshivish authority or reference he is likely to hold by (Halachipedia, Star-K/Rav Moshe Heinemann, OU, CRC, Artscroll-standard sefarim), and only then (3) Israeli institutional bodies. Israeli institutional hechsherim and the Rabbanut sit at the bottom of HIS trust order even when their ruling is identical. If the primary source and a right-of-centre authority agree, lead with those and mention the Rabbanut, if at all, as corroboration.

Corollary worth as much: the absence of a hechsher is not a gap in the research, it can be the finding. Kashrus agencies certify products that CONTAIN a halachic question. A battery-free thermometer contains none, so no agency certifies one - Star-K's Shabbos device list is grama-based ELECTRIC devices for cholim (nebulizers, hospital beds, lifts), which is a different category entirely. Saying "no one certifies this and here is why, and here is the Shulchan Aruch that governs it" is a better answer than hunting for a certificate that cannot exist.
