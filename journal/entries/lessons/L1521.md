**L1521 · A 'chip-off recovered poorly' report is a price band and a tool limit, not a dead end: read the band, and remember the dumps persist**

A lab reporting "very bad results" is usually a PRICE DECISION, not a failure. Read the band, not the adjectives.

Evidence -- 300DDR ticket 73459, SanDisk 256GB flash (controller 20-82-00388-B0 / NAND Z4480104744), read 2026-09-15:
- 2026-09-14, Brian: "full recovery using PC-3000 Flash ... only documents are working. Anything larger is corrupt due to this tool not fully supporting your controller and NAND chip-type. Our next step is trying again using a different tool."
- 300DDR's own terms (2026-08-11 Brian + their terms page): payment is due after data is recovered AND after the shop approves the FILE LISTING. <99% recovered = the shop's choice: approve and pay $500, or decline and pay a $100 per-chip labor fee. Nothing recoverable = $0. >=99% = $500, no option to decline.

Three durable readings from this:
1. "Documents work, anything larger is corrupt" is the signature of a PARTIAL TRANSLATOR MATCH, not media damage. PC-3000 Flash needs ECC + descrambling + FTL mapping for that exact controller; a partial match resolves single-page chains (documents) and drops anything spanning blocks (photos, video, archives). Diagnosis, not bad news.
2. If the chip-off succeeded, THE DUMPS PERSIST. The next attempt is re-translation of the same dumps with a different reconstructor -- no second chip-off, no second shipping risk, and a different lab can quote it. So a partial result is not the end of the road; it is the end of THAT TOOL.
3. The lab's <99% band has its own price ($500 accept / $100 decline). Decide only against the file listing, never against a percentage, and never before the retry the lab says it is running. On this case pass 1 was <99% and pass 2 was already in progress, so no decision was due at all.

Cost/benefit shape carried over for the shop: approve -> lab $500 vs customer advanced tier $700 (~$200 margin, same as a full recovery); decline -> lab $100 and the customer owes only the diagnostic.
