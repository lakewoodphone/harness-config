**L3674 · A simple customer notice is ONE sentence - four corrections in one message exchange, against a rule already written in the SMS runbook ('too long and AI like'). Never re-offer a settled choice.**

**Owner, 2026-10-12, on a pickup text I drafted for the Kogel job:** *"too long, he already paid for our drive no?"* and then, after I shortened it, *"leave that out just write it so you can come get it anytime"*. Then: *"make sure you are learning from my corrections."*

## The correction, stated plainly

**A simple customer notice is ONE sentence. Mine was three paragraphs, then two, and he still cut a clause out of the second one.**

What I wrote first:

> Hi, your data is ready and waiting at the shop.
> We've put everything onto a new 256GB drive, and it's all paid up, so there's nothing to settle when you come. Just stop by whenever it suits you and ask for it.
> If you'd rather have it on your own drive, bring it in instead and we'll put it on for you.

What he wanted:

> Hi, your USB is out for pickup and it's paid for. It's in the 24/6 pickup, so you can come get it any time.

**Every extra thing I added was something he already had no use for:**

| I added | why it was wrong |
|---|---|
| *"your data is ready and waiting"* | he does not need the job described back to him — he has been waiting eleven weeks for one thing |
| *"we've put everything onto a new 256GB drive"* | he bought the drive; he knows what it is |
| *"there's nothing to settle"* | restating "paid for" twice in one message |
| *"just stop by whenever it suits you and ask for it"* | two clauses for one fact |
| *"if you'd rather have it on your own drive, bring it in instead"* | **offering an alternative the customer has already decided against** — he chose our drive and paid for it; this line reopens a closed decision |
| *"because copying takes a while - it's about 240GB"* | a reason nobody asked for, in a message whose only job is to say come and get it |
| *"even when we're not there"* | an explanation of the 24/6 arrangement, when "you can come get it any time" already says it |

## The rules, actionable

1. **One fact, one sentence.** If the message has a single job — paid, ready, come get it — it is one sentence. Two at the very most.
2. **Never explain the mechanism.** How the pickup works, why copying takes time, what we did to the drive: none of it belongs in a notice.
3. **Never re-offer a settled choice.** Once the customer has chosen and paid, the alternative is closed. Offering it again reads as the shop not knowing what was agreed.
4. **Do not restate the job back to them.** They know what they brought in. "Your USB" is enough.
5. **`Hi,` and nothing else** — no first name, and now I know better than to think a name makes it warmer.
6. **When the owner trims a draft, trim it — do not re-add the operational advice** (SMS runbook §2, and learned again here).

## And the meta-lesson he was pointing at

**He had to correct me four times in this one message exchange — once on length, once on content, once on the shop's own operation — and this is a repeated pattern, not a one-off:** the SMS runbook already carries his 2026-09-16 correction, verbatim intent — *"too long and AI like"* — and I wrote a long AI-like draft anyway.

**A correction recorded in a document did not change my behaviour, because I read the document as reference and not as instruction.** The fix is not another note; it is that **any draft sent to him is measured against the shortest version that carries the fact, before it is shown.** If a sentence can be deleted without losing a fact, it should already have been deleted when it reached him.
