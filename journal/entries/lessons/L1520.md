**L1520 · Not running has four meanings - broken, orphaned, dismissed, deliberately disabled - and collapsing them lies**

**"Not running" has four different meanings and a health check that collapses them will lie. On this estate, collapsing them produced a CRITICAL that was entirely false.**

Measured 2026-09-15, after three separate false alarms in one week on the same Home Assistant estate.

Every one of these states presents as *the same thing* in the raw data -- an entity or integration
that is not doing anything -- and each needs a different response:

| state | how the data marks it | what it means | correct response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Broken** | `state: unavailable`, integration `setup_retry`/`setup_error` | something failed | fix it |
| **Orphaned** | entity attribute `restored: true` | HA reinstated a registry entry with **no integration behind it**; it can never recover | delete the registry entry |
| **Dismissed** | config entry `source: "ignore"` | a discovery the owner said no to | leave it, never set it up |
| **Disabled on purpose** | config entry `disabled_by: "user"` | someone turned it off deliberately | a decision to keep or reverse |

**The three failures this caused, in order, each after I had already fixed the previous one:**

1. **Dismissed read as broken (L230).** The only `zha` entry is a discovery dismissed in 2025 while
 Zigbee runs on Zigbee2MQTT. `not_loaded` is that entry's *correct, permanent* state. The tool
 published `SECURITY_INTEGRATION_DEGRADED -- zha` as **CRITICAL every 30 minutes for three days**.
2. **Orphaned read as broken (this one).** `SECURITY_SENSORS_UNAVAILABLE` reported 10 blind
 security entities. **All ten were `restored` ghosts** -- nine `binary_sensor.cam_*_alarm_*` from a
 deleted template plus `lock.boltchecked_apartmentdoor` -- while the three real door contacts were
 live and reporting. There are **377** such ghosts on the estate, and they are the bulk of what
 the surface was calling "400 unavailable entities".
3. **Deliberately-disabled read as broken.** Having disabled three `dahua` entries whose NVR
 channels have no camera (D147), the tool immediately raised
 `SECURITY_INTEGRATION_DEGRADED` as **CRITICAL** about my own decision.

**The rule.** Before classifying "X is not running" as a fault, ask *which of the four it is*, and
make the tool answer that from the data rather than from the name. The tell is cheap once you know
to look: `attributes.restored`, `source: ignore`, `disabled_by: user`.

**Why it matters more than the noise.** Both false CRITICALs were about a *security system*, and
this is the same surface whose job is to notice a door contact going blind at 01:48. A monitor that
cries CRITICAL about ghosts teaches its reader to skip the CRITICAL. The fix is not to silence the
categories but to name them: each is still reported, at `low`, with its reason, so a reader sees
that it was considered and excluded.

**Result on the live estate, same read, after the fix: zero critical findings**, down from three --
and `EVIDENCE_CAPTURE_FAILING` disappeared on its own merit, because the camera path was genuinely
repaired.
