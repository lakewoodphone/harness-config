**L5346 · The operator runs ONE icon and hands back the hardware. Anything I ask him to read off a screen is my work, not done.**

Corrected by the owner 2026-10-15, verbatim: *"you never do read me these lines, the usb logs them and i bring it
back to you thts it, i don't have time to do your work."*

I had just handed him a five-step procedure for opening a live-access window on a customer machine, and step 1
was *"read me those lines"* — the launcher's `READY` block — and step 2 and 3 were *"move the stick to this
machine… put the stick back in the target"*. Three of the five steps existed because I had not done my own
reading. The log already carries the same information, and the hardware already comes back.

**The rule.** For a job whose last mile is physical, the operator's part is: **plug it in, double-click the icon,
hand the hardware back.** If a step in my procedure requires him to read a value, transcribe an address, or
shuttle a device mid-window, the step is a missing read on my side. Read the tool that produces the value, or
design the step out.

**What the same mistake cost, concretely — three answers that were already in the kit:**

- **The window is 8 hours by default.** `lib/AccessWindow.psm1:32` `$script:DefaultTtlHours = 8`, ceiling 72
 (:33), and `Run-LptAccess.ps1:51 [int]$TtlHours = 8`. I had him shuttling the stick *mid-window* for fear of a
 deadline I never looked up. He brings it back **once** and there is a working day inside the window.
- **The private session key is in the run folder on the stick.** `Run-LptAccess.ps1:215`
 `$kp = Join-Path $runDir 'session_ed25519'`, and the script's own comment (:204-205) says it plainly: *"Only
 the PUBLIC half is written to this machine; the private half never leaves the stick, which travels back to
 us."* So the channel opens on the target, and I use it after the stick is in my hands. That is the design;
 I had invented a second shuttle for no reason.
- **The capture is bounded at 30 minutes** (`Run-LptAccess.ps1:47 [int]$MaxMinutes = 30`), so "capture first,
 then open" is a bounded wait, not an open-ended one.

**And a divergence worth keeping, found while looking for the above.** `DESIGN.md:82` states T1's target holds
*"A session keypair; our private half never leaves us"*, and `:211` says T1 leaves *"no secret at all - only our
public half"*. But the one-click path generates the keypair **into the run folder on the stick**
(`Run-LptAccess.ps1:215`), so on the icon path the private half does leave us — it leaves on a stick that has
been at customer premises, which is what `:211` says the kit does not do. `R6` (`:48`) tolerates it
(*"stick holds no key/secret outside the run folder"*), so this is a documented-but-contradicted property, not
an unseen bug: the *supplied-key* path (`-PublicKeyPath`, which `DESIGN.md` is describing) keeps the private
half on our side, and the *icon* path does not. **They are not the same tier and the doc describes only one.**
Not urgent: the key is per-run, its authorization line is removed by the teardown, and the stick is ours — but
the two paths must not be described as one property.

**The transferable shape.** A procedure I write for someone else is a measurement of how much of the job I
finished. Every "then tell me what it says" is an unfinished read; every shuttle is an unfinished channel.
