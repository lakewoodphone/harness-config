**L1512 · A missing log line is not evidence of a missing subsystem: silence is not a result until you check the logger**

**A missing log line is not evidence of a missing subsystem -- and silence is not a
result until you have checked what silence is made of.**

Measured 2026-09-15. `INFO Lifecycle sweep complete` had appeared 39 times in three
hours and then, after the 13:35 restart, never again. The loop was demonstrably alive
(`/autopilot/status`: enabled, thread_running) and demonstrably reaching the call site
(`financial_alert_sweep` at autopilot.py:6017 recorded 14:08:10 and `activity_sync` at
6061 recorded 14:08:38, with the sweep call at 6023 between them). Every gate read open
from a separate process (enabled=True, interval=120, monotonic clock reset on restart).
I concluded the sweep was dead, built two diagnostics for that conclusion, and only
then discovered the truth: **the root logger sat at WARNING with zero handlers, so
every `logger.info` in the application was discarded**, and WARNING and above reached
the journal only through Python's `lastResort` handler (bare message -- no level, no
module, no timestamp). The sweep had been running every 120 seconds the whole time.

The instructive part is what the evidence *could* have told me: the pre-13:35 INFO
lines came with an `INFO ` prefix that the app never formats, because a third-party
library (Twilio's HTTP client) had called `logging.basicConfig()` at import in that
process. A log line whose format does not match the code that supposedly emitted it is
a finding about the *logger*, not about the subsystem.

Rules:
- Before concluding anything from the absence of a log line, ask what the line would
 have been formatted by, and read the effective level and handlers. `logging`
 misconfiguration impersonates death perfectly.
- Verify at the effect, never at the narration: the sweep's own returned summary
 (`task_lifecycle_updated=37`, `priority_task_goals_created=3`) proved it ran in one
 call, after an hour of inference.
- "Silence means X" is a claim requiring the same evidence as "the reading is X". This
 is P3 ("reading the wrong data and believing it") wearing a new costume: I did not
 read a stale database, I read a *missing* log and believed it.
