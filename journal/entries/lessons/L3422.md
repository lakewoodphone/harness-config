**L3422 · The mesh fleet measured over 32 dispatches: unsteerable children, a broker on one node, a cap of 10, and a PowerShell node**

**How the mesh fleet actually behaves, measured on 2026-10-09 over 32 dispatches.**

- **A `subagent` child runs on whichever node the placement broker picks, and it cannot be steered
 mid-run.** All 32 children named `ZABZ-TECH`; none ran locally. `list_agents` returns *"no
 subagents"* for them and `send_message` to a returned job id fails with *"is unavailable"*, because
 the mesh child belongs to the remote engine. **So a brief cannot be corrected after dispatch.** The
 one I needed to correct — rule 9, the duplicated message table — had to wait for the next wave,
 and its absence produced a wrong note in the pilot. **Put everything the reviewer needs in the
 first prompt; there is no second chance.**
- **The broker lives on `secratary` and is a single point of failure.** When its ssh went over the
 banner-exchange timeout, dispatch failed outright: `broker-unreachable: … secratary-ts:http://
 localhost:3091/place — ssh exited 255`. Three batches failed, were re-dispatched, failed again, and
 succeeded on the third attempt about ten minutes later. **A failed batch returns to the queue; it
 is not lost and it is not done — check the job list, do not assume.**
- **The background-job cap is 10 per owner.** Dispatching 12 in one message makes the 11th and 12th
 return `Error: background job limit reached`. Waves of ≤10, and re-dispatch as slots free.
- **A Windows mesh node's remote shell is PowerShell, and its `scp` wants a Windows path.** `ssh
 desktop-ts "ls -1 C:/x"` fails; `cmd /c "dir /b C:\\x"` works but the inner escaped quotes are
 re-parsed by PowerShell and break the path, so `cmd /c "dir /b C:\\x"` with **no inner quotes** is
 the form that works. `os.name == 'nt'` on the *caller* says nothing about the node: try the node's
 own shape first and fall back.
- **One batch reported `exit 1` after writing all 25 lines.** The failure frame is not proof the work
 was lost. Check the artifact — `(Get-Content … | Measure-Object -Line).Lines` — before re-running.
- **A pilot batch is worth its round trip.** Two batches (50 orders) run first exposed the wrong-note
 failure mode and cost 15 minutes; discovering it after 26 batches would have cost the whole run.
- **Cost of a batch: about 5–7 minutes for 25 orders**, ~5.4 KB of brief per order. 641 orders / 26
 batches finished in roughly 90 minutes wall-clock across three waves.
