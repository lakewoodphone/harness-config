**So the provenance is good on 66 of 68 and worth 47,414.04**, and only two - the 2026-08
`personal-amex-94045` case I worked and one `chase-scraper`-anchored one - have a reading the tool cannot
vouch for. My own summary has repeatedly implied that most of this is a reading problem and that the
transactions were probably fine. **The data does not say that.** Three cases investigated this session
did point at readings (the month-boundary pair on 7542, the mirror pair on 94045, the R4-impossible
savings chain), but three cases out of 66 is not a pattern, and I was generalising from the ones I had
happened to touch.

WHAT THE CLASSIFICATION DOES SAY, and it is more useful than the thing I was saying:
 * The two `statement/plaid-api` sources agree in 3 cases and disagree in 63, so the disagreement is not
 a property of one feed. A bad source would show up as one arm of this table and it does not.
 * 47 disagreements are statement-versus-statement, the highest-authority pairing available, summing
 32,288.31 - **that is the core of the problem and it cannot be dismissed as a scraping artefact.**
 * The 6 paired ones are mostly statement/statement and plaid/plaid, so R5's pairs are inside the
 same-authority set rather than being a cross-source curiosity.
 * A `chase-scraper` source appears here that I had not met; it is worth knowing it is in the anchor
 mix, because a scraper reading is a different kind of evidence from a statement.

THE HONEST POSITION AFTER TONIGHT. 65 real defects, 47,414.04 of them between readings the tool trusts,
and the causes are unexplained case by case. What has changed is that the audit now knows which is which:
provenance on every reading, R4 rejecting impossible balances before any transaction is examined, R5
collapsing six of them into three, and a worklist that carries the warning rather than a bare number.
**Progress on an audit is not the same as progress on the thing audited**, and this table is the honest
measure of how much of the latter there is.
