**L3341 · L: Three plausible readings of a pricing rule, all commercially wrong - do not re-derive a money model, read it**

2026-10-05. The owner asked me to work out how to word the shop''s 50 fee. I produced three
successive versions, each internally coherent, each confidently presented, and each wrong:

 1. "the 50 is credited toward the repair" -> nothing is credited
 2. "diagnosis comes off the repair" -> backwards; diagnosis ADDS
 3. "50 on top of every job" -> in the attempt case it is already inside

The owner''s reaction to the third was "ahhhhhh ... do you even understand our current pricing
structure". I did not, and I had been answering as if I did.

WHY ALL THREE WERE PLAUSIBLE
Each was a defensible reading of PART of what I had been told:
 - the pivot plan doc (2026-10-05) really does say "credited toward the repair" - true for the
 attempt case, which the doc was describing;
 - the owner really did say "non refundable" - true always, but that says nothing about whether
 the 50 is inside or on top;
 - the Q1050 jack really was "90 if it works, 50 to attempt, comes off the 90" - the attempt
 case again, mistaken for the general rule.
So I had four consistent facts and assembled them into a model that contradicted the owner.

WHAT ACTUALLY FIXED IT
Asking ONE question at a time, in a fixed order, and stating back what I had heard after each.
It took five questions. Every previous attempt had been me proposing a synthesis instead.

THE RULE FOR MONEY MODELS
A money rule is not something to reason toward; it is something to be told and then read back.
When the owner corrects a pricing answer, STOP synthesising and ask a single closed question
whose two answers are both concrete prices. "Is it inside the 100 or on top of it" is answerable
in one word. "How does your pricing work" is not.

COROLLARY: the same instinct that made me build the wrong model also made me ask him to confirm
it instead of asking him to state it. Confirmation only tests the version I already invented.
