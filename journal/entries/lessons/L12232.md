## Supplement to D9347 — the describing-model evidence, with one correction of my own

**My error.** I told the owner the first measurement would be "one hour of labelling, zero spend". That was
wrong. A measurement worth trusting needs TWO independent annotators, and their agreement is the ceiling the
whole result is read against; one person labelling their own frames proves nothing. Real cost is about 400
dollars of labelling plus under 50 dollars of API, over 3-5 days. A smaller unpaid first pass is possible but
it is a smoke test, not a measurement.

**New facts that harden the recommendation.**

1. **A yes/no question to a describing model is a broken gate.** Measured binary-VQA yes-rate is 90.22% where
 the ground-truth yes-rate is 42.17% (arXiv 2601.12430); 91.98% against 50% on SugarCrepe. So "do the
 sleeves cover the elbow?" answers yes almost always. That kills the simplest form of the idea outright.
2. **The frozen-embedding shortcut is three times worse.** A trained head on frozen Fashion-CLIP features
 scores 21% F1 against 64.0% for the VLM (015:192). Zero-shot label strings are format-valid but not correct.
3. **Real time on a mid-range phone does not exist for a describer.** Measured on a Pixel 8a: a 2.48B VLM at
 2.41 s to first token and 4.3 tokens/s, so roughly 7 s for a 20-token caption, against about 20 ms for Pose
 lite. The bottleneck is not the phone's tier, it is the class of model.
4. **Cloud price, current:** 0.56-1.68 dollars per 1,000 frames, about 0.84-2.52 dollars per user per month at
 50 frames/day (Gemini list pricing). 015's 0.12-1.02 figure was Chinese-provider pricing and is superseded.
 005-team-a.md is stale on both price and the training plan; 015 supersedes it.
5. **The legal position is sharper than "vendor terms".** COPPA treats a photograph containing a child as
 personal information, so disclosing it to a vendor needs verifiable parental consent AND a written
 service-provider contract; the 2025 amendments (compliance date 2026-04-22) add biometric identifiers. New
 Jersey P.L.2026 c.25 (2026-06-30) makes personal data from a known child sensitive data, routes it to
 COPPA-compliant processing, and bans its sale at 50,000 dollars per record.

**Consequence for the pending question.** Even in the "ambiguous slice may leave" branch, it can only ever be
our own rented GPU with a self-hosted policy model — never a commercial AI vendor — because disclosure to a
vendor is a consent-and-contract problem, not just a retention one. The recommendation stands: nothing leaves
the device at launch, geometry does the work on-device, and a constrained-label VLM is a self-hosted arbiter
for the ambiguous ~10% only if it earns that by measurement.

**The test that would settle it (design accepted):** 400 real screenshots from shop devices, 100 each with
elbow, knee, neckline and a tight-but-covered garment visible; two annotators, adjudicated, their agreement
reported as the ceiling; three arms — the existing fused score, a constrained-label VLM on the crop, and
geometry. Two numbers per attribute: violation recall at no more than 10% false-blur, and coverage (the share
judged without abstaining). Falsified if the VLM arm beats geometry by 10 points or more of recall at the same
false-blur budget on two or more attributes.
