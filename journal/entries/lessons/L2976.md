**For a venue's proprietary mechanics the players are the source, not the marketing — I invented a difficulty system that does not exist**

2026-09-28, comparing Activate Games and The Gravity Vault.

Activate's own site says, accurately, *"difficulty levels range from 1-10, unlocking as you beat them."*
From that single sentence I told the owner that a 14-year-old could sit at level 3 while a 23-year-old ground
through level 9 **in the same room, on the same machine**. **That is impossible and I made it up.** The level
is a property of the group and the room: everyone plays the same level together and the room advances when
they beat it. What differs between players is **score**.

The player community states the behaviour precisely where the company states the intent:

- **Levels unlock permanently on the profile** — beating level 4 keeps level 5 open on this visit *and on
 future visits*.
- **Competitive scores do not count toward the monthly, yearly or all-time leaderboards**; only co-op play
 does.
- **The queue is a physical door queue.** There is no app and no central line: you stand at a room's door,
 the occupying team is forced out when you choose that room, and you rotate. Hence the community's real
 tactics — farm the unpopular rooms (Hoops), avoid the viral one (Mega Grid), ask the staff when it is
 quietest.
- **Five is the optimum team size.** Every level-1 game is beatable by two, but several level-10 games are
 described as effectively impossible with two, and a regular calls three "a severe disadvantage."

**RULE:** when the question is *how a proprietary system behaves* — queueing, difficulty, progression,
whether a space is private to your group — the vendor's marketing describes the intent and the user community
describes the behaviour. Read both, and cite the community for mechanics.

**The tell, which is worth more than the rule.** The owner broke it with one sentence: *"how can 2 people
play the same game at the same time at different levels?"* That question is unanswerable, **and an
unanswerable question is what an invented mechanic produces.** A description of a system that generates
impossible questions is falsified by the question alone. Treat *"wait, how can that possibly work?"* as
evidence that the model is wrong, not as a request for more detail — this is the same failure as the invented
Topgolf price earlier the same day (L2850), and the second time in one day that a question caught an
over-claim that sounded plausible.
