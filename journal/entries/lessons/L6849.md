**L6849 · Build the document from the store's own fields, and let the tool find what your memory omitted - it found two high-severity rows immediately**

Measured 2026-10-16, the day after I published an owner-question document that attributed five answers to the owner he never gave. The fix was not care, it was a tool: cfo-owner-facts.py reads owner_decision_queue row by row and prints each row's own field values with a flag for every state that matters - NO-ANSWER (24 rows), ANSWERED-BUT-OPEN (5), EXPIRED-DEFERRAL (4), DEFERRAL-AS-ANSWER (3), TERMINAL-EMPTY (0, printed so a zero is visible). Two results justify it. First, run against the queue it flags every one of the five rows I got wrong: rows 110 and 169 as DEFERRAL-AS-ANSWER, 463 and 519 as NO-ANSWER (I had quoted answers for both), 261 and 531 as terminal (I had listed one open and asked him the other). Second, and more useful, it found two HIGH severity rows my hand-written document did not contain at all, both with a deferral that had come due and nothing resurfacing it: id 228, 'the business is taking roughly 4,500 a month out of your personal accounts and you are now out of personal money, at what point do you stop funding it' (due 9 October, seven days overdue), and id 167, 'the Elantra has had no fire, theft or collision cover since 19 May 2026' (due 14 October, five months uninsured). A human writing from memory produced five fabrications and missed two of the most important open questions; the same human with the fields in front of him produces neither. The rule: when a document makes claims about a system or a person, generate it from the store's own fields - and when the store has a field whose name says one thing and whose sibling field says another (status says answered, resolved_at says resolved), print both and let the flagging be mechanical.
