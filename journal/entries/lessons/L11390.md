**L11390 · L11327 · The allowlist does not follow the CNAME: 102 of 113 targets refused, and every health check said healthy**

**Measured 2026-10-20.** Two feature failures eight weeks apart, same filter, same shape:
`voice-to-text/why-not.md` (2026-10-15) records *"the allowlist does not follow the CNAME"* —
`updates.cdn-apple.com` allowed, its target `updates.g.aaplimg.com` NXDOMAIN. Today the location
failure is the same thing one level up: `gs-loc.apple.com` allowed while iOS 26 asks
`gs-loc-new.ls-apple.com.akadns.net` directly and is refused.

A sweep this session found the size of it: of **113 CNAME targets** reached from the **106 most-queried
allowed names**, **102 are refused**. The device survives on the address that happens to ride along in
the entry answer; the moment a service asks the target, or Apple moves the address down the chain, a
feature dies silently and no health check notices, because every check asks "is the container up".

RULES:
1. **A default-deny resolver's allowlist is a policy about NAMES, and services are served from
 CHAINS.** Verifying the entry name answers is not verifying the service. Follow the chain — that is
 what `40-cli/tools/dns-chain-canary.py` does, and it exits 1 on a refused hop.
2. **Retry volume is the tell.** A name asked thousands of times an hour by a device that also failed
 is a service the device needs, not something it stumbled into. The filter's own log names the
 breakage; nobody was reading the deny side.
3. **When a fix is written, sweep for siblings before closing it.** The dictation fix named three
 hosts and stopped; the same defect family covered every Apple CDN path, including location. A fix
 scoped to the reported symptom is a fix that schedules the next incident.
4. **"We fixed this in August" is a claim about a file, not about a device.** The August change was
 applied, persisted, and is live today — and the phone still could not locate. The acceptance test
 has to be the user-visible behaviour, not the config value.
