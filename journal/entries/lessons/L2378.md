**L2378 · Severe colicky cramps then a large 'clot' passed in one piece on continuous norethisterone = think decidual cast (membranous dysmenorrhoea), not a period or a miscarriage**

Reported 2026-09-24 by the owner about his wife: extreme period-like cramping in the evening, then next morning one large mass passed ('almost 2 tablespoons', 'like she had her whole period in one shot'), then bleeding essentially stopped. That exact sequence is a named entity and it is documented with THIS drug at THIS duration.

Primary source: Hamasho et al., Clin Case Rep 2026;14(9):e73549, PMID 42746505, PMC13576626 - 'Norethisterone-Associated Decidual Cast Expulsion Mimicking an Acute Gynecologic Emergency'. Verbatim abstract: 'Decidual cast is a rare benign condition that may mimic acute gynecologic emergencies. It should be suspected in patients with severe colicky pain followed by sudden relief after tissue expulsion, especially with progestin use such as norethisterone. Accurate diagnosis and histopathology prevent unnecessary surgery.' Their case: continuous norethisterone 5 mg daily for 2 months (started to delay menstruation - the same indication and duration), sudden severe colicky pain, ~2h later a 7x4.5 cm pinkish-white fibrous mass passed with immediate pain relief and minimal bleeding; urine hCG negative; ultrasound normal, no free fluid; histopathology = pseudodecidualised stroma, inactive glands, no gestational tissue; managed conservatively, drug stopped, asymptomatic at 3 months. Supporting: Appelbaum Obstet Gynecol 2010 PMID 20664428 (norethindrone acetate, 16yo, membranous dysmenorrhea from excess progesterone); Shaw & Baker Aust NZ J Obstet Gynaecol 1985 PMID 3863594.

Operating rules this gives a future session:
- The signature is cramping FIRST, relief ON expulsion, then bleeding stops. Reported mean latency on continuous progestin ~4.7 months; it can happen at 2 months.
- It is routinely misdiagnosed as miscarriage or ectopic. Separate them with a urine hCG AND, if the specimen was kept, histopathology (gold standard). Tell the patient to keep and refrigerate the specimen and photograph it.
- Benign, no surgery, conservative management. Whether to continue the progestin: case authors discontinued; other authors say continuation is reasonable (low recurrence). Prescriber's call.
- NOT dose-dependent per current literature - an idiosyncratic endometrial response; PCOS was a co-factor in the published case, not a requirement.
- Halachically relevant: the cast is pinkish-white fibrous tissue, not a red clot, so what the rav is told about its character matters; a possible miscarriage is a different conversation from a blood clot.
