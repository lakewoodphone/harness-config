## Correction: the shop was closed, not deaf -- I read a capture gap as a business outage

This corrects H2171 ("the shop has been effectively deaf to inbound SMS"), P2142's framing,
owner-queue row 136, and the volume alarm I added to `comms_freshness` at 2026-09-22 03:00Z
and replaced within the hour.

**What I claimed.** Inbound customer SMS had collapsed to 2 a day from 20-40, therefore the
shop had been losing customers since 2026-09-18, possibly for 42 hours.

**What is true.** Both halves are wrong or unproven:

- **The shop has been closed.** Inbound calls, a completely independent channel, fell in
 lockstep with SMS: 53 on Fri 2026-09-18, then **1, 1, 0, 3** across Sat 09-19, Sun 09-20,
 Mon 09-21 and the first hours of Tue 09-22. Monday 2026-09-21 recorded **zero** calls
 against a Monday baseline of 84 on 09-14. The stretch is Shabbat followed by Yom Kippur.
 A closed shop and a dead channel have identical SMS signatures.
- **Nothing was lost.** Silence on both channels at once is a shut business, and the call
 harvest had been sitting there the whole time saying so.

**The two mistakes, named.** I never asked whether the business was open, and I treated one
channel's silence as the business's silence when an independent channel was already
measuring the business's actual activity. The second is the generalisable one: **before
calling a channel dead, read a channel that cannot fail the same way.**

**What survives from the earlier entry:**
- The duplication loop was real and is fixed (4,637 rows for 201 distinct messages; verified
 `{"read": 0, "stored": 0, "skipped": 0, "errors": 0}` after the fix).
- The Dialpad dispatch stall was real: both webhook subscribers silent from 2026-09-20T08:12:43Z
 to 2026-09-21T16:02Z. Its business impact was nil, because the shop was shut.
- `comms_freshness` now refuses to assert a consequence it cannot measure, counts distinct
 messages rather than rows, and cross-checks the shop's own call volume before calling a
 channel dead. All three are improvements; only the last one is what caught this.

**The rule.** A metric that can only see one channel will mistake that channel's quiet for the
world's. When a signal says "nothing is arriving", the next question is not "why did the source
stop" but "is there anything for it to carry" -- and there is usually another channel, already
being collected, that answers it.
