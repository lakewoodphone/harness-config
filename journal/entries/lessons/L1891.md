**L1891 · Correcting L1890: one confirmed broken call site is not 62 unverified ones - count what you measured, not what you matched**

**CORRECTION to L1890, recorded within the hour because L1890 stated more than was measured.**

L1890 claimed ~14 (later counted as 62 live call sites across 25 files) frontend reads "fail identically"
because they call `.query()` on the tRPC hooks client. **That generalisation was not measured, and it
misstates the evidence.** The corrected position:

**What IS measured (a real, reproduced defect):**
- `useFaqs.ts` called `trpc.faq.getPublished.query(input)` where `trpc` is `createTRPCReact(...)`.
- On the LIVE production page that threw `TypeError: e[i] is not a function` from inside the tRPC proxy, four
 times, and **no request to the API appeared in the browser network log** - so the call died in JavaScript
 before the network layer.
- After switching that one hook to `vanillaTrpcClient`, the same page sent the request and rendered 28 real
 published FAQ questions with **0 console errors**.

**What is NOT measured and must not be asserted:** that the other 24 live files **cannot** work. Two readings
are both still open for them:
(a) `createTRPCReact` in this tRPC version may also expose a `.query()`-capable proxy, in which case only the
FAQ hook was broken (by a subtlety not yet isolated), or
(b) the whole pattern is broken and those pages have always rendered empty or fallback states.

**Why the distinction matters, and the actual lesson:** I inferred the general case from one instance plus a
count of textual matches. A grep that finds 62 occurrences of a pattern tells you the pattern is *present*; it
tells you nothing about whether it *executes*. **Count what you measured, not what you matched.** The
generalisable half of L1890 stands: a fallback that renders believable content hides a fetch that never ran,
and the way to catch it is to open the page and read the network log - which is exactly what found this.

**The test that will settle it**, assigned and not yet run: for each of the 25 files, determine whether the
`.query()` line is on a reachable path at runtime, and where it is, reproduce a failure in a real browser.
Until that exists, the honest statement is "one confirmed broken call site, 61 candidates unverified".
