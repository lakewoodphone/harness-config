**L3256 · Decide recipes from outcome reports by people who cooked the dish, not from ingredient arithmetic - the owner was right and it changed four decisions**

On 2026-10-04 the owner challenged my method directly: "you never tasted these things, so find people who
did and analyze what they say and then make your recipe decisions, isnt that a smarter way to do it?"
It is, and I had not done it. I had built a lahmajin recipe from a Turkish regulatory specification, a
test-kitchen recipe and my own arithmetic, and defended it with tables. I had never tasted the dish and
had checked no outcome claims from anyone who had.

Going to the kosher kitchens and their comment threads changed four decisions in that recipe:

- BASE. No kosher home cook in any source rolls paper-thin. They use frozen mini pizza dough rounds,
 flour tortillas, or a soft dough rolled a quarter inch. I had specified a 3 mm hand-rolled round at
 500F, which is a commercial-bakery product, not a home one.
- BINDER. I had pomegranate molasses as the binder. The kosher versions use 4 tbsp of tamarind paste or
 PRUNE BUTTER as the binder and 1 tbsp pomegranate molasses as an accent - both, for different jobs.
- TEMPERATURE. I had 500F for 4-6 min. Three independent kosher sources give 350F for 15-20 min plus a
 4-5 min broil.
- THE FIX FOR THE FAILURE. I had invented a fat-and-portion analysis. The actual reported failure is a
 wet sticky bottom, and the fix the recipe's author gives in her own comment thread is MORE MEAT (1.25
 or 1.33 lb instead of 1 lb) plus a thicker binder. The named commenters are Esther (wet bottom) and
 Sorah Adler (the meat mixture slid off the rounds).

The generalisable rule: for anything where I cannot verify the outcome myself, the evidence is
outcome reports from people who did it, and the comment threads are where failures are named. A
specification says what a dish IS. A cook says what goes WRONG. Kosher-community sources are doubly
valuable because they have already solved the kashrus problem in a kitchen like this one.

A second-order finding worth keeping: the kosher.com Lachmagine recipe does not publish an oven
temperature at all, and the site's own team answered a reader's question with "I'd guess 350 degrees F".
On a recipe site, the comments can carry information the recipe lacks.
