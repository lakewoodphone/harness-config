**L7870 · The rule that decides is the CHANNEL, not the recipient (family chat is a separate system)**

The owner corrected me within the hour: *"the family chat system is not part of the regular rate
limits and is a completely separate thing."*

I had built the wrong thing and had written it down as a fix. My reading was "a family member is on
the owner's number list, so remove the number" -- ledger 526 as first filed. That fixes one person.
The rule is about a CHANNEL: Family Chat is not his attention channel, so none of the owner's
attention rules are about it, in either direction, whoever is on the list.

What was actually wrong, and is now fixed in `owner_text_relay.py` (`d4ffe3aad4d0`):

- `ceiling_for(to)` keyed only on the RECIPIENT, so a family message to the owner was cut to his
 320-char reading ceiling. Now `ceiling_for(to, subsystem, door)` and a `SEPARATE_CHANNELS`
 exemption decides first: family traffic gets the transport limit (1600), never his reading limit.
- The rate FLOOR fired on the last delivery to the owner regardless of channel, which is exactly how
 `sms_log` 4280 came to read "we texted **him** 9 minute(s) ago" about a message to a relative.
 The floor now carries `not separate`.

Pinned by a test that proves the asymmetry rather than the exemption in general: the SAME text from
the `app` door is FLOOR-held while the family message is not (24 tests, all pass).

The generalisable mistake is mine and it is an ordering mistake: I let a property of the RECIPIENT
decide which rules apply, when the property that decides is the CHANNEL. A rule written per-recipient
looks right for every case you think of, and then one of your relatives is on your number list and
your own attention budget silently starts throttling your family.

App-side half remains open and is ledger 526 (re-framed): `_owner_leg()` calls `owner_text.gate` at
`twilio_client.py:677` for every recipient, so the channel exemption must be carried there too. The
relay is now the reference implementation.
