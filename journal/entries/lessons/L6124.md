**L6124 · 196 rows are excluded from every balance flow as opposite-sign duplicates and it is undetermined whether they should be**

2026-10-13. 196 rows are excluded from every balance flow because they were marked as duplicates OF A
ROW WITH THE OPPOSITE SIGN. Whether that is right is undetermined, and it is material.

WHAT I FOUND WHILE WORKING THE LARGEST SIGN-FLIP. `personal-amex-94045` 2026-08: the tool's chain flow
is 5,068.64 and the account's own balance fell 1,359.02, a 6,427.66 gap. Inside that window the ledger
holds 87 rows of which **18 are marked duplicate - and every one of the 18 is a CREDIT**. Reading
`load_transactions` explains why they matter:
 # A duplicate is not a movement; counting it would invent money.
 if dupe: continue
so the tool's flow EXCLUDES them. My own first query included them and got 4,221.36; excluding them
reproduced the tool exactly at 5,068.64. That is the second time in two rounds that reproducing the
tool's own arithmetic was the only way to know what I was looking at.

THE ROWS THEMSELVES. 2026-07-03 credit 258.05 "AplPay THE HOME DEPOLAKEWOOD" is marked duplicate. The
same account holds 2026-07-03 debit 258.05 "The Home Depot" and 2026-07-04 debit 258.05 "The Home
Depot". A credit at the same merchant for the same amount on the same day is what a REFUND looks like -
and a refund excluded from the flow is money invented in the opposite direction from the one the
comment is guarding against.

HOW WIDESPREAD, measured across the whole ledger:
 duplicate-marked rows with a resolvable original : 2186
 of those, OPPOSITE direction to their original : 196 (36,392.13)
 same direction : 1990
 by account: personal-amex-94045 98 | personal-paypal 33 | escape101-boa-checking 18 |
 (no account) 16 | lpt-chase-7542 11 | lpt-chase-3929 8 | personal-amex-22005 6 | ...
**Ninety-eight of the 196 sit on the very account whose sign-flip I was working**, which is what made
this worth chasing rather than noting.

WHY I AM NOT CALLING IT A BUG. The estate already knows sign-mirror pairs exist and has a rule for
them, in this same tool: `R1 sign mirror - the 2026-07-01 manual batch wrote -277727 and +277727 for one
Amex card 25 minutes apart`. So a credit and a debit of one amount a few days apart can genuinely be ONE
event recorded twice with the sign flipped, and marking one a duplicate is then correct and protective.
It can equally be a purchase and its refund, or a transfer out and a transfer in, which are two real
events and must both count. **From the ledger alone I cannot tell which a given pair is**, and the
distinction changes the flow, the balance chain, and therefore the reconciliation.

AND IT DOES NOT EXPLAIN THIS WINDOW either way: including the 18 credits gives a flow of 4,221.36 and
excluding them 5,068.64, against a real movement of -1,359.02. Neither reconciles. So the sign-flip has
another cause, and this finding is a separate question that the same investigation surfaced.

WHAT WOULD SETTLE IT. For each of the 196, the merchant is the tell: a refund at a retailer (a CREDIT
at The Home Depot, Walmart, Amazon, Swifty Gas) is a real event, while an opposite-sign pair at a BANK
or a card-payment line is the mirror artifact. Splitting the 196 by merchant class and reading the
largest ten is a bounded next step, and it feeds straight back into the reconciliation because those
rows are currently excluded from it.
