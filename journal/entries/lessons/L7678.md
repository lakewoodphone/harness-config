**L7678 · An automation that drives a LAN device from another network is not configured, it is assumed — and the assumption dies silently at a cutover**

MEASURED 2026-10-18, the Shabbat automation.

Before the 2026-10-11 cutover the company API ran on `secratary` (192.168.50.77), inside the office LAN. It drove the Shelly plug at 192.168.50.103 and Home Assistant at homeassistant.local:8123. Both work only from inside that LAN. The design doc in `shabbat_orchestrator.py` even states the assumption: "the secretary can also call the plug directly as a backup executor."

Then the authority moved to `secratary-cloud` in a Hetzner datacenter. Nobody re-checked that sentence. `ip route get 192.168.50.103` now answers `via 172.31.1.1 dev eth0 src 178.156.194.1` — i.e. out to the public internet. The journal for that unit contains **1,835 "Shelly unreachable" warnings and not one success**, starting at the first line of its retention window. The automation was dead from the cutover and reported it only into a journal nobody reads.

The owner found out by walking into the shop on Shabbat with the cameras on and the interior door locked.

THE GENERAL RULE. When a host moves networks, every component that is addressed by a *private* address or an mDNS name is a silent casualty — it does not error at deploy time, it errors at the moment it is needed. A cutover checklist must include, for every automation, "what does this one talk to, and is it reachable from the new seat?" and the answer must be a command output, not a memory.

Second, narrower: `shelly_plug._base_url()` read `getattr(settings, "shelly_plug_port", None) or 80` and **no `shelly_plug_port` field existed on `Settings`**. A setting the code reads but the model does not declare is a setting that silently does nothing, and it was what made the plug impossible to front with any proxy. Check both halves: the reader and the declaration.

Third: the failure was SAFE and still wrong. The interlock could not prove the door released, so it refused to cut power — correct, by design, and completely invisible. A safety interlock that refuses loudly only in a log is, from the owner's side, the same as a system that does not work. Every refusal must reach a human who can act on it.
