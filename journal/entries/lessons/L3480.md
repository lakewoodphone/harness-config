**L3480 · An idempotency check keyed on a DERIVED hash is not idempotent - hash the original**

WHAT HAPPENED. photoio ingested the owner's photos into a batch. Its duplicate check looked up the ORIGINAL image's sha256 in a ledger dict that was keyed by the CONVERTED JPEG's sha256. Nothing matched, so a second ingest of the same folder wrote 42 ledger rows for 21 photos. It happened to be harmless (the on-disk files deduped by name), but the check was inert: change the JPEG quality or the resize edge and every old photo looks new, re-converting the whole bin and re-billing every reading.

THE FIX. Keep BOTH hashes and key identity on the ORIGINAL (source_sha256), keeping the converted hash as a secondary. Verify by re-ingesting the real folder and watching the count not move: "photos before: 21 / after: 21".

GENERALISATION. Whenever a pipeline is "transform input, store output", the dedup key must be the INPUT. A derived artefact's hash answers "did the transform change?", never "have I seen this before?".

CAUGHT BY a test, not by production - the assertion was `status == skipped-duplicate` on a second run.
