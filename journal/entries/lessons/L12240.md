**L12240 · Fashionpedia licences and the garment-rung minimum model set, measured from the JSON**

Verified the garment-dataset licence stack by parsing the Fashionpedia train annotation JSON (542,193,045 B) rather than trusting the prior stream's summary. The 51.2% figure reproduces exactly: 23,370 of 45,623 training images are CC BY-NC/NC-SA/NC-ND = 51.22%. Cleanly commercial is 8,514 = 18.66% (CC BY 6,650 + PD 50 + Unsplash 1,274 + Pexels 456 + Burst 70 + Freestocks 14). What the prior stream missed: the other 30.1% is not neutral — CC BY-SA 7,131 (15.63%) and CC BY-ND 6,064 (13.29%, "no derivatives" vs training) are ambiguous, plus 544 images with no licence found. So the usable slice is 18.66% clean / 51.22% barred / 30.1% ambiguous, not "18.7% clean, rest NC".

Fashionpedia ToU read at source: annotations + ontology + websites CC BY 4.0; "does not own the copyright of the images" — image use must follow Flickr/Unsplash/Burst/Freestocks/Kaboompics/Pexels terms. Any mirror (e.g. HF detection-datasets/fashionpedia) that labels the whole thing cc-by-4.0 is repeating the annotation licence over images it does not cover.

Attribute coverage measured per-mask, not assumed: sleeve part masks 59,448 with a `length` label on 56,867 = 95.7% (wrist 34,116, short 13,141, three-quarter 6,162, elbow 3,147, sleeveless 298); dress 87.7%, skirt 88.7% with hem-length values; `neckline type` on 90.7% of 34,258 neckline part masks with 25 values including plunging/sweetheart/off-the-shoulder — so "no dataset labels neckline" in commit 319b097 was wrong, depth is what is missing, not shape; silhouette tight(fit) on 34.0% of tops. Zero of 294 attributes name opacity/sheerness; zero of 46 categories are swimwear or underwear; there is no body/skin class anywhere in Fashionpedia.

Durable consequence: Fashionpedia can train sleeve, hem, neckline and cling, and cannot train clothing-versus-skin at all. Since LIP and CIHP are non-commercial, the skin class must be first-party crops on our own photographs — 81.34% of Fashionpedia's training images are not cleanly usable anyway.

Architecture: the four garment-question types collapse onto two runtime artefacts — one 3-class parse (upper garment / lower garment / skin) on the Apache-2.0 PP-HumanSegV2-Lite backbone at 160-192 px with contour-to-joint projection, plus the MobileNetV4-Conv-S garment-category head. Patch sampling at joints is not cheaper: ~20-28 patches by bisection at 2.4 ms/patch (Pixel 6, given) is 48-67 ms serial (arithmetic, not measurement) against one 15.9 ms binary-head pass (SD855, given), and patches cannot do opacity because opacity is an interior statistic and the interior is what a patch route cannot locate without a mask. No published number exists for landmark-patch clothing-vs-skin accuracy; three searches returned nothing.

Also: "Fashionpedia-Tanwar" does not exist as a dataset — 30 Fashionpedia mirrors on the HF API, none named Tanwar; "Tanwar" appears only as a cited author in the paper's references. Do not cite it. DeepFashion2 and DeepFashion-MultiModal are research-only including derived data; iMaterialist 2019's Kaggle metadata licence is literally "Subject to Competition Rules"; SHIFT15M is CC BY-NC 4.0; Fashion-IQ images come via DeepFashion so they inherit research-only; ModaNet's LICENSE is the COCO BSD-3 software licence for the annotation format, not a grant over the PaperDoll images.
