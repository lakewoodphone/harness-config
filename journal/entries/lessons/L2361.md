**L2361 · A soft loop on a pipe is a capstan, not a slider: friction scales with the load it holds, so the hold-down and the brake are the same friction**

**A soft loop on a pipe is a capstan, not a slider. Friction is proportional to the load the loop carries, so the hold-down and the brake are the same friction - and a loop only slides when it is holding nothing.**

**Measured/derived 2026-09-23**, Shlock sukkah rain cover. A 3/8 in rope loop round a 1.660 in galvanised rail,
loaded radially with P, has total normal force N = pi x P and axial drag F = mu x pi x P. At P = 94 lb: 74 lb with
mu = 0.25. Twenty points: ~1,500 lb, against a 30-50 lb hand pull.

**The correction I had to make to myself.** I told the owner to tie the loop *loose* so it would slide. Wrong: above
about 10 lb of tension the slack is used up and the loop is a snug loop however it was tied; what matters after
installation is the load-induced tension, and that is set by the wind, not by the knot. A slack rope is soft; a
taut rope is a hard line.

**The generalisation worth keeping.** Three things follow whenever a part must both hold a load and travel:
1. sliding drag scales with the load being held, so a part cannot be strong in hold-down and free in travel;
2. the friction that makes it hold is exactly the friction that stops it moving - a rolling contact is the only
 way out of the trade;
3. and a *bought* hold-down says so: the split-ring pipe hanger family is designed to grip a pipe (that is why the
 same makers sell separate "pipe stops"), so it is a clamp, not a slider, however snug its bore.

**Evidence for the whole chain:** a nine-child adversarial mesh audit on 2026-09-23; the loop claim failed B1, the
sealing claim failed B2, and the thickness claim failed B3, each independently. Also confirmed on site by the
owner: the bought 2.00 in hitching rings "just fit" the 1.660 in rail and friction-lock the moment they are pulled.
