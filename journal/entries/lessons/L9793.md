## The DeepSeek number, settled: two prices, not one — and 84 % of the work is in the expensive window (2026-10-19)

**This closes the chain L9791 → L9792.** L9791 said the ledger overstates DeepSeek by 2.9× (one card
vs another). L9792 corrected the number against the bank but blamed "missing calls". **Both
explanations were wrong, and the right one was in the same file I had already read.**

**What is measured, in three independent records**

| record | what it says |
|---|---|
| `model_usage`, 7 days | 534,474,098 input / 3,803,697 output / 4,590,592 cached tokens |
| `app/pricing.py` | a **single** card per model: either `deepseek-v4-flash` (0.44, 1.32) or `deepseek-flash` (0.15, 0.60) |
| `accounting.db` debits | $159.00 in 7 days, **$636.00 in 31 days**, $892.52 since 2026-08-18 |

**The missing third fact: DeepSeek's own peak table.** Peak is $0.30/$1.20 per million, off-peak
$0.15/$0.60, and the comment in `pricing.py` says which window is which — the US business day is
off-peak, the early morning US is peak. Price the measured tokens by the UTC hour they were actually
spent in:

```
peak (00:30–16:30 UTC) 449,213,751 in 3,057,023 out ← 84 % of all input
off-peak (16:30–00:30 UTC) 85,475,042 in 747,468 out

peak 449.21 × 0.30 + 3.06 × 1.20 = $138.43
off-peak 85.48 × 0.15 + 0.75 × 0.60 = $ 13.27
 = $151.70 / week = $650 / 30 days
```

Bank: $636 / 31 days = $143.6/week. **Agreement within 5 %.** Nothing is missing; the calls are all
recorded. The ledger is wrong only because it carries **one** price for a provider that has **two**.

**Two corrections of my own reasoning, kept rather than tidied away.**
1. I published a 2.9× overstatement after comparing two cards and believing whichever one flattered my
 arithmetic. A price list is not a bill.
2. I then read the bill, found it disagreed with both cards, and invented a cause — "calls are missing"
 — without checking whether a *third* price existed. An unexplained gap is not evidence of a hidden
 mechanism; check the domain (here: time-of-day pricing) before concluding the data is broken.

**The optimisation this exposes, which is worth more than everything in the storage audit.** 84 % of
input tokens are spent in the peak window. The same work in the off-peak window costs half: **$650/month
→ about $340/month**, at identical token volume, no model change, no capability lost. What it costs is
scheduling — the departments and the CEO kernel would have to concentrate their thinking in 16:30–00:30
UTC. That is a decision about when the company works, so it is recorded with the arithmetic and left to
the owner rather than flipped silently.

**Two side-facts measured while settling it.** The phone engine runs **11,874 assistant messages in 7
days** on the same key (14.08 M in / 10.31 M out) recorded only in its session files — ≈$16.60/week the
company ledger never sees, ≈10 % of the account. And `model_usage` becomes a budget instrument the
moment `app/pricing.py` prices **per hour against the provider's peak table**; that two-line change has
not been made.

**Applies to:** any provider whose price depends on when you call it (DeepSeek today; others later).
Price by the hour the spend happened, not by the card that is easiest to find.
