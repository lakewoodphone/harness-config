**L6254 · ROOT CAUSE: the flip-phone MMS wave is a US Mobile Warp outage, acknowledged by US Mobile on 2026-10-12 - and it refutes my own user-agent hypothesis**

**L · 2026-10-15 (night) · ZABZ-TECH · ROOT CAUSE FOUND: the flip-phone "picture messaging is dead" wave is a US Mobile (Warp/Verizon) MMS failure, acknowledged by US Mobile — and it refutes the user-agent hypothesis I had published hours earlier**

**The finding.** This is not our filter, not our rebuilt Message+ app, and not the handsets. It is a **network-side
MMS origination failure on US Mobile's Warp (Verizon) network, affecting basic flip phones**, and **US Mobile support
said so in writing on 2026-10-12**:

> *"We're aware of an issue causing MMS not to work on some basic flip phones, and our team is actively investigating
> a solution. Unfortunately, there isn't a fix available at this time…"*

The same day a shop reported US Mobile senior staff saying they *"see the problem"* and are **"working with Verizon to
fix it"**, and another user was offered a **free T-Mobile (Light Speed) SIM** for their E4610 *instead of a fix*. A
separate thread opened 2026-10-09: *"MMS stopped working, happening specifically on US Mobile Warp… mostly on basic
phones"*, attributed by a poster to *"their recent Warp 5G SA migration"*. Thread poll (169 votes): **Warp 80 broken
vs 58 working**; T-Mobile Light Speed 18 working vs 7 broken.

**Dating:** US Mobile's CEO announced ~2026-09-15 that Warp 5G Standalone was ready, with **physical-SIM rollout in
October** — and the new core explicitly covers 4G devices, so an LTE-only E4610 rides the same migration. SA observed
live ~**2026-10-08**; MMS broke **Friday 2026-10-09**. Trigger = correlation plus one forum attribution, not a vendor
statement of causation. The failure itself = documented.

**I WAS WRONG, and it is recorded.** I published the **empty MMS user agent / UAProf URL** as the surviving
explanation (L3826, §0b). It is refuted:
1. MMSC vendor docs: UAProf governs content adaptation of messages being **delivered**, not submit authentication.
2. MMSC submit auth is **APN-IP correlation / WAP-gateway header enrichment** (`X-MSISDN`); MM1 *"does not define how
 the MMSC identifies and authenticates the subscriber"* and the handset-to-MMSC connection *"contains no information
 about the MSISDN"*.
3. A **captured real Verizon transaction** sent a generic `User-Agent: Android-Mms/2.0` with a **mismatched Sony**
 `x-wap-profile` and got `HTTP/1.1 200 OK`.
4. **Decisive:** the failure is **not deterministic by model** — in the same window an E4610 that "can send and
 receive" and E4610s that cannot, plus "two LG Classics that can only send, and one E4610 that can send and
 receive". A device-keyed rule would break every E4610 identically. It does not.

The empty UA is a **real measurement that is not the driver** — kept in the record so nobody re-chases it.

**Onset correction:** the business record's earliest *sending* complaint is 2026-10-06, but the carrier outage begins
**2026-10-09**. Different faults: the 10-06 caller describes a **delay** ("takes over an hour"), which matches the
**long-standing** slow-MMS complaint documented on these devices since November 2025; the 10-09-onward cluster is a
**total** send failure. Do not merge them.

**One of my own measurements does NOT fit and is not being explained away:** the bench handset also failed to send on
a **direct Verizon SIM**, which "Warp-only" does not explain. Candidates: that line is affected too, the handset had
a wedged MMS state at the time (**10 orphaned VZWAPP connections** against a healthy 1), or the single attempt was
not clean. **First thing to re-test on the next phone.**

**What to tell customers, and what to stop doing.**
- Tell them the truth: US Mobile has acknowledged an MMS fault on flip phones and is working with Verizon.
- US Mobile's own workaround is a **T-Mobile (Light Speed) SIM** — that was offered to an E4610 owner in place of a
 fix.
- **Stop spending bench hours on this.** A handset failing today may pass tomorrow with nothing changed on it. Re-test
 rather than repair, and do not sell a repair or a re-filter as a fix.

**Transferable rules — the expensive ones from today:**
- **A carrier's own support text beats every log I can read.** Two hours of log forensics on one handset did not find
 what a forum post of US Mobile's support script found in minutes. When a fault arrives across a *population* on a
 *date*, ask the carrier first and read the forums second; do the bench work last.
- **A population-wide, dated onset is never a device property.** I had the right shape at §0b and still chased a
 device-side mechanism. Non-determinism by model was the tell: if one E4610 works and another does not on the same
 network, the fault is not in the device.
- **Never publish a cause on one observation.** I did it twice today (the `smspush` line, then the user agent). Both
 were refuted within the hour. Say "leading candidate" and name the test that would kill it.
- **Do not merge a chronic complaint with a new outage because they share symptoms.** The slow-MMS issue on these
 handsets predates October by a year; the October wave is a different fault with a different owner.
- **Keep the measurements even when the hypothesis dies.** The hold-time numbers (US Mobile ~1.05 s vs Verizon 16.7 s
 and 50 s+), the orphaned-connection counts, and the empty-UA reading are all still true and are now the baseline
 for re-testing after the carrier fixes it.
