**L1945 · A same-day order is never modified by email - the phone is the only channel that beats a shipping cutoff, and a modify request can stall the shipment instead of changing it**

Rule: never ask a retailer to modify an order that has to ship the same day. The telephone is the only channel that can beat a same-day cutoff. Email cannot, and a modify request can actively stall the delivery by routing it to a human queue.

Evidence, 2026-09-17. B&H order 1132651452, 1 x Samsung 870 EVO 2TB, $533.11, quoted "Get it by Fri, Sep 18" with expedited delivery on a free-next-day promo whose stated cutoff is 6pm ET. I recommended the order and the owner placed it. The owner then sent a modify-order email and B&H auto-replied: "We have received your email and we will respond to you as soon as possible, typically within 1-2 business days. Your Reference Number is: 111265206." A 1-2 business day SLA against a 6pm same-day cutoff means the email cannot possibly arrive in time - and the order is now held in B&H's review queue. It was on track for Friday; after the email its date is unknown. B&H is also closed Sun Sep 20 and Mon Sep 21 for Yom Kippur.

Second-order rule, from the same session: never ask a retailer to ADD to an in-flight order. Adding requires a human and a human takes a queue turn, which is precisely how this order ended up stalled. A second order placed separately ships on the same next-day terms with no human in the path.

I contributed to this by answering the modify question ("call 800-606-6969") only AFTER the owner had already sent the email and reported back. The instruction to call should have been given the moment the owner's request to modify touched an order that had to ship today - and once he sends an email it is too late for the answer to be "call".

How to apply:
1. Before recommending any expedited purchase, check the vendor's order-cutoff time AND their support SLA, and say both out loud. If the cutoff is same-day, state in the same breath that any change must go through the phone line.
2. When a request involves changing a time-critical order, lead with the channel and the hours, not with the analysis. "Call now, here is the number" is the whole answer when a cutoff is hours away.
3. Never have a retailer modify an order that must ship today - cancel-and-rebuy or place a second order.
4. A hold is not a cancellation. Get the actual state (released / cancelled / shipped) confirmed in writing before building a plan on either outcome.
5. Price the fallback BEFORE it is needed, and record it next to the order: here it is Best Buy, sold by Best Buy, same model, same $499.99, next-day.
