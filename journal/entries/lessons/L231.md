**L231 · 2026-09-14 - The startup spike is stop-timeout kills and deploys, not a crash loop**

**Closing the `startup` flag from P74: 70 start-rows in two days is unclean shutdowns plus my own deploys, not a crash loop**

I flagged `startup` = 70 in under two days (~one per 41 minutes) as "either a crash loop or a watchdog
fighting something", and said it was cheap to check. It is checked, and the answer is neither:

- `systemctl show secretary-api.service -p NRestarts` → **`NRestarts=0`** since the current start. There
 is no restart storm right now.
- The journal does show the failure mode: **`secratary systemd[1]: secretary-api.service: Failed with
 result 'timeout'`** (Sep 12 18:05, Sep 14 04:30) and `Scheduled restart job, restart counter is at 1`
 (06:01, 06:03). The unit's `TimeoutStopUSec` is 90s, so this is the service failing to shut down
 cleanly inside its own stop timeout and being killed — not crashing under load.
- Roughly eight of the starts are my own deploys this session, which is the L229 effect at scale.
- `MemoryPeak` 2.86 GB against 22 GB of RAM: the process is large but headroom is not the constraint.

**So the honest entry is: ~8 deploy restarts, a handful of stop-timeout kills, and the rest unexplained
by this check** — which is a weaker statement than "crash loop" and a stronger one than "normal". If a
future session wants it settled, count `ExecMainStartTimestamp` changes over the window rather than
`activity_log` rows, because the log line records a startup the service may have initiated for reasons
other than a restart.

**The methodological half, since it is the same shape as everything else this session.** My flag was a
number plus two guesses about its cause. The number was real; both guesses were wrong; and the
correction took one command. Raising a suspect number and *not* spending the one command is how a real
signal turns into an unfounded worry that a later session has to re-investigate — which is exactly the
cost this journal exists to remove.
