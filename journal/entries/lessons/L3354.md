## "Unanswered" is only true as of the moment you read it — a same-session read goes stale in hours

**Measured 2026-10-07/08, Golembick, 300DDR board. I was wrong and the owner caught it in one line:
*"i think she was responded to already, did you sync with dialpad?"***

**What happened.** The 300DDR sweep read the Golembick SMS thread at ~**13:00Z**. At that moment the last message
was genuine: her 00:11Z question, with only the after-hours autoresponder after it. **A reply then went out at
17:04:43Z** — Dialpad `5426561577951232`, from `+17326552355`, answering her money question in full. **Four hours
after my read, on a channel that is live and moving all day.** I did not re-read. I asserted *"her question is
unanswered"* again in a later turn, wrote it into the 10-07 board row **and** into a §3 list of "everything
waiting on a human", and built a draft text for it. **The 73896 case file already said ANSWERED** — the tell that
I was working from a stale read was that the case file disagreed with me and I did not notice.

**Why this is a distinct failure from L3412** (stale git clone). L3412 is *stale storage*. This is **stale
observation of a live channel**. Both produce confident wrong numbers about current state; only the second happens
purely because time passed *during* the session, with nothing on disk changing to warn you.

**The rule.** An absence claim — *unanswered, not paid, never contacted, nothing received* — **decays from the
instant you read it**, and it decays fastest exactly where it matters most: a live SMS or call channel during
business hours. So:
- **Re-read the channel in the same step you assert the absence**, never reuse a read from earlier in the session,
 no matter how much work has happened in between. The cost is one cache query; the cost of being wrong is a
 customer told twice, or a colleague chasing a reply that already exists.
- **State the read time with the claim.** *"Unanswered as of 13:00Z"* is true and useful; *"unanswered"* is a
 claim about now that you are not entitled to make.
- **When a case file disagrees with your conclusion, believe the disagreement and go look.** A second source
 contradicting you is the cheapest correction signal in the system and I walked past it.

**Method that would have caught it, and did catch it once asked:** read `dialpad_sms_cache` freshly filtered to
the number, **and** cross-check the websocket journal —
`journalctl -u dialpad-sms-ws --since <date> | grep <phone>` — which logs a `STORED` line per emitted message. The
journal showed the 17:04Z outbound plainly. **Two witnesses, one of them independent of our own table.**
