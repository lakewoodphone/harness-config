**L12171 · A business-day window copied from a handoff was two days wrong; recompute windows, never restate them**

**The "ship in 1-3 business days" window was never arithmetic — I restated it from a handoff instead of recomputing it, and it was two business days wrong.**

Measured 2026-10-21 on ZABZ-YOGA with Get-Date: 2026-10-14 was a **Wednesday**. The business days after it are Thu 15 (1), Fri 16 (2), Mon 19 (3). So Sticky Brand's ship-by date expired **Monday 2026-10-19**. Today, Wed 2026-10-21, is business day **5** — two business days past their promise.

H12024 records "ship in 1-3 business days = by ~2026-10-21. Today it is due, not yet overdue." That sentence is wrong by two business days, and I repeated it to the owner verbatim tonight without checking it. He caught it by asking "why is today one to three business days after the 14th". The correction is the number: **overdue since 19 October**, not due today.

Rule this earns: any "N business days from <date>" claim carries the weekday of <date> and the enumerated dates, or it is not a claim. When a handoff states a window, recompute it — a handoff is a claim about the day it was written, never a fact about tonight. This is the same failure as trusting any other unverified field.

Consequence for the work, not just the record: the posture changes from "wait for the parcel" to "the vendor is two business days late and has not answered the 2026-10-20 chase" (Gmail id 1a15c265a63ef409 to hello@thestickybrand.com). Under D9026 the levers are a harder push or a local short run for the first units — not a re-order of 300.
