**L7103 · A percentage disk alarm cannot see a step change - a 28.7 GB write went from 76.7 percent to 100 percent between two samples - and the pre-write guard that could have caught it was not called**

Measured 2026-10-16 on secratary-cloud. /var/log/disk-guard.log samples the disk against [warn 90% crit 95%] and its last line before the incident reads 'ok - / at 76.7% (28.74 GB free), /tmp at 55.0%, 0 backup dir(s)'. Then a 28.72 GB VACUUM INTO of the 28.7 GB authority db landed in /root/.cfo/backups at 04:45:48, and a 28.7 GB file is NINETEEN PERCENTAGE POINTS on a 150 G disk: the disk went from 76.7 percent ok to 100 percent between two samples and never passed through warn. So a threshold alarm cannot see a step change larger than the gap between its samples, and no amount of tuning that threshold fixes it - the sample interval times the write rate is the blind window, and one atomic 29 GB write is bigger than the whole window. THE ESTATE ALREADY HAS THE RIGHT TOOL and it was not called: /root/bin/space-guard.sh takes the bytes you are about to write and refuses with exit 3 if the result would fall below an 8 GiB floor; its own header records that this exact catastrophe happened on 2026-10-14 when a 27.5 GB VACUUM INTO spent a disk doubling in one day, and the sources README says it 'should be called by anything that writes more than'. Measured on this write, right now, it refuses: avail=1546 MiB need=27656 MiB after=-26110 MiB floor=8192 MiB -> REFUSED, exit 3. THE CONSEQUENCE: the ledger pre-write backup guard (3x the ledger, 2.0 GB floor) now refuses too, so cfo-resolve.py --apply-rules exits rc=1 and THE LOOP CANNOT WRITE ANY CATEGORY AT ALL until space is freed. What I freed without destroying anything: 19 of my own session backups gzipped in place (473 MB -> 112 MB), every one verified with gunzip -t, plus the systemd journal vacuumed and package caches cleared - 542 MiB total, taking free from 1.0 GB to 1.6 GB, still short of the 2.0 GB the writer needs. THE LESSON: for any single write that can be a meaningful fraction of a filesystem, the only correct check is a PRE-WRITE check on the size of the write, not an alarm on the state of the disk - and when a guard exists, its call site is the thing to verify, because an uncalled guard and no guard look identical from the outside.
