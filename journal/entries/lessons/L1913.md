**L1913 · Price a vendor's failure case, not its success case: quote the vendor's own sentence about no-data charges before any margin arithmetic**

Rule: before doing money arithmetic on a vendor's charges, quote the vendor's own charge structure from the vendor's own words - and state which part is outcome-independent.

Evidence, 2026-09-17. I presented the owner with a per-drive loss model for the Fishman data-recovery case built on the assumption that 300DDR bills its full flat rate ($500 on the 2TB, $300 on the 1TB) when a drive yields no data. That produced "-$405 / -$205 on a failure, a recurring loss on ~36% of jobs". The owner corrected it in one sentence: the flat rate is payable only on success; on a no-data drive 300DDR charges nothing except a $100 non-refundable open-cover fee if that drive's metal cover was opened, plus non-refundable shipping. The corrected position on a failed drive is +$135 (cover not opened) or +$35 (cover opened) - profitable, not a loss.

Worse than the arithmetic: the truth was already written in the case file I had just read. The 2026-09-03 chances-form entries state the no-data-no-charge terms in full. I read the file, then repeated a model that contradicted it. Reading a source is not the same as extracting from it the one line that governs the question being asked.

How to apply:
1. For any vendor fee, find the vendor's own sentence about what is charged WHEN THE OUTCOME IS FAILURE. Success pricing is usually the loud number and the failure number is the one that decides the deal.
2. Separate the two kinds of cost explicitly when presenting money: outcome-dependent (paid only on success) and outcome-independent (bought/consumed regardless). Only the second kind is real exposure and only it justifies a deposit.
3. In this business the outcome-independent exposures are: a $100 non-refundable open-cover fee (only if the HDA cover was opened), non-refundable shipping, and a donor part on a head swap (average ~$195 / ~$168 on our two current drives) - the donor is bought and consumed by the attempt whether or not data comes back.
4. Once the failure case is understood, the deposit's job is to cover the outcome-INDEPENDENT cost only. A deposit sized against the success fee is sized against the wrong number.
5. Do not present a corrected number by re-deriving it yourself a second time. Retract in the record, cite the owner's sentence verbatim, and mark the old table DO NOT USE - a corrected table next to an uncorrected one is how the wrong number gets reused.

Related: L1606 (never let a hardcoded/stale assumption drive a live number), and the Fishman case file section "RETRACTED 2026-09-17 by the owner".
