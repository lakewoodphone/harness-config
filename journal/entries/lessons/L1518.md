**L1518 · iPad soldered charge-port flex: pin count, difficulty, killer mistakes**

Follow-up to L1517 (A1893 charge port, no solder-free part). Numbers and difficulty, sourced 2026.

PIN COUNT. Two different counts matter and they are not the same thing. (a) The Lightning connector itself has 8 contacts. (b) The flex-to-logic-board interface is a staggered multi-pad comb, not a 2-4 wire splice - repair.wiki's iPad 6 pin map (page now offline, read from search cache) lists 14 pins in diode mode for iPad 6, with GND at pins 1, 5 and 14. Pad count rises across generations: an iPad 8 attempt reportedly ripped 22 pads (Jesse Cruz/VCC Board Repairs, Facebook). Practical rule: you solder every pad in the comb, so treat it as a 12-16 joint array on iPad 5/6.

DIFFICULTY. Conceptually easy, execion-wise unforgiving. Rate: moderate (3/5) for someone with hot-air + iron experience, not-a-beginner (5/5) otherwise. Authoritative walkthrough for this exact design family (iPad 5/6/7/8/9, Air 1-5, Pro 9.7/10.5/12.9 g1-g2) is VCC Board Repairs 'The Right Way to Repair an iPad Charging Port' (youtube xn02Glir1pw, Feb 2026) - the same design as A1893.

What makes it hard, in order:
1. The flex is GLUED to the board as well as soldered. You fight glue while the solder is molten. Most of the bench time is glue, not solder.
2. There are COMPONENTS UNDER THE STICKER that covers the solder joints. Too much heat = dead components. This is the board-killer mistake.
3. The flex has alignment WINDOWS (small squares). Solder blobbed into a window destroys alignment. Use a polarizing light, not bare eyes.
4. Kapton/glue difference: the first portion of the flex is glue only, no solder - snapping it frees the flex; snapping too far rips pads.
5. Ripped pads turn a 1-hour job into a microscope + jumper job. Seen constantly in the trade.
6. Root cause is often LOOSE BRACKET/PORT SCREWS letting the port wiggle - retighten or the replacement fails the same way.

PROVEN TECHNIQUE (VCC): flood the joints with 63/37 leaded 183C solder first, hot air 380C / 50% airflow plus iron knife tip, wiggle until it releases with no force, never low-melt (weak joint, extra wick step). Clean, tack the new flex, then solder in sections with a fat blob and thick flux, hold ~5s per section. Verify with a USB meter AND a TriStar/tail tester BEFORE reassembly - a partial joint reads fine visually and fails electrically.

TIME: ~81 min teardown-to-reassembly first time (LML Repair 22-step guide); the solder step itself ~10 min when it goes well. Board must come out, display adhesive comes off first.

DIAGNOSE FIRST. Most 'bad port' iPads that come in are not bad ports - rule out a no-power board fault (Tristar/PMIC) before quoting, or you sell a port swap that fixes nothing. If the shell is only bent and pins/pad are intact, straighten and retest, no board work.

BUSINESS ANGLE: part is ~\ (MobileSentrix), so the entire price is bench labour. VCC Board Repairs (Las Vegas, mail-in) states B2B pricing for repair shops - the outsource path if the shop does not want board work in-house.
