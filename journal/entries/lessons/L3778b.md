**L3778b · The stale-database failure recurred on the owner text line, live and unnoticed**

**Measured 2026-10-13 on secratary-cloud.**

`/root/bin/textsend.py::app_db_path()` returned
`/root/personal-secretary-mvp/data/secretary.db` - a 1.8 MB copy holding **0 `sms_log` rows and 1
`owner_text_turn` row** - while the live app (systemd `company-api`) writes **`/root/authority.db`**
(4,103 `sms_log` rows, 92 turns, 26 GB). Cron sets no `DATABASE_URL`, so the CLI resolved its
fallback constant.

The consequence is the shape, not the size: the 20-minute rate floor, the one-at-a-time turn check,
the quota and the `sms_log` flood brake inside that sender were all enforcing themselves against an
**empty file**. The rules written to protect the owner had never once seen his traffic. This is the
same failure this estate named as its worst: a stale copy of a database, read and believed.

It was invisible because *both* states look identical from the outside. A sender whose policy store
is empty and a sender with nothing to do print the same thing.

**The durable fix is not the path - it is the check.** `textsend.py doctor` now prints both
resolutions, whether they are the same file, and both row counts, and exits non-zero on divergence;
`AITEXT_APP_DB_ASSERT=1` raises at import.

**Generalise:** when two components on one channel each own a copy of the state, prove they resolve
the SAME store with a command, not with a code review. Import the other component's resolver rather
than re-implementing its precedence.
