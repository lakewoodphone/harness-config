# `READY_FOR_PICKUP` is an ASSERTION that a device is paid for, not evidence that it was

Measured 2026-10-14 on the authority, reading the note the rule was derived from.

## The false rule that got into the record

Journal `H3664` (2026-10-14, written by a concurrent session) states, as a shop rule it had discovered:

> a finished order sitting in `READY_FOR_PICKUP` with no payment row is to be treated as collected, and
> note 2885 puts it in production's own words ("which in this shop means paid and on the shelf")

The quoted phrase is real. **The inference drawn from it is backwards, and the note's own ACTION
disproves it.** Note 2885, verbatim and in full:

> Record correction 2026-10-09: the device was released to the customer's husband late Friday
> 2026-03-29 with the balance unpaid - the owner's own closeout note on this order says so ("balance
> remains unpaid"), and the payment rows show 650.00 quoted and nothing collected. **The order had been
> left in READY_FOR_PICKUP, which in this shop means paid and on the shelf. Status moved to
> AWAITING_PAYMENT so the receivable stays visible.** An Amazon USB was also handed to the customer and
> still needs billing.

Read the two halves together. The note states the convention as the *reason the row was wrong* — the
order looked paid, it was not, so it was **moved out** of `READY_FOR_PICKUP` into `AWAITING_PAYMENT` "so
the receivable stays visible". The session read the convention as licensing the opposite conclusion —
that the state implies payment, therefore treat it as paid and stop.

## The correct rule

**`READY_FOR_PICKUP` is a claim about the world: a device paid for, sitting on the shelf. It is an
assertion, not a reading.** It follows that:

1. The state is **not evidence** that money was taken, and it never was. The owner's ruling of
 2026-10-14 (see `D3151`) — that old money which cannot be established is recorded as UNVERIFIED,
 neither owed nor settled, and not chased — stands on its own reasons and does not need this fiction
 to support it. Not chasing and "was paid" are different statements, and conflating them is the thing
 that hides money.
2. Where the record **contradicts** the state — a note or status row showing a balance unpaid — the
 correct action is the shop's own: **move the order to `AWAITING_PAYMENT` so the receivable stays
 visible.** That is what note 2885 did, on a 650 receivable.

## Why the difference is not academic

If an unpaid order may rest at `READY_FOR_PICKUP`, then **the state which means "paid and on the
shelf" is also the state uncollected money hides behind**, and no reconciliation that trusts status can
find it. Measured 2026-10-14: **5 orders at `READY_FOR_PICKUP` carry a note indicating an unpaid
balance.** Three are settled or dispositioned (one is this very order 430, one has an owner decision not
to pursue, one was resolved from a Stripe record). **Two are genuinely uncollected and sit at
`READY_FOR_PICKUP` today** — `WO2608060002` (a BlackBerry KEY2, screen fitted, "did not pay waiting
for pickup", roughly 160) and `WO2603290007` (a Kyocera, workbook "did not pay", 105).

Both are already documented at length by other sessions, so no further note was added — but neither has
been moved out of the paid-looking state, and `WO2608060002`'s device has been on the shelf since
2026-08-21.

## The transferable rule

**A convention tells you what a state MEANS; it is not a reading of what HAPPENED.** When a record
states its own convention, read what the record then *did* with it — the action is the evidence, and
here it pointed the opposite way from the inference that was drawn. A rule inferred from a phrase, with
the phrase's own example contradicting it, is the exact shape of the `wrong-data` failure this estate
counts as its most common.
