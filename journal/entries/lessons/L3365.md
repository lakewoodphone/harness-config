**L3365 · Gating the transport is not gating the channel: TwiML is a second delivery path, and the record of a reply is a third place to be wrong**

GOAL ROUND 11. I fired a REAL signed inbound SMS at a booted survivor (signed with the app's own twilio.request_validator, from a reserved +1555 number Twilio cannot deliver to, with SURVIVOR_MODE and MOCK_OUTBOUND_COMMS as belts) and it took THREE runs to get right, each run finding a hole the previous one could not see. RUN 1: the survivor ANSWERED the customer - '<Message>I still have your message and I'm routing it now...</Message>'. TwiML messages are delivered by TWILIO as the response to our webhook, so they never pass through app/sms_service.send_message and my survivor gate never saw them. My 'no autonomous sending' claim had a customer-facing hole, found only by firing a real message. RUN 2: after gating the response body at every TwiML return (14 sites, one helper), the reply was correctly suppressed - but sms_log grew TWO rows, one inbound/received and one OUTBOUND/SENT for the reply that had just been suppressed. Because the reply BRANCHES persist their outbound row before handing the text to TwiML, so gating delivery left a log that claimed a customer had been answered. That lie is not cosmetic: a returning primary reading its own sms_log would conclude the customer was already answered and never reply - a customer question silently dropped by a log entry. RUN 3: after moving the gate to the top of the inbound handler (after the inbound row is written, before any reply branch), sms_log gained EXACTLY ONE row, direction=inbound status=received, and the response was the empty <Response></Response>. RULES: (a) gating a TRANSPORT is not gating a CHANNEL - enumerate every path by which a message can leave, including ones the platform delivers for you (TwiML, webhook responses, provider-side replies); (b) gate the RECORD as well as the ACTION, because a log that claims an action happened is what a future agent will reason from; (c) the ordered 'before, after, after' comparison - same input, read what actually changed - is how you find the second and third defect, and stopping at the first success would have shipped a lie.
