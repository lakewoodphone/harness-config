**L6128 · Set B needs no fix: 73 are the counted half of correct pairs and the other 27 are refunds**

2026-10-13. Set B needs no fix. The two fields disagree, the flow is right, and last round's
"improvement" was coincidence rather than correctness. Recording the correction because I nearly wrote
to the ledger on the strength of it.

WHAT THE 100 ROWS ACTUALLY ARE. For each set-B row I looked for its twin on the same account at the
same date, amount and direction, and then asked the one question that matters: does that twin carry a
pointer?
 twin HAS a pointer 73 -> the twin is excluded, this row is counted, and the pair contributes
 exactly ONE movement. That is CORRECT.
 twin also unmarked 0 -> no pair is counted twice.
 twin is live 0 -> no row is being counted alongside a genuine second event.
 NO twin at all 27 -> these are the only candidates for a real defect, and they are not one.
So 73 of the 100 are not an error at all; they are the counted half of a correctly-marked pair. My
earlier framing - "76 rows the flow wrongly counts" - was wrong about 73 of them.

AND THE 27 ARE REFUNDS. Listing them settles it: 21 of 27 sit on `personal-amex-94045` and every one is
a CREDIT at a retailer - Amazon Marketplace, Cafotteria, Twilio, Dave & Buster, Lowe's, Kava Coffee,
Swifty Gas, Glatt Bite, 1715 Madison Ave - dated April and May 2026; the rest are Zelle lines on
unmapped rows. A credit at a retailer is a REFUND. There is no twin because a refund's original charge
is on a different date, which a same-date key cannot match. On a credit card a credit reduces the net
charges, so COUNTING them is correct - and the tool counts them because they carry no pointer. The
status is the field that is wrong for these 27, not the pointer, and the consequence of that wrongness
is exactly nothing.

SO LAST ROUND'S TEST WAS MEASURING THE WRONG THING. Excluding set B moved 7 disagreements closer to zero
and 3 further away, netting 3,215.59 - and I read that as evidence the status was right. It is not. The
test removed 13,403.30 of movement, which included 73 correctly-counted rows and 24 correctly-counted
refunds, and seven deltas happened to land nearer zero. **A number moving in the direction you hoped is
not the same as a number moving for the reason you assumed**, and the tell was there: three cases got
worse, which a correct rule should not do. I should have stopped at the counterexample rather than
averaging it away against seven agreements.

THE DECISION IS THEREFORE NOT TO WRITE. I had argued myself - on 7-against-3 and 3,215.59 net - towards
setting the pointer on 76 rows, and had held back only because the signature was not clean. The
signature was not clean because there was nothing there. Nothing in set B changes the flow, so there is
no correction to make, and the two fields disagreeing is a cosmetic inconsistency in a table rather
than money in the reconciliation.
