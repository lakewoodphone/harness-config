**L1515 · Do not put an unverified 'what it's worth used' number in a repair-vs-replace recommendation**

Correction to D153, same day. That entry said a working used ThinkPad X201 "is worth about $50-100".
I had not verified that, and it does not match what is checkable from this machine.

Verified instead: a complete RENEWED ThinkPad X201 (i5-540M, Win 10, 90-day warranty) retails at
$197.99 USD at printerready.com (page fetched 2026-09-15). Live eBay prices could not be read: eBay
search returns 403 to this fetcher (see P147). Groupon/price.com.hk/UK listings that surfaced in search
were either old (2015) or non-US, so they are not usable comparison points.

Effect on the case: the argument is the same but the numbers are starker - the $155-160 panel job costs
about what a whole renewed replacement laptop does, and a ~$105-120 cable job buys most of one. The
$50-100 figure should not be repeated in a customer conversation.

Rule this proves: when a repair-vs-replace comparison goes into a customer-facing recommendation, the
replacement price must be a fetched page with a URL, not an impression of what old laptops "go for".
