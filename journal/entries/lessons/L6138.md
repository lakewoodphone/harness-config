**L6138 · I broke the one-question-per-subject rule twice; the tax tool now enforces it**

2026-10-13. I broke the queue's one-question-per-subject rule twice in one hour, caught both, and the
tool now enforces it.

THE RULE I TRIPPED OVER, which the estate had already written down. `owner-queue-sweep.py` exists
because two one-shot sweeps put 115 raw per-item rows into a queue whose only currency is the owner's
attention, and the genuinely-his head of that queue then waited 10.6 days behind the pile. Its rule:
"at most one question per subject, and the question text IS the rule". I filed tax rows without knowing
it, then read it.

THE TWO BREAKS, both mine, both found by measuring instead of trusting:
 1. #476 (hand) and #479 (tool) were the SAME Q3 2026 estimated tax obligation. The calendar lists it
 under two names - "Federal estimated tax (1040-ES)" and "Q3 Estimated Tax Due" - and the first
 version of the tool keyed each question on the raw NAME. -> #479 resolved as a duplicate.
 2. #475 (hand) and #481 (tool) were the SAME 2025 return. The tool's dedup can only match keys IT
 wrote, and the hand row never carried one. -> #481 resolved as a duplicate.

WHAT THE TOOL DOES NOW, and it is stricter than either fix:
 * a `subject()` normaliser, an alias table plus parenthesis stripping, so "Federal estimated tax
 (1040-ES)" and "Q3 Estimated Tax Due" are ONE subject and "NJ-927 + WR-30 (payroll)" and
 "NJ-927 Q3 2026 Due (employer quarterly)" are ONE subject;
 * grouping by SUBJECT and YEAR, not by deadline, so one form is one question and its deadlines are
 enumerated inside it: "NJ ST-50 sales tax: 2026-07-20 (85 days PAST); 2026-10-20 (in 7 days). Are
 these filed, and if not who is doing it?";
 * key `taxdl:<subject>:<year>`, so a form's next year IS a new question and a form's second quarter
 this year is not;
 * FILED counted from the WRITER'S OWN OUTPUT, never assumed.
Measured: 10 obligations down to 5 questions. First apply `FILED 2, refused-by-queue 2, already-pending
1`; second apply `FILED 0, refused-by-queue 2, already-pending 3`, i.e. idempotent.

THE LESSON WORTH KEEPING, because it will recur. A dedup key is a CONTRACT between writers, and a
human filing a row by hand signs nothing. **When filing by hand for something a tool also files, put
that tool's dedup key in the context** - it is the only thing the tool can match on, and without it the
queue grows a duplicate that looks like diligence. Both duplicates here were manufactured by acting
correctly in one place and not knowing the contract in the other.

QUEUE STATE: 157 pending, five tax rows, and #475 remains the one that matters - the 2025 return,
critical, two days out.
