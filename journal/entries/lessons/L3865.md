**L3865 · Correcting H3659: the E4610s were not refused registration, the bench position was too weak; a dirty SIM tray fakes a dead radio**

Correction to H3659, same evening. That entry said both E4610s were "refused registration, so the line or the IMEI binding is the cause". That was wrong, and the owner's next move falsified it in ten minutes: he carried all five upstairs and every handset registered.

Measured after the move, 2026-10-19 evening:
- 615373543, 615963123, 616202539 (E4610, Warp/Verizon, PLMN 311480): `mServiceState=0 0 ... IN_SERVICE`, `gsm.network.type=LTE`, operator "US Mobile".
- 558800328253 (E4810, Dark Star/AT&T, PLMN 310280): IN_SERVICE on LTE.
- One handset held service only after its SIM card and tray were cleaned - a real fault class that looks exactly like a dead radio (card reads `READY`, PLMN known, no registration).

The signal numbers at the desk the same evening, now that they attach: the three E4610s sit at `rsrp -108 dBm`, `rsrq -14 to -19`, `rssnr -132 to -28` - attached to a link with SINR at or below zero. The E4810 on AT&T sits at `rsrp -94`, `rsrq -11`, `rssnr 46`.

LESSON: "registration refused" and "too weak to finish registering" print the same words. I read `Searching for Svc.` plus `rsrp -94` and concluded the network was rejecting the device. The only way to tell them apart is the same handset measured in two locations - which is exactly why `lpt rf-watch` was built an hour earlier and then not run. Run the instrument before drawing the conclusion.

SECOND LESSON: a dirty SIM tray presents identically to an RF fault. Clean the contacts and re-read before writing a phone off.

Also fixed in the code: `PLMN_NAMES` now includes 310280 (AT&T / US Mobile "Dark Star"), so the telephony check names the carrier instead of printing a bare number.
