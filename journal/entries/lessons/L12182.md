**L12182 · The loop built 1096 better than my subagent: check its last two hours, and never wire two scales together**

Third duplication in one night, and the first one where my version was **worse than the one already merged**.

## What happened

I briefed a subagent on ledger 1096 ("the device ladder has no level to run on"). While it worked, the
wake loop built and merged the same item through `feat/parent-approval-1068` (PR #120). The merge
conflict on the three `classify` call sites is what revealed it: mine said
`ladderLevel = RuleRepository.ladderLevel(ctx)`, theirs said `ladderLevel = HouseholdLadderLevel.current(ctx)`.

> **Lesson 1: the loop works the same backlog from the same register. Before briefing a subagent on an
> item, check what the loop has pushed in the last two hours.**
> `git log origin/main --since=2.hours --oneline`, then `harness-config/scripts/unmerged-branches.ps1`,
> then `work.py show <id>` and read its **attempts** — not just its state. A claim by me (attempt 2116
> at 01:05Z) did **not** stop the loop finishing the item at 03:14Z. The claim prevents *claiming*, not
> *work already in flight*.

> **Lesson 2, and this one is about design: two scales are not one scale.** The filter level is 1..3
> (a knob bundle). The ladder is 1..8 rungs of attribute rules, with 9 and 10 retired by the owner. My
> child fed `modesty_level` straight into `ladderLevel` — arithmetically harmless (1..3 is inside 1..8)
> and semantically wrong, because it lets one control answer two questions. The loop's
> `HouseholdLadderLevel.kt` docstring says exactly this: *"Feeding `modesty_level` in here would change
> what a child can see while looking like plumbing."* It reads a **separate** `ladder_level` key that the
> broker writes, and the server side (`owner_level.py:98,157,174,354-355`) exists on main to feed it.
> I checked which side had the server half — `git grep ladder_level origin/main -- server` returned the
> loop's writer and `git grep ladder_level agent/kf-level-wire -- server` returned **nothing** — and that
> settled it. **A wiring claim is worthless without its other end; check both ends before choosing.**

## What I did with it

Aborted the integration, kept my branch unpushed, and closed the ledger item **against the loop's
evidence** rather than mine: `test_ladder_level.py` 22 passed, `HouseholdLadderLevelTest` BUILD
SUCCESSFUL, and five call sites confirmed — the loop wired **five** where my brief named three
(`NsfwPrefilter:55` and `MitmWsBridgeServer:383` were both missed by me).

## The pattern, stated plainly

Tonight the loop finished, ahead of me and correctly: the level-preset trunk (7 ledger items), the
on-device modesty meter (220 cases), the parent approval gate (1068), and the ladder level (1096). Twice
I filed items that duplicated its work (1072/927, 1073/928). Once I built a duplicate that was also
wrong (this). **My value is not in racing it on implementation. It is in integration, verification,
the dedup nobody owns (D9311: ladder staging vs meter policy), and the owner-facing gaps.** A subagent
brief must therefore start with "prove the loop has not already done this" and end with a claim to the
ledger *plus* a check of the loop's recent pushes.
