**L11518 · L11457 · Strange problems get a register, not a diagnosis — and a counter-observation is half the entry**

**Owner's correction, 2026-10-20: *"the core problem here is not that … that you knew already. The core
problem here is strange problems … I need this documented all the information wise."***

He is separating two categories that I had merged. The first — a command that says `ok:true` when it
only queued — is a *known* failure mode; documenting it again is restating what he already knows. The
second is the **strange**: an observation that contradicts what the system should do and has no
mechanism yet. A phone whose service returns by itself after a profile is removed. A device that reads
as 23 to the person holding it and 25 in the registry. A single name refused 8,539 times in 22 hours by
nobody in particular.

The strange ones are expensive not because they are hard but because they are **re-narrated from
scratch every few weeks**, each telling slightly different, and nothing accumulates. They also attract
the worst behaviour available to an agent: a confident cause invented to close the loop.

RULES:
1. **A strange observation is a row in a register, not a paragraph in a handoff.** Standing file:
 `waze-fleet/70-evidence/ANOMALY-REGISTER.md`. One entry per anomaly, with the observation, its
 evidence, the candidate explanations, what has been RULED OUT (with the evidence that ruled it out),
 and the one test that would settle it.
2. **Never delete an entry when it stops being a mystery.** Mark it explained, with the date and the
 mechanism. The explanation is the asset; the mystery was the index to it.
3. **Record the counter-observation at the same time as the observation.** "36 recovered when unlocked"
 is worth little without "25 did not, and was never unlocked" — that pairing is what turns a story
 into a comparison, and it is the thing a retelling drops.
4. **Write the confounders down before the hypothesis.** The strongest one today (removing a profile
 rebuilds the iOS network stack, which re-registers an eSIM) would have saved an hour of theorising
 had it been listed first.
5. When he says "document this fully", he means **the information**, not a verdict. A document that
 ends in a conclusion he did not ask for is worth less than one that ends in the list of what is
 still unknown.
