**L1640 · Every alarm needs a stated escape condition: severity must follow freshness, not a count**

**Every alarm needs a stated escape condition. Severity must follow freshness, not a count.**

Measured 2026-09-15. `check_attention_debt` went CRITICAL whenever *any* critical-urgency row was
held, so two Google security alerts from six days earlier held the owner's whole badge red --
indefinitely, since nothing in the rule could ever turn it green until the rows aged out. That is
the same failure the shelf-life work had just repaired one layer down: an alarm that can never go
green is an alarm that gets ignored, and the reader loses the ability to tell "act now" from
"this has been sitting there".

The rule adopted: **CRITICAL means a critical-severity item was *observed* inside 48 hours**,
using the shelf life's own clock (`COALESCE(last_seen_at, created_at)`, the last time the
condition was seen), and anything older is HIGH *and states its age*. After the change the badge
reads "2 critical message(s) held, newest 5.9d old" -- a sentence a reader can act on -- instead of
a red light with no date on it.

Generalisation worth keeping: when you write a severity rule, write down what would make it go
green, and test that case. If the answer is "nothing, until the rows are deleted", the rule is a
countdown to numbness. The question count got the same treatment: only questions young enough to
still be answerable count as debt (`QUESTION_DEBT_HOURS`), and the rest are reported as a note.
