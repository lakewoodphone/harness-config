**L1494 · Measure the payload of your own tools: my note about ps_health was 4x out of date**

Measure the payload of your own tools; my note about it was 4x out of date

My own lesson from an earlier session said "ps_health returns 90 KB and poisons context". When I
finally measured it today it was 385,651 bytes -- 4.3x larger -- because a rolling log grows
quietly: each subsystem appends a run record and keeps 50, and nothing about the code changed.
The note was true when written and had rotted into being wrong, which is worse than absent
because it looks like knowledge.

HELD RULE: a recorded measurement about a growing structure needs a date and a re-measure, or it
is a claim about the past wearing the present tense. The 90 KB figure was cited in this journal
as a reason to avoid a tool; it stayed roughly right in advice and was badly wrong in
magnitude.

And the shape to look for: when a payload is unexpectedly large, break it down BY KEY before
optimising anything. Here it took three measurements -- total, then top-level keys, then inside
`health` -- to find that 295 KB of 345 KB was a single field (`recent_runs`) inside a nested
`subsystems` map. The fix was then one default and one constant.
