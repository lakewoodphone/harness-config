**L3653 · There is no back-link: 74 per cent of orders have no total, and name matching invents identities**

2026-10-13. I said I would produce a back-link list for the 268 unlinked payments. I built it, and
it does not work. Recording that clearly, because the honest result is that these payments cannot be
attributed retrospectively and the only fix is forward-looking.

ATTEMPT 1 - MATCH PAYMENT TO ORDER by customer name plus amount. Result: **1 of 268.**
 THE REASON, and it is not a bug in the matcher: only **170 of 646 orders carry a positive total**
 - 476 of them, **74 per cent**, have a null or zero `total`. So for three quarters of the orders
 there is no quoted amount to compare a payment against at all. An order with no total cannot be
 matched to any payment by amount, and the shop's own convention is that `orders.total` IS the
 quote. The missing totals are the blocker, not the matching.

ATTEMPT 2 - MATCH PAYMENT TO CUSTOMER by name. Result: **64 "matches", and most are false.**
 Live examples of what a token match produces:
 charge description "Moshe Ifrah" -> matched "Moshe Basch", "Moshe Chaim Lachman"
 charge description "Phone" -> matched a customer literally named "mrs pom phone"
 charge description "cable repair and sd card" -> matched a customer named "SD Card"
 The shared tokens are "moshe", "phone", "sd card". These are not identities, they are common
 words, and 198 further payments name someone who is not in the customer table at all.
 So name matching is worse than no matching here: it would write 64 confident-looking wrong links
 into the record, and a wrong link is harder to detect later than an absent one.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE. The 268 payments, worth 28,142.70, **cannot be attributed** from the evidence
that exists. The link was never recorded at the point of payment and nothing afterwards reconstructs
it. This is not a data-repair job; it is a missing-field problem, and missing fields cannot be
back-filled from data that does not contain them.

CONSEQUENCE, which changes what the owner is being asked. #470 offered back-linking the 268 as an
option alongside making the terminal carry the work order. That option is now known to be
unavailable, and the row's recommendation is corrected: **the forward fix is the only fix.** What
remains genuinely his is the product question - should the terminal REQUIRE a work order, or be
allowed to ring up a walk-in sale - because that decides whether the field can be mandatory.

THE GENERAL LESSON. A back-link is only possible when the join key was recorded. Before promising
one, check that the key exists in the historical data, not just that it exists in the schema: here
`payment_transactions.orderId` exists as a column and is null on 268 rows, and `orders.total` exists
as a column and is null on 476. Two nullable columns, and the promise died on the first look at
their fill rate. **Measure the fill rate of the join key before promising the join.**
