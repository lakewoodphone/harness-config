**L3058 · A fan-out whose output I never collect is worth less than doing it myself**

Measured 2026-09-29, preparing Chol Hamoed day-trip options.

**What I did:** fanned a research job out across **six `subagent_local` jobs**, each told to write its report to a specific path under `/home/zabz/phone/trips-2026-09-29/`. Then I moved on to my own research and **never checked that the reports arrived.**

**What happened:** when I finally looked, **not one of the six had written a file** - the output directory held only pre-existing content - and **no job remained in the job list**. The session had run across a long gap, and the child work was simply gone. I then tried a second fan-out through the `workflow` tool, and that run **was cancelled**: no output either.

**Net cost: almost the entire day, and a deliverable that had to be written from my own partial research and labelled as leads rather than as a completed survey.**

**THE RULE: a delegated job is not done when it is dispatched, it is done when its output is in hand.** Fan-out without a collection step is not parallelism, it is **hope**. Specifically:
- **Verify the artefact, not the dispatch.** A job that says it will write to a path has not written to it until you have listed or read that path.
- **Collect before the session gap.** Child work that lives only in a job that finishes while the parent is busy can vanish entirely. **Pull the output the moment the job reports, and write the durable copy into the repo immediately** - not after the next piece of work.
- **Prefer the tool that returns results to the caller.** The `workflow` tool returns its results in the call; `subagent_local` writes to a directory I have to remember to read. When the collection step is the failure mode, use the construct that does not have one.

**And the corollary about honesty in the deliverable:** when the sweep fails, **say so in the document rather than padding it out.** The day-trip file now opens with a status section stating that the full regional sweep did not complete, that the shortlist is leads rather than a survey, and listing every gap by name. **A thin document that admits its thinness is usable; a thin document dressed as a complete one is a trap for whoever reads it next.**
