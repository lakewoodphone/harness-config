**L3120 · A scheduled job whose program was deleted is invisible; check the schedule against the filesystem in one breath**

A scheduled job whose program has been deleted is invisible by construction, and the fix is to read the schedule and the filesystem in the same breath.

THE EVIDENCE, 2026-09-30

Five crontab entries on `secratary` called programs that did not exist (`scripts/dialpad-harvest-cron.sh`,
`scripts/dialpad-sms-harvest-cron.sh`, `scripts/dialpad-recording-gap.py`,
`scripts/dialpad-transcript-gap.py`, `scripts/ha/check-security-absence.py`), and two more existed
without an execute bit (`scripts/secretary-startup.sh`, `scripts/site-uptime-check-cron.sh`).

The call harvest had been dead ~28 hours and had not fetched 61 calls. The site uptime check had
been dead 24 days. Neither produced any signal that reached a surface: cron's only output is a line
in a per-job log, and this host has no `mail` binary, so `sh: …: not found` and silence look the
same.

WHAT THIS TEACHES, IN THE FORM A FUTURE SESSION CAN ACT ON

1. `crontab` is data outside version control. A `git checkout` in the deployed tree can delete the
 program while the schedule keeps firing. Both halves have to be checked against each other; no
 amount of git hygiene alone closes it.
2. "The job is scheduled" and "the job runs" are different claims, and only the second one is
 testable. `cron_target_guard` checks the first; running the job once by hand is the only cheap
 proof of the second.
3. An ABSENCE has no symptom. Every check in this estate that measures a CONSEQUENCE (comms
 freshness, this one) reports late — 28 hours here. The cheap, early check is the structural one:
 does the thing the schedule names exist, and is it executable.
4. When restoring, take the NEWEST surviving copy and prove it by hash. Three independent snapshots
 from 2026-09-28 existed and were byte-identical, which is what made the restore safe rather than
 a guess.
5. A file that comes back without its mode is only half restored. Two of the seven defects were
 mode, not existence.

THE MECHANISM, SO IT IS NOT REDISCOVERED

`~/bin/cron-target-guard.py` on the authority: parses every crontab job, resolves paths through
`cd … &&` chains, `flock`/`timeout`/`nohup`/`env` wrappers, `~` and `$HOME`, strips redirections so
a log target is not mistaken for a program, and reports MISSING / UNREADABLE / NO EXEC BIT, exit 1
on any of them. `--selftest` proves the detector fires; `--write-status` leaves
`~/.cron-targets/status.json`. Wired hourly and to `sources/cron-targets.py` so a failure becomes
ledger work. Falsified 2026-09-30 by injecting a missing target and watching it fire, then restoring.
