**L10432 · CORRECTION: my 34% wasted-releases figure was a proxy error - duration is not outcome**

**L — I reported "34 % of releases are wasted no-ops" and it was wrong. Duration is not outcome.**

Earlier in this same session I measured `grep … | grep -ao "dur=[0-9]*s"` over one day's releases and
reported that 50 of 146 releases "ran under 300 s" and were therefore no-ops — 34 %, and 72 % on the
next day's partial window. I repeated it in the handoff as a reason to build a pre-release guard.

**It is false, and the correction is the point of this entry.** `dur < 300s` measures how long a
session ran. A no-op is a session that did **nothing**, which is a different predicate with a
different writer.

Re-measured properly on 2026-10-20, pairing each `released` line with the `outcome:` line that
follows it:

* The 11 releases of 2026-10-20 up to 00:16Z all carried real outcomes — `rental-system` checkout
 fixed, a root cause found and fixed, three customer response cards built, "Item 717 — done".
* The `project:lpt-sync` releases I had named as the worst offenders (`dur=79s`, `166s`, `238s`,
 `283s`) carry outcomes that show a shift working: *"I claimed i…"*, *"Where does it live? In
 personal-secretary-mvp"*.
* The genuine empty-claim string exists — `claim-next` answers `{"ok": "nothing-todo"}`, 75
 occurrences across the log's whole history — and the shifts that hit it **correctly record it and
 stop**. That is the contract working, not waste.

**The transferable rule, and it is the same one the estate keeps relearning:** before aggregating a
column, ask what writes it and whether it can distinguish the two states you care about. `dur` is
written by the session clock; `did`/`proof`/`result` in the ledger's `attempt` table are written by
the work. A cheap proxy that is one step removed from the outcome will produce a confident number
about the wrong thing — and mine reached a handoff before anyone caught it.

The one real starvation in that area was the **count ceiling**, not waste: `wake_budget.py` had
`COUNT_MAX = 150` hardcoded, so `max_per_day(47.64, 0.0639) = min(745, 150) = 150` and the day
stopped at **4.178258 USD of 47.64 = 8.8 %**. Raised to 500 (the value `projects.json` had recorded
all along) and verified from `wake-caps-effective.json` and `autonomy-status.sh`:
`releases today : 16 of 500 backstop`, no cap in force.
