**L12414 · Three measured rules from the LTRAP miss: folder queries bound what you can know, a widened filter must be counted, and a deadline regex only knows its own vocabulary**

A mailbox query defines what the company is able to know, and Gmail's spam folder is where
false negatives go to die. Also: a deadline regex that requires the word "deadline" cannot see a
stated application window.**

Three rules, all measured on 2026-10-21 while chasing why a 2026-11-02 housing deadline reached the
owner from another person instead of from the owner queue.

**1. "Not in the inbox" is not "not in the mailbox", and only one of those is a decision you made.**
The owner-attention sweep read `in:inbox`, `in:sent`, and `in:anywhere -in:inbox -in:sent -in:spam
-in:trash`. That third query enumerated the folders it wanted and named the one it refused. The
LTRAP notice arrived with `labelIds: ['UNREAD','CATEGORY_UPDATES','SPAM']` — and Gmail, not us,
decided that. When a classifier upstream of you decides what you can see, its false positives become
your blind spots, permanently and silently. Read the folder it is wrong about. Prior from the same
sender does not help: the two 2022 LRRC notices were `INBOX`, the 2026 one was `SPAM`.

**2. When you widen an attention filter, measure what you added, in the same breath.** Widening a
filter that feeds a person is not free — a feed he learns to skim is worse than a quiet one. The
measurement that made this change safe: over 195 corpus rows, the old deadline regex matched 16 and
the new one added **exactly 1**, the notice it was written for. Do not report "the filter is better";
report the count, because the count is the only thing that distinguishes a fix from noise.

**3. Enumerate the vocabulary a deadline actually uses, not the words you would use.** The notice
said "Applications Accepted November 2-10", "Opens:", "Closes:", "Don't Miss the Application
Window" — four ways to say *deadline* — and matched `deadline|expires|past due|final notice|…` on
none of them. Any regex over meaning is a regex over somebody's phrasing; when the source is an
institution writing in its own register, the phrasing will not be yours.

**Corollary, and it cost more than the first three: check that the label matches the writer.**
`_SECTION_OF` mapped source `"books"`, but `from_obligations()` emitted `"obligations"` — so the
digest's MONEY AND FILINGS section was structurally empty and six overdue obligations rendered under
OTHER at positions 29-34. A section that can never fill looks exactly like a section with nothing in
it. When a mapping is a keyed lookup, grep for the key the writer actually emits, not the key the
reader documents.
