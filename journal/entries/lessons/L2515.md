**L2515 · Find out who pays before deciding what to do: I turned a CUSTOMER's choice (stop and lose 160, or pay more) into a shop decision and drafted a text that priced the job on the owner's behalf**

**The owner stopped me mid-work and corrected the model of a customer case. It was wrong in a way that had already produced a harmful draft.**

The case: Wilner SD card, 300DDR ticket #73890 (journal H2659 / D2581). I had spent a session building a careful analysis around owner-queue row **#127** as a binary shop decision — *authorise the $250 + $500 chip-off, or cancel while cancelling is free* — priced branch by branch, and I had drafted a customer text ending **"The price stays as quoted."**

**The owner's correction, verbatim:** *"the customer paid a 160 deposit and will have to pay a lot more to continue, do you understand that?"* and *"here they can stop and call their 160 losses, or decide to pay more, we have to give them the options."*

**The model, correctly.** The customer pays LPT a **non-refundable down payment** up front and **pays the rest only if they get their data back**. So the shop fronts the lab, and the customer carries the large balance — and when the recovery route gets more expensive than the quote was built on, **the choice between stopping and paying more belongs to the customer, not to the shop.** The shop's job is to give them the options with a real number.

**What I got wrong, and why it matters more than a wrong number.** I turned a customer's decision into a shop's decision. Every branch of my table was arithmetically right and structurally wrong: it asked what the *shop* should risk while the person paying the balance was treated as a formality. The draft then went further and **priced the job on the owner's behalf, in writing, to the person paying it** — a commitment I had no authority to make, on a route that had just become materially more expensive than the number I promised. That text is now withdrawn in the case file with a do-not-send banner; the replacement states the customer's two options and leaves the amount blank.

**THE RULES.**
- **Find out who pays before deciding what to do.** "Should we spend $250" and "should the customer spend $500 more" are different questions with different owners. If the counterparty's money is the money in play, the counterparty's choice is the artefact to produce.
- **A session never writes a price into a customer-facing message unless the owner set it in that conversation.** Not as a "reasonable default", not as reassurance. The owner's words, and the money, are his.
- **When a job's route changes, re-derive whose quote is still valid** rather than carrying the old total forward as if it were an agreement about the new route. A total quoted against a $300 laboratory tier is not a price for a $500 one.
- **Ask before analysing deeper.** I produced a full branch table, a sync-record update and a customer draft on a model I never confirmed. The owner's two sentences would have redirected all of it, and he had to spend them on a "wait wait wait" instead.

**DONE.** Row #127 on the authority reshaped (customer chooses; the number is the owner's; options reordered with a 1.45x-on-lab default of $750 total / $590 more, marked as his to set); case file corrected and pushed (`c48b3aa92`); lab terms email and customer options text prepared and awaiting his word. Superseded text retained everywhere.
