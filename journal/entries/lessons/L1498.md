**L1498 · Three pain entries in one session had a stale premise: re-measure a pain before acting on it**

Three pain entries in one session had a stale or mis-scoped premise; re-measure before believing one

Instances from 2026-09-14/15, all found by measuring the entry's claim against the system rather
than acting on the entry:

 * H217 said "nothing in the system watches a paid supplier order that never got tracking ...
 That watchdog does not exist." It existed, was scheduled every 6 hours, and worked -- one
 CRITICAL, the right one, no false positives. I had already designed a replacement and was
 about to build a duplicate of a working job.
 * P7 said the MCP bridge returns "huge payloads". True, and the number in my own note (90 KB)
 was 4.3x out of date: ps_health had grown to 385 KB.
 * P5 said "work assigned to agents that do not exist: work silently vanishing". 27 of 28
 blueprint agents have a live trace and the oldest pending job in the queue was 0.8h old, so
 nothing was stranded. What was real is one missing alias in one table nothing scanned.

WHY THIS KEEPS HAPPENING, and it is not sloppiness: a pain entry is written when the pain is
FELT, and it is read later by someone who has no way to tell whether the world moved. The entry
records the symptom and the diagnosis, not the measurement's date or the conditions under which
it was true. A fixed bug, a changed config, or a growing structure all silently falsify it.

THE HABIT: before building or fixing from a pain entry, spend one command re-measuring its
central claim. Three for three this session, the re-measurement changed what the right action
was -- from "build a watchdog" to "check the one that exists", from "add a wrapper" to "fix the
source", and from "build a reconciliation check" to "add one alias and widen one query".

The corollary for writing them: an entry that states a MEASUREMENT should carry its date and its
method, so the next reader can tell a stale number from a current one. "ps_health returns 90 KB"
rots; "ps_health returned 90 KB on 2026-09-11, measured with curl | wc -c" does not.
