**L11711 · The record did not fire: a lesson, a detector and a plan all existed, and the outage happened anyway — the four mechanisms, and the gates that close them**

THE QUESTION THIS ENTRY ANSWERS: a staged, GO-preflighted upgrade went down on a failure this estate had already written a lesson about, a day earlier, on the other machine. Why did the record not fire?

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED, in the order I did it. I searched the journal for "yoga update dsh engine upgrade", got H10220 (the plan: switch-yoga.ps1 is dry-run proven, preflight GO, one stop-window away), H10350 and L10367, and stopped searching. H10220 told me the next ACTION. The failure CLASSES were in L10238, which I never opened; it names `ctx.webRuntime` and `{{cwd}}` as the two things that had already taken ZABZ-TECH down, and names `dsh-update/tools/config-assumptions.mjs` as the control for them. I then ran the switch, which had never called that tool, and the yoga died on `{{cwd}}` in the owner's hands.

WHY THE SEARCH MISSED IT — four mechanisms, none of them "I forgot":

1. THE RECORD IS INDEXED BY WHEN IT WAS LEARNED, NOT BY WHAT IT GUARDS. A lessons entry is found by the words of the failure ("prompt variable", "{{cwd}}", "persona-suffix", "assumption"); a session that arrives with a plan searches the words of the PLAN ("yoga update switch preflight"). Two vocabularies, and only one of them is in the arriving session's head. Measured: the plan's words hit three entries, the failure's words hit the one that mattered.
2. THE HANDOFF IS WRITTEN BY THE SESSION THAT WAS INSIDE THE WORK, so it describes the next action and inherits that session's conviction that the class is handled ("dry-run proven", "preflight GO on 8/8"). A plan is the least reliable document in the estate precisely because it is the most recent and the most confident.
3. A DETECTOR WITH NO CALLER IS INVISIBLE FROM THE OUTSIDE. config-assumptions.mjs existed, was correct, and was referenced by nothing in the update path. The same shape is already written down in cli.mjs for settings-effective: "a detector no verb can reach is not a gate; it is a document". I read that sentence while writing tonight's guard and did not connect it to the tool I was holding.
4. MY OWN CHECK PRINTED THE ANSWER AND I DID NOT CONSUME IT. Before the first switch the compose check printed `cwd_tokens=3`. I read it as a baseline number. There is no rule in my surface that says "any non-zero in a check you just ran is a blocker until you explain it in writing", and no gate consumes my own output — so a number I print and do not judge is not a check, it is decoration.

WHAT CHANGES. (a) `dsh-update/docs/KNOWN-BREAKAGE-CLASSES.md`: every class this estate has paid for, with the journal id, the gate that catches it, and the exact search words, linked as the first thing to read in RUNBOOK and README. A session arriving with a plan now has a way to search for the failure without knowing its vocabulary. (b) `dsh-update/docs/UPDATE-SYSTEM.md`: phase 0 of any update is "read the record", and the phases after it are programs that refuse. (c) The three gates that make the class unskippable: config-assumptions is a blocking guard in `preflight`, a refusal inside `install-engine` before it stages, and STEP 2.5 in the switch before it writes. (d) For my own output: the plan's verification block now has to name its expectation ("rows N, tokens 0, ctx 0") so a number cannot be read as neutral.

THE GENERALISATION, and it is the one worth keeping. Documentation that is not attached to a program does not survive a deadline. The estate had the analysis (L10238), the detector (config-assumptions.mjs) and the plan (H10220) — and the outage happened anyway, because the only place those three meet is a human reading them in the right order. Every one of them is now a place a program refuses to continue.
