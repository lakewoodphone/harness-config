## Three faults, three footprints - "10 chats" bounded only the ordering fault, and the DESTRUCTION landed on a disjoint set

The owner asked a sharp question on 2026-10-13: *"only 10 of her chats should be broken and thats it? everything
else should be working properly and those 10 the only problem should be arriving out of order?"* The answer is
**no**, and I had been at risk of implying it was yes by leading every report with "10 chats".

Measured on the stores themselves:

| fault | footprint | overlap with the 10 |
|---|---|---|
| a message she sends is numbered inside the history | **10 chats**, 8 of them groups | that IS the 10 |
| **the allocator destroying her recovered messages** | **6 chats already damaged** - Coco Bar, Facebook Business, Felder Art, Hadara Ceramics, Penina Sebag, SURI | **0 of them** |
| duplicate `ZSORT` slots | 17 chats | 10 inside, 7 outside |
| newest message not at the highest `ZSORT` | **140 chats** | 5 inside, 135 outside |

**The quiet, silent fault is the worse one and it is not confined to the ten.** It hit her **one-to-one business
threads** (Felder Art, SURI, Coco Bar) - the conversations that matter most commercially - while the *ordering*
fault hit her busy vendor **groups**. Two different sets of conversations, two different kinds of harm, and the
one nobody can see is the one that deletes her messages.

**And the 6/10 are lower bounds, not totals.** `Z_PRIMARYKEY.WAMessage.Z_MAX` was at 1519 twenty minutes after
the app was installed and **one counter serves the whole store**, so every object the app has created since -
five days of them - has taken an id that already held one of her messages. What the snapshot shows is where the
app had got to by 22:12 on 08 October, not the extent of the damage.

**The rule worth keeping:** when one fault is loud and a second is silent, never let the loud one's blast radius
stand in for the system's. Report each fault with its own footprint, and say explicitly whether the sets
overlap. "10 chats are broken" was true, and reading it as "the rest is fine" would have been wrong in the
direction that loses customer data.

Also killed tonight: the same fault produced two different numbers in two artifacts (the case file counted
replaced AND deleted rows; the audit's `--compare` counted only replaced). The script now reports both, with
each damaged row's conversation and whether it is in the broken set, so the artifacts cannot disagree.
