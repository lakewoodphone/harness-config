**L3782 · A no-draw fault cannot be caused by the display - attribute a lid-related symptom to what carries power**

The owner challenged this on 2026-10-13 on the Ullman case (WO26101312747, Lenovo IdeaPad 3 NM-D471, 0 A at 19 V): "hold in, it won't charge because of the display, is that what you are saying does that make any sense". It does not, and the record said it twice.

THE PHYSICS, because this will come up again. 0 A with the adapter holding its voltage means the board is taking no current at all. The display harness carries the eDP main link, the backlight rail, backlight enable/PWM and the panel's low-voltage rails - every one of them is generated downstream, by the board, from rails the board makes only after the charger has enabled the input path. A damaged panel, a chafed cable or a shorted backlight rail cannot stop the charger drawing current from the adapter. If a downstream short were holding the charger's input FETs off, the adapter would show a current pulse or fold, and the documented NM-D471 no-power signature is ACDRV pulsing to about 2.5 V then collapsing, not a flat zero. "No charge" and "no display" are two different faults that arrive in one sentence when the customer describes them, and they must be separated before either is written down as a cause.

WHAT THE MEASURED CLUE ACTUALLY POINTS AT. The customer's own history - a month of shutdowns specifically when he opened and closed the lid, then dead - is worth more than the sentence it arrived in. Flexing a chassis and losing power is the classic fractured DC jack or cracked input solder joint. The same board in this shop's Kreisman case (WO2607290002) measured 19.4 V at the port and nothing downstream: an open circuit between the jack and the main rail. The lid clue points at the POWER INPUT, which is where the trace was already going.

TWO RULES.
1. When a symptom follows a mechanical action, attribute it to the mechanism that carries power into the machine, not to the subsystem the customer happened to name. He said "I replaced the screen and now it does not charge"; the wiring that matters is the wiring that moves when the lid moves.
2. Keep a suspect in the plan for the right reason. The display harness stays in this case for exactly two: it is a two-minute EXCLUSION (unplug it at the mainboard, re-apply 19 V, still 0 A means it is out), and once the board is fixed a chafed harness may be a SECOND, independent defect that leaves the display dark. It is not a candidate cause of 0 A, and writing it as one is what made the owner stop the session.

FIXED: the case-file front matter and its 0 A bullet, and the sync record's internalSummary and nextAction - all four said or implied the hinge or the screen could cause the no-power. Production notes 2 and 3 already had it right, so no production note was added. lpt-hub pushed, origin/main `403c383fc` (verified with `git ls-remote`). This was the SECOND correction to this same case in one session, and both were the same defect: a plausible narrative promoted above the measurement.
