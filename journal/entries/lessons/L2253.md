## What this shift was asked to do

Source the phone for the Kosher Waze / LPT NAVIGATION fleet against the owner's criterion, given verbatim on
2026-09-20: *"i just [want] the cheapest reliable ones with eSIMs"*. US-spec is mandatory (47 CFR 2.803(b), no
used-equipment exception, a supplier contract does not satisfy it). Mine the shop's own history first. Price with
evidence. Deliver a landed cost. Write it down.

Shortlist written to `lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/waze/PHONE-SHORTLIST-2026-09-20.md`.

## The measured result

**Two filters did almost all the work, and they are cheaper to reuse than to rediscover:**

1. **eSIM** admits only iPhone XS/XR (2018) and later. **China/HK/Macao stock of the iPhone 11 and 12 has two
 physical nano-SIM slots and no eSIM at all** — identical money, a unit that cannot join the fleet.
2. **Current iOS** then removes the **XR, XS and XS Max**: iOS 26 dropped them, iOS 27 did not restore them, and
 they are stuck on iOS 18. They have eSIM and they are cheap, and they are still the wrong buy.

**Verified US FCC IDs, from FCC-filed exhibits (not guessed):** iPhone 11 A2111 = **BCG-E3309A**; iPhone SE 2020
A2275 = **BCG-E3500A**; iPhone 12 A2172 = **BCG-E3542A**; XR A1984 = BCG-E3220A; XS A1920 = BCG-E3218A;
XS Max A1921 = BCG-E3219A. All grantee Apple Inc. iPhone 11, SE2 and 12 run iOS 26 **and** iOS 27.

**What survives both filters and is cheapest: the iPhone SE 2020 and the iPhone 11.**

### And then the bulk price inverts the conclusion

- **iPhone 11 wholesale is readable:** **$125/unit** (Cell Dealers, 521 units, Tampa) and **$135/unit**
 (gsmExchange, 39 units, US seller, "used & tested"). Both hide the *seller* behind a paywall or premium login —
 readable, not transactable.
- **SE 2020 wholesale is gated on every single channel:** Today's Closeout (50 units, MOQ 10) MEMBERS ONLY;
 gsmExchange SE2 offers all read `USD1`; WholesaleBStock MOQ $10,000; WholesaleiPhonesBusiness behind signup. The
 only SE2 *lot* readable anywhere was a **cracked-parts** lot ($80 for 3, Mercari) — excluded.
- **The iPhone 11 has essentially NO bulk discount:** a 39-unit US-spec wholesale lot at $135 equals its
 single-unit achieved sale of $135.

**The durable finding: the cheapest unit and the cheapest *buyable* unit are not the same phone, and the gap is
not price — it is access.** The SE 2020 is ~$50 cheaper and its wholesale price cannot be read without an account
the owner has forbidden me to open. The iPhone 11 is $50 dearer and its wholesale floor is a readable number.
One free account would settle it; that is the single highest-value open action.

## Reliability, answered only from our own records

**Honest first: this shop has no statistically meaningful iPhone repair record.** Across the whole `lpt-hub` tree
there are a handful of iPhone cases (11 Pro Max screen, 13 earpiece, 14 Pro screen, 14/15 Plus VBAT short, 16 Pro
battery) and **two iPhone 11s in the fleet**. Anyone claiming a failure rate from that is inventing one.

What the records *do* give is the specific fault this product will actually suffer and proof we can fix it:

- **Mrs. Mizrahi's iPhone 11** (2026-06-29): water damage corroded the **charging-port flex → Prs0 missing in the
 panic log → reboot every ~3 minutes.** Diagnosed from the panic log, part identified, fix priced at
 **$4.42 OEM-pull** (MobileSentrix).
- **The shop's own SE 2020 memo** (2025-12-29) names the *same* failure as the SE2's leading fault: *"The 'Panic
 Full' kernel panic, often triggered by a failure in the charging port flex cable … is the leading cause of
 random restarts in this model."*
- **Our product design** puts the device on **constant car power** — the charging port is its most-stressed
 connector.

**The reliability answer is a convergence, not a guess: same dominant failure on both candidates, same cheap part,
and we have already diagnosed it from a panic log on an iPhone 11.** That is the only reliable-in-our-hands
property the record supports, and it holds for both phones.

## The trap I nearly walked into, and the one that is still live

**Nearly:** treating "cheapest" as a single-unit price and recommending the SE 2020 outright. The SE2 is genuinely
cheaper *and* has a documented SE2-specific sourcing risk worth money: *"Low-cost third-party replacements often
lack True Tone and offer significantly lower brightness, rendering the device unusable in direct sunlight,"* plus
**"boosted" batteries with software-manipulated health readings** circulating in the refurb market. On a dashboard
in daylight that is a customer-visible defect, and it is exactly the kind of saving that costs more later.

**Still live:** the previously-verified eBay 5-lot (Sage Sustainable Electronics, $135.80/unit, 60-day seller-paid
returns) **could not be re-confirmed today — eBay returned 403 to every fetch route**, item pages and search alike.
Its 60-day return window is what removes the QC-rejection line, so **if it is still live it is the best
risk-adjusted buy on the board** and the shortlist does not know that yet. A search snippet proves it was indexed,
not that it is live. Do not re-cite $135.80 as current without opening the page.

## Landed cost, labelled honestly

iPhone 11 A2111, central case: **$125-135 unit + $5-15 freight (ESTIMATE — not sourced) + $0 duty** (HTS
8517.13.00.00 = 0%, and US-domestic stock means no entry, broker or MPF) **+ $0-10 parts + $0-34 QC-rejection
adjustment** (measured precedent: a 5-lot at $135.80 with one rejection is **$169.75 per usable unit**)
**≈ $140-160 landed, ≈ $170-185 with one rejection in five.**

The structural lever remains the bench: a clean-IMEI unlocked iPhone 11 with a cracked screen (~$60) plus a screen
($12.29-24.16) lands at **~$73 spare-capacity / ~$107-108 loaded** — a $28-63/unit advantage that is the only real
lever in this market. Its cost is customer-visible: a replaced iPhone 11 display **loses True Tone permanently even
with a genuine Apple screen**, and Apple gives the 11 **no calibration path** (Repair Assistant is iPhone 15+), so
it shows a permanent **"Unknown Part"** row in iOS Settings. That is a product decision, not a sourcing one.

## Durable rules this shift earns

1. **Run the two filters before any price work: eSIM (iPhone XS/XR or later) and current iOS (drops XR/XS/XS Max).**
 Together they close the China/HK grey market and three cheap-but-dead models, and they cost nothing to apply.
2. **"Cheapest" must be split into cheapest *unit* and cheapest *buyable* unit.** They diverged here by ~$50 and
 the difference was account access, not market price.
3. **Our own repair record cannot answer "which model is reliable" at this volume — say so.** It *can* name the
 dominant fault (charging-port flex) and prove the fix is cheap and in-house. That is the honest form of the
 answer, and it is more useful than a fabricated failure rate.
4. **A gated price is not a price.** Every SE2 bulk channel was gated and the correct output was "could not read",
 not a plausible number.
5. **An expired or unverifiable listing must be re-verified before it carries a decision.** The Sage 5-lot's 403
 today, and Cellpex's expired rows, are the same lesson twice.
