**L2788 · State the premise when a time word is ambiguous - solved Sunday night when he meant Monday evening, 2026-09-28**

Owner, 2026-09-28 01:45 ET: "hold on, tonight shes' coming home at about 6, unless you think today is still yesterday and you are confused"

What happened: he wrote at 2026-09-27 22:24 ET - "i want to go on a chol hamoed trip with my wife tonight". At 22:24 on a Sunday, "tonight" can only mean Sunday night, so I spent ~3 hours solving Sunday night: movie radius scans, every venue's closing time, late-night food. Everything kept coming back shut, because on a Sunday night in this region everything IS shut by 10-11pm. At 01:45 he revealed the trip is MONDAY evening (she gets home ~6pm), so the night I solved was over before I started.

The failure is not the reading - 22:24 "tonight" really does mean that night. The failure is that I never said the assumption out loud. I reported "everything is closed" four times without ever asking "which night do you mean by tonight?", and the closed-everything finding is so implausible on its face that it was itself the signal to check the frame. A result that says "nothing in the region is open" should trigger a premise check before it triggers a research spiral.

Rule: when a request's answer keeps coming back as a flat negative, state the premise you are working from in ONE line early ("working on Sunday night - tell me if you mean Monday") and keep working. Do not silently commit to one reading of a time word.

Second-order cost: the report at reports/chol-hamoed-5787-trip-options-2026-09-28.md was written and presented to him with the wrong night as its whole framing. Corrected with a visible CORRECTION block at the top rather than rewritten - the Sunday-night findings are true and stay.

Likely path to the misread: his wife is away at college and comes home Monday ~6pm; the trip was always going to be the evening she is home.

GENERALISE: "tonight", "tomorrow", "this week" from a person writing after 10pm are ambiguous exactly when it matters most. One clarifying clause costs nothing; three hours of forensics on the wrong night costs the whole session.
