## L — Two rules from the 300DDR #73890 episode: send with the owner's word, and never audit a partner

**Written 2026-09-25 to close two dangling references.** The Wilner case file cites lessons `L2522`
and `L2523` and pain `P2477`. **None of the three ids exists** — `journal.py show` returns "no entry"
for all three, and searching the journal for the quotes the case file attributes to them returns
nothing. The knowledge was recorded only in the case file, so the references pointed at air. This
entry is the lessons; the file has been repointed at it.

**L2522 as it was meant — an outbound message to a partner needs the owner's per-message word.**
On 2026-09-24 at 14:07 a six-item terms email (`1a0d53e9f870eb3c`) went to 300DDR on ticket #73890.
It was written and sent without the owner approving that message. His reaction, quoted in the case
file: *"you are making them not like us... I could have answered many of those questions."* He then
had to spend the next hour retracting it: *"just say the sorry it was a mistake and that's it"*,
which produced the three-line apology (`1a0d56a2da5a038b`, 14:55). **The rule: no message goes to a
vendor the shop depends on without his explicit word on that message in the current conversation.**
This is the same hard stop that governs customer mail, and it applies to vendors too.

**L2523 as it was meant — do not send a partner a questionnaire that reads as an audit.**
Of the six items in that email, **four were already answered in the lab's own earlier emails** — the
rate, that the deposit is applied toward the total, the decline fee, and what they need to start.
Asking a partner to re-confirm things they have already told you reads as distrust. **Read the
vendor's own prior correspondence before asking them anything**; if the answer is already in the
thread, cite it rather than re-asking.

**P2477 as it was meant — read the `To` header back after every outbound email; the labels describe
what we did with the message, not who received it.** The first apology attempt (`1a0d569a455a6e45`)
returned `ok: true`, carried the right thread, and was labelled `SENT` — and its `To` header was
`info@lakewoodphoneandtech.com`, **ourselves**. `reply_to_message` had derived the recipient from a
message we had sent, so it replied to us. The lab never saw it, and the record said it was sent.
**Verify the `To` of the sent message, not the fact that a call succeeded.** This one is confirmed by
direct inspection and remains true of every send path that replies to an outbound message.

**Why this entry exists at all.** A case file that cites an id which does not resolve is worse than
one that cites nothing: it looks like the evidence base is deeper than it is. Both `journal.py check`
(reported 141 warnings, mostly dangling refs) and any future reader are misled. **If you cite an id,
write the entry.**
