**L3236 · Never delete a launcher-managed game's folder - the launcher re-downloads it. Uninstall removes the RECORD, deleting only removes files**

WHAT HAPPENED: I deleted C:\Program Files\EA Games\Titanfall2 (63.67 GB) after the owner
approved it. I verified the path, deleted it elevated, confirmed the folder was gone, and told
him it was done. IT WAS NOT DONE. Two minutes later the EA App saw its install missing,
treated it as damage, and RE-DOWNLOADED the entire 63.67 GB - into the space a concurrent
cleanup had just freed. He went from 110 GB free to 19 GB, and the game was fully back
(2180 files, v2.0.11.0, exe signature Valid).

THE RULE: never delete a launcher-managed game's folder. A launcher defends its own install
record. The correct removal is the launcher's own uninstaller, which removes the RECORD as well
as the files:
 "C:\Program Files\Common Files\EAInstaller\Titanfall2\Cleanup.exe" uninstall_game -autologging
(found in the HKLM Uninstall key, DisplayName "Titanfall(tm) 2"). Deleting the folder leaves the
record, and the record causes the repair.

THE GENERALISATION, which is the transferable part: BEFORE deleting anything a manager process
knows about - a game launcher's install, a package manager's store, a service's data directory,
a database's file - ask what the MANAGER does when the thing goes missing. "Verify then delete"
is not enough; the manager's reaction is part of the operation. I checked the path was correct
and never checked what EA would do.

ALSO RECORDED: the cleanup itself worked - _worktrees 30.67 -> 9.15 GB and _fleet 16.29 ->
7.58 GB, 99 node_modules trees removed, ~30 GB freed. That part is real and reusable. It was
simply outweighed by a 63.67 GB re-download that my own method triggered.

AND A HARD BLOCKER FOR FUTURE SESSIONS: elevated work cannot be launched from an agent session
on this machine. `Start-Process -Verb RunAs` returned ERROR_CANCELLED three times in a row
(the Intel driver installer twice, the uninstaller once), though it succeeded twice earlier in
the same session. UAC here is ConsentPromptBehaviorAdmin=5 with PromptOnSecureDesktop=1 - a
credentials prompt on the secure desktop. When an elevated step is needed, hand the owner the
exact command for an admin PowerShell he opens himself rather than launching it and hoping.
