**L2985 · Settings readback is stable, not racy: all three unregistered namespaces are explained by renames, and the absent keys by a partial import**

## RESOLUTION of P2732's open question: the settings readback result is STABLE, and all three missing namespaces are explained by renames -- it is not a timing artefact and not a new 0.1.7 regression

MEASURED 2026-09-28 (ZABZ-TECH), candidate `0.1.7-rc.2`, three consecutive runs of
`lib/settings-effective.mjs` against the same staged home:
```
 run 1: ran=true verdict=BREAKS namespacesRegistered=18 namespacesOurs=9 BREAKS=14 present=2 absent=11 different=3 noNamespace=7
 run 2: ran=false (the boot did not come up; no reading)
 run 3: ran=true verdict=BREAKS namespacesRegistered=18 namespacesOurs=9 BREAKS=14 present=2 absent=11 different=3 noNamespace=7
```
Runs 1 and 3 are IDENTICAL in every count. A deterministic result, so the hypothesis I was
investigating -- that the readback queried before the loader settled and saw a partial namespace list --
is REFUTED for the namespace set. That hypothesis came from finding `llm-deepseek` and `agent-loop` in
the failing output, which turned out to be a misreading: those two namespaces DO register.

THE THREE NAMESPACES ACTUALLY REPORTED AS UNREGISTERED, and each is explained:
 * `agent-presets` -- 0.1.7 RENAMED that entry to `agent-preset-registry` (P2731), so a settings
 section by the old name has no owner. This is the settings-side proof that patching the registry
 ROW's `config.default` is mandatory rather than optional.
 * `ui-onboarding` -- 0.1.7 maps it to `ui-settings-general`; the engine's own
 `LEGACY_SECTION_ENTRIES` (`dsh-settings/lib/index.js:303-308`) says so explicitly. The VALUE is not
 lost, it lands under the new entry; the READBACK is comparing against the old section name.
 * `spend-guard` -- the plugin activation race (P2730/L2979), seen here from a third angle.

THE `absent=11` KEYS ARE ALSO EXPLAINED, by something already known: they belong to namespaces that DO
register (`llm-deepseek`, `llm-pi-ai`, `agent-loop`), and the run booted against a staged home whose
legacy `settings.yaml` import was PARTIAL (P2731 -- renamed before the first write, and my early kill
cut it short). On 0.1.7 settings reach the engine via that one-shot import, so keys absent from the
profile are exactly what a partial import predicts.

CONCLUSION: the readback is working, and its 14 BREAKS are a composition of four issues this session
already knows about, not a new regression in 0.1.7. TWO FIXES follow:
 1. the readback must follow `LEGACY_SECTION_ENTRIES` when resolving a section name to an entry, or it
 will keep reporting renamed-away names as unregistered; and
 2. a CLEAN post-switch reading can only come from a home whose import COMPLETED -- i.e. after the real
 first boot on the new engine with a settings document present and no early kill.
