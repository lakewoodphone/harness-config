**L3374 · Readiness that lives in a document is readiness you have not tested - the first run of the preflight disqualified both candidate hosts**

GOAL ROUND 21. The survivor host had been chosen in PROSE (annex C: '2-4 vCPU, 4-8 GB, 40-80 GB'). I replaced the prose with survivor-preflight.sh and ran it against the two live Ubuntu 24.04 hosts, and it immediately disqualified BOTH of them - not on the things I had written about, but on disk: 20 GB free where the seeded 16 GB database needs 32 GB transiently (download AND extract) before the first boot, so the floor is ~46 GB and the 80 GB class is the right one, not the 40 GB one. Box 1 also has only 1.9 GB RAM against a 3.5 GB requirement. That discovery would otherwise have arrived at 3am with the office already dark and the survivor half-built. THE SAME ROUND, A SECOND FINDING I DID NOT EXPECT: to make the owner's 'yes' cost one command instead of an afternoon, I wrote company-provision-survivor.py --plan, and its FIRST action - validating the credential rather than trusting its presence - found that the HETZNER_API_TOKEN in the repo .env returns HTTP 401. It is dead, and no working Hetzner credential exists on the laptop or on either box. A plan that prints 'token present (64 chars)' and then dies on 401 wastes the reader's time; this one reports 'invalid' before anything else and then says exactly what IS and IS NOT blocked - which turned out to be one step (creating the server) and nothing else. RULES: (a) turn a readiness claim into a preflight that returns a verdict on the real candidate, because prose cannot know that a disk is too small; (b) validate a credential, never merely its presence - 'present' and 'works' are different facts and only the second one is useful; (c) when one step is blocked, say which step and what remains possible, because 'blocked' without a boundary reads as 'everything is blocked' and stops work that could continue. Also flagged while looking: the same .env holds HETZNER_PASSWORD, the plaintext CONSOLE password for the account - not needed by any automated flow, and able to do anything the account can including deleting backups. Recommended into a password manager and rotation; not used.
