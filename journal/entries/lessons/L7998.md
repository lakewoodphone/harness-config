**L7998 · Blind-critic iteration on the sign: the method, the mesh staging trap, and the seven findings that moved the design**

Owner asked (2026-10-18) for two separate iterations on the Waze Device sign, both driven by a critic that does not
know the goal: (1) words, (2) graphics/layout as picture-after-picture. Ran both.

**The method works and it is measurable.** Same five questions to a cold reader before and after: the v12 sign got
"would you stop and read it in a shop — **no**"; candidate G got "**yes** ... it earns the three seconds". Cold copy
reads moved from "blunt, price-first, unpolished" to "blunt, practical, no-BS".

**Operational: the mesh places `subagent` children on ZABZ-TECH, not here.** A local path passed to a child does not
exist on that node, and the child correctly refused to review an image it never loaded. Stage any asset on the node
first: `ssh desktop-ts "mkdir C:\Users\ezabz\code\_blind"` then `scp file.png desktop-ts:C:/Users/ezabz/code/_blind/`.
Verify with `dir` on the remote. Text-only prompts need no staging.

**What the cold readers found, and what it changed:**
- The incumbent's worst defect, named independently: **no photograph of the object anywhere.** A 285-dollar physical
 thing, no picture of it. Next: after three seconds they could not say what the object *is*.
- **"285, one time" with no currency symbol read as a quantity, not a price** ("never says 285 *what*").
- **Itemising the parts invites valuing the parts.** "The iPhone 11, the cable and the SIM" produced "a cable is 5,
 a SIM is free, so most of that is markup" and "the phone is the bait, the 8 is the annuity" — the copy created the
 price-comparison the business wanted to avoid. Fixed by naming the device as a device, not as a parts list.
- **"nothing else to buy" printed above an 8-a-month is read as engineered**: "the page is designed to feel like a
 one-time purchase while it is a subscription."
- **The data line is the single most damaging element on the sheet, twice, independently**: "150 MB is absurd for a
 navigation device" and "a cap you set sounds like permission to bill past it"; "at home rates is never defined".
 So megabytes came off the sheet (this also restores the owner's own 2026-10-16 rule, "hours of driving, not
 megabytes", which the audit had since reversed without a recorded decision). The cap promise stays.
- Best line on the sheet, both reads: **"A shop, not a website. Buy it at the counter, bring it back to the counter."**
- Graphics verdicts that were acted on: two-tone headline splits one name into two; the mark is an unchosen favicon
 whose green appears nowhere else; micro-caps "ONE TIME"/"FOR THE DATA" read as a template; a background arc made
 the middle third "look dirty"; "SOLD" at 0.17em tracking reads as "SOLED"; the warranty line plants "drop" in the
 customer's head; the Israel line was buried third.

**Layout:** a 4-zone poster (teal top panel with the khaki price card, paper middle of four equal bands with a khaki
left bar, teal base band), not the 8-block stack every previous version used. Seven renders, `lpt-signs/v13-a..g.html`,
candidate `v13-g.html`. Ink on paper with two teal blocks instead of a full-bleed flood, so it prints on any printer
with no settings.
