**L12738 · Correction to L12223: I reported my window's start as the outage's start; and a maxed cap does not explain the first outage**

**Correction to L12223, and the mechanism is the lesson: I reported the start of my data window as the start of the outage.**

L12223 says the first hosted-refused job is `2026-10-20T05:32:51Z` and calls the block `9.7 hours`. Both are artefacts of where my census began, not properties of the outage.

Measured since, job-level, by the billing thread in `C:\Users\ezabz\_ci2`:
- **First refusal anywhere in October: `2026-10-20T03:31:18Z`**, run `39364724515`, job `Preflight`, labels `ubuntu-24.04`, `runner_name` empty, steps 0, duration 3s.
- Last hosted success before it completed `2026-10-20T03:30:37Z` — a **41-second** gap.
- Window 1 ends: last refused started `15:47:36Z`; first hosted success after `15:58:33Z`.
- Window 2 begins `2026-10-21T05:41:46Z` (last hosted start `05:35:58Z`), still open at 13:50Z.

So it is **two outages separated by 13.6 hours of health**, not one block of 9.7 hours. A reader of L12223 alone would think a single cap trip cleared itself; the truth is the gate died, recovered, and died again — which is a different operational problem.

**The rule, stated so it can be checked:** when a census reports "the first X is at time T", the report must also print the window's own start and end. If T is within one bucket of the window start, the honest sentence is "the first X in my window", never "the first X". My `block_edges.py` did print `hosted job last started BEFORE first refusal: None` — the script told me the answer was clipped and I did not read my own output.

**Two further corrections to L12223's arithmetic:**
1. It costs against **3,000 included minutes** and calls the monthly figure ~74. The plan tier has never been confirmed: `gh api /user` returns no `plan` field at all, and `deploy-env.yml`'s own header states "roughly 465 of GitHub's 2,000 free private-repo minutes" — the Free-tier number. At 2,000 the figure is ~80. Both are assumptions; say which.
2. **A maxed spending limit is not supported by the arithmetic.** Gross billed hosted minutes `2026-10-01T00:00Z` -> first refusal = 7,797 = ~47 of spend, i.e. ~78% of a 60 limit on the 3,000 reading and lower on the 2,000 one. A 60 cap had not been reached when window 1 began. So window 1 may be a **declined payment**, whose annotation is word-for-word identical. That is why the owner must read BOTH the payment method and the budget, and why "raise the limit" may fix nothing. Lower bound caveat: one of the two repos was only partly sampled, so the true gross is higher.
