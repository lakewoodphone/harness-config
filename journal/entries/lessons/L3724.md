**L3724 · pkill on the wrapper left the rsync children writing: I measured a stick my own jobs were hammering and nearly called it counterfeit**

TWO failures in one hour, both mine, both the same shape: I measured a device while my own jobs were still using it, and I trusted the number.

MEASURED 2026-10-13 on the Weinreb stick (PNY USB 3.2.1 FD, 128 GB nominal). Sequence:
1. A first throughput reading of **215 MB/s** - that is page cache, not flash. The sustained rate then settled at 11-19 MB/s.
2. I wanted to stop the copy to test the stick, and ran `sudo pkill -f "weinreb-stick-copy.sh"`. It reported the process gone. **It killed the wrapper SCRIPT only: the three `rsync` children survived, were reparented to init, and kept writing to the stick for the next half hour.** A `pkill` matched against a wrapper leaves the real workers alive.
3. A separate `dd` read-back from an aborted tool call was ALSO still running against the stick.
4. I then measured the stick with `dd` - 300 MiB, bs=1M, conv=fsync - and got **1.3 MB/s**, then 924 kB/s with 64k blocks and 254 kB/s with 8M blocks, and I was two steps into concluding the stick was counterfeit.
5. Control: the same 300 MiB dd to the fast bench SSD gave **104 MB/s**, so the method and the machine were fine - which made the stick look worse, and that was the wrong inference.
6. The truth: `ps aux` showed my own rsync children AND my own dd still on the device. Two of my jobs were competing on a **cacheless** stick.
7. After killing them properly, flushing (`umount` returned in 2s) and re-measuring with nothing else on the device: **17.0 MB/s** - squarely in line with the shop's other sticks (14.5, 19.79, 22.48 MB/s). **The stick was never the problem.**

RULES THAT FOLLOW
- **Kill the workers, not the wrapper.** Match on the real command (`rsync ... <dest>`, `dd ... /mnt/stick`) or kill the process group. Then VERIFY with `ps aux` / `fuser -v <mount>` that nothing is left, before drawing any conclusion about the device.
- **Never measure a device while any of your own jobs is on it**, and never conclude "the hardware is bad" until `ps`/`fuser` says the device is idle. This is the same lesson as L3512 (an A/B where one arm changes two things proves nothing) arriving by a different route.
- **State the regime with every throughput number** (L3512): 300 MiB, bs=1M, conv=fsync, single stream, device idle. A number without its regime is not a measurement.
- **A read-back immediately after a write measures RAM, not flash** - my "3.5 GB/s read" was page cache. Use `iflag=direct`, or drop caches, or accept that the read number is meaningless.

The general form, worth keeping: when a device looks catastrophically slow, the first hypothesis is now "one of my own jobs is still on it", and the check is `ps aux | grep <device>` plus `fuser -v`, not a spec sheet.
