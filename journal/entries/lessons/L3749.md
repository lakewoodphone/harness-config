**L3749 · docs/mesh numbers are an allocation, not a guess: two sessions took 172 in four minutes and only the pre-commit check caught it**

**L — `docs/mesh/<N>-` is an allocation, not a guess: two sessions took 172 within four minutes, and the collision was only visible at commit time.**

Writing this session's records, I surveyed `docs/mesh` at **05:05:00Z** and found 170 taken and 171 free, so
I prepared a master record as 171 and an inventory as 172. Four minutes earlier, at **04:56:33Z**, another
session had committed `170-MASTER-RECORD-ATTENTION-BADGE-AND-FLEET-AUDIT.md`; at **05:06:55Z**, between my
check and my install, it committed **both** `171-CLOUD-AUTHORITY-AUDIT-START-HERE.md` **and**
`172-MASTER-RECORD-SEARCH-SYSTEM.md`. I installed at **05:10:37Z**. Result: two files numbered 171 at one
point, and `172-CRON-PORT-INVENTORY.md` sitting beside `172-MASTER-RECORD-SEARCH-SYSTEM.md`. Resolved by
yielding 172 (theirs predates mine) and `git mv`-ing the inventory to 174, which renumbered every internal
cross-reference too.

**What made it visible at all** was one command run immediately before staging, not at write time:
`ls docs/mesh/17[0-9]-*.md | sed -E 's|.*/([0-9]+)-.*|\1|' | sort | uniq -c`. Before that, I had already
checked "is 171 free" twice and been right both times — the number became stale in under two minutes.

**Rule: a number from a shared namespace is claimed by the COMMIT, not by the file you wrote.** Check for
the number, stage, and **check again between `git add` and `git commit`**, because another actor can take
it in that window and nothing will report it. If two files do collide, the one already committed keeps the
number and the other moves — a rename with history (`git mv`) plus a sweep of every internal reference
(new-title line, cross-doc pointers, and any `§` reference another document makes to it). Never resolve a
collision by leaving both.

This is the same shape as `P13` (concurrent sessions share one repo) and it recurs for every shared
namespace this estate allocates by hand: journal ids (bumped by the tool, so safe), `docs/mesh` numbers
(not safe), worktree and branch names, and settings keys. Only the journal's allocator is atomic.
