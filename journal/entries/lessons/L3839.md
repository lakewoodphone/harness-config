**Pricing a job where the customer supplies the part: our own fitted quote minus the part's street value - then check it against the shop's labour-only precedent.**

The owner's rule for a part we supply is `(cost + shipping) x 1.4 + 65` (`lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/PRICING-RULE.md`). That rule has **no term for a customer-supplied part**, and its flat `+ 65` labour term reads as if it would apply. It should NOT be applied blindly, and here is the arithmetic that shows why.

Worked 2026-10-12 on Halon / 8482217775 (Qin phone battery, `lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/cases/qin-battery-halon-8482217775-2026-08-17.md`):

- Our standing offer to this customer was **75 fitted, our cell included** (sent 2026-09-24).
- The customer is buying a cell for about **40** in her own words (2026-09-16: "in total the battery would be $40 or so").
- `75 - 40 = 35` for the fitting.
- **35 is independently the shop's only recorded "labour only if he brings the part" battery figure:** `dell-inspiron-3593-weingarten-2026-06-15.md`, 2026-06-15 - "$40 charging port + $30 battery = $70 with parts, or $35 labor only if he brings parts".
- **Applying the rule's flat 65 instead would produce 65 + her own 40 = 105 to her, against our own 75 all-in offer** - which punishes the customer for sourcing the part and makes our standing offer look absurd. The formula is for a job where we supply the part.

**The method, transferable:**
1. Take the fitted price we already quoted for that job.
2. Subtract what the part costs the customer on the open market (their own figure, or the maker's).
3. Cross-check the remainder against a recorded labour-only figure for the same class of job in `lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/cases/`.
4. If the two agree, that is the number. If they disagree, prefer the case precedent and say so.

Also confirm the shop even **does** customer-supplied parts before treating it as a policy question - it does (`google-pixel-6-screen-unknown-9086928082-2026-09-18.md`, "quoted - $120 with customer-supplied screen"), so this is a price question, not a permission question.

**Never expose the formula, the cost or the supplier to a customer** - PRICING-RULE.md, Sourcing section. The customer sees the total only.
