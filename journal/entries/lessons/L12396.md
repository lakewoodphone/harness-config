**L12396 · Bag of devices: the fault is usually the chain or the credentials, not the hardware (4 rules, measured)**

Measured across one 13-unit intake on 2026-10-27 (Karmel bag, WO26102712780), where all four of these cost time or nearly cost money.

1. READ THE RAIL BEFORE BLAMING THE PORT. Four units that afternoon read 4.5-12 V low on a weak chain and were perfectly healthy on a proper one: 4.5 V/2.2 A on an "MFi" charger, then 14.8 V/1 A on a PD chain; 5 V/3 A, then 15 V/1.8 A; 5 V/2.9 A, then 14.7 V/1.7 A. A device sitting at 5 V has not been given a fair test, and "it only takes 10 W" is a statement about the cable and adapter until proven otherwise. The same rule saved two units from being mispriced as repairs.

2. THE OWNER'S PRICES ARE PRE-TAX, AND THE LABEL MUST CARRY BOTH NUMBERS. He said it once, plainly: "It's all pre tax." A label reading only "25.00" is a label that collects 25.00 when the customer owes 26.66. Every price label now prints "25.00 + TAX" over "PAY 26.66" - and when a figure is revised (30 -> 25), the superseded tape must come OFF the device, because two tapes on one unit is how the counter collects the wrong amount twice.

3. A LOW CURRENT ON A DEVICE AT 0% IS USUALLY A PRE-CHARGE, NOT A DEAD BATTERY. One LG sat at 0.44 A / 2.2 W showing 0% and "charge more before turning on"; it then went to 0.96 A / 4.6 W and finally negotiated 8.96 V / 1.6 A / 14.3 W and climbed 0% -> 31% on its own. The battery branch was written up as a real possibility and it was wrong. Give a deep-discharged pack time before quoting a battery.

4. ON AN iPAD, "UNAVAILABLE — TRY AGAIN IN N MINUTES" IS ONE RUNG ABOVE "DISABLED — CONNECT TO iTUNES", and the second one costs the customer all their data. Guessing at a passcode moves a device from the first state to the second. On a multi-device intake, collect passcodes from the customer BEFORE testing, and once a device shows the countdown, stop attempting it - a screen repair does not require an erase, but a locked-out device does.

The transferable shape: on a bag of devices, the fault is usually the supply or the customer's credentials, not the hardware. Test the chain, ask for the passcode, and only then price a part.
