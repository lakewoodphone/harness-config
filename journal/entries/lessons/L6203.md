**L6203 · The corpus's owner set is 14% machine - and it was measured, not assumed**

MEASURED 2026-10-14T01:45Z by check_actor_v2.py over /home/zabz/evolution-corpus-v2/owner-turns-final.jsonl: of 1,005 sessions carrying owner turns, **141 (14.0%) contain the mesh parent-agent handshake 'Your parent agent id is', and 132 carry it in their FIRST turn**. All 141 are inside owner-sessions.tsv, i.e. inside the set both round 1 and round 2 call 'the owner'. HOW IT WAS CAUGHT: not by me and not by a script first - by a mesh reader on shard-002, which reported that 12 of its 20 'owner' sessions were dispatch children and that every one of the 20 ids appears in owner-sessions.tsv while none appears in dispatch-sessions.tsv. I then measured it deterministically. WHY ROUND 1's ACTOR RULE MISSED THEM: finalize_corpus.py excludes a dispatch only when 'turns <= 3 AND len(first_turn) > 300 AND BRIEF.match(first_turn)' - a dispatch with four turns, or with a brief phrased outside that narrow regex, survives into the owner set. THE CONSEQUENCE: for those sessions the classified 'ask' is a machine-written brief and the tone is a parent's, so a census over the raw set partly measures the machine. 14% is not a rounding error and it moves the headline numbers. WHAT WAS DONE: check_actor_v2.py counts it with the signatures printed so the number is auditable; aggregate_readers_v2.py drops those sessions and reports BOTH denominators so the size of the correction is visible rather than hidden. LESSON, TRANSFERABLE: an actor separation rule that is a regex over 'looks like a machine' is a heuristic, and heuristics on this corpus leak. The only durable separators found so far are the ones already proven: 'agentPreset' presence, and the mesh's own handshake string, which is a machine's signature of itself and cannot be forged by the owner's typing. When a set's name asserts who is in it, measure the membership; do not infer it from the shape of the text.
