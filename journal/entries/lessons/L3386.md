**L3386 · Three of my monitors were reporting 'unknown' and I read it as silence: a probe validated against its own mock, an endpoint that never published the field, and a recovery alert that cried flap**

THE OFFICE CAME BACK, AND THEN I FOUND TWO MORE OF MY OWN CHECKS THAT COULD NOT FIRE. This entry is about those, because the pattern is now unmistakable: three times in one night I "verified" a monitor against my own assumptions rather than against the real thing.

FINDING 3 - THE LOOP-DEAD PROBE WAS INERT, and it is the probe that matters most. The heartbeat's outcome probe reads payload["autopilot"]["loop_running_ok"] from /ready. The REAL /ready returns 129 bytes: ok, time, identity_store, ceo_agent_id. No autopilot key. So the probe answered "unknown" on every run since the day it was written, and the alarm built to catch the 2026-07-22 thirteen-day silence - a healthy host that did nothing for thirteen days while nobody noticed - could NEVER fire. And I had "verified" it with a hand-written mock that returned the key I expected, written by the same hand that wrote the probe. The mock and the probe agreed on a fiction; the endpoint was never consulted. FIXED BOTH ENDS: /ready (app/main.py) now publishes a real autopilot block - enabled, last_tick_at, seconds_since_tick, loop_running_ok, stale_after_seconds - computed from tick_telemetry.started_at, which the autopilot writes when it ticks and nothing else writes. activity_log would have been wrong: the app writes a row there at startup, so a freshly-booted but idle process would look busy, which is precisely the failure being detected. Verified on a deployed host (the survivor), and verified locally against the real service. AND THE MOCK IS NOW BUILT FROM CAPTURED LIVE PAYLOADS (captured-health.json, captured-ready.json, recorded from the running service), so it cannot agree with an assumption again: if the real shape changes, the mock no longer matches it and the tests fail.

FINDING 4 - THE RECOVERY ALERT WAS NOISE DURING A FLAP. While the owner worked on the office network the link flapped (up 15:04, 500 at 15:06, up 15:10) and he received "the COMPANY IS BACK" for each recovery - several texts about outages he was never told about, because each dip was shorter than the three consecutive failures needed to announce a DOWN. FIXED: a recovery only speaks when its outage was announced (the down mark must be newer than the outage's start); otherwise it is logged as "a flap is not news". Verified both ways: an unannounced flap stays silent, an announced outage still gets its recovery message.

RULES: (a) validate a probe against the REAL service, never against your own mock - if the same hand writes both, they will agree on a fiction, and the fiction will look like a passing test; (b) build test fixtures by CAPTURING the live response, and treat a shape change as a failure rather than something to paper over; (c) a surface that exists to be watched from outside must publish what the watcher needs - an endpoint that returns only what its author found convenient leaves the watcher blind while looking healthy; (d) an alert that closes a loop nobody opened is noise, and noise in an alerting channel is how the channel dies; (e) when a check reports "unknown", that is a FINDING, not a neutral state - three of my checks were reporting unknown, and I read it as "nothing to say".
