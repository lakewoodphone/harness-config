**L1954 · Acer bottom-label N-codes are chassis FAMILY codes, and this family's DC jack is SOLDERED**

**The rule.** Acer prints an **N-code** on the bottom label ("N20C5", "N22C6"). It is a **shared chassis FAMILY code, not a model.** A search for `N20C5` does not name a machine.

**Measured 2026-09-17** (research pass `81c215e3`, five sources): **N20C5** is printed on **Aspire 1**, **Aspire 3 (A315-58 / A315-58G / A315-59)**, **Aspire 5 (A515-56 / A515-56G / A515-57T)** and **Aspire Vero (AV15-51 / AV15-52)**. Sources: eBay 335201848742; Amazon PHONSUN B0B9JWPXYN; Acer Community threads **703045** (A515-56-50TS, N20C5), **690085** (Vero AV15-52-791N), **698575** (Aspire 1). The shop already has one of these on the books — Weissman's **Acer Aspire 3, chassis N22C6** (2026-08-12).

**What it does and does not change for a repair.**
- It does **not** change a DC-jack job: **one aftermarket SKU** covers A315-58, A315-59, A515-56, A515-57T, A317-33/53, A115-32, SF315-52, SF314-42/57, SF313-52, SP314-54N.
- It **does** change everything else. Read the **product name and SNID** off the label (`Aspire A315-58-XXXXX`), or `wmic csproduct get name,identifyingNumber` / `Get-CimInstance Win32_ComputerSystemProduct`.

**The jack on this family is SOLDERED to the motherboard — no DC-in board, no cable harness.** Four independent sources, one of them a hands-on replacement on an **A515-56-51G2** with the identical symptom (oldgod.ru, 2026-02-15: *"on these laptops the connector is soldered to the board"*), plus iFixit 895978 on the **A315-58-350L** and PHONSUN's listing. Treat it as a **hot-air-and-preheat job**, not a $12-iron job; the documented hazard is lifting pads while clearing the old solder.

**🛑 The expensive trap: the PREVIOUS generation's jack is a CABLE.** **A315-42 / A315-54 / A515-43 (chassis N19C1 / N19C3)** uses harness part **50.HEEN2.005 / DC301015**, sold on eBay and Amazon under **nearly identical titles**. Ordering by title buys the cable version and **it will not fit an N20C5 board**. Verify the listing names **A315-58 or A515-56 (or N20C5)** *and* says **"soldering type"**.

**USB-C cannot charge this family.** Notebookcheck's **A515-56** review: the USB-C port *"supports neither Power Delivery nor DisplayPort"*, Cons list *"– No Power Delivery"*, and *"the battery can't be charged via USB-C."* Argos spec for the **A315-58**: *"does not support PD fast charging."* **So never offer a USB-C dock or PD brick as a workaround or a stopgap on these machines — the barrel jack is the only power path.**

**Connector and adapter:** **3.0 mm OD × 1.1 mm ID × 7.7 mm, centre positive, 19 V** (Acer Community 714816, A315-58: *"Output 19V 2.37A; Power 45W; Tip Size 3.0mm x 1.1mm"*). **45 W / 2.37 A is the Acer-stated figure.** The belief that an **i7** SKU ships **65 W** is **aftermarket-only — no Acer source was found.** 65 W is still the sensible brick to stock (same voltage, same tip, more headroom), but **never state an Acer 65 W specification to a customer.**

**The wrong-tip imposters**, which matter because a bad tip mimics a worn jack exactly: **3.5 × 1.35 mm** enters a 3.0 × 1.1 mm jack but cannot grip the 1.1 mm centre pin (the classic "seats but wobbles, charges only pushed to one side"); **3.0 × 1.0 mm** is sold widely as the same part; **5.5 × 1.7 mm** (the generic "yellow tip" sold as "for Acer Aspire") **will not enter at all**. **Caliper the plug — do not eyeball it.**

**Chassis quality is a liability.** The technician inside this exact model: the case *"is crumbling all over… the screen mount broke."* Acer Community 725767 reports bottom-case screws falling out; over-tightening parts brass inserts. **Photograph the machine before opening it** — opening it can create cosmetic damage the shop gets blamed for.
