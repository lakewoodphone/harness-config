**L1947 · A reader that reports capacity but fails every read makes a GOOD card look dead - validate the reader with a card you just saw read**

**Lesson (2026-09-17, Goldstein microSD): a card reader that reports a plausible capacity but fails every
data read will make a perfectly good card look dead. Validate the READER with a card you have just seen
read, or you are measuring your own toolkit.**

Found by accident, chasing a different question. The shop's `14cd:1212` "Super Top microSD reader (SY-T18)"
(serial `121220160204`, port 1-11):

| | patient card (`onn` 32 GB) | a second card (128 GB) |
|---|---|---|
| enumeration | no media at all (`Media removed, stopped polling`, 0 B) | media present, **117.12 GiB reported correctly** |
| READ CAPACITY | `Device not ready` | **works** |
| every data read | never got that far | **`Medium Error` / `Unrecovered read error`** at LBA 0, LBA 112, and on 1 MiB reads — `dd: Input/output error` |

**It read zero sectors from either card.** And its blind spot is the dangerous kind: capacity comes over the
SD **CMD** line, data over the **DAT** lines, so a reader with damaged data lines (or a half-dead bridge)
reports a **correct capacity** and then fails every read. Present that to a technician and it looks exactly
like a dead card. **A card is never "dead" until a reader that has just read another card says so.**

**The rule, which is the same discipline the adversarial pass demanded twice on this same case:**
- **A control that has never controlled anything is not a control.** "Another card" is not a known-good card.
 "This reader read cards before" is not "this reader reads cards now". The only valid control is **a card
 you have just watched read successfully in the same reader, on the same port, in the same sitting.**
- **Prefer a native SD host over a USB bridge** when you need a second opinion: a phone, a camera, a
 Raspberry Pi, or a Mac's built-in slot. A mass-storage bridge is a computer of its own between you and the
 card, and it can fail in ways that imitate the card.
- **Check the shop's drawer for this class of reader.** One that reports capacity and cannot read data is a
 trap: it converts a recoverable card into a "dead" verdict and sends a customer away.

**Recorded against our own history too:** this reader (serial `121220160204`) was the *second* reader in our
**Tabak** (2026-09-09) and **Wilner** (2026-08-31) "dead card" conclusions. Those conclusions also rested on
the first reader showing 0 B, so they stand — but **the second reader contributed nothing valid in either
case**, and a future session should not cite it as independent corroboration.
