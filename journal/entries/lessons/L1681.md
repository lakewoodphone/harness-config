**L1681 · Applied is not the same claim as had-an-effect (C-008 in place, unmeasurable); and a fix aimed at the wrong layer looks identical to a fix that failed - group the survivors by their provenance field**

**L1680 · Two rules from applying axis C's indexer fixes, 2026-09-16.**

**1. "Applied" and "had an effect" are different claims, and only the second one is worth reporting.**
C-008 was a correct-looking structural fix: `else: pass` silently dropped a phone node when its label
was shared by more than `_MAX_PHONES_PER_NAME` numbers, so the change to `union.find("phone:" + d)`
should have given those numbers their own person row. It is in place and it compiles. **It changed
nothing.** `people` went 1,483 → 1,477 (DOWN), and asking the table directly settled it: **zero rows
have exactly one phone and no name**, and all 11 unnamed rows are email-only.

I reached that answer twice. The first attempt was an A/B test that removed the line — which left the
`else:` block empty and failed to compile, so it proved nothing (the script's own md5-verified restore
worked, which is the only reason it was safe). The second attempt asked the database a direct question
instead of re-running the layer twice. **When you cannot tell whether a change did anything, find a
question whose answer differs under the two hypotheses; don't re-run the world hoping a total moves.**
And report the honest version: "in place, structurally correct, no measurable effect on today's data"
— not "applied ✓", which a reader will take as a win.

**2. A fix aimed at the wrong layer looks exactly like a fix that did not work.**
C-009's diff was **not even runnable**: it omitted the closing parenthesis of the `add_contact(` call.
Closed and applied, it cleared **1 of 14** concatenated phone numbers. The tempting conclusions were
"it failed" or "the data is weird". The actual answer came from grouping the survivors by `basis`:
**11 of the 13 were `address_book:manual` — the owner's own contact book** — and `dialpad_ui_contact_row`,
the table C-009 targeted, is not even in the same database. The phone field was being passed whole at
`commsindex.py:642` (`[v.get("phone")]`), so a cell reading `+17127758888+01197236194444` became one
26-digit person with a real customer's name attached.

A shared `_split_phone_tokens()` at both call sites took it **14 → 6**. And the last 6 come from a
**third** layer again — 4 of them `basis=name`, out of `comms.address` itself, which is a different
function from everything so far. **Each layer you fix reveals the next one, and the way to find out
which layer you are looking at is to group the survivors by their provenance field, not to re-read the
plan.** `basis`, `source`, `fetched_at` — the field that says where a row came from is usually the one
that tells you which code to open.

**Corollary worth keeping:** a plan written from measurements can still contain SQL that would not
compile, and a fix that addresses a real defect can address the wrong *site* of it. Read the artifact,
then group the survivors.
