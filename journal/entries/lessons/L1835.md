**L1835 · The layout viewport is not the visible band on iOS: a fixed sheet's first element is what the keyboard hides**

**Rule:** on iOS, a `position: fixed` sheet anchored to the **layout** viewport is not anchored to what the reader can see. When the keyboard is up the visual viewport is shorter AND **offset**, so a sheet pinned to `top: 0` starts above the first visible pixel — and the FIRST element in the sheet is what disappears. If a phone layout must be right, assert it in pixels against `window.visualViewport`, from a client plugin; a stylesheet cannot read that number.

**Evidence.** The owner, twice in one day, 2026-09-14. First: *"only part of it shows up, I can't scroll, and I can't click"* (Submit — fixed by making the question card a full-screen sheet). Then: *"I can't scroll up enough to see the actual question. I just see the options for responses"* — the question text is the first child of the card, so `inset: 0` put it above the visible band while the options stayed in the middle. A headless Chrome at 393x852 measured that layout as **correct**; the failure only appears once the visual viewport is offset.

**How to test it without a phone.** `scripts/question-card-live-probe.py --band 240,420` forces `visualViewport.offsetTop/height` in the real app and reads the geometry back. Measured 2026-09-14: before the fix the card's title sat at y=39 with the band at y=240 (`fits:false`); after it, y=279 (`QUESTION VISIBLE IN BAND: True`).

**Generalisation.** The same trap applies to anything anchored to the viewport on a phone — the composer, the attention badge, a toast. `100dvh` fixes the HEIGHT of that class of bug; only `visualViewport.offsetTop` fixes the OFFSET.
