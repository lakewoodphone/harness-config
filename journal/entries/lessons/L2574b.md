**L2574b · I audited and fixed the public site while the console the business runs on every day was unusable - the operator's own screens are the product**

Recorded 2026-09-23, after the owner looked at the staff console and said: "I'm surprised you called this
like we're basically done."

WHAT I DID WRONG: for three days the work was measured against the wrong surface. I audited and fixed the
PUBLIC site - truthful hours, the fabricated FAQ address, the Google sign-in anchor, the closure notice,
the customer-login gate - and reported each as a win, correctly. Meanwhile the console the business
actually runs on every day was, in the owner's words, "too many things happening": a nav with seven or
eight entries, two floating buttons, an AI workspace nobody uses, a work-order form with so many fields it
is slow to fill at a counter, and charging buried away from the work order. None of that appeared in any
audit I ran, because I never audited the operator's own screens. My "customer-facing half" framing was
true and useless: the shop's customer-facing half is the counter.

THE RULE: **the surface that the owner and the staff touch every day is the product; everything else is
secondary.** Before reporting a product area as done, walk its screens as the operator does - the nav, the
main flow, the form - and count what they have to see and click. A B-fix list (SEO, auth, hours) can all
be green while the thing they use hourly is unusable, and the owner experiences only the second one.

SECOND RULE: "integrated and verified" is not "ready". My report that ended "the guard is proven live" was
accurate and was read as "we are done". When a milestone is green, say in the same breath what is still
not usable, so the owner is never surprised by his own product.
