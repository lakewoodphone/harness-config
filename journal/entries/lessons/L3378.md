**L3378 · A production communications row's createdAt for a CALL is an ingest time, not the call time: it was 6.5 days late and it manufactured a wrong story about a customer**

**Recorded 2026-10-08 (ZABZ-YOGA). Found by asking "what actually happened in that call" instead of
trusting the row that said it happened.**

**The fact.** On production, `communications.createdAt` for a **call** row is when the row was
**ingested**, not when the call happened. Measured on mrs rosenbaum (+17326783677):

| the call | production `communications.createdAt` | `dialpad_call_full.date_started_ms` | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 136 s outbound | 2026-09-10 01:15:25 | **2026-09-03 16:49:59** | **+6 d 8 h 25 m** |
| 32 s outbound | 2026-09-01 10:34:25 | **2026-08-31 19:11:08** | **+15 h 23 m** |

The offset is not fixed - six and a half days on one row, fifteen hours on another - so it cannot be
corrected by subtracting anything. It is a harvest lag, and it varies.

**How it bit.** I told the owner *"the last contact of any kind with her, 28 days ago"* from the
production row. The true last contact was **35 days** earlier, on 2026-09-03. Worse, the whole
interpretation of the case followed the wrong number: I read that call as a live, unanswered
follow-up from the shop and built the story "she has been waiting on us for 51 days" on top of it. The
transcript says the opposite - **the customer closed the job herself on that call**. A wrong timestamp
manufactured a wrong narrative, and the narrative then read as evidence.

**The tell, and it is worth carrying.** Two completely unrelated customers both showed outbound calls
at `2026-09-10 01:15` in production - kaplan at 01:15:21 and rosenbaum at 01:15:25, four seconds
apart. Two customers cannot be called four seconds apart by one person. **Coincident timestamps across
unrelated records are a harvest artefact, not a coincidence in the world** - that is the signal to go
and read the source-of-truth table rather than the derived one.

**The rule.** For any claim about *when* the shop spoke to a customer - last contact, response time,
"we followed up", "they never called back" - read **`dialpad_call_full.date_started_ms`** (and
`duration_seconds`, `state`) on the authority. The production `communications` row is fine for *what
was said* and for the message body; it is not a clock. `transcription_text` on the same table is also
where the actual content of old calls lives, which is how this case was settled at all - the case file
had recorded the device as possibly an "LG Q6" for seven weeks while the 2026-08-18 call says *"the
MP3 player thing"* in the shop's own voice.

**And the general shape, which is the third time tonight:** a derived table was read as if it were the
source, and it made a confident wrong statement about a customer. The first was the open work-order
board (L3422), the second was duplicate comms rows (P2862/L3352), this is the third. The column that
lies is always the same kind: a timestamp or a status that the *pipeline* wrote rather than the
*event*.
