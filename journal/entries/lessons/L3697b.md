THE SPLIT, measured after W2789:
 24 Amazon's 2026-08+ CATEGORY-ONLY layout. Subjects read "Delivered 4 items: Personal Care, Skin Care,
 and more" - a category, no product names, and the body agrees. **No parser can recover these**

 **The parser reads the BODY only**, so all 28 are dropped while the naming subject sits in the same
 row. That is recoverable.

AND A TRAP WORTH MORE THAN THE RECOVERY. A subject yields ONE item and a count; the body yields all of them.
So a subject-derived item list is PARTIAL - and **an unmarked partial list is worse than an honest gap,
because an order showing one item LOOKS itemised.** A session optimising for the count would take itemisation
from 92 per cent to 96 per cent and quietly make 28 orders look complete when each is missing most of its
lines. Filed as item 79 with three requirements: the fallback items must be MARKED, the marker must be
queryable, and a subject-derived row must never outrank a body-derived one. `amazon_orders.first_item_text`
already stores a first-item string, so the concept exists in the estate and the marker can follow it.

THE GENERAL SHAPE, which is tonight's lesson again. The number that moved last round - unitemised 80 to 52 -
was worth having, but the number that is LEFT was worth more: it splits into a group where effort is wasted
and a group where effort pays, and only classification tells them apart. **A backlog is not a quantity, it is
a set of causes, and the cheapest thing to do with one is count the causes before working any of them.**
