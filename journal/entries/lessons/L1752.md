**L1752 · The split-brain was a ghost store: ask who WRITES a store, not what it contains**

EVIDENCE: 2026-09-16, ZABZ-YOGA. Two live production databases appeared to be diverging in both directions, which looked like a hard reconciliation problem. It was not. The reconciliation was the easy half; the real defect was an unfinished migration, and the way to see that was to ask *who writes this store* rather than *what does it contain*.

WHAT WAS MEASURED
- The Heroku store has exactly ONE non-local backend: pid 13240 from client_addr 71.104.140.242 (the office). `ss` on secratary matched the single socket (local port 57836) to uvicorn pid 1059488 = secretary-api.service.
- The schedule is visible in the store's own data, not just the crontab: sync rows cluster at minute :09 (102 rows) and :39 (106).
- **109,702 of 116,893 communications (93.8%) are our own push.** The website's own rows STOP DEAD at 2026-08-31 22:28:06.776Z.
- The store's own user has NO <local> backend across 4 samples, so the Heroku dyno is not even connected to its own database.

THE CONCLUSION
The website moved off Heroku on 2026-08-31 to the container Postgres on the Hetzner box. The migration repointed the BACKEND's DATABASE_URL and never repointed the SECRETARY's PHONE_TECH_FULL_DATABASE_URL. So `secratary` has spent sixteen days faithfully pushing Dialpad history into a store that nothing customer-facing reads. **It is not a second live store; it is a write-only ghost at an abandoned target.** Deliberate machinery, abandoned target, not legacy code and not an accident.

WHY IT MATTERED THAT NOBODY NOTICED
The push machinery was given a cron on 2026-09-15 precisely BECAUSE the website had gone 11.5 hours without a customer message. The fix was applied to the wrong end: a schedule was added to a writer aimed at a dead target. Both monitors of the push can only fail invisibly because cron discards their exit codes, and `production-comms-freshness.py` currently reports `ok:true` while measuring the retired store. A health check that resolves against the wrong database is worse than no health check: it is a green light wired to nothing.

FOUR RULES EARNED
1. **Ask who WRITES a store before you compare what it CONTAINS.** I had already measured the row-level fork exactly (111,749 shared uuids, 621 one way, 5,144 the other) and drawn the wrong shape from it. The writer census took one query with a sharper question and reversed the plan.
2. **A migration is not finished when the new path works; it is finished when the OLD path has no writers left.** The backend switched in one commit on 2026-08-31. Nothing enumerated the other writers, so the second one kept running for sixteen days.
3. **A freshness check must name its store.** `production-comms-freshness.py` says ok:true. It is measuring the abandoned database. Any monitor that answers "is production fresh?" without naming which production is a false green waiting to happen.
4. **When a store is retired, its READERS are the bigger half of the migration than its writer.** Here: 1 writer to repoint, 31 readers to repoint. The reader list includes `daily-partner-sync.ps1`, which exports partner PAYOUT orders from a database frozen at 2026-08-31, and a report family that produced the commercial "August growth" figures cited in the company's own audit log. Those numbers were computed from a store nobody had written to for two weeks.
