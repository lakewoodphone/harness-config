**L1956 · Three of my own test harnesses produced FALSE FAILURES in one session - validate the instrument before trusting a failure**

**Lesson (2026-09-17, one session): three of my own test harnesses produced FALSE FAILURES. A test that has
never been shown to pass is not evidence — validate the instrument before you trust any failure it reports.**

Every wrong conclusion I reached today, and the instrument bug behind it:

| what the harness said | the truth | my bug |
|---|---|---|
| "the replayed block changed across a power-cycle, therefore the card" | proves nothing about card vs reader | `authorized=0` re-enumerates the **bridge** too — an invalid inference, not a code bug |
| "NO MATCH — bytes differ from what Windows read" | hashes were **identical** | compared SHA-256 **case-sensitively** (`A-F` vs `a-f`) |
| script hung the session | — | unbounded read loop against a device that stalls; no `timeout`, no fail-fast |
| "reader CANNOT handle this large card" | the card reads at **36.5 MB/s** | `dd of=/dev/null \| wc -c` counts **stdout**, and I had sent the data to /dev/null — so it reported FAIL for **every** device, always |

**Four false or void results in one session, and every one of them looked like a real finding.** The one that
scares me is the last: a fail-fast "safety" check that always fails is worse than no check, because it was
about to make me declare a perfectly good 128 GB card dead.

**The rules, all of which are the same rule applied to code instead of hardware:**
- **Run the test against something you already know PASSES before you trust it failing.** The bench's own
 SSDs gave 175 and 95 distinct blocks and a file that genuinely is one block gave exactly 1 — that is why I
 could trust "one identical block at every LBA" on the patient card. Apply the same to every harness.
- **A comparison is part of the instrument.** Case-fold, trim, and print both sides before declaring a
 mismatch. My hash comparison printed values that were visibly identical next to the word "NO MATCH" — the
 output contradicted the verdict and I nearly reported the verdict.
- **Every read loop gets a `timeout` and a fail-fast pre-check.** One unbounded loop cost a session hang and
 the owner's confidence.
- **When a harness reports failure, suspect the harness first — then prove it with an independent path.**
 Here: `dd` said fail, raw SCSI `sg_raw` said `SCSI Status: Good`, `blkid` found a filesystem, and a
 36.5 MB/s read followed. Three independent signals contradicted the harness; one of them was right.
- **False alarms are not free even when caught.** They cost the owner's attention, they nearly binned a good
 card, and each one has to be retracted in writing. The cost of a 10-second control run is nothing next to
 that.

**And the same discipline is what finally made the customer's verdict trustworthy:** the reader was proven
good on a 64 MB card (byte-exact against a second host) *and* a 117 GiB exFAT card (36.5 MB/s) **before** its
reading of the patient card was believed — and the reading came back identical. **Validate the instrument,
then believe the measurement.**
