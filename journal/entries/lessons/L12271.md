**L12271 · A device tree is not an inventory - the event log is**

A device tree is not an inventory of what was ever connected - the event log is. Whiteman Dell Latitude 7430 (WO2609030005, customer 1624), 2026-10-22: I concluded from Get-PnpDevice non-present entries that "Windows has never seen a mouse on this laptop" and told the owner so. Wrong. A Logitech Unifying Receiver (USB\VID_046D&PID_C52B) had enumerated on 2026-10-18 12:56:23, recorded as Microsoft-Windows-UserPnp event 20003 "Driver Management ... add Service HidUsb", corroborated by C:\Program Files\LogiDownloadAssistant created the same minute. The phantom was missing because the tree gets pruned AND because a USB controller uninstall+reboot - which we ran on this machine that afternoon - deletes and re-creates the root hubs, wiping the record of every device that ever worked. When two queries disagree about a device's history, the device tree is the one that lies. Before concluding "this device never worked here", read Microsoft-Windows-UserPnp 20001/20003 and Kernel-PnP in the System log, and DEVPKEY_Device_LastArrivalDate on devices still present. Cost of getting it wrong: I told the owner the machine was sold with dead USB ports, and that the firmware update was the only change - when the ports were proven working six days after hand-over and two candidate events sat in the window.
