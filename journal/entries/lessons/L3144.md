**L3144 · You cannot audit what a system pays for by grepping for key names: pydantic resolves them by field name, so a used key looks unused**

2026-09-30, round 26. I ran a by-name audit of the 31 credentials in the provider env, counting referencing files across the app, /home/zabz/bin, harness-config/scripts and ceo-kernel. It returned three keys with zero references anywhere: QWEN_API_KEY, SPIDER_API_KEY and TAVILY_API_KEY. Two of those conclusions are known wrong or expected: SPIDER was added by me today so zero is correct; TAVILY is PROVEN in use, because the app calls _search_tavily in app/web_access.py and its usage endpoint reports 2,601 credits consumed on the Researcher plan with 1,601 already on pay-as-you-go. WHY THE METHOD FAILED, and this is the transferable part: app/config.py resolves provider credentials through a pydantic settings model, and pydantic-settings maps a field named tavily_api_key to the env var TAVILY_API_KEY by CONVENTION, so the literal string TAVILY_API_KEY never appears in the source. Where an alias is written explicitly, as with HA_SECURITY_API_KEY at config.py:125, the name IS present - which is exactly why the audit looked reliable on most keys and silently under-reported on the ones resolved by convention. The general rule: an absence of a literal string is not evidence of absence of use, whenever a framework resolves names by convention. For a money question the only trustworthy test is a RUNTIME one - does the provider have a config field, and does a real call succeed. Two consequences: first, no cancellation list goes to the owner off a grep, because cancelling a live provider on a false negative breaks something that was working; second, this is precisely why the capability registry being built in this round carries a REACHABLE flag derived from runtime probing and not from text search. My own by-name audit was the wrong instrument, and checking how resolution worked before acting on it is the only reason a wrong answer did not reach him.
