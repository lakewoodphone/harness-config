**L2352 · An unrecorded recommendation is not a recommendation; and never scrape Amazon search pages**

Kind: a recommendation the owner acts on but that never reaches the journal does not exist. On 2026-09-22 he said "i want to buy a thermometer like you recommended" - and there was no record of it anywhere: journal search "thermometer" = no match; ps_memory_search = 0 rows; grep of ~/.dsh/sessions = none; VS Code workspaceStorage = none. Either a past session recommended it and died without writing, or he is remembering a conversation in another harness. Either way the cost is his: he was about to spend money on my advice and I could not reproduce the advice.

Rule: when I recommend a product, a purchase, a dosage, a vendor, a route - anything he might act on later - it goes into journal/decisions in the same turn, with price, source URL and the reason. A conversational recommendation is not a record. This is the same failure class as P4 (the improvement loop does not close) and the same cost as reporting a stale database as truth: the next session cannot verify what the last one said, so it either re-researches from zero or, worse, answers from vibes.

Also learned (cheap): do NOT scrape Amazon search-result pages - one firecrawl_scrape of an amazon.com/s?k= page returned 224 KB of junk (sprite URLs, ad redirect links, base64 extension errors) with zero usable prices, and spilled to a temp file. Amazon product detail pages by ASIN, or a search engine snippet carrying the price, are the efficient path.

**L2352 · One non-GSM-7 character halves the SMS budget and silently chops the message**

# One non-GSM-7 character halves the SMS budget and silently chops the message

Measured 2026-09-23T20:16:44Z (owner's phone, sms_log 908 -> the delivered joke): the reply
arrived as "Why do radiologists make terrible comedians? Because every time they tell a
joke, they say \"I'll know more once I see the full..." — 129 chars, 1 segment, gsm7, the
punchline gone. The owner asked why, and correctly pointed out that longer texts DO reach
him.

Cause: `trim_to_segments` (app/sms_service.py) picks its character budget from
`_is_gsm7(text)`, which is all-or-nothing. GSM-7 and 2 segments = 153*2 = 306 chars;
ONE curly quote, em dash or ellipsis anywhere = UCS-2 and 2 segments = 67*2 = 134 chars.
The model's joke was >134 chars, so it was cut at a word boundary with "..." appended.
Reproduced byte for byte by calling the pre-fix function on the joke plus one em dash.

The longer texts he remembered (410, 1006 chars in sms_log) never went through that
function: proactive owner messages use `send_message`, which does not trim. Two send paths,
two limits — which is why "you sent longer text" was true and consistent with a chop.

Rule: never derive a truncation budget from a whole-message encoding test; fold the
typographic characters that trigger the switch FIRST and re-measure. And when a truncation
does happen, log it at WARNING with before/after sizes — the only log line for the chopped
reply was "129 chars, 1 segment(s) [gsm7]", from which nobody could tell that 100+
characters had been dropped.

Fixed: `fold_typography_to_gsm7()` + folding inside `trim_to_segments` + a TRIMMED warning
in both `trim_to_segments` and `_prepare_sms_chat_reply`; regression tests in
`tests/test_sms_trim_gsm7_cliff.py`. Emoji/Hebrew still get the UCS-2 budget.
