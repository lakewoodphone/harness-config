**L2079 · The vendor's automatic arrival email is the shipment's proof of life: a ticket number is not a shipment and a silent inbox is a finding**

Rule: 300DDR's arrival email is a free independent confirmation that a device is actually in the lab. If a ticket has been open more than a day or two with no "We just received your device" from helpdesk3@300dollardatarecovery.com, the device did not arrive. Check for that email before telling anyone a drive is being recovered.

Evidence, 2026-09-18. Swept the whole 300DDR domain family in lakewoodphoneandtech@gmail.com over a 20-day window. Ticket #74048 (Weissman, WD PC SN740 512GB) appears EXACTLY ONCE - the 2026-09-15 confirmation email. No arrival email. By contrast the two tickets that genuinely arrived the same day both got one, automatically and within minutes of each other: #73890 21:22 UTC and #73896 21:17 UTC, both reading "We just received your device! Your recovery rate is $300". The email is automatic on check-in, so its absence is evidence, not silence.

What the absence exposed: the Weissman case had two machines disagreeing about whether the parcel left the shop. The office Mac mini recorded "shipping out today (2026-09-15)" and set the status to SHIPPING TO LAB; the bench record said not-yet-shipped. No carrier and no tracking number existed anywhere. The missing arrival email resolves the disagreement in favour of "never shipped" - two weeks were lost to an assumption, and the customer paid a $160 deposit on 2026-09-15 against a "~2 weeks" promise that has now expired.

Generalised rules:
1. A ticket number is not a shipment. A ticket is issued by a form submission, before anything moves.
2. "Shipping out today" written in a note is an intention, not a tracking number. Only a carrier + tracking number is evidence of shipment.
3. Prefer an automatic vendor-side confirmation over our own notes whenever one exists. It is generated by a system that has no incentive to be optimistic.
4. An absent confirmation is a finding. When a system reliably emits a signal on an event, silence about that event is data.
5. Never let a customer-facing promise of "it is being recovered" rest on our own assertion when an independent check is one email away.

Also from the same sweep, same session: Golembick's case had been marked UNFIXABLE at the bench (controller-level, chip-off or nothing) and the customer was told it was unrecoverable - while the lab read 100% of the sectors by fixing an interface fault and produced a raw file listing. A failed bench reflow is not proof of a dead controller. See L-series entry on that case and the case file itself.
