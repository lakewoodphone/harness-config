**L11008 · A body-file that starts with its own title gives the entry two headings and a broken sha**

When you append to the journal with `--body-file`, the body must NOT begin with a title line.
`journal.py append` writes `**<ID> · <title>**` itself, immediately under the `<!-- e:... -->` marker.
If the body file's first line is also a heading, the entry ends up with two, and the metadata `sha`
(computed over heading + body by `entry_hash()`) then disagrees with what `check` recomputes —
`ERROR ... metadata sha=5b12... does not match the body (748c8f...)`.

Found 2026-10-20 on `entries/lessons/L10818.md`, which carried two different phrasings of its own
title. Nobody else's entry to rewrite, so the words were left alone and only the metadata was
repaired — recompute with the tool's own functions, never a hand-rolled hash:

```python
spec = importlib.util.spec_from_file_location('journal', '<repo>/journal/tools/journal.py')
j = importlib.util.module_from_spec(spec); sys.modules['journal'] = j; spec.loader.exec_module(j)
e = j.parse_entry(Path('<entry>.md'), '<kind>') # Entry is a dict, not an object:
want = j.entry_hash(e['heading'], e['body']) # use e['heading'], not e.heading
```

`parse_entry` returns a **dict subclass**, so `e.heading` raises `AttributeError` while `e['heading']`
works. `journal.py index --force` does not repair this — the sha lives in the entry's own trailing
`<!-- j2 ... sha=... -->` comment, not in the index.

Two rules that follow: a body file starts with prose, never with `**L123 · ...**`; and when `check`
reports a sha mismatch, repair the metadata rather than the wording, because the wording is the record
and the hash is the opinion.
