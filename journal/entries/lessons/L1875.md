**L1875 · _wallpaper_label() prints the DRN for lakewood but a per-site RANK elsewhere, so 'CHICAGO 1' is not a DRN**

**`_wallpaper_label()` prints the registry DRN for `lakewood` but a per-site RANK for every other site. A label like `CHICAGO 1` is therefore not a DRN and must never be joined as one.**

Deployed `fleet_api.py`, `_wallpaper_label(drn_number)`:

- `site == 'lakewood'` → `f"DEVICE {drn_number}"` — the DRN itself.
- any other site with more than one device → `f"{site.upper()} {n}"` where `n = COUNT(*) FROM fleet_devices WHERE site = ? AND drn <= ?` — a **rank**, not the DRN.
- a lone device at a site → the bare site name uppercased (e.g. `BALTIMORE`).
- any exception → falls back to `f"DEVICE {drn_number}"`.

Proof this is live in the wild, not a code-reading exercise: the owner's hand-kept sheet ends with two rows labelled `CHICAGO 1` and `CHICAGO 2`. Chicago holds exactly two devices — `drn 1006` (`c4xffwnpcv`) and `drn 1007` (`jcjg4k457p`) — which is precisely what yields those two labels, and the sheet's serials match that ranking exactly. So the owner typed those rows off the screens.

Consequences:

1. A sheet row labelled `CHICAGO 1` resolves to **DRN 1006**, not to DRN 1. Joining it as a number is silently wrong.
2. Site labels are **not stable identifiers**: add or retire a device at a site and every label at that site shifts on the next render.
3. Because the labels appear verbatim on the sheet, their presence is direct evidence the sheet was transcribed from device screens — which is what makes the row 10–43 discrepancies meaningful rather than mere typos.
