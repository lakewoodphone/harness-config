## L — The reaper was fine; the 15-minute tick simply had not come round yet

**Closes the open question in handoff H12282** (2026-10-21 22:50 UTC), which recorded 22 items sitting in
`running` with `lease_until` of 22:41:35 and 22:44:57 and said the reaper had possibly been "RUN, not
TESTED".

**The reaper works.** Measured 2026-10-21 22:53Z on the authority:

```
python3 ~/bin/work.py reap
 -> reaped {"count": 23, "items": [1061, 1072, 1073, 1081, 1005, 1006, 1062, 1063, 1068,
 1074, 1079, 939, 1064, 1065, 1075, 1076, 1077, 1078, 1080, 991, 1007, 1017, 964]}

project counters: todo 7 -> 30, running 24 -> 1
```

And the reason nothing had happened on its own: **the last recorded sources tick was `2026-10-21T22:37:01Z`**,
which is *before* the earliest lease expiry of 22:41:35. The scheduled reaper had not had a tick since the
leases expired. `reap` runs from `run-wake-sources.sh` on a 15-minute schedule; at 22:53 the next tick was
still pending.

**So "expired but still running" was true and also harmless**, for up to fifteen minutes. That is the design:
a lease is honoured until expiry, and reap sweeps on its next tick. There is no reaper defect here, and I am
recording that explicitly because H12282 left the possibility open and a future session would otherwise
re-investigate it.

**Two things this taught.**

1. **Distinguish "expired" from "unreaped" before calling it a defect.** The check is one command: run
 `work.py reap` and read the count. A count above zero means the mechanism works and the schedule simply
 had not fired. A count of zero *with* expired leases is the defect. Do not infer the second from the
 first — they look identical in `state='running'`.
2. **A scheduled job's last-run timestamp is the first thing to read, not the last.** `tail` the sources log
 for the tick time before theorising about why nothing changed. Here the tick time alone explained it, and
 I had already written a handoff that framed it as suspicious.

**Honest note on what I did.** Running `reap` by hand is state-changing: it freed 23 claims. Those leases had
all expired, so the system's own contract already treated them as free, and the scheduled reaper would have
done the same within fifteen minutes — but if the orchestrator was merely slow rather than gone, freeing the
leases lets another actor claim the same items. The lease exists precisely to time-bound that, so this was
the mechanism working, not me overriding it. Recorded because the alternative reading is worth a reader's
attention.
