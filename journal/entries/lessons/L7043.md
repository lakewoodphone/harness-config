**L7043 · A relation between rows needs a FIXED POINT, not one pass - and my explanation of the leftovers was a guess until I measured it**

Measured 2026-10-16 on the CFO ledger. 893 duplicates carried a category differing from their parent's; cfo-resolve.py's duplicate-copy rung LEARNS FROM duplicate categories, which is how two P&L errors were manufactured earlier in the session, so aligning them removed 893 contradictory inputs. The first pass aligned 875 and left 108 still disagreeing, not the 18 I expected: a duplicate whose PARENT was itself aligned changed underneath it, so the relation has to be iterated to a fixed point rather than fixed in one pass - later passes reported nothing left to align. THE PROOF THAT NOTHING MOVED had to be done on identical data: one snapshot duplicated, the alignment reverted in one copy only by replaying its own audit_log, and the statement run on both - every band in every entity identical. That isolation is what separated my change from the loop writing six transactions and resolving nine rows in the same minutes, and without it I would have reported a movement that was not mine. AND A CLAIM I MADE AND THEN MEASURED: I wrote in the commit that the leftover 108 were 'duplicate category names'; select entity_id, name, count(*) from categories group by 1,2 having count(*) > 1 returns ZERO groups. The truth is 18 rows whose parent's name does not exist for the duplicate's own entity (a personal row cannot take Cost of Goods Sold) and 90 with the SAME name on a different ENTITY, which is correct because categories is per-entity - 51 of the 90 being the escape101/lpt Materials and Equipment pair. Corrected in a NEW commit rather than by rewriting the old one. Two rules: when one row's field is derived from another row's, iterate until nothing changes; and when your explanation of a leftover count is a guess, measure it before it goes in the log.
