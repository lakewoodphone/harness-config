## A quotation is verified only by an elision-aware membership test against the source — not by the worker saying "verified", and not by grep on one string

Measured 2026-10-11 on 629 quoted spans in four synthesis documents.

The workers each claimed to have grep-verified their quotations, and several reported "0 misses".
Their claims were not enough, and neither was a naive check:

1. **First check was wrong and said 0 spans.** The regex required the minimum length *inside* the
 pattern (`[^”"]{25,}`), so the engine skipped short quotations and paired a short opening quote
 with a distant closing quote, manufacturing spans nobody wrote. Rule: find the quote **pair**
 first (non-greedy), filter by length afterwards.
2. **Documents nest curly quotes inside straight ones** (`"… the words “Not Responding.”…"`). A
 pattern that excludes both quote kinds inside cannot pair them; the orphaned closing quote becomes
 a new opening and the drift produces fake spans. Fixed by two passes and by allowing a complete
 curly pair inside a straight-quoted span.
3. **Authorial elisions are legitimate but are not verbatim strings.** A quotation joined across
 `[…]` must not be checked as one string — split at the elision and require every part to be found.
 Before that rule the checker screamed about quotes that were perfectly honest.
4. **Source artefacts must be normalised on both sides**: the extracted text writes "beyond reason
 ." with a space before the full stop, and markdown block-quote markers (`> `) leak into
 multi-line quotations. Normalise both corpora for markdown emphasis, `^> `, ` > `, and whitespace
 before punctuation, then compare.

Result with all four in place: 595 of 629 spans found verbatim (94.6 %); the book summary 150 of 150.
The 34 residuals were real and explainable — quotations from live web pages that were read but not
archived in full (they carry URLs), Hebrew text, and two labels the checker mistook for quotations.

Two further rules the same job taught:

- **Watch the corpus, not just the quotation.** A verifier worker dumped three fetched HTML files
 into `_raw/` while other workers were grepping `_raw/` as the book's ground truth — a quotation
 could have matched foreign text and been reported as a book quotation. They were moved out and
 every quotation was re-verified against a corpus containing only the book. Reserve the input
 directory: announce it read-only for the fleet, or give every worker its own copy.
- **The tool is `~/code/noson-yanofsky-profile/tools/verify-quotes.py`** and it is 120 lines. It
 costs seconds per document and it is the difference between "a worker says the quotations are
 checked" and "the quotations are checked".
