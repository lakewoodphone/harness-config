**L12231 · First live pay-notice send: E.164 recipients, what SENT means, and reading the recipient back**

Three facts from the first live pay-notice send (2026-10-21, WO26101812753 draft 68).

1. **A draft copies `customers.phone` verbatim, and Dialpad rejects a bare 10-digit number with
 400.** `pay-notice.service.ts` sets `toPhone` from `order.customer.phone`; this customer is
 stored as `7329983106`; the provider wants `+17329983106`; the send failed once with
 "Dialpad SMS request failed with 400" and left a `FAILED` communications row (142427) rather
 than a false send. **58 of 603 production customers store a phone with no country code** - one
 in ten customers cannot be texted by this flow until the recipient is normalised.
 Fix in the service (`normalizePhone`); the CLI compensates at draft time *and* at the sender
 seam so a draft already stored can be sent without rewriting an approved body.

2. **"SENT" is the provider accepting a message, not the customer receiving it.** The flow
 recorded `providerMessageId 6467859792044032`, and `GET https://dialpad.com/api/v2/sms/<id>`
 answers **404** - there is no per-message read, and no Dialpad API exposes handset delivery.
 The honest sentence is "accepted by the provider, delivery to the handset unconfirmed", and it
 is the sentence the case file and production note now carry.

3. **Read the recipient back from the row, not from the word SENT.** Verified after the send:
 `communications 142428 phoneNumberTo = 7329983106` - the customer's own last ten digits, and
 neither the shop business line (+17326552355) nor the pay-by-phone line (+18483222660). This
 estate has already produced a green `SENT` addressed to itself (pain P2477), so the check is
 the customer's digits in the row, every time.
