**L7038 · The duplicate-copy rung will pair two payments that share only an amount and a date - so a book can inherit the wrong category from an unrelated charge**

Measured 2026-10-16 on the CFO ledger, the day after finding the same mechanism. A 500.00 Chase card payment from BoA checking (CHASE CREDIT CRD DES:EPAY ID:XXXXX50491) was categorised RENT because rule CR-73dcaeabe747, method duplicate-copy, cited a 2026-06-02 500.00 row - a Zelle payment to Adeena Zabrowsky, the book's recurring monthly rent - as a duplicate of it. The two rows share an amount and a date and nothing else: one is a person-to-person Zelle, the other is a card payment from the same account. The rule's own evidence showed the disagreement (one copy Rent, one copy Credit Card Payment) and the wrong one was written. Corrected: the rule is retracted to a rejected file with the contradiction quoted as its reason, the row is corrected Rent -> Credit Card Payment after a backup with a recategorize audit_log entry, and the arithmetic is lpt net income -44,371.04 -> -43,871.04, delta +500.00, with escape101 and personal unchanged. Measured after: the lpt Rent category is exclusively the monthly Zelle to Adeena (twelve 500.00 payments plus four smaller), and a sweep of every active row whose description names a card payment (des:epay, autopaybus, automatic payment, payment thank you, credit crd des, des:ach pmt, des:retry pymt, online payment - thank, amex credit card payment) finds ZERO sitting in a P&L band, so the class is empty. Together with the previous round this is 2,608.30 of P&L error found and corrected while pct_clear stayed at 99.28 throughout. Two lessons worth keeping: a matching rung that pairs rows on amount and date alone is a rung that can attach a completely unrelated charge's category, so any duplicate-copy rule should require the descriptions to agree too; and the way to find these is internal consistency - does a rule's own evidence agree with itself - plus a band check on the rows whose description contradicts their category, neither of which any coverage percentage can see.
