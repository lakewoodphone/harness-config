**L11326 · L3821 · A spoken device number is a wallpaper label, not an identity — unlock by serial, and 'ok:true' is an enqueue**

**Measured 2026-10-20.** Bachrach asked to unlock "devices 36 and 23". The About screenshots showed
serials `JGQWHQWTXG` and `H299XHJ2CF`. In `fleet_devices`, `JGQWHQWTXG` is DRN 36 but
`H299XHJ2CF` is **DRN 25** — because on 2026-07-12 two handsets were both registered as DRN 23 and
the registry later split them 23 / 25 while the phone's wallpaper kept the old number. Unlocking
"23" would have pushed RemoveProfile at `H4624MWN9D`, a different phone, and reported success while
the phone in his hand stayed locked.

Two rules fall out, and they are general:

1. **A device number spoken by a human is a wallpaper label, and the wallpaper is a render of a
 mutable registry field.** `fleet_devices.drn` is mutable (`/renumber`, `/renumber-v2`), and
 `_wallpaper_label` / `_derive_site_device_name` are computed at render time and never re-pushed.
 So after any renumber there are three possible numbers for one phone: the wallpaper, the
 registry, and the on-device name (`ID-0017` was a DRN of 17 once). **Ask for the serial.** It is
 the only identifier that is physically attached to the handset and stable across every rename.
2. **`remove-lockdown` returning `ok:true` is an enqueue, not a removal.** The proof is the device's
 own `Acknowledged` per identifier, plus a `ProfileList` afterwards. Five of thirteen identifiers
 in this run came back 12075 "not installed" — expected — and the three that mattered were
 Acknowledged. Never report "unlocked" from the API's response body.
