**L3342 · Money arithmetic must state its units: a netting table that cancels to zero cannot conclude a number**

The Weinberg final-pay table stood for a week as: '-597.29 (the vacation week paid in error) + 597.29 (the half week he worked, never paid) = 597.29 owed'. Two identical magnitudes cancel. The table concluded a figure it never derived, and it was not noticed because the number itself was agreed by the owner from another line of reasoning entirely.

What was actually being netted was a FULL unpaid worked week (gross 1,326.92, net 1,189.10) against a HALF week paid in error (gross 663.46, net 597.29) - the residual is one half week. Net-from-net gives 591.81; gross-to-gross gives 663.46 gross = 597.29 as a half-week check. The 5.48 gap is the withholding table (9.97% on a 20h week, 10.38% on a 40h week).

Rules: (1) every money table names its unit per line - gross, net, or all-in including employer tax; (2) if two lines in a netting cancel, the table is wrong or the entry is spurious, and the total will not equal either line; (3) 'half a week' has three prices (gross 663.46, cash 597.29, all-in 736.11 out of the account) and quoting the wrong one is how a correct decision produces an unreadable file.
