**L3102 · A ferry instruction that names only the destination sends the owner to the wrong boat when two operators share one waterfront**

MEASURED 2026-09-29, St George, Staten Island. The plan said 'park at St George Ferry Terminal and take the ferry'. Two services leave from that same waterfront: the **Staten Island Ferry** (NYC DOT — free, no ticket, 25 minutes, every 30 minutes, 24 hours, berths at Whitehall Terminal, 4 South St) and **NYC Ferry** (a separate paid operator, small catamarans, its own landing a few hundred yards away, destinations Wall St/Pier 11, Battery Park City and Midtown West/W 39th St). At 18:11 the owner had parked and was standing at the NYC Ferry landing reading its departure board — 'To Midtown West/W 39th St-Pier 79 — 22 MIN', 'To Wall St./Pier 11 — 33 MIN' — convinced it was the ferry in the plan. It cost the 18:15 boat and a walk.

THE RULE: any instruction of the form 'take the ferry' must carry (a) the OPERATOR's name, (b) the FARE test — free and no ticket means the Staten Island Ferry, anything with a scanner or a ticket machine is NYC Ferry, (c) the DESTINATION terminal by address, and (d) how to tell the boats apart from the dock: the Staten Island boats are the big orange-and-white double-ended ones; NYC Ferry's are small catamarans. Same discipline for stations and lines: name the platform side and the operator, because '50 St station' also has an uptown entrance and a downtown entrance on opposite sides of Broadway, and 'the Broadway line at 50 St' is only the 1 — the 2 that stops there late at night goes to Brooklyn and never reaches South Ferry.

WHY IT GENERALISES: the failure was not ignorance of the facts — every one of them was in the plan, sourced. It was that the instruction compressed a two-operator waterfront into one word. Compression is where accuracy dies, and the person reading it is driving.
