**L3699 · Drafting a customer SMS for this owner: the accepted shape, measured over five drafts**

One customer text (the Mizrachi/Rahimi Nord N30 callback, 2026-10-12) took **five drafts** to satisfy
him. Every correction was his, and each cost a round trip. The accepted shape, and what he rejected.

**The shape he accepts: three lines.**
1. An apology that owns the fault (*"sorry about this one… that was our work, so it's on us"*).
2. **One** sentence saying what was actually done, in **his** words.
3. *"There's no charge, and it's out for pickup whenever you would like to stop by."*

**What he cut, and therefore what not to write next time:**
- The explanation. *"too much info, more concise, just apology and say…"* He does not want the customer
 told how many times the device came back, what was found, or why it failed. History belongs in the
 case file, not in the text.
- The walk-in hours, even though they are useful and come from the shop's own boilerplate.
- Any wording he did not choose for the technical claim. He supplied *"we bonded it with stronger
 adhesive, we used thicker tape"* and expected it reproduced, not improved.

**Availability wording, exactly: "it's out for pickup whenever you would like to stop by."** "Ready for
pickup" was rejected; bare "it's out" was rejected as too abrupt.

**The expensive mistake: never write a claim about the work that the conversation has not established.**
A passing remark of his — that newer batteries have stiffer, unbent connectors — went into a draft as
*"we put a new battery in."* It was wrong, it was never said, and he caught it in one line: *"no it's
the same new battery it's just i'm telling you the reason."* A customer message is a statement of fact
about their device. If the fact is not established, ask, or leave it out.

**The loop, mechanically:** one message file at one path, edited in place; preview (never `--send`)
after every revision; show him the exact fenced body plus the `To` number; one question — send or
change what; send the same file on his yes.

**Two validator false positives, both on correct messages the same day** — the script exits 1 on each
and neither is a reason to change the text: numbers with no dollar signs (walk-in hours), and money
language with no amount (*"there's no charge"*, where the missing amount is the point). Send with
`--force`, and name the warning and the reason to him in the same message — the override must never be
silent, and correct content must never be edited to satisfy a validator.

**Also measured:** the runbook's second delivery witness, the `dialpad-sms-ws` journal, is unavailable —
that unit is inactive on `secratary-cloud`. The `dialpad_sms_cache` row is still written live
(`created_at` 18:42:21Z → `fetched_at` 18:42:23Z on this send). Cite the cache row and say the
websocket witness was unavailable; do **not** go starting services on the office box to restore it,
that host is a frozen rollback anchor.

`lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/CUSTOMER-SMS-RUNBOOK.md` §9, so the next session pays the cost once.
