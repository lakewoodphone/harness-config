**L3528 · Before recording 'nothing catches this', grep the estate's own verifiers - they may already count it against a baseline (correcting P3021)**

**Correction to P3021, written the same evening (2026-10-11).** P3021 claimed that the sync records'
failure against `CUSTOMER_CASE_SYNC_RECORD.schema.json` was unnoticed — *"the instruction is either
being ignored silently or would block the work, and nothing anywhere catches it"*. That is false, and
one grep of `tools/customer/audit.py` shows it:

```
line 82: # MEASURED 2026-09-17 by validating all 383 records with jsonschema: 315 fail the repo's own schema.
line 90: SCHEMA_INVALID_BASELINE = 315
C12-schema-conformance: ERROR only if the count exceeds the baseline; otherwise INFO,
 "N of M records violate the schema (baseline 315 — pre-existing, unchanged)"
```

So the non-conformance is **known, measured, counted at a recorded baseline, and deliberately allowed
to stand** — the verifier's job is to stop it getting *worse*, not to make it go away. The measured
run on 2026-10-11 reported `C12-schema-conformance ERROR=0 WARN=0 INFO=1`, i.e. at or below baseline.
The two Weitman records are two of the known 315, not a new discovery.

**The lesson, which is the part worth keeping: before recording anything as "nothing catches this",
grep the estate's own verifiers for the thing.** This estate has a four-surface audit that already
counts a great many known-bad states with explicit baselines, and a session that reports one of those
baselines as a fresh defect spends the owner's attention on work that was already decided, and makes
its own record less trustworthy. The same shape appeared earlier the same day: `lptcase check`'s
"case count mismatch" and "case-without-sync-record" rows looked like alarms and were counting bugs in
the checker, not defects in the data.

What survives from P3021: the *stale-schema* observation itself is still true and still worth one
sentence — the contract tells a session to validate against a schema the corpus is known not to
satisfy, which is why a session that does what the contract says will "find" 315 pre-existing failures.
The fix is documentation (say so in the contract, next to step 1), not a migration.
