**L3350 · Correction to L3347: heat-press PTFE sheets are not all 0.1 mm - the thermal loss is a range, 1 degC to 8 degC, so quote the range and tell the owner to dial up**

L3347 computed the thermal cost of a PTFE cover on the shop CP14+ mat at 0.1 mm thickness and got 0.76 degC. That was an ASSUMED thickness, not a measured one, and I quoted it to the owner as though it were the answer. Then the Amazon listing for TransOurDream B0DK5CQPP8 (16 x 20 in, 4 pack) stated Product Dimensions 20 x 16 x 0.04 in - 0.04 in is about 1.0 mm, five to ten times the assumption. Corrected range, same formula dT = q*t/k with q = 150 W / 0.07875 m2 = 1905 W/m2 and k(PTFE) = 0.25 W/m.K: 0.13 mm (5 mil film) -> 1.0 degC; 0.2 mm -> 1.5 degC; 1.0 mm -> 7.6 degC. The 0.04 in is probably packed/rolled thickness rather than one sheet, and a reviewer says 'they're not thick in the sense that they block the heat', so the real figure is likely near 1.5 degC - but that is still a guess.

LESSON: (a) when a number depends on a spec I have not read, get the spec before quoting the number, or quote the range and say which end is assumed. (b) heat-press Teflon sheets are usually woven PTFE-coated fibreglass, not smooth film - they are stiffer, they lie flat, and reviewers report they do not slide, which is the property that matters when the owner says he will not fiddle with it. (c) the practical instruction is not the degree count anyway: tell him to set the CP14+ about 10 degC higher than he does today and verify once.

Also recorded for the shop: the owner will not cut a sheet. Buy a size that covers the mat uncut. CP14+ mat is 350 x 225 mm, so any sheet at least 350 mm on one axis covers it whole; the tidy fit is 12 x 16 in (305 x 406 mm), margin 56 x 80 mm. 16 x 20 in also covers but drapes about 150 mm off the end of the machine.
