**L2313 · A cannot-take-agent-work alert is a transient until the broker says otherwise; subagent provenance on ZABZ-TECH is the mesh placement file**

## LESSON 1 — a "cannot take agent work" alert is a transient until the broker says otherwise; and the mesh IS reachable from ZABZ-TECH (measured 2026-09-21 20:19-20:25 ET)

TWO FACTS, BOTH MEASURED, THAT CONTRADICTED MY FIRST READING:

1. The owner's badge showed 5 mesh-node alerts in `owner_message_queue` — `secratary`, `zabz-tech`, `zabz-yoga-1`, `zabz-tech-linux`, `lakewooechsmini`, all "cannot take agent work: timed out after 4000 ms ... (last capacity reading 305s old)", newest 2026-09-21T18:05Z. That reads as a mesh-wide outage.
2. The broker's own live view at 2026-09-22T00:22Z said the opposite: `curl -s http://127.0.0.1:3091/nodes` -> 5 nodes, **all `state: ok`, `unreachable: false`**, `zabz-tech` `latencyMs: 40`, `freeMiB: 43337`, `accepts.oneShot: true`, `accepts.fleet: true`. `~/.personal-secretary/mesh-placement.json` agreed: `total: 5, usable: 5, bad: []`, reading `cache`.

SO THE 18:05 ALERTS WERE TRUE AND TRANSIENT. The monitor itself documents the cause: `mesh-placement-monitor.py` forces `?fresh=1`, which re-reads every gate against a **hard 4000 ms wall-clock deadline**, and "under load produces spurious timeouts — measured 2026-09-20, a node that answered in 25 ms on the wire was reported as 'timed out after 4000 ms' on a forced refresh." The default path is the cached document (<=15 s); the forced fresh read is meant for CONFIRMING a failure, not for reporting one.

**THE RULE.** Never report a mesh outage from an alert row or from a gate probe of my own. Ask the broker: `curl -s http://127.0.0.1:3091/nodes` (cached, cheap) and only then `/nodes?fresh=1`. A 4000 ms deadline under load is a measurement artifact, and an alert queue accumulates those artifacts forever because nothing re-checks them.

**ALSO MEASURED — provenance of a `subagent` call from ZABZ-TECH is the mesh, not local.** I dispatched 7 `subagent` children at 00:19:32Z and `~/.dsh/mesh/placements/` gained **exactly 7** `remote-*.json` files at 00:19:33Z, each `node: zabz-tech`, `tier: fits`, `source: broker`, with a broker lease (`leaseTtlSec: 900`) and rationale naming `transport v1 (ssh --profile headless) MEASURED to work`. One placement per child, same second as the dispatch: the children ran on the named node under a lease. So "the tool named `subagent` is bound to the remote provider" holds on this machine, and the correct provenance line is the placement file — not a guess from seeing node processes.

**A LOCAL PROBE THAT LIES.** `Test-NetConnection secratary-ts -Port 8002` returned False while `Test-NetConnection 100.84.72.88 -Port 8002` returned True, and `:3086` was False on both despite the gate listening on `127.0.0.1:3086`. The gate is reached over **Tailscale Serve on 443** (`https://<node>.tail93e6e6.ts.net/mesh/capacity`), not on the raw port; and the raw-port probe against a hostname can miss the path the broker actually uses. Probe the broker's URL, never a port by hand.
