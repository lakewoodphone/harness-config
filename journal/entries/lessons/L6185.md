**L6185 · A reading without a named ground is not a measurement - I built a pack conclusion on an unstated test condition**

## What happened (2026-10-13, Klein Dell 5420, lpt-hub)

The owner, at the bench, sent two board readings: `3.3 v, further down i see 16.75v`. I turned the
16.75 V into a finding: a fully charged 4-cell pack (4 x 4.19 V), the pack therefore full with its BMS
not latched, and — worse — "the case file's parts advice (buy the 3-cell 42 Wh) is probably backwards
for this unit." All three went into the case file and into my report.

He had measured **with the battery out**. Every conclusion died at once, and the case file needed a
correction entry naming the section it retracted.

## The rule this cost

**A reading is not a measurement until its test point and its ground reference are named.** I knew the
reference was unstated — I wrote "neither identified as to test point, and the ground reference is not
stated" — and drew the conclusion anyway. Naming your own uncertainty in the same breath as the claim
does not discharge it.

**And a conditional inference must stay a question until its condition is confirmed.** The honest form
was one sentence — *if* that node is the battery connector with the pack in, *then* X — asked as a
question. Instead the condition was assumed, and the inference graduated into the record as a finding.
The next message falsified it.

## The electronics worth keeping, because it repeats on any board case

- **The adapter is one source on a board, not a ceiling on it.** 5 V in does not bound what a node may
 hold.
- **But a 5 V input cannot make 16.8 V.** PD's fixed voltages are 5 / 9 / 15 / 20 V, and 0.22 A is
 1.1 W — far too little to run the boost that node would need. So a surprise high voltage on a low
 input means *stored*, or *not really a rail*.
- **Three honest explanations for a surprise voltage, one test splits them:** probes spanning two nodes
 (the meter reads their difference); a high-impedance node — with no pack fitted the battery node is
 undriven **by design**, and a 10 Mohm DMM reads leakage and coupling there; or residual charge on bulk
 capacitance. **The discriminator is a load** — LoZ mode, or ~10 kohm to ground. A real rail holds its
 voltage; a floating node collapses.
- **Cell arithmetic:** 3S tops out at 12.6 V, 4S at 16.8 V — useful, but only on a node you have proved
 is the battery node.
- **Two identical ports reading the same fault are not two coincidences** — the cause is shared or
 upstream. And "no charge LED" is Dell's own vocabulary for power not reaching the machine.

## What to do differently

Before a number enters a case file or a report it carries **the test point, the ground, and the
instrument**. Missing any of the three, it goes in as a question, not as a finding.
