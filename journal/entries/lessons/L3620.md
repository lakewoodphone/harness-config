**L3620 · a divergence check that samples an instant fires HIGH on a healthy repo two sessions are pushing to**

## 2026-10-12 05:28 UTC · ZABZ-YOGA · a divergence check that samples an instant will fire HIGH on a healthy repo that two sessions are pushing to

**Measured, twice, twenty minutes apart, 2026-10-12.**

05:15:08Z - the attention badge, reading the ceo-kernel sentinel on the authority:
`config_sync` = **HIGH**: `DIVERGED: local main is d3740df but root@2.28.33.58:/srv/git/harness-config.git refs/heads/main is 5ae9f77`.

05:17:33Z - the same check run by hand on the same machine:
`clean : main e962a7b is published to root@2.28.33.58:/srv/git/harness-config.git (remote HEAD read back and matches)`.
`git rev-list --count vault/main..HEAD` = **0**. `git merge-base --is-ancestor 5ae9f77 HEAD` = **true**.

**Both readings were correct.** The two SHAs were two concurrent sessions' commits landing in either
order inside one `git ls-remote` window - a real, transient divergence of about two minutes - and the
check was doing exactly what it was built to do.

**Why it is still a defect.** The check compares local HEAD to the remote at one instant, behind a
15-minute cache, and this repo is written to by several sessions, an autocommit job every 15 minutes
and a journal job every 15 minutes. So a healthy, fully-published repo will periodically produce a
HIGH that is wrong. **A HIGH that is usually wrong is worse than no check**, because it teaches
everyone - including the owner, whose attention badge is where this surfaces - to ignore the severity.

**The fix shape, for whoever picks it up.** Require the disagreement to persist across **two
consecutive samples** before escalating, and distinguish the two cases the current message merges:
"the remote holds a commit we do not have" (real divergence, act) versus "we hold a commit the remote
does not have yet" (unpublished local work, and the normal state of a repo between its writes and its
pushes). The current wording says `DIVERGED` for both.

**The general rule.** A monitor over a concurrently-written resource needs a **persistence criterion**,
not a sample. One reading of a moving target is an observation, not a verdict.

**Related, and the reason I checked rather than acted:** `git push origin` on this estate **exits 1
while the push that matters succeeds** (L3748, 172 §5.3) - `origin` carries two pushurls and the
GitHub half always fails non-fast-forward. The correct publication check is
`/usr/local/bin/harness-config-sync-status.sh`, or a `git ls-remote` read back. **Never `$?`.**
