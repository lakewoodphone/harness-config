**L12246 · RETRACTION: the 22 ledger claims I called orphaned were live work - check the attempt table, not the process table**

## L — I called 22 live ledger claims "orphaned" when they were being worked, and the wrong finding was the dangerous part

**Retracts a claim in handoff H12280** (2026-10-21 22:30 UTC, ZABZ-TECH), which said: *"OPEN AND
UNRESOLVED: 22 ledger claims with nothing working them"*, and listed the evidence as
`in flight: 0 dispatch process(es)` plus no `orchestrator` process, cron entry or systemd unit on the
authority.

**The claims were live.** Measured 2026-10-21 22:22Z, minutes after writing that:

```
1072 attempt 1963 by zabz-orchestrator at 2026-10-21T22:20:28Z -> None (2 minutes old, in progress)
1062 attempt 1955 by zabz-orchestrator at 2026-10-21T22:17:35Z -> None
1061 attempt 1953 by zabz-orchestrator at 2026-10-21T22:17:35Z -> None
```

Fresh attempts with no outcome yet. Something was working all 22 rows at the moment I called them orphans.

**The mistake in one line:** `in flight: 0 dispatch process(es)` counts **wake-dispatch** processes. A
different orchestrating session claims straight from `work.py` and never appears in that counter, so a
healthy parallel actor is indistinguishable from an abandoned lease in that one reading. I checked four
places for an `orchestrator` process **on the authority** and found none — but the claimer does not have to
run on the authority at all; `work.py` is reachable over ssh from anywhere. I searched the wrong box and
treated its silence as evidence.

**Why this one is worse than a wrong number.** The finding said the leases were live and that I had
deliberately not force-released them — but it also said the next step was to "check whether `zabz-orchestrator`
is a real orchestration session". A session that skipped that check and went straight to `work.py reap`, or
to clearing `claimed_by`, would have **freed 22 leases out from under a working actor** and manufactured
exactly the duplicate work this system exists to prevent. `reap` only frees EXPIRED leases, so the scheduled
reaper was never the risk; a human or agent acting on my finding was.

**The rule.**

1. **Before calling a claim stale, check the attempt table, not the process table.** `work.py show <id>`
 shows `-- attempt N by <handle> at <time> -> <outcome>`. A recent attempt with outcome `None` is work in
 progress; that is the authoritative signal, and it is one command.
2. **A counter that measures one mechanism is not a measurement of the activity.** `in flight` counts
 dispatch processes; it does not and cannot see a session claiming directly. Say what the counter counts
 before drawing a conclusion from its zero.
3. **Absence of a process on host A is not absence of the actor** when the tool it uses is reachable from
 every host. Search where the claims come from, which is the `claimed_by` handle, and look for that handle.
