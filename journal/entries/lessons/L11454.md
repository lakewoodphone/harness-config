**L11454 · L11392 · Journal ids collide across hosts: L3822 is two different entries, so a citation must name the machine**

**Measured 2026-10-20.** Two journal entries carry the same id on two machines and say different
things:

| host | `journal.py show L3822` |
|---|---|
| secratary-cloud (authority) | "A repo that configures a live filter is not the filter that is running" (2026-10-14) |
| ZABZ-TECH | "secratary-ts IS the frozen anchor … the credentials table exists with 0 rows" (2026-10-15) |

The mechanism is `journal/alloc/bands.tsv`: id ranges are reserved **per host**. Ids are unique per
host, not globally. A worker on ZABZ-TECH reported to me that "L3822, P3178 and L6216 do not exist" —
and it was right about its own checkout and wrong about the record, because its tree holds 4,185
entries and nothing before 2026-09-11 while the authority holds 5,814.

RULES:
1. **A journal id is not a citation unless it names its host.** Write `SECRATARY-CLOUD#L3822`, or quote
 the title as well. I cited L3822/P3178/L6216 bare in two documents today and one of them was read
 back, correctly, as a different entry.
2. **"Search the journal" is a statement about one checkout.** Before concluding an entry does not
 exist, say which host's tree was searched and how many entries it holds. The August location work
 is invisible from ZABZ-TECH and looks like it never happened.
3. A merge of these trees will silently drop entries. Until ids are made global, treat the authority as
 the record and every other checkout as a cache with an unknown lag.
