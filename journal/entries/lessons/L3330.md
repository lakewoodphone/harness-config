**L3330 · Frum design market is two-tiered (institutions pay, households do not) and has no entry-level door**

FRUM DESIGN MARKET, measured 2026-10-05 - two findings a future session should not have to rediscover.

(1) THE PRICE IS TWO-TIERED: a frum INDIVIDUAL/SMB client pays roughly a third to half of general rates (published menu: flyer USD 50, logo 100 single/200 multi, Hebrew Shopify 700/1,000/1,500, frum agency hourly 50 vs a general directory band of 70-150), while a frum INSTITUTION pays at or above general rates (a school identity refresh was grant-funded at USD 10,000; a synagogue website 5,000 plus 39.95/mo plus 50/hr; ShulCloud managed shul sites 162/314/465 per month; non-profit art director 100-140k). The buyer type, not the community, sets the price. The sobering datum: an imamother designer with TWENTY years' experience reported about USD 400/week at 15 hours = an effective USD 26/hour (2021). That is the strongest argument for aiming at employment or institutions rather than a treadmill of small local jobs.

(2) THERE IS NO ENTRY-LEVEL DESIGN JOB IN THIS COMMUNITY. A full census of every frum board (Candibots read in full, all 117 postings, plus Macher, JobsGemach, YiddishJobs, JewishJobs, JewishStaffing, NBN): zero of 14 design/web/marketing postings said "no experience necessary" or "will train"; 9 required prior experience, 6 a portfolio, 0 a degree. Frum market carries ~11-16 live design postings at any moment; Macher lists Education 329 and Administration 90 against Graphic Design 4. The two genuinely remote design roles found were both part-time, both non-frum employers, both portfolio-first. Design is enterable here as assistant-then-designer or as self-employment, not as an advertised junior job.

ALSO WORTH KEEPING: frum/yeshivish job boards publish almost NO pay while Jewish non-profit boards publish it as standard. TTI quotes 4,800 on its own site while New Jersey's state training directory lists the same provider's programme at 6,900 with "consumer report card: not reported". No frum design school publishes a placement rate.

MY OWN ORIGINAL MEASUREMENT, reusable: 12 real Lakewood-area business websites audited with axe-core in a browser (script in ~/code/home/family/chaya-design-2026/artifacts/audit-method.md). 11 of 12 had at least one detectable WCAG failure; 159 instances total; colour contrast on 9 of 12; 4 sites had no H1; 2 had no meta description; the slowest home page took 3.7s to load and the heaviest was 5.3MB. The script is copy-paste and needs no installation - it is a sales tool as much as an audit tool.
