**L6264 · The journal fork is a HALF-LANDED repair (item 163, 2026-10-11) sitting uncommitted on ZABZ-TECH - read git status before merging five trees**

The journal fork is not an accident of neglect - it has a HISTORY, and that history was sitting unread on the machine that owns it.

`git status` on ZABZ-TECH (2026-10-15) shows **239 untracked entry files** under `journal/entries/`: D3132-D3137, H3602-H3629, L3774-L3815, P3169, P3798-P3803 - exactly the id neighbourhoods where ZABZ-TECH holds a DIFFERENT body from yoga and the authority. They are the output of **item 163, a cross-machine duplicate-id repair done on 2026-10-11**, recorded in `docs/mesh/163-journal-repair-crossmachine-ids.md` (20,820 B) with its mapping in `163-repair-manifest.json` (42,741 B) and its own verifier. It was never committed and never propagated, so three days later it reads as a fork.

MEASURED the same day, across five trees: union 4,527 ids, 4,913 distinct bodies, **364 ids carrying different bodies**, 449 ids confined to one tree, and `journal.py check` reporting **0 errors in all five** because it has no cross-tree rule. 210 of the 364 have ZABZ-TECH as the odd tree, in 15 contiguous runs - the signature of a block renumbering, which is what 163 was.

THREE THINGS THIS CHANGES:
1. ZABZ-TECH is not a stale copy. **Do not correct it toward yoga for those ids.**
2. The resolution is the LOSSLESS one: keep every distinct body in the union and let `journal.py dedupe` / `repair-ids` renumber the losers. That reproduces 163's intent mechanically rather than re-deciding it by hand, and `journal.py` already ships both commands - nothing needs building.
3. The fork is a **partially-landed repair**, so round 7's job is to LAND it, not to clean up after it.

This is the fourth time in one round that a document describing work already done sat unread while a session was about to redo it. `git status` on the machine that owns the work is cheaper than any analysis, and it is where this was found.
