**L2341 · Hot-holding rule: 140F is the line, not the oven dial**

Asked to confirm a covered Anolon pot of chicken soup could hold at a 225F oven setting for hours. Researched it (3 mesh subagents, zabz-tech) plus Pyrex primary sources. Answer split in two, and only one half was about cookware.

COOKWARE IS FINE. Across every current Anolon line the floor is 400F oven rating (Advanced Home / Advanced / Advanced Ceramic / SmartStack); most are 500F. The strictest number in all Anolon literature is 350F, for a GLASS LID on Anolon X and Ascend (Anolon X care manual: "oven safe to 500F when used without the lid, or 350F when used with the lid"; Ascend set copy: "500F (350F with lids)"). Anolon X replacement-lid pages say 500F - the care manual wins; it is the safety document and repeats 350F in four languages. Handles: Anolon publishes no numeric handle limit, only material. Silicone-clad handles ride pots rated to 500F, so 225F is a non-issue. Confirmed 2026-09-18.

THE 140F LINE IS THE REAL CONSTRAINT. USDA: danger zone 40-140F, max 2h cumulative; hold hot food at or above 140F. A 225F oven is NOT hot holding equipment - air conducts at ~0.026 W/mK vs 0.61 for water, a covered pot is insulated by its own air gap, and as the soup approaches holding temp the gradient collapses. A barely-warm stockpot of poultry is the textbook Clostridium perfringens setup (CDC names large batches held at unsafe temps, poultry and gravy). Also: cooling after any hold must be 135->70F within 2h then ->41F within 4 more (FDA Food Code 3-501.14).

PLASTIC LIDS NEVER GO IN AN OVEN. Pyrex's own FAQ: "Pyrex lids comprised of plastic must not be used in conventional or convection ovens because they will melt." Their warranty page separates "GLASS LIDS - can be used in a preheated conventional or convection oven" from "PLASTIC AND WOOD LIDS - DO NOT use in a conventional or convection oven." Same rule from Snapware. So "Pyrex storage = oven safe" is true of the glass and false of the lid, and the lid is what people leave on.

The transferable rule, which is the reason this is filed: when a temperature question is about a dial or a material rating, answer the food-safety line too, because the material question is usually already fine and the safety question usually is not. A confident "yes, the pot handles 225F" would have been true and useless.

Primary sources: anolon.com per-SKU spec fields; cdn.shopify.com Anolon X care PDF; pyrexhome.com FAQ + product-warranties-safety-and-usage; fsis.usda.gov danger-zone-40f-140f (live page 403s to non-browser clients - read via Wayback); fda.gov/media/181882.
