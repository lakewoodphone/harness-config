**L5536 · A compatibility screen can fit, light up and never bring touch up: match the part number off the machine's own label, dry-test before bonding, and read what the supplier's warranty actually returns**

**A screen bought as "LCD assembly with digitizer" for a whole model family can fit, light up perfectly, and never bring its touch controller up. The supplier's own returns policy tells you the thing that decides whether that costs you anything: dry-test it before you bond it in.**

Measured 2026-10-15 on the Greenfeld Surface Laptop 4 (13.5", model 1950). Part: MobileSentrix
`107082076801`, a **refurbished assembly listed for Surface Laptop 3/4/5 13.5" — 1867 / 1868 / 1950 /
1951 / 1958 / 1959**. Fitted as a bare-panel swap (old panel separated from the original lid, new panel
bonded in). Result: **picture perfect, digitizer dead** — `Intel(R) Precise Touch and Stylus (Intel(R)
PTS) - Base Driver - Port #1` reporting **Code 10 / `STATUS_DEVICE_POWER_FAILURE`** and **no
`HID-compliant touch screen` child at all**, touch dead in UEFI too, all four display connectors re-seated
and inspected with no change — while the same assembly's **sensor cable** worked, since the machine only
powers on with it connected.

**The three transferable facts:**

1. **A one-SKU-fits-six-models compatibility part is not the part.** The machine's own panel label reads
 **`M1082392-009` / `LQ0DD1K0K016`**. A multi-model assembly of a different revision is a candidate
 cause of a panel that fits and displays but whose touch never enumerates. *(Hypothesis, labelled as
 one — not established.)* **Ask for the part number off the machine's own label and have the supplier
 pre-verify against the machine's serial**, not against the model name.
2. **Dry-test before you bond.** MobileSentrix's own FAQ defines it and recommends it: plug the display
 assembly in **without removing the adhesive backing or fitting it**, confirm picture *and touch*, and
 then install. This job bonded first and tested second. That is the difference between a clean return
 and an argument.
3. **Read the returns policy before you need it, and read what a "refund" actually is.** Their policy:
 lifetime warranty on replacement parts, **no restocking fee**, request via the account dashboard
 (Product Returns / RMA) with a **PR** number by email, shipping needs no prior authorization — but the
 money comes back as **account credit at the lower of current or purchase price, not cash**, and the
 exclusions are **internal or physical damage, accidental damage, misuse, and "improper installation
 techniques"**. On a bonded, heat-and-card panel swap that last exclusion is live, so **photograph the
 flex tails and the panel back before shipping** and describe only what was actually done.

**Reading a bot-walled supplier page:** MobileSentrix answers a scripted fetch with HTTP 403; the policy
was readable through `https://r.jina.ai/<url>`. On the authority, `browser_goto` / `browser_use_run`
cannot run at all — *"Failed to launch chromium browser… Unsupported chromium channel"* — so the proxy is
the working route.
