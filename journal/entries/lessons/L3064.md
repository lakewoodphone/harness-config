**L-new · The double-quote claim is dead (refuted twice), and the real open hazard is that `sh -s` plus stdin hangs when dispatched FROM the Windows desktop**

MEASURED 2026-09-29, by two independent audit lanes, from source and by execution.

**1. `L3060` is refuted, twice, and it must not be acted on.**
`L3060` (same day) claimed a double-quote character in a mesh prompt kills the remote one-shot with
exit 1 and no output, and told future sessions to keep `"` out of every brief. Two lanes independently
killed it:

- The taxonomy lane RAN the builders and showed the quote survives literally: the task goes in as a
 single-quoted word (`remote-script.js` `taskWord`/`shQuote`) or as base64 through
 `-EncodedCommand` (`ssh-transport.js` `remoteArgv`). No interpolation layer sees it.
- The load lane ran a controlled probe pair against the live mesh: a prompt with NO quote and a prompt
 with TWO double-quotes both exited 0, both returned a final message, both named `zabz-tech`, and the
 quoted spike came back **verbatim** (`SPIKE: say "quoted" once`). 6815 ms and 5804 ms.

So the mechanism `L3060` asserts does not exist on this path, and the rule it imposes — write every
brief without double quotes — is a constraint on the owner's and the fleet's writing that buys nothing.
The visible symptom `L3060` recorded (exit 1, no output, stderr tail looking like the prompt cut at a
fixed character) is **better explained by the command-line ceiling**, since measured:
a 12 000-character task produces an argv of 33 859 against a Windows cap of 32 767 and fails with
`spawn ENAMETOOLONG`. That is lesson **L3063**, and unlike `L3060` it was reproduced deterministically.
**Corrected by supersession, not by editing: `L3060` stands as the record of having been wrong.**

**2. The live open hazard: `sh -s` with the script on stdin HANGS when dispatched from `zabz-tech`.**
The load lane could not complete a single REMOTE child turn from the office desktop. Its own words: the
provider's transport returned nothing at every bound — 180 s with a real brief, 45 s with a script that
ran no child, 25 s hand-rolled — while a plain `ssh linux-pc-ts "hostname; echo DONE"` over the SAME
file-descriptor shape exited 0 in **309 ms**.

**This does not reproduce from ZABZ-YOGA.** In the same session, on the fixed code, the same `sh -s`
stdin shape completed real child turns from this laptop to `zabz-tech-linux` (**exit 0, 5176 ms**) and to
`secratary` (**exit 0, 5080 ms**), both framed, both with a correct host. So the shape is not
universally broken and the POSIX fix is not implicated.

**What is established:** the failure is a property of the DISPATCHING CLIENT, or of that client's
`ssh.exe` handling of a program fed on stdin, and not of the target node, the generated program, or the
quote characters. **What is not established, and must not be assumed:** whether the cause is the Windows
OpenSSH client, the remote sshd, or the way a hand-rolled probe left stdin open. The load lane said so
itself rather than guessing.

**Why it matters enough to record:** three of five nodes in this mesh are POSIX. If a Windows client
cannot reliably deliver a stdin script, then every fan-out *from the office desktop* to the Linux nodes
is dead, and that is half the mesh seen from the machine with the most headroom. It is the next thing to
reproduce properly, by running the fixed transport from `zabz-tech` itself with the package's own
`createSshTransport` — not with a hand-rolled probe, which is where the ambiguity entered.
