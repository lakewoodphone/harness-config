**L3419 · READY_FOR_PICKUP is the shop's completed state - it is terminal, and lpt audit does not know it**

**`READY_FOR_PICKUP` IS the shop's completed state. Treat it as terminal, and do not trust any tool
that calls it open.**

The live production `OrderStatus` enum carries exactly seven labels —
AWAITING_DIAGNOSIS, AWAITING_APPROVAL, AWAITING_PARTS, AWAITING_PAYMENT, READY_FOR_PICKUP,
CANCELLED, UNFIXABLE — and **no `COMPLETED`**: `select 'COMPLETED'::"OrderStatus"` fails with
`invalid input value for enum`. On 2026-10-07 the owner settled the seven-state design and the
`order_status_seven_states` migration mapped `COMPLETED` → `READY_FOR_PICKUP`. The deployed router
still carries the alias table and says why in his recorded words: *"`COMPLETED` → `READY_FOR_PICKUP`,
because a device is paid for before it goes on the shelf and that IS the completed state."*

Consequences, all measured 2026-10-09:

- A twin store showing `COMPLETED` against the live store showing `READY_FOR_PICKUP` is **the
 migration, not a discrepancy**. 397 of the 425 `READY_FOR_PICKUP` orders look exactly like that.
- `READY_FOR_PICKUP` must never be "closed out". It is already closed.
- `lpt audit` does not know this. Its N4 rule ("ready for pickup, never collected") reports **400**
 finished orders as problems, and "open and untouched" a further **200** on terminal rows — 600 of
 its 1,077 findings, on a design the owner settled two days before. The tool's own stated principle
 ("a status whose own rows mostly carry a `completedDate` is closed"; 372 of 425 do) is simply not
 applied to `READY_FOR_PICKUP` yet.
- Reopening one needs the app's `reopenReason`, and the tRPC layer refuses without it. The sanctioned
 writer bypasses that layer, so **a reopen is possible and therefore a decision** — never do it on
 an inference, because it puts a device back on the board that may not exist.
