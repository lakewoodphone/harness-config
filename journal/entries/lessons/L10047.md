**L10047 · The DeepSeek peak window is Mon-Fri 01:00-04:00 and 06:00-10:00 UTC - L9793 measured against a retired window**

**Corrected 2026-10-19T22:15Z (ZABZ-YOGA). This entry supersedes the numbers in `L9793`, which stands
as written. `L9793` was right that DeepSeek has two prices and that the internal card was wrong; it was
wrong about which hours are which, and every figure derived from that window is wrong with it.**

## The window

DeepSeek's live pricing page, read 2026-10-19T21:38Z at
<https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing>, says verbatim:

> Off-peak rates are half of the peak rates. Peak hours are 01:00 - 04:00 and 06:00 - 10:00 UTC,
> Monday through Friday, excluding Chinese public holidays. All other hours are off-peak, including
> weekends and Chinese public holidays in full.

Peak is therefore 7 hours × 5 weekdays = 35 of 168 hours = **20.8 % of the week**, not 67 %.
`L9793`, the 2026-10-19 cloud audit and owner-queue #830 all used **00:30–16:30 UTC**, which is the
*retired* scheme. That one error turned a measured 27.7 % peak share into a reported 84 %, and turned a
97.7/month saving into a 310/month one.

**The correct window was already in this journal, written 2026-09-11 as `L43`** — "`deepseek-official`
bills 01:00-04:00 and 06:00-10:00 UTC Mon-Fri at exactly 2x off-peak". The audit did not search for it.
A lesson written down and never searched is worth nothing; `journal.py search "<the area>"` costs 27 ms
and would have prevented the entire error.

## The corrected money

`model_usage` on the authority, 7 days to 2026-10-19T21:38Z, provider=deepseek:
peak 149,219,510 in / 987,588 out (27.7 % of input); off-peak 389,175,866 in / 2,861,502 out;
cached 4,637,824 (0.86 % of input).

Priced per hour at the published card, cached input credited at its own rate:
**105.18 per 7 days = 450.8 per 30 days.** Not 650.

The bank figure "636 in 31 days" is **top-ups, not consumption**. `GET api.deepseek.com/user/balance`
at 21:47Z returned `total_balance 145.77` (granted 95.45, topped up 50.32), `is_available true` — an
unconsumed credit balance. So debits accumulate instead of tracking use, and two independent readings
(tokens × card = 450.8; topped up since 2026-08-18 minus what is left ≈ 408) agree with each other and
not with the bank. **Ask what WRITES a number before trusting it: a debit answers "how much cash left",
never "how many tokens were spent".**

## The prize, correctly sized

- Perfect scheduling, every peak token moved off-peak: saves half the peak-priced portion =
 **97.7/month, 21.7 %**. That is the entire scheduling prize, and it requires delaying work.
- DeepInfra Flex on the same model generation (V4.1-Flash): **0.112 in / 0.336 out, no hour
 dependency**, cheaper than DeepSeek at *every* hour including off-peak. Routing there is
 **263.9/month vs 450.8 — a 41.4 % cut with nothing delayed.**
- **Fireworks Standard is 0.30/1.20, exactly DeepSeek's peak.** A Fireworks key buys nothing at any hour.

Routing is worth twice the scheduling and carries none of the starvation risk. That reverses the order
the handoff proposed.

## Two traps found in the same hour

1. **A window that is another window's residue must be declared a catch-all.** DeepSeek defines off-peak
 as "everything peak is not". On a Saturday the peak window does not apply at all, so the off-peak
 bands leave 01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 uncovered. A plain "union covers 24 h" check passes, and the
 resolver then finds no price for Saturday morning. Coverage must be proved *per weekday*.
2. **`/opt/company/.env` cannot be sourced by bash.** Unquoted values containing spaces make bash run
 fragments as commands (`line 365: wCsW4vFx: command not found`). Sourcing it under `set -u` *and*
 redirecting the sourcing stderr to `/dev/null` kills the caller with **no message at all** — I lost
 three probes to my own `2>/dev/null`. Read that file with a parser, never with `.`.
