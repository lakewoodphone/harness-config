**A manufacturer's dealer locator is not evidence the store exists — a listed kite dealer's shopfront is now a pawn shop**

2026-09-28. Asked where to buy a good kite near Lakewood, I first answered from **Premier Kites' own New
Jersey dealer locator** — the authoritative-sounding source — and put **Cobra Kites, 2608 Route 37 East, Toms
River** near the top of the list.

It does not exist. Google's business record for that exact address returns **"Q's Loan" — a pawn shop**, at
2608 NJ-37 Store E. Cobra Kites survives only as an internet shop (`cobrakites.com`), with no storefront, and
Yelp independently marks the Toms River listing **CLOSED**.

**Why it was easy to get wrong, and will be again:** a dealer locator is maintained by the *manufacturer* for
*its* convenience. A closed retailer drops off a locator slowly or never — there is no incentive to prune, and
the page keeps its authority while the street changes underneath it. The same is true of directory listings,
chamber-of-commerce pages and "top 10" round-ups. All of them describe a business that *was*.

**THE CHECK, which is nearly free:** before sending anyone to an address, resolve the *address itself* in a
live map provider and see what business is actually there now. In this case a single query returned a pawn
shop where a kite shop was supposed to be, and settled it without a phone call — which mattered, because the
owner had explicitly said he was not going to call anyone and a bad address is a wasted trip.

The general form: when a plan depends on **a physical place existing**, verify the place, not the listing. A
dealer list, a directory, a search snippet and a "now open" badge are all claims about the past. The category
and rating on a live map record are much closer to the present — and when the shop turned out to be real, that
same record was what confirmed it (Kitty Hawk Kites, Barnegat Light, is categorised by Google as a **"Kite
shop"** with 4.6 stars, which is what promoted it to the top of the list).
