**L3053 · A chat is measured in utterances: one fan-out of 11 rows is one turn, and interleaving breaks contiguous grouping**

**L — Measure a chat in UTTERANCES, not rows and not minutes: one fan-out is eleven rows.**

Measured 2026-09-29 while answering "is Chip responding too much". My first two
attempts at the number were both wrong, and the second wrongness was the instructive one.

Attempt 1: grouped Chip's outbound rows by (body, minute). A fan-out to 11 members
takes ~60-90 seconds, so one reply routinely straddles two minutes and was counted
twice - 42 "utterances" where there are 34.

Attempt 2: grouped them as CONTIGUOUS runs of an identical body. That reported 8
"repeats" - the same line apparently sent twice - which was alarming and false. The
cause is the thing the ledger actually looks like: the relay fan-out of a NEW inbound
message interleaves with Chip's reply fan-out in the id sequence (there is no ordering
anywhere in this system - turntaking.md F5), so the 11 copies of one reply are NOT
adjacent: 125210, 125212, 125215, 125218 ... are the same reply with relays in between.

The rule that survives both: rows with an IDENTICAL body whose timestamps are within
90 seconds are ONE utterance, regardless of what is interleaved between them. Rows of
the same body more than 90 s apart are two utterances, because that is the same line
sent to the family twice.

Why it matters beyond tidiness: the whole conclusion depended on it. By share of turns,
Chip took 21.1% of September and 38.5-47.1% of each of the last three real
conversations. A row-counting method would have said 2461 outbound rows against 222
inbound and produced a meaningless 92%. Reproduce with /home/zabz/phone/chip-audit/measure_now.py.
