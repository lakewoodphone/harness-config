**L6263 · Owner-queue resolutions strand ledger items; write-only tables; liveness derived from a directory**

# Lessons from the 2026-10-15 owner-queue triage and attention audit

**L-1. Resolving an owner-decision row strands every ledger item parked on it.** `work.py lint-refs`
reports them as `STALE` and exits 1, but nothing acts on that, so the items sit blocked on a row that no
longer exists. Measured: resolving 46 rows produced **14 STALE items in one run**. **So: after any batch
of queue resolutions, sweep `work_item` for rows whose `owner_row` just resolved and either clear the
reference (`work.py owner-row <id> --clear`) or close the item with the reason.** A resolved question
that leaves its work blocked is worse than an open question.

**L-2. Before trusting a subsystem's output, find its reader.** `owner_attention_findings` had
**27,869 rows written in 30 days and no SELECT anywhere on the estate** — a write-only table. The sweep
did the work and threw it away. **So: `grep -rln '<table>' /` before believing that writing a table is
delivering a thing.** The presence of rows is not the presence of a consumer.

**L-3. A boolean derived from "the directory contains any file" is a false positive waiting to happen.**
`google_voice.py:3250` reports `session_exists = profile_exists or legacy_exists`, and `_profile_exists`
is only "the dir has any file in it" — so a leftover `BrowserMetrics` file made a dead account look
live, and the observer launched Chrome against it in a loop for months. **So: a liveness field must be
derived from the thing that must be live (a cookie that has not expired), never from the artefact
directory existing.**

**L-4. A 30-day window over a subsystem that is 2 days old invents a defect.** I reported the
attention digest as "fired twice in 30 days" — a failure. It had not existed for 28 of those days; the
timer was created 2026-10-13T02:24:57 and the digest sent on 10-13 and 10-15 and was correctly held on
10-14. Same shape as a file's mtime not being its content's freshness. **So: before calling a rate a
failure, find when the producer started, and report the rate against the window it actually had.**

**L-5. A bare `except Exception` around all the database work in a delivery gate fails closed and
silent, and one bug becomes total.** All 2,780 `gate_error` denials in 30 days were **one** `TypeError`
(`getattr expected at most 3 arguments, got 7`) denied by the catch-all at `app/owner_text.py:2749`,
over three days. **So: a gate that decides whether the owner is told something must fail OPEN and LOUD —
narrow the except, and make a failed decision-write visible rather than swallowed.**

**L-6. The owner's own texts to the AI line were filed as questions to the owner.** Queue rows #458 and
#459 were his inbound messages, and #458 was his complaint that the AI asks too much at once. The
system asked him whether it should answer him. **So: before filing an owner question, check whether the
"counterparty" is the owner — his own number will be in the same table as the customers'.**

**L-7. A blocked item that has been re-measured 13 times is not blocked, it is miscategorised.** Item 20
sat blocked on a merge the owner was the only legal actor for, with the branch proven and the tests
green, across 13 attempts. The blocker was a policy, and the policy was wrong. **So: when an item's own
attempt log repeats the same blocked conclusion, the thing to question is the blocker.**
