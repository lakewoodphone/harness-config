**L2346 · Verify the sentence you wrote, not just the quotes you fetched (an unsourced inference about aristocratic brides)**

RULE: verification has to cover the sentence you WROTE, not only the quotes you fetched.

CASE (2026-09-22, GoT historical-accuracy research). I had verified, with a real source, that canon law set the minimum marriage age at 12 (girls) / 14 (boys) and that ordinary people in northwest Europe married in their mid-20s. Then I wrote, as a bridging sentence and without any source for it: "most aristocratic brides were older than 12 too." That is not what the evidence says. A second research pass found the opposite: canon law's ages were a floor used at the top of society, young noble marriage did happen (Isabella of France at ~12; Margaret Beaufort married at 12 in 1455 and bore Henry VII at 13, with a birth that nearly killed her and left her unable to conceive again), though it was often only a betrothal with consummation deferred.

WHY IT MATTERS: every quote in that report was individually checked and correct. The error lived in the connective tissue I added to make the quotes read smoothly - an inference doing the work of a fact. Quote-level verification does not catch it, because there was no quote to check. The only defence is to go back over your own prose and ask, sentence by sentence, "which fetched source says THIS sentence?" - and to delete or flag anything that has no answer.

DO: after a verification pass on sources, do a second pass on your own claims. Mark every sentence that is inference rather than quotation, and either support it or say plainly that it is a judgement.
