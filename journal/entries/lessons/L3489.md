**L3489 · On the 29 mm label roll, characters per line IS the font size - cut the text, do not look for a bigger printer**

MEASURED 2026-10-08, after the owner rejected the first Heber callback label as too small:
'the text is way too small, print a bigger label with larger words'.

THE MECHANISM
label_printer.render() calls _fit() per line against the usable tape width (306 px - 2*8 px at 300 dpi
= 290 px). So each line is auto-sized DOWN until it fits, and the longest text on the label decides how
small the type gets. Nothing about the printer, the dpi or the roll was wrong.

THE ARITHMETIC THAT FIXED IT
First tape: longest line 45 chars -> lines rendered ~13-16 px (~1.2 mm cap height) -> rejected.
Second tape: longest line 20 chars -> same fitter set every line 2-3x larger -> accepted.
Practical ceiling on 29 mm: about 7 lines of about 20 characters. Ten lines means 45 characters means
tiny.

HOW TO APPLY IT
When a label must be read at a glance, budget the text BEFORE writing it: 7 lines, about 20 characters
each. Cut history, city, and reminders off the tape and leave them in the case file - the tape carries
only what the reader must not get wrong. Verify with --dry-run --out <png> and READ THE PNG; do not
send tape blind.

ALSO
The top three lines are the anchor at ~26 px ('CALL HEBER' / phone / one fact). Keep them under 15
characters or they shrink with everything else.
