## L — The offsite backup was dead for 37 hours and every alarm was correct; nothing was reading it

MEASURED 2026-10-09. The company's offsite copies exist in TWO independent forms, both to
Cloudflare R2, and ONE of them had been failing silently for 37 hours while the monitor
printed CRITICAL hourly.

WHAT EXISTS (so nobody rebuilds it):
 * push-db-offsite.sh (secratary ~/bin, cron 10 5 * * *) gzips the raw DB to
 authority/secretary.db.gz in R2 (bucket lakewood-rentals-media), boto3 with a token in
 ~/.lpt-r2.env mode 600, SCOPED TO ONE BUCKET and verified refused by others. Writes a
 .sha256 sidecar and verifies remote byte count.
 * company-offsite-push.sh (secratary ~/bin, cron 40 1,7,13,19 * * *) streams the newest
 6-hourly snapshot tarball to Hetzner Box 2 (lpt-apps-01), which verifies bytes and writes
 it into a SEPARATE restic repo psm-backups/company-offsite. Separate repo on purpose:
 the nightly lpt-apps job runs `restic forget` with no tag filter and would otherwise
 evict the company's copy.
 * report-offsite-age.sh (every 30 min) reports the newest TRUSTED company copy to the vault.

THE THREE FAULTS, all found 2026-10-09:
 1. company-offsite-push cron named /home/zabz/personal-secretary-mvp/scripts/server/
 company-offsite-push.sh, but the script had MOVED to /home/zabz/bin/. Exit 127 every
 6 h since 2026-10-07T19:40 (16 runs). Last good snapshot 2026-10-07T13:40:16 - exactly
 the "52h old" the monitor reported.
 2. push-db-offsite staged its ~6.6 GB gzip in /tmp, which on this host is a 12 GB
 RAM-BACKED tmpfs (pain P2892). 2026-10-09 05:10: "gzip: stdout: No space left on
 device / COMPRESS FAILED". Staging now goes to /home/zabz/.offsite-stage on real disk
 with a REFUSE-IF-TOO-LITTLE-SPACE guard.
 3. The lpt-apps restic repo held a stale lock from PID 2603923 created 2026-09-15
 (558 h). Cleared with restic unlock.

RULES
 * When a job reports exit 127, the SCRIPT MOVED. Check the path before believing anything
 about the job's logic.
 * Never stage a multi-GB temp file in /tmp on this fleet: /tmp is tmpfs and it is RAM.
 * An hourly CRITICAL that nobody reads is not monitoring. The monitor was 100% correct for
 37 hours. What was missing was anyone consuming it.
 * Two repos, never merged: the company's offsite and lpt-apps' own.
