**L1972 · A document's own subtotal is not evidence - re-sum the rows before you spend against it**

**A subtotal a document prints about itself is a claim, not a measurement. Re-derive it from the rows before any money decision rests on it.**

**Measured, 2026-09-18.** The Shlock build sheet (`lpt-hub/research/miscellaneous/2026-09-17-shlock-build-sheet.md`) printed `LOCAL-ANY-DAY $1,065.61` and a grand total of `$1,512.26`. Re-summing its own section 3b row by row - taking the low and high end of every line carrying a range - gives **$1,300.83 - $1,376.35**, i.e. the local spend was **understated by $235.22 - $310.74**, about a fifth. The same re-sum reproduced the Amazon subtotal at **$446.65 exactly**, which is the control: the method is right, the input was wrong.

**Why it mattered and would have mattered more.** The number was about to be used for exactly the decision it corrupts: whether the May brace hardware box has to be re-bought ($0 vs $347) and whether the whole build fits a budget. A shopping list is the one document where arithmetic *is* the deliverable. Every line item was already correct and complete - nothing was missing - so a reader checking the list would have found nothing wrong. Only re-adding it exposes the error.

**The rule.** When a total gates a purchase, a budget or a go/no-go, re-sum the rows and say which method you used. If a document states a range somewhere, the honest total is a range, and its low end is not the sum of the low ends of unrelated estimates taken as if confirmed. Prefer deriving totals from a machine-readable source over transcribing a number someone typed.
