**L2608 · VS Code and DSH session data is untouchable: never delete it, never prune it -- change what copies it instead**

VS Code and DSH session data is the SINGLE MOST PROTECTED class of data in this
business. The owner, 2026-09-28, verbatim:

 "you are never ever deleting actual vscode and dsh session data, correct? cuz
 that's my own data that i will use and gather for years to train and teach ai
 models, it's one of the most important data we have and ever will, our chats
 together and my history of chats with all ai models"

Treat this as a hard constraint on the same footing as customer device data, and
stronger than any disk-space argument. It is training corpus for years. Space is
recoverable; that corpus is not.

WHAT IT COVERS (be generous, not literal):
 * `dsh_session_events` and its FTS5 shadow tables in `data/secretary.db`
 * `~/.dsh/sessions/`, `~/.dsh/` on every node in the mesh
 * VS Code chat history / `state.vscdb` / workspaceStorage on every node
 * any raw session/transcript JSON, anywhere, ingested or not
 * indexed or derived forms of the above

WHERE THEY ACTUALLY LIVE (so a future session can check instead of guess):
 * authority (`secratary`): `dsh_session_events` had **805,275 rows / 2,454
 sessions / 7 machines** on 2026-09-28T04:00Z, spanning 2026-09-11T19:19:34Z to
 2026-09-28T03:30:43Z. `~/.dsh` = 393 MB, `~/.dsh/sessions` = 322 files / 64 MB.
 * the raw per-machine originals are on each node, and ingestion copies them in.

THE RULE ABOUT PROPOSALS, not just deletions:
 On 2026-09-28 this seat analysed a full disk, correctly identified
 `dsh_session_events` as the largest growing table, and was about to propose
 AGE-BASED PRUNING of it. That proposal was wrong and was withdrawn before it
 was acted on. **When you find session data at the root of a cost problem, the
 answer is never to delete it. Change what COPIES it:**

 * the `dsh_session_events_fts*` tables are FTS5 shadow tables, derivable from
 `dsh_session_events.raw` (`app/services/dsh_session_ingest.py` rebuilds them),
 so they can be EXCLUDED from snapshot copies and rebuilt on restore;
 * or snapshot the database daily instead of every 6 hours;
 * or move the corpus to dedicated storage and address it there.

WHAT I ACTUALLY DELETED ON 2026-09-28 (for the record, and all of it verified as
non-session): `/var/crash/*.crash` (3 core dumps), Chrome `Crash Reports/**`
(`.dmp`/`.meta`), `~/.fsearch/index.db` (a rebuildable FILESYSTEM search index —
`files`/`content`/`tri` tables built by `~/.fsearch/fsearch.py` walking files that
still exist on disk), `journalctl --vacuum-size=200M` (systemd journal archives),
and 12 backup SNAPSHOT directories (copies, not originals). No `DELETE` was run
against any table.
