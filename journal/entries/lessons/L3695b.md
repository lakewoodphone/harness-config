**L3695b · Clause 3's bound is confirmed by the tool itself: no in-ledger source reaches before 2026-07-01**

2026-10-13. Clause 3's bound is now confirmed from the tool's own documentation rather than inferred from my
failures. The statement mail is the only pre-2026-07 source, and nothing can extract its detail.

WHAT `cfo-balances.py` SAYS ABOUT ITS OWN INPUTS, verbatim from its docstring:
 `balance_snapshots` (source `snapshot`). The only in-ledger balance store. Measured 2026-10-02:
 1573 rows, all `balance_type='current'` except one, from `plaid-api` (1546,
 2026-08-04..2026-10-02 - the nightly cron), `chase-scraper` (14) and `manual` (11), both ONE-SHOT
 on 2026-07-01, and `manual-boa-reconcile` (2). **It cannot give any month before 2026-07-01.**
So:
 * Plaid snapshots begin 2026-08-04 and are the only automatic source;
 * `chase-scraper` contributed FOURTEEN rows in a single manual run on 2026-07-01 and is not a running
 scraper - which retroactively answers the question I raised at round 97 about a `chase-scraper`
 source "worth a look". It is one afternoon's work, not a feed;
 * **nothing in the ledger can date a balance before 2026-07-01**, and the statement mail is the only
 instrument for the months before that.

WHY THAT CLOSES THE QUESTION. I had been describing clause 3's bound as "needs the statement PDFs",
inferred from the fact that five alert emails I read carry a balance and no transaction list. The
docstring says the same thing from the other end: the in-ledger balance stores cannot reach those months
at all, so the statement mail is not merely the best source for them, it is the ONLY source, and its
absence of item detail is therefore a hard floor rather than a preference. **The residual - 47,414.04
between two authoritative readings - is not recoverable from anything on this machine, and that is now a
documented property of the inputs rather than a limit of my effort.**

AND ONE THING I HAD WRONG, CORRECTED. At round 97's census I read `chase-scraper` as a live source in the
anchor mix and said it deserved a look. It is 14 rows from one run on 2026-07-01, the same day as the 11
`manual` rows. **Both are the one-shot 2026-07-01 batch the tool's R1 and R2 rules were written to defend
against** - the same batch whose month note says "of which 7 are demonstrably defective". So the two
non-authoritative disagreements in that census are not evidence of a dubious feed; they are two readings
from a known-bad batch, and the tool already warns about both.

THE HONEST CLAUSE-3 STATEMENT, then: the audit is complete at 65 real defects, every reading carries its
provenance, R4 rejects impossible balances before any transaction is examined and R5 collapses six of them
into three - and the remaining 47,414.04 requires an input this machine does not have and cannot obtain.
