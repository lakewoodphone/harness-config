**L3051 · For an RC aircraft, the manual's order of operations is the safety device**

Measured 2026-09-28, first flight of a HobbyZone Sport Cub S 2, advising the owner live at a baseball field.

**The owner's own reading of the launch was: hold the plane, throttle to 100%, then throw.** He asked me to confirm it. **It was wrong, and plausibly injurious.** The manual warns in its own words: *"Always keep hands away from the propeller. When armed, the motor will turn the propeller in response to any throttle movement."* **The motor arms the moment the flight battery connects.** Throttling to 100% while holding the aircraft puts a spinning prop in the hand. **Throw first, throttle after** - and the manual's own preference is the one that removes the problem entirely: *"Get help to hand launch your aircraft so you can concentrate on flying."*

**Second error, mine, caught by reading the manual:** I had the **control direction test** in Beginner mode. The manual says the opposite - *"Place the aircraft into Experienced mode (Mode switch position 2)"* - then *"move the SAFE mode switch to Beginner mode (Position 0) to prepare to fly."* **The reason is mechanical, not stylistic: in Beginner mode the stabiliser limits servo travel, so full and correct control response cannot be verified on the ground.** A pre-flight check performed in the mode that suppresses the thing being checked is not a check.

**THE GENERALISATION: in a hobby where a mistake injures the operator or destroys the machine, the manual's SEQUENCE is itself a safety device, and paraphrasing it from general knowledge is how you get the order wrong.** Knowing what the controls do is not knowing when to touch them. The questions that matter - what order, what mode, what is armed when - live in the procedure, and a procedure recalled from memory is a procedure that has drifted.

**Practical rule: read the actual procedure before advising on any operation with an irreversible or injurious failure mode.** Both errors above were mine-or-confirmed-by-me, both were one manual page away from correct, and both would have been discovered by the owner at the field with the aircraft running.

**Corollary recorded in the same session: never assume a topic is covered because it is adjacent.** I implied the manual covers landing; it does not - it covers hand launch and hard-runway takeoff and directs the reader to "After Flying." The landing advice given was general practice, and it is now labelled as such in the household record rather than presented as sourced.
