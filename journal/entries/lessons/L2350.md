**L2350 · The unit-price-as-total defect is unique in the case corpus - measured, with the detector and its negative controls**

Closing the loop on the suspicion recorded in H2403 ("any other bulk or multi-unit case is
suspect by the same mechanism and nobody has looked"). **Measured 2026-09-22: it is unique.**

Method, so this is not re-run from scratch. Detector = for each of the 193 cases in
`lpt-hub/index/cases.db`, take the front-matter `price` P and look in the body prose for the
same figure presented as a PER-UNIT price: `N x $P`, `$P each`, `$P per phone|device|unit|line|
piece|item|handset`, or `P each`. A hit means P is a unit figure and the case total is N x P.

Result: **0 cases.** Heichal was the only one, and its front-matter is now 840.

Negative controls worth keeping, because they are what makes the 0 trustworthy:
- **Do NOT detect on `N x $X` alone.** That pattern matched 60+ cases and almost all were
 noise: screen resolutions (`1920x1080`, `1366x768`, `5184x3888`), NAND/SPI hex addresses
 (`0x8000`, `0x4141`), unit costs quoted from a supplier (`5x$1.35` = a component price, not
 a customer price), and throughput figures (`300x235`). A detector built on it would have
 produced ~20 false positives and buried the one real find.
- The one genuine multi-unit money case, `data-recovery-347-534-6208-2026-09-01` (price
 1920), is correct and says so in the body: "`price: 1920` = the accepted deal ($900 + $700
 on success, **plus** the $320 deposit)". It also records what it superseded. That is the
 pattern Heichal should have followed and now does.

The generalisable rule, which is the reason to keep this record at all: **when a case's price
is a sum over several units or several lines, the front-matter must hold the sum and the body
must show the arithmetic.** A single number in front-matter with no visible arithmetic is not
wrong by itself - it is just unverifiable, and unverifiable is what let `130` sit there for
four weeks while the order shipped and nobody collected.
