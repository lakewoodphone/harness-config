**L5792 · CORRECTS L4704 and H4744: the Fios service belongs to the OFFICE, not the house - a service record belongs to an address, not a household or an SSID; plus iOS has no band picker and phyRate reads 6 Mbps whenever a client is idle**

CORRECTS L4704 and H4744, both written earlier on 2026-10-15, and the queue amendment made at the same time.

WHAT I CLAIMED: "Verizon Fios 300 was installed at this address on 2026-09-14, order NJ12338123101, about 30 a month, ONT serial ALCLECEF1FB204 - and the ONT is not feeding the router. So you pay 30 for fiber you are not using and 85 for the cellular line." I presented that as a measured finding and amended owner queue row 207 on the strength of it.

WHAT THE OWNER SAID, 2026-10-15 evening, verbatim: "You know the office is on [Fios] but the house is on [T-]mobile router that I put a wire to my Verizon Router. And now the Verizon one is the router." So the Fios install is the OFFICE's, and the house's only uplink is the cellular gateway.

WHERE I WENT WRONG, precisely: every measured fact was right - the CHR30A's WAN is 192.168.12.245 via 192.168.12.1, that address is the T-Mobile TMO-G4AR, the router is a second router behind it - and then I attached the fiber record to the wrong building. The record even warned me: H206 recorded that the order's service address is 1001 W KENNEDY BLVD and listed it as open item 2, "the order's service address may not be the owner's home". I read the same document and took a caveat it had already flagged and resolved it in the wrong direction, because the SSID in the install photos (Verizon_ZYFY4N) matched the SSID of the router I was talking to, and I let that coincidence beat the written caveat. Same model of router, same SSID pattern, two sites.

THE RULE, and it is the second time this shape has bitten: a service record, a bill or an order belongs to an ADDRESS, not to a person, a household or an SSID. Before attaching one to a site, read the service address on the order and check it against the site - and if a prior entry already flagged that address as uncertain, that is a question to answer, not a detail to average over. Property and utility records repeat the mistake the audit already named: the wrong data read confidently.

WHAT SURVIVES, unchanged and re-verified from the router after the correction: the house's uplink is the T-Mobile cellular gateway behind a second router; the stack costs 0-1 ms and 0% loss and is not the slowdown; the LAN has no IPv6 because no prefix is delegated; the path MTU is 1436 against a 1500 WAN; the phones sometimes land on the 2.4 GHz radio. Only the fiber inference and everything built on it is withdrawn. Row 207 re-amended: the cellular line is the house's only internet, so cancelling it leaves the house with nothing unless something replaces it.

TWO SMALLER FACTS WORTH KEEPING FROM THE SAME EVENING, both verified:
- iOS has no Wi-Fi band picker. Apple's own page (support.apple.com/en-us/102285) documents exactly one related setting, "Wi-Fi 6E mode", at Settings > Wi-Fi > (i) on the connected network, and it is an OFF switch for 6 GHz that appears only once the phone is already on a 6E or 7 network. Nothing in iOS forces a band.
- The CHR30A's `phyRate`, `currentRxModulationRate` and `currentTxModulationRate` collapse to the 6 Mbps basic rate whenever a client is idle. Read twice the same day, the same iPhone showed 25.8 Mbps and then 6.0 Mbps with nothing else changed. Compare clients by `maximumRxModulationRate`, `rssi` and `snr`, never by `phyRate`; and treat any single device-table read as a moment, not a condition - the same two phones were on 2.4 GHz at 14:38 UTC and on 5 GHz at 01:06 UTC.
