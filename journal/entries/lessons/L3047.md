**L3047 · A route under time pressure needs the clock and the live observation, not the forecast**

Measured 2026-09-28, Toms River kite run, Chol Hamoed.

**The forecast was the wrong instrument twice in one evening.**

1. NWS gridpoint PHI 81,90 said 5 mph NW. Live station observations at the same moment: KMJX Toms River 11.1 km/h = **6.9 mph** NW, KBLM Allaire **8.1 mph** NW. The owner said it himself before I checked - 'even if wind is 5 probably not true'. **A gridpoint forecast is a model cell, and an ASOS station is an instrument.** For 'can I do this activity right now', the station is the truth and the gridpoint is an estimate: api.weather.gov/stations/<ID>/observations/latest.

2. The instinctive move - drive east to the shore for more wind - was **backwards**, and only the observation showed it. The offshore buoy (NDBC 44091) read 1.4 m/s = **3.1 mph**, and the wind was NW. NW blows offshore and suppresses the sea breeze, so the coast was the lightest air in the region, not the windiest. **Check the wind DIRECTION against the geography before recommending a drive toward water.** An offshore wind at the beach is dead air; a sea-breeze day is the opposite and looks identical in a temperature forecast.

**The second clock.** Sunset 18:45, civil twilight 19:11, and wind 0 mph from 20:00. Three separate clocks, and they order the evening: the kite must fly BEFORE the ice cream, not after. A plan that sequences two activities without checking when the enabling condition expires is a plan that produces a kite bought at 19:30 for a calm night.

**Rule:** any 'go do X now' question gets (a) the live instrument reading, not the forecast, (b) the wind direction against the local geography, and (c) the expiry time of whatever makes X possible. Then the sequence writes itself.
