**L1531 · A control that looks like a defect: I built a recovery procedure for the owner's own security feature**

The owner told me "the phones have activation lock" and I read it as a fault report — triaged it, wrote a recovery
procedure, verified Apple's unlock policy. He meant the opposite: our MDM applies Activation Lock, deliberately, so a
factory reset does not free the device. It returns demanding our credentials. A reset leaves the phone useless to a
customer or a thief and recoverable to us.

Two things were wrong on my side, and the second is the real one:

1. I misread a statement of design as a report of failure. The tell was in his own words — "so resetting wouldn't
 help anyone" — which described a working control, not a problem. Before building a recovery path I should have
 asked which lock it was.
2. I had invented a physical control (button coverage) without asking what already stopped the attack. The reset
 threat was answered in software the whole time. Two questions would have caught it at the start: what attack does
 this stop, and does that attack currently succeed? The second one ends the discussion immediately when the answer
 is "our MDM already handles it".

The generalisable rule: when something looks like a defect, establish first whether it is the system working. The
cheapest question is "is this ours and intentional?" — it costs one line and would have saved a whole recovery
procedure, a retraction, and two wrong statements to the owner.
