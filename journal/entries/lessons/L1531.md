**Fifth instance of one defect: an aggregate with no time bound reports history as live.**

Measured 2026-09-15, in my own kernel, in five rounds:

| check | what it read | what was actually true |
|---|---|---|
| `attention_debt` | CRITICAL: 42 urgent/critical held | 40 of them were briefings from weeks earlier |
| `check_delivery` | "flowing" / then permanently severed | measured a channel the owner had deliberately closed |
| `tick_completion` | CRITICAL: collapse windows | every window had ended six weeks earlier; today 94% |
| `session_archive` | HIGH: 21 sessions 14.7h stale | that machine had nothing new to ship |
| `agent_dead_weight` | HIGH: 5 agents completing nothing | they last ticked on 2026-07-21/22, $0.02 each |

Five checks, one mistake, three different flavours of it: no time bound (dead_weight, tick_completion),
the wrong clock (delivery, attention_debt), and a quantity that cannot distinguish failure from quiet
success (session_archive).

The rule I want to apply *before* writing the next check, not after:

1. **Bound the window**, and for any per-entity age, say what a *quiet* entity looks like -- if quiet and
 broken are indistinguishable from here, report it and do not colour the badge.
2. **Ask what would make it green.** If the answer is "when the history rolls out of the window" or
 "never", it is a countdown to numbness.
3. **Keep the history, name it, and stop shouting about it.** Every fix above left the record intact
 (aged rows, historical windows, retired agents) — which is why the corrections are believable and why
 nothing had to be deleted.

The badge went 6 → 3 attention in four rounds, and every one of the three removals was a false alarm
rather than a repaired fault. That ratio is itself the finding: **my monitoring was noisiest where
nothing was wrong**, and the real faults (a fail-open guard, a Windows-only path decoder, a duplicated
work queue) were found by reading data, not by reading the badge.
