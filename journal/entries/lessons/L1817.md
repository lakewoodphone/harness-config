**L1817 · A watcher task may only be closed by evidence of the OUTCOME, not by having been written**

**Rule.** A watch/follow-up task may only be closed by evidence that the **outcome** happened — a delivery,
a shipment, a payment, a tracking number. Closing it because the email was sent, the chase was drafted, or
"someone is looking into it" retires the only thing standing between the owner and silence.

**Measured evidence (2026-09-14 → 2026-09-16).** Task `#24869` ("ALCO sukkah tarp #216040-00 — chase ship
date + tracking") was created 2026-09-14 02:09:18 on the authority, with the vendor's status endpoint in its
description, and marked **done at 02:33:41 — 24 minutes later**, with no verification of any kind. Its own
stated purpose was to survive vendor silence. Two days later the delivery notice (Ellie Wilson, 2026-09-16
11:00 EDT, "your order has already been delivered") was sitting **UNREAD** in the inbox and every durable
record still read "awaiting reply". Tasks `#21518` and `#21497` (both "track the ALCO order") were closed the
same way on 2026-08-30 — the order then went unlooked-at for 16 days.

**Why this is a class, not an incident.** This is P52b's "a promise with a date has no keeper" in its purest
form: the keeper existed and was retired by the act of writing it down. The general shape is that *task
status is treated as a proxy for world state*. It is not. Status is a claim about our own bookkeeping; the
outcome is a fact about the world.

**What to do instead.** Any watcher/commitment row carries (a) the outcome that closes it, (b) the source
that proves the outcome (carrier tracking, portal receipt, bank line), and (c) a date-bound poll that
escalates. `done` requires the evidence pasted into the row; the absence of evidence leaves it open and
loud. Until the promise-keeper exists, the manual stand-in is a task with the evidence requirement written
into its description.
