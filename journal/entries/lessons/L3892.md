**L3892 · Never write 'the X you asked for': an agent's claim of owner intent cancelled an insurance policy; and 'unable to open database file' was an fd leak, not disk**

Two findings from 2026-10-20, both worth keeping because both will recur.

## 1. An agent attributed an intent to the owner that the record did not support

Asked why his business insurance policy `PWC1292029` was cancelled, the owner said *"i think you told me to do that"*. He was right, and the transcript is exact — session `24645fb9-5ac0-4c95-8562-eab1a835c3f4`, 2026-10-19 21:47:13Z:

> "They reference request 2643901, **which is the cancellation you asked for** — signing both completes it, and not signing leaves the policy running."

The agent's own reasoning at that turn says *"recommend signing both"*. So it was a recommendation, not a relay.

**The defect is the clause "the cancellation you asked for."** The only visible carrier ticket, 2643901, is titled **"Add Payroll Suspension"**, and its forms arrived at 14:18Z — hours before any agent spoke. The owner appears to have asked to stop a payroll-based premium for an employee no longer on payroll, and was sent **cancellation** forms for the whole policy. The agent then told him the cancellation was what he had asked for.

**Rule:** never write "the X you asked for" unless you can quote the owner asking for X. Attending a request the owner made is not evidence about a different request the counterparty sent. When a counterparty's document is titled differently from what the owner asked for, that difference IS the finding — surface it, do not smooth it over. The cost here was a cancelled insurance policy that the owner believed he had chosen.

Related and also worth keeping: the pre-existing work order `C12-CANCEL-WORKERS-COMP` was surfaced to him repeatedly since at least 17 September on the reasoning *"NEXT has been debiting 28 to 32 a week for an employee who no longer exists"*. A standing work order about a premium is not a decision to cancel a policy; it is a prompt to ask.

## 2. `unable to open database file` was a file-descriptor leak, not a disk

The estate's entire search surface refused — `estate:code`, `estate:docs`, `authority:sessions` all returned `OperationalError: unable to open database file`, while the tool correctly reported each as **a refusal, not an empty result**. My first hypothesis was the disk, because `/root/authority.db` is 34 GB on a box that had been at 91–92%. **The hypothesis was wrong:** 225G total, 161G used, **56G free, 75%**, inodes 17%.

The real cause: esearch PID 312159 held **1,023 open file descriptors against a soft `RLIMIT_NOFILE` of 1,024** — 342 x `authority.db`, 342 x `authority.db-wal`, 168 x `usearch.db`, 165 x `usearch.db-wal`, all read handles opened hours earlier. Each request's short-lived pool thread cached one SQLite handle per store in `threading.local`; the threads died and the handles did not close, leaking roughly 7 per query until `sqlite3.connect` returned `SQLITE_CANTOPEN`. Manual read-only *and* read-write opens of both databases as root succeeded — which is what ruled out permissions and corruption.

Fixed: `prlimit --pid <pid> --nofile=65536:524288` restored service with no restart; then the leak was patched in `server/esearch.py` (`Store.close_local()` / `Engine.close_local()` called in the pool task `finally` and the response `finally`), a `LimitNOFILE=65536` systemd drop-in was added, and the service restarted to load both. Verified after: **19–20 fds** (was 1,023 and climbing ~14 per query), **zero** `unable to open` lines, and real queries answering — `estate:code` 9 hits, `estate:docs` 14, `authority:sessions` 3, `estate` 21 with `did_not_answer: []`.

**Rules:**
- `unable to open database file` means "the process cannot obtain a handle", and a full disk is only one of four causes. Check, in this order: **fds (`ls /proc/<pid>/fd | wc -l`) and `RLIMIT_NOFILE`**, permissions on the file *and its directory* (SQLite writes `-wal`/`-shm` beside it), free space and inodes, then the file's existence.
- A tool that distinguishes **refusal from empty** is doing its job. Three stores refusing in unison is a service-level fault, not three independent empty results — and treating it as "no matches" would have closed a real outage as a clean negative.
- The patch is **uncommitted** in `/root/code/unified-search` (diff `/root/esearch-fdleak-20261020.diff`). Until it is committed, the next restart reinstalls the leak.
