**L3790 · L — iPhone 11 is LTE-only, and 'Using offline maps' is Waze's startup label, not our filter**

**Two things the owner asked about on 2026-10-14, both now settled.**

**"It says 4G, not 5G."** An iPhone 11 has **no 5G radio** — Apple's specification for it lists
LTE bands only ("Gigabit-class LTE with 2x2 MIMO and LAA"), and 5G arrived with the iPhone 12. `4G`
beside the bars is this hardware's ceiling, not a degraded state. Nothing to fix, and no judgement
about the SIM or the network is available from that label.

**"Waze says 'Using offline maps'."** That is Waze's own label — a persistent status in the top-right
of the map screen, introduced early 2024 replacing a plainer "offline" warning, with **no change in
functionality**. Two properties matter:

- it is set when Waze **cannot reach its servers at startup**, and
- it **sticks after connectivity returns** until the app is force-quit or the phone is toggled
 through airplane mode. That is a Waze behaviour, not a fault on our side.

Waze ships **no offline map** and cannot compute a route offline — client-side routing uses cached
tiles only, with no live traffic and **no ETA**. So a route computed with working data plus an
"offline maps" label is a **stale label**, not an offline session. The diagnostic consequence:
**do not chase this banner as a network fault; force-quit the app first and see whether it returns.**

**And the check that closes it, which is the reusable part.** The owner's instinct was that our
filter was to blame. That is answerable by exhaustion, not by argument: list **every hostname the
filter has ever refused** (`grep -E '(^| )(default-blocked|blocked) ' | awk '{print $NF}' | sort -u`
over the container log) and look for anything the app's path needs. On 2026-10-14 that set was
Apple/iTunes/iTunes-bag/Akamai/DigiCert plumbing, Apple Maps CDN, `firebaseappcheck.googleapis.com`
and `www.google.com` — and **zero `waze.com` hostnames**, with every waze host the devices asked for
answered (`ctilesgcs-am` tiles, `rt-xlb-am`, `renderer-am`, `wig-am`, `tts-assets.voice`,
`inbox-am.ingress`, `cres`/`cresg`, `gapi`). A default-deny filter is exactly the kind of thing that
silently breaks an app, so the answer has to be the complete refused-set, not the rule list.

The one thing in our stack that genuinely could have caused this: the filter answered **SERVFAIL**
when its single 3-second UDP query to a single resolver failed, with `AllowFailover: false` behind it
so the device had **no other resolver**. 11 occurrences, four for Waze startup hosts. Fixed — see the
win entry for how it was proved.
