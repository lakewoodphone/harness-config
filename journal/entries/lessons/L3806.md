**L3806 · The journal's 63 id collisions cannot be repaired by any automatic rule: mtime is a rewrite time, and the body date is a date the entry mentions**

The journal's 63 cross-machine id collisions cannot be repaired by any automatic rule available, and I nearly executed one that would have silently mis-mapped between 27 and 63 entries. Stopping was the correct action.

MEASURED 2026-10-14 ~12:35Z from ZABZ-YOGA. The authority's `journal/entries` was tar'd and staged locally, then compared entry by entry:

 local=4056 auth=4037 both=4022 identical=3960 collisions=63

So **3,960 of 4,022 shared ids are byte-identical** — the divergence is 63 ids, not a broad split. Those 63 are genuine: both sides hold a legitimate entry under the same id, written by different hosts. Inspected examples: `D3147` is *"A DSH window slot owns its own blank session"* (ZABZ-YOGA) versus *"Ullman IdeaPad 3 (NM-D471): one price, not a menu"* (SECRATARY-CLOUD); `H3632` is *"Evolution round 2"* versus *"Mendelowitz closes clean"*; `H3641` is *"the loop build fixes the completion-truth test"* versus *"Family chat: the owner's phone was removed by his own rate floor"*.

**The rule everyone reaches for is mtime, and on this estate mtime is a rewrite timestamp, not an allocation timestamp.** The authority's colliding files cluster on a handful of identical mtimes — 03:09 for L3794-L3797, 00:19 for several P31xx, 22:03, 23:44, 20:10, 17:38 — because a bulk rebuild wrote them, not because 4 entries were authored in the same minute. Judged by mtime, **50 of 63 ids go to the authority and my own just-minted entries are renamed** — including the entries this session wrote and cited in `PROGRAM.md`, `55-company-design.md` and the handoff. The rule would have broken the references it was meant to preserve.

**So I tried the entry's own recorded time instead, and that fails too.** Taking the first `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` in the body: for 31 local and 34 authority entries that finds a value, but it is frequently a date the entry *mentions* rather than when it was written — local `L3788` reads 2026-09-25, authority `L3783` reads 2026-10-06, and both `L3778` and authority `L3796` read 2026-10-11 05:43, which is the cutover timestamp being quoted. And for the remaining pairs only the entry header's day-granularity date exists, so **13 pairs tie exactly** (D3147, D3149, L3776, L3777, L3779, L3780, L3784-L3787, L3793, P3176, P3182).

 decisions where the two rules disagree = 27 of 63
 ties the second rule cannot decide = 13 of 63

**Therefore: do not renumber on a heuristic.** Renaming one side arbitrarily makes roughly half of every citation silently wrong, and a citation of `D3147` is *already* ambiguous. The honest options are (a) read each of the 63 pairs and decide, 63 small decisions by someone who can see both sides, or (b) change the model so ambiguity is representable: keep both files under distinct names and have `show`/`resolve_alias` report "this id means two entries" instead of inventing a winner. **Option (b) is the design answer; option (a) is the migration.**

**What is not in doubt is the cause.** Both hosts mint ids from their own tree's maximum, so two machines that do not see each other's newest entry mint the same number. Nothing is broken in the *storing*; the allocation is local. `resolve-journal-merge.py`'s docstring says the cause was fixed at the source on 2026-09-15 because `git_max` reads remote-tracking refs — **but these four checkouts sit on four different branches and evidently do not fetch each other, so the fix is not in force for this fleet.** My own `P3181` was minted at 12:14 today against an authority `P3181` written at 01:40: the defect is live, not historical.

**The fix that prevents recurrence, and the order to do it in:** (1) allocate ids from ONE authority, or reserve a per-host id band (`ZABZ-YOGA` → e.g. H7xxx, `SECRATARY-CLOUD` → H5xxx) so two hosts cannot collide by construction; (2) point `idguard` at WORKING TREES — it reports 0 collisions today because it compares generated indexes in refs, which is why 63 went unnoticed; (3) then repair these 63 by reading both sides; (4) then schedule the union.

**The general lesson, and it is the fourth one of this shape in one session:** a *plausible* field is not the *right* field. mtime looked authoritative and is a rewrite artefact; "first date in the body" looked authoritative and is the first date the entry talks about. Both produced confident, internally consistent, wrong answers. I made the same class of error three times today before catching it (`L3796` predicate matched the denial, `L3801` window straddled two timestamp formats, `L3803` boundary was a file mtime rather than a process start) and this time I caught it **before** acting. The check that catches it every time is the same: **print the raw field next to the predicate, and ask what writes that field.**
