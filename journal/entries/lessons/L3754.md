**L3754 · I repeated a delegated audit's finding as a defect without reading the line it named - the code had a paragraph explaining why it was the safe choice**

MEASURED 2026-10-12. A parallel audit reported that frontend/src/features/staff-intake/components/StaffSaleChargePanel.tsx:179 defaults to taxIncluded = true, 'the opposite of the owner's rule', and I relayed that to the owner twice as an open defect. Reading the twelve lines above the statement showed the opposite: it is documented as DELIBERATE AND THE SAFE DEFAULT, with the reasoning spelled out - *'type 180, take 18000 cents ... at a counter, silently charging 191.93 for a figure someone typed as 180 is the failure that matters'* - and the comment explicitly says the owner's own words are about the OTHER screen (StaffOrderChargePanel), which is already pre-tax and adds on top. So the reported defect was a considered decision with a test suite pinning it, on a surface with a different contract.

THE RULE, and it is the estate's oldest one wearing a new coat: a finding is not evidence. Before relaying a delegated claim - especially as a defect, and especially in the money path - open the file at the line the finding names and read the surrounding code. A comment that explains WHY is the most likely thing to be sitting exactly where the finding points, because that is where somebody else already thought about it.

WHAT IT COST: two user-facing statements that were wrong, and a place on the open list that would have been 'fixed' into a behaviour that charges a walk-in customer more than the figure typed. The same session had already killed two other delegated claims the same way (a tax-key redaction helper that does not exist; a pay-notice minting order defended by an evaluateOrder gate) - that is THREE of four delegated claims wrong in one audit. Treat a delegated finding as a lead to check, never as a fact to repeat.
