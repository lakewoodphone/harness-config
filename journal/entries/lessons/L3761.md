**L3761 · Freshness: ledger-first, heartbeat only as fallback — never the newest of the two**

## The rule

When a "is X fresh?" check has several candidate signals, **do not take the newest of them.**
Prefer the source's own direct evidence and fall back to a proxy only where the direct
evidence does not exist at all.

## Why — the measurement that forced it

Building `finance-freshness` (money-clock fleet, 2026-10-13) I first let `last_success` be the
**maximum** of:

- `transactions.created_at` — when a row for THIS source landed in the ledger; and
- `data/autopilot_state.json` → `subsystem_health.<name>.last_ran_at` — when the PULL last ran.

That read plausibly and was wrong. `amazon_sync` is **one** heartbeat shared by two registry
sources, the LPT lane and the personal lane. On 2026-10-13 `amazon_sync` was healthy-looking
at 2.9 days, so `lpt_amazon` reported **OK** — while that lane's own newest row in the ledger
was **68.1 days old (n=1)**. A shared heartbeat papered over a source-specific 68-day hole.

Fixed by ledger-first, heartbeat only where the source has no ledger evidence of its own. That
is also exactly what the fleet brief specified, and the brief was right:

 derive last_success from the ledger itself ... plus, where a source has no ledger
 evidence, from whatever real signal exists

## The trade-off, stated so it is not "optimised" away later

Ledger-first means a genuinely **dormant account** can read STALE while its pull is fine (a card
with no new charges for 15 days against a 14-day window). That is the right way round: the pull's
own health is reported on a **second, independent axis** (`pull_state` = ok / failed /
unscheduled / no-heartbeat) on the same row, so the reader can tell "quiet account" from "dead
pull". If the heartbeat had overwritten the ledger's answer, they could not.

## Corollaries

- **Two axes, not one.** "Is what we hold too old?" and "is the thing that feeds it still on a
 clock?" disagree exactly when the books look fine because nobody has noticed the pull stopped.
 Measured case: `lpt_ebay` read OK at 4.9 days while `ebay_sync` had failed every run for two
 days. Only the second axis saw it.
- **A source you cannot judge is `COULD NOT ASK`, never `OK`.** An empty result is a refusal.
- **Reuse the registry's own predicate builder** rather than re-deriving which rows belong to a
 source, or the check and the registry will silently drift apart on exactly the sources that
 matter.
- **Naive SQLite timestamps are UTC.** This ledger's `created_at` arrives in two spellings
 (`2026-10-12T04:31:35.353421+00:00` from python, `2026-10-09 04:30:30` from
 `CURRENT_TIMESTAMP`); parse both and attach UTC, and say in the docstring that this is a fact
 about the writers rather than a guess.
