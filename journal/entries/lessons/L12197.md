**L12197 · Name-based disk sums overcount pnpm node_modules by ~57% - the ZABZ-YOGA 83 GiB mystery was hardlinks, and every forest tool shares the defect**

2026-10-22, resolving a five-week confusion. A file-by-file volume scan of ZABZ-YOGA (12,667,067 files, 1,720,873 dirs, 121 min) sums to 541.36 GiB by name and 539.95 GiB allocated, on a 475.83 GiB volume that fsutil, calibrated against a 2 GiB test file, says holds ~458 GiB used. The 83 GiB difference is the SAME BYTES UNDER SEVERAL NAMES. pnpm installs one copy into a content-addressable store (C:\\Users\\ezabz\\AppData\\Local\\pnpm\\store, 1.55 GiB / 88,050 files) and hardlinks it into every project: a package.json in three different projects reports 87 links. Five worktrees scanned together measure 7.63 GiB logical but 3.26 GiB unique - 4.37 GiB shared across 359,953 of 511,336 files (70%). CONSEQUENCES. (1) Every absolute size this estate reports for a tree containing node_modules is inflated, typically 2-3x; ranking survives, magnitudes do not. (2) Deleting a worktree frees only its unshared share - the 121 safe worktrees are 101.97 GiB apparent but ~44 GiB real. (3) Two instrument defects cost hours and must not recur: DirEntry.stat(follow_symlinks=False) returns st_ino = 0 on Windows, so a dedup keyed on it confidently reports 'no sharing' (first run: unique == logical == 541.36 on a 475.83 GiB disk - arithmetically impossible, which is the tell); and os.scandir('C:') means the current directory on drive C, not the root, so a run aimed at C: silently scanned ~/code. RULE: when a total exceeds the physical disk, stop testing hypotheses and scan for link counts - and validate any dedup by asserting that it found SOME sharing, because a dedup that finds none when sharing exists is indistinguishable from no sharing at all.
