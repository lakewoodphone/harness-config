## Correction: the reply that was "never sent" was sent -- our record was blind, not the customer

Written minutes after H2171, P2142 and L2254, which all said a customer had gone unanswered.
They were wrong in the same direction, and the owner corrected me from the Dialpad thread itself.

**What actually happened, in his words and his screenshot.** Levi (732-575-4823) left a
voicemail and a text on 2026-09-16 asking whether the shop repairs old Sony Video 8/Hi8
players. The owner replied **from Dialpad on Thursday at 11:58 am** -- refusing the job --
and Dialpad shows the message as `Delivered`.

**So three conclusions I published today are false:**

- P2142: "the duplicate-task churn around Levi ... no reply ever sent". The reply was sent.
- H2171: it lists the customer as unanswered under BROKEN. He was answered.
- L2254 and the owner-queue row: the same error.

**What survives, and is worse than what I wrote.** The outbound reply exists in Dialpad and
does not exist anywhere in our pipeline: not in `dialpad_sms_cache`, not in RDS
`communications`, not in the twelve tasks that were opened and closed about him. So the
mirror is not a record of the conversation -- it is a record of what our own export/import
loop happened to copy. That makes it unusable as evidence about a customer, in either
direction: it showed a message repeated 61 times that was sent once, and it showed no reply
to a message that was answered days ago. Both errors were confidently reported.

**The rule this leaves.** A pipeline that is known to be self-feeding must not be used to
make a claim about a human. Before saying a customer was ignored, ignored twice, or ignored
thirteen times, read the thread where the conversation actually lives -- here, the Dialpad UI.
Count copies as copies. And when the owner says "what the heck, we responded days ago", the
record is the thing on trial, not the response.

**Also learned:** the Dialpad web UI is intact and carries the real threads. It is the ground
truth, and it is the fallback ingest path, not a last resort.
