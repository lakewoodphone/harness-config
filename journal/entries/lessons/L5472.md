**L5472 · Never diagnose a charge fault from 5V on a machine that never powered up - on a Surface, an unseated display sensor strip looks exactly like a dead charge circuit**

**A panel-only screen swap on a Surface leaves the machine with a boot-critical part disconnected — and the failure looks exactly like a dead charge circuit. Measured on a Surface Laptop 4 (1950), 2026-10-15.**

**What happened.** After a bare-panel swap (new LCD panel bonded into the original lid), the machine was put
back together with the new panel and it booted and ran. Later, with the **old** panel connected — and with
**no panel at all** — it would **not power on**, and a USB-C ammeter showed it sitting at **5V** with no
Power Delivery contract. The natural reading, and the one the owner made, was *"it is not accepting charge
at all"* — a charge-path or board fault. It was neither.

**What it actually was.** The **top sensor strip** in the Surface's display assembly is required for the
machine to turn on. iFixit says it as a warning (*"Watch out for the Sensors up the top. If you damage
these the device will fail to turn on. It basically bricks completely without the sensors"*). Measured
here, in three states:

| state | result |
|---|---|
| old panel's display tails, **no sensor cable** | **no power-on**; USB-C stays at **5V**, no PD contract |
| **no panel at all** | same — no power-on |
| old panel's display tails **+ the new panel's top sensor cable** | **boots: 19V negotiated, Windows comes up** |

**The two lessons, and they generalise past Surface:**

1. **On a bare-panel swap the panel's sensor strip is a separate, boot-critical part — it must be
 connected before any power test, and it must never go back to a customer unseated.** A machine handed
 back without it simply will not turn on, and the customer will bring it straight back.
2. **Do not diagnose a charge circuit, a port or a board from a voltage reading on a machine that never
 came up.** 5V and no PD negotiation is what *an unpowered machine* looks like; a laptop that is merely
 off and full also draws almost nothing. The reading was treated as evidence of a charging fault and it
 was not evidence of anything except that the machine had never started. **The question to ask first is
 "what does the firmware require that I have removed?"** — not "why is the charger at 5V?".

**One genuinely useful side effect:** the sensor cable is **separable** from the display tails, which made
a hybrid rig possible — old panel for the picture, new panel for the sensor cable — that booted the
machine and proved the sensor arm independently. On a bare-panel job that lets you prove the boot-critical
half before anything is closed up.

**A related trap on the same machine, same night:** the owner tried an external monitor over USB-C and got
nothing, and concluded the port was dead. The port **does** do DisplayPort Alt Mode on this model
(Microsoft: *"yes, Surface Laptop 4 indeed supports DisplayPort Alternate Mode (DP alt mode) via its
USB-C port"*). The documented failure mode is **power arriving and no picture** — a cable or alt-mode
mismatch, not a dead port; a charge-only USB-C cable passes power and no video.
