**L3483 · subagent fan-out has a hard concurrency cap of 5 including the parent session - 4 children was one too many**

MEASURED 2026-10-11, ZABZ-TECH. Four parallel subagent calls (the mesh provider) were dispatched for a market-research job. Two of the four died before doing any work, with the provider's own diagnostic:

 RATE_LIMIT: Too many requests. Your current concurrency is 5, which exceeds your concurrency limit of 5 based on your remaining balance. Please top up your balance to restore your concurrency.

Both failures happened at the child's FIRST model call, after the mailbox and host report - so the child was placed, started, and then refused. The other two completed their searches. So the cap is roughly **5 concurrent model calls including the parent session**, and a 4-child fan-out sits right on it and tips over under any extra load.

RULES THAT FOLLOW:
1. **Default to 2-3 children, not 4+**, and only widen if the work genuinely partitions and the account has headroom. Rule 1b says orchestrate on large jobs; it does not say launch a wide fleet on every job. A fan-out that gets rate-limited is slower than a serial run, because the failures cost a full placement each before dying.
2. **Read the diagnostic literally and do not retry blindly.** Mine says concurrency, not quota exhaustion - 'based on your remaining balance' describes how the limit was DERIVED (5), and 'current concurrency is 5' says we are AT it. It is a cap, not necessarily an empty account. Retrying the same width will fail the same way.
3. **When a child dies on the provider, do that piece yourself.** The parent session's own tool calls - web_search, a real browser via playwright, a shell - do not consume the subagent concurrency budget. For a handful of product-page checks that is both cheaper and faster than re-fanning.
4. **A failed child is not a failed job, and must be reported as a failure.** The placement record said 'the run succeeded' while the child itself was refused; only the child's own stderr showed the rate limit. Read the child's diagnostic before believing a completion.
