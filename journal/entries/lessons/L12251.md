## The dictionary "cannot be blocked by DNS" conclusion rests on a test that had the failover hatch open — and the four-month window is not our block either

Read 2026-10-22 16:00-16:15 UTC. Supersedes the conclusion in `L12196` ("host lists cannot block them") and the options ranking in `fc1950f6c`'s investigation doc. Cites `L12249`, `L12250`, `D9352`, and the device record for DRN 2007.

**What the device record shows (nanomdm `commands` + `command_results`, enrollment `00008030-000E38E9227B802E`).** Twelve installs of `com.abletel.waze.sirifilterdns`. Every one from 2026-10-13 19:38 to 2026-10-15 00:09 carried `AllowFailover=false` and was Acknowledged. On 2026-10-21 the profile was pushed three times: 00:44 Error, 00:46 Error, **00:47:49 Acknowledged with `AllowFailover=true`**. It has not been pushed since. The repo carried `false` at `2007f71a` (2026-09-10); the flip to `true` appears only in `95db6c7d` (2026-10-21 03:03). `grep -c failover` on the investigation doc and on session `718e2f8c`: 0. **So the one test that "ruled out DNS" — 42 names refusing every Apple name, reboot, tap — ran with the documented escape hatch open. A refusal cannot be measured while the device is permitted a second resolver.**

**What the allowlist snapshots show, read from the backups.** `.env.bak-20261021T004033Z` (content dated 2026-10-14 16:26, 61 names): `updates.cdn-apple.com`, `updates-http.cdn-apple.com` and `appldnld.apple.com` were **allowed**, `iphone-ld.apple.com` blocked+not-allowed. `.env.bak-20261021T020608Z` (78 names): same three still allowed, `iphone-ld` now on **both** lists. Yesterday's `.env`: the three payload hosts moved to blocked. So the payload host was open throughout the window the owner remembers as blocked, which means **the four-month window is not explained by our resolver refusing it** and must not be cited as evidence that we ever blocked a re-download. The record still contains no retest of a removed dictionary from that period.

**The honest position after all of it.** (1) Dictionary payload is `updates.cdn-apple.com` 255 / `appldnld.apple.com` 149 / `updates-http.cdn-apple.com` 119 out of 523 catalog assets, zero itunes or mzstatic — verified from Apple's own catalog. (2) Our resolver refuses all three today. (3) Our PAC still sends all three `DIRECT` at `DIRECT_DOMAINS` lines 2-4. (4) The one clean refusal test ran with `AllowFailover=true`. Therefore "DNS cannot block this" is unproven, and so is "it used to be blocked". What *is* established is that no test in the record isolated the resolver.

**The test that settles it.** `AllowFailover=false` on one device, the three payload hosts removed from the PAC's `DIRECT_DOMAINS`, reboot, tap one dictionary. Fails → the fix is the profile key plus the PAC correction, no entitlement, and `mesu.apple.com` can stay allowed because 255 of 523 payloads come from `updates.cdn-apple.com`. Succeeds → the flow is not DNS-dependent (local asset or a path outside our control) and only a packet-level view can name it: the existing IKEv2 tunnel with a LOG rule in front of `WAZE_VPN_EGRESS`'s REJECT (`L12249`).

**Do not** tell the owner the old behaviour was our block until that test passes. The memory is real; the mechanism behind it is not in the record.
