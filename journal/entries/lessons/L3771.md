**L3771 · In a mixed human/machine corpus, identify the actor from the session, not the event flag**

MEASURED, 2026-10-13, against the authority corpus (6,927 sessions / 2.03M events).

The obvious way to find "the owner's turns" in the DSH corpus is to filter
`dsh_session_events` on `type='user/message'` and `data.source.kind='user'`. It is wrong,
and wrong in a way that silently inflates every number downstream.

It returns 9,807 turns. The most repeated phrase among them is
`reply with exactly meshprobeok and nothing else` x1,009 -- the estate's hourly mesh health
probe -- and 46% of the turns exceed 2,000 characters, which is a dispatched task brief, not
a person typing. DSH starts a session three ways and `source.kind` cannot tell them apart:

 * a person, in the GUI -> metadata_json.header.agentPreset is set
 * a dispatch, whose BRIEF is the first
 user message and also the only one -> agentPreset set too, first turn >300 chars and
 opening `You are...` / `READ-ONLY recon...`
 * a health probe or cron child -> no agentPreset at all

FROZEN DEFINITION (finalize_corpus.py): owner_session := agentPreset present AND NOT
(turns<=3 AND len(first_turn)>300 AND role-brief regex). Result: 1,059 owner sessions,
4,372 owner turns -- 15.3% of the corpus.

TWO CONSEQUENCES WORTH MORE THAN THE NUMBER:
 1. Any measurement of this owner taken over raw "user" events is contaminated by ~5,900
 machine sessions. Predecessor audits on other corpora had the same trap available.
 2. 1,298 dispatches carry a HUMAN preset -- 98.69% of them `zabz`, the owner's own
 interactive profile. The dispatch path reuses the interactive profile, so machine work
 lands in his identity, in his archive, and in any per-preset cost or behaviour measure.

METHOD RULE: when a corpus mixes human and machine actors, identify the actor from the
SESSION, never from a per-event flag that both actors share. Verify by sampling the longest
and most-repeated items; a poisoned set is visible in its extremes before it is visible in
its counts.
