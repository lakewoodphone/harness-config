**L7423 · A hand-placed zone box is a plausibility generator: crop the region before publishing its number**

**What I tried:** quantify beard density per facial zone from 10 photos so the "cheeks are thinner than the chin" claim would rest on a number instead of my eye.

**Method:** pure Pillow, downscale 10x, fixed relative boxes per zone, skin luminance = the local 90th percentile of luminance, beard pixel = luminance below 62% of it.

**It produced numbers, and every one of them was an artefact.** The `cheek_left` box reported beard fraction **0.487** in IMG_1182. I cropped that exact box and looked at it: `jpg/../crops/IMG_1182-cheekleft.png` is **his hair and the glasses frame**, with no cheek in frame at all. Two independent defects, both silent:

1. **The zone boxes did not land on the zones.** A hand-drawn relative box on a face that moves between frames is not a measurement of the face, it is a measurement of wherever the frame happened to put that rectangle. Across the 10 frames `jaw_right` read 0.0, 0.115, 0.25, 0.269, 0.34, 0.378, 0.41, 0.737 and 0.865 — a range that cannot describe one jaw over one photo session.
2. **The skin reference was the wall.** "Local high percentile of luminance" picks the brightest thing in the box, and in a shot against a white wall with a window behind him, that is the background. A dark window edge or shadow then reads as beard.

**Worse, the per-zone spread contradicted what the photographs plainly show.** The claim I was trying to support was that the chin/moustache are denser than the cheeks. The aggregate said `cheek_right` 0.314 vs `chin` 0.318 — i.e. equal. That is the opposite of the visible reality in IMG_1187 and IMG_1188, and it is wrong because of defects 1 and 2, not because the eye was wrong.

**What replaced it:** no automated density number at all. The face read was delivered from direct visual inspection of all 10 frames, quoting which frame shows what, and the plan cites the frames rather than an invented index. The rejected numbers never reached the owner and are not on the page.

**Transferable — the general shape:** a measurement whose boxes are placed by hand on a moving subject is a *plausibility generator*, not a measurement. It returns a float regardless of whether it hit the target, and the float looks like evidence. Before publishing a per-region statistic from images, **crop and actually look at one region you measured** — that one look is what caught this. Cost of the check: one crop and one image read.

**And the rule it tests:** "print the raw value next to the predicate, so the number that decided the branch is visible" (L3796/L3798/L3801 lineage) is necessary but not sufficient. The nought-check here was not the number, it was **whether the number was about the thing it claimed** — 0.487 was printed next to `cheek_left` and was entirely about hair. Raw value next to the predicate, *and* a crop of the predicate proving it is the predicate.
