**L1934 · Compare the formula price to the machine's used value BEFORE the quote - on aging machines the formula can exceed the machine**

PRICING-RULE.md gives (part + shipping) x 1.4 + 65 and NOTHING in the shop tooling compares that result with what
the machine is worth. MEASURED 2026-09-17, the first MacBook display quote under the rule: a used genuine Grade A+
complete top lid for A2338 is USD 289.99 + 15.85 freight = 305.84 landed, so the formula gives 493 -> about USD 495
to the customer. A WORKING 13" 2020 M1 sells for about USD 400 (Swappa 2026-09-17: average sale USD 402 for
M1/256GB, USD 428 for M1/512GB, low USD 268, recent sales USD 279-515). So the repair costs MORE than the machine
on the genuine used part (495), on an aftermarket part (about 435) and on iFixit own part (about 760).
Margin is not the problem - gross is roughly USD 170-190 whichever part is chosen. The problem is that the customer
is being asked to spend USD 495 to own a USD 400 laptop, and nobody in the flow notices.
RULE: price the part, compute the formula number, then set it beside the machine CURRENT used value BEFORE the
quote goes out. Where the repair exceeds the value, say so plainly in the same breath as the number - in the case
file and to the customer - and let the customer choose, rather than letting them discover it afterwards.
WHERE TO GET THE ANCHOR, cheaply: Swappa price pages carry an "updated" date and recent individual sales, so they
give a dated used-value number in one read. Use one before any large repair quote on a consumer device.
NOT A ONE-OFF: the shop A1932 case (2026-09-07) already wrote "assess vs used-market value before committing to
expensive board work" - three weeks earlier - and it never became a rule, so it was rediscovered here from scratch.
