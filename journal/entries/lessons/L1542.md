**L1542 · An audit probe must be chosen so it cannot change state: a malformed body proves reachability, and when a probe does execute, re-read the state**

**Evidence, 2026-09-15 (PART-120).** Auditing the tRPC guard chain found 43 procedures across 8 routers declared with a bare `procedure` builder that applies no authentication. Two of those routers were live on the public internet. To prove it I had to *call* them — and that is the dangerous part of the method, because the natural probe for a finding is the very action the finding says is dangerous.

**The rule: for every probe, choose the form that cannot change state.**

| Surface | Probe chosen | Why it was safe |
|---|---|---|
| `terminal.getReaders`, `getLocations` | plain read | read-only |
| `terminal.createConnectionToken` (a mutation) | POST with `{}`, a **malformed** body | tRPC rejects the input before the handler runs, so the endpoint's *reachability* is proven and no token is minted |
| `terminal.createTransaction`, `processPayment` | **not called at all** | these create and process real payments; a read-only proof of the exposure was already sufficient |
| `print.submitJob`, `printReceipt` | **not called at all** | they drive a physical printer |
| `a2pAutoReply.updateConfig` | `{}` — **and this one executed** | see below |

**The one that got through, and what I did about it.** My first sweep sent `{}` to `updateConfig` as if it were a harmless shape probe, and it returned 200. That is a public write of the customer's SMS auto-reply config, so it may have written. I re-read the config immediately and compared it field by field, then read the service: it uses `prisma.a2PAutoReplyConfig.upsert` with `update: { enabled: data.enabled, ... }`, and **Prisma skips `undefined` fields** — so an empty object was a no-op and the live template was intact. The check was necessary and the luck is not the method.

**The general form, worth keeping:** before sending a request into a live system, ask *"if this works, what did I just do?"* For a mutation whose body I do not fully control the semantics of, the answer is "an unknown write", and the correct move is a malformed body (proves reachability, cannot execute) or no probe at all (when a read-only proof already exists). Corollary: when a probe *does* execute, re-read the affected state and compare it — do not assume the handler ignored it.

**Same family as L203 and L1539:** an action that appears to change nothing still has to be verified, because "it returned 200" and "it changed something" are different claims.
