**L5029 · Off vs restart decides the diagnosis: one question before any instrument**

**A PC that turns OFF mid-game and a PC that RESTARTS mid-game are different faults, and one
question separates them.**

Pantoja intake, 2026-10-15 (WO26101512749, customer reported "my PC completely turns off while
gaming", owner recollected "turns off and restarts"). Both sentences are now in the case file
because they are not the same fault:

- **Latched protection trip** (PSU OCP/OPP): the machine is dead until the mains/PSU switch is
 cycled. Corsair's own PSU engineer: the only way to reset a latched OCP shutdown is to flip the
 switch on the back of the PSU.
- **Rail droop below PWR_OK, or a bugcheck with auto-restart**: it comes back by itself. Intel's
 ATX 3.0 guide: PWR_OK is de-asserted when the 12 V / 5 V / 3.3 V rails fall below threshold, so
 the platform resets.

So the first bench question is *"does it come back on by itself, or must you switch it back on at
the power supply?"* — it is free, the customer can answer it, and it splits the differential before
any instrument is used.

Supporting structure from the research done for this case (sources in the bench plan):

- Seasonic's own knowledge base describes this symptom almost verbatim: the PC "may work perfectly
 fine for hours running light tasks, but shuts down minutes after launching a game… that's a sign
 that the PSU is hitting its limits or reacting to sudden power spikes", and GPU peak draw "can
 trigger the power supply's overload protection mechanism".
- ATX 3.0 permits excursions to 200 % of rated power for 100 µs, 180 % for 1 ms, 160 % for 10 ms —
 and greater excursions are permitted **only on 12VHPWR**, never on two legacy 8-pins. A PSU whose
 OPP trips at 115-135 % of rated therefore trips *inside* what the design guide allows.
- A display-driver timeout (TDR) does **not** power a machine off: a successful TDR logs "stopped
 responding and has successfully recovered" and the desktop survives; only a failed recovery
 bug-checks (0x116). "It's the GPU driver" is only sayable with a bugcheck in the log.
- Kernel-Power 41 proves an unclean stop and nothing else. Non-zero `BugcheckCode` proves a real
 Stop error; zero is compatible with a plain power cut. **41 is not a PSU diagnostic.**
- Thermal trips and PSU trips both tend to leave **no** Windows record, so throttle-then-trip versus
 instantaneous cut comes out of an HWiNFO log, not the event log.
- NZXT publishes the GPU re-seat procedure as a recognised failure mode, and a customer repaste is
 itself a reason to re-check the 8-pin CPU feed, the cooler mount and the fan headers.

The order that follows, and it is the order in
`lpt-hub/hardware-diagnostics/intake-pantoja-nzxt-gaming-pc-2026-10-15.md`:
one question → event log → read the actual parts → re-seat and inspect → GPU-only and CPU-only load
arms → EXPO off → thermal log → substitute a known-good PSU. Note also that a build sticker is not a
SKU: `D-MB3G-5GB16-L-1G-BLK` has zero public hits and must not be decoded into a configuration.
