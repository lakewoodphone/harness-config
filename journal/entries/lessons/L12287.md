**L12287 · The owner's Downloads folder is not the attachment store - search his own files before saying a document does not exist**

A document the owner says he gave you is far more likely to be in his own Downloads, Documents or
Pictures folder than in the DSH attachment store — the store holds only what was pasted into a session,
and a PDF that the state portal or a print-to-PDF produced lands in `C:\Users\<user>\Downloads` and
never touches the store at all.

Measured 2026-10-23, and it cost him a round of justified anger. Tasked to "pull up our application" for
his SNAP interview, I enumerated every image attachment on ZABZ-YOGA, ZABZ-TECH and secratary-cloud,
opened contact sheets of 94 candidates, and told him "there is no copy of your application in the
record". He answered: *"not true, i gave you teh screenshots and you better not have lost them."* He was
right. `submitted application.pdf` (375,631 bytes, 14 pages, mtime 10-09 02:29) was sitting in
`C:\Users\ezabz\Downloads` with three earlier drafts beside it — `review snap applcation.pdf`,
`second review.pdf`, `third revision.pdf` — from a 02:04→02:29 revision sequence on 10-09.

Two failures, both mine. The search was aimed at the wrong corpus and then reported as a fact about the
world rather than about the corpus: "there is no copy" when I had only checked one kind of place. Rule 1
exists for exactly this — a negative from a partial search is not a negative.

What to do instead, before saying a document does not exist:

1. `Get-ChildItem "$env:USERPROFILE\Downloads","$env:USERPROFILE\Documents","$env:USERPROFILE\Pictures\Screenshots" -Recurse -File` filtered by date and name — one call, seconds, and it is where a browser download, a portal print, and a Windows screenshot actually live.
2. Search by the *content* words, not just the filename: the Downloads listing surfaced the four SNAP PDFs because they were named in plain English.
3. Then the attachment store, then the session transcripts.
4. Say which corpora answered and which refused, and never let a partial search become "there is none".

The second lesson is about the tell: when the owner insists material exists and the search says it does
not, the search is wrong until proven otherwise. He does not remember what he pasted into which chat —
he remembers that he did the work. Look one layer down before contradicting him.

Related: the extraction that followed (`pypdf`, 14 pages, 31,979 characters) turned a 6-hour-old
"there is nothing" into the exact answers to every question the county will ask, which is what he needed
at 11pm before an 08:00 interview. The case file now lives at `~/code/home/benefits/2026-10-23-snap-interview/`
and this note is the reason its README has a section titled "Where the application was actually found —
read this before searching again".
