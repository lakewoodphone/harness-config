**L3415 · A how-to for the owner needs a source or an observation, not a plausible mental model**

**A how-to I hand the owner must carry a source or an observation — never a plausible mental model.**

2026-10-08. Asked how to remove a lift-and-turn tub stopper, I told him: turn the knob counter-clockwise several rotations, it rises as it turns and lifts free. He turned it about 30 times; it never rose and never loosened. He then had to come back and ask again.

The truth is the opposite of what I wrote. The stopper is held by a **slotted brass post in the centre of the strainer crossbar**, removed with a **flathead screwdriver**; the knob is either captive to the lid or retained by a set screw, so turning it transmits no torque and can never release anything. Two vendor guides say this explicitly, and they took one search to find.

**Cost:** one real physical attempt by the owner, and contamination of a document that is otherwise properly sourced — a reader cannot tell my invented step from the verified material around it.

**Rule.** In any procedure document I keep for him, each step is one of three things and must say which: **VERIFIED** (I observed it work), **SOURCED** (with the citation inline), or **UNVERIFIED** (say so in the sentence itself, not in a footnote). A step with no provenance is not allowed to read like a step with one. The failure was not that I guessed — it was that the guess was formatted identically to the knowledge.

**Applied fix:** `~/code/home/maintenance/2026-09-27-bathroom-drain-slowness.md` — section 11.1 now carries a SUPERSEDED banner, and section 12 carries the corrected two-part assembly (knob vs centre post), the full removal tree including the set-screw and channel-and-pin variants, a hard stop for a seized post, and the sources (plumbingsupply.com and thespruce.com, both read 2026-10-08).

**Related and worth not relearning:** the stopper usually does not need to come out at all — the overflow plate on the wall takes 2 screws and gives better access to the trap, and the Zip-It passes beside a raised stopper.
