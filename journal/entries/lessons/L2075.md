**L2075 · Restoring a guard rail is not undoing what it guarded, and an ungated fallback executor turns a safe refusal into the disaster it refused**

**L· 2026-09-19 · Restoring a guard rail is not the same as undoing the thing it guarded — and a fallback executor that is not gated turns a safe refusal into the disaster it refused.**

Two shapes, found in the same hour, both of the "looks correct, is not" kind.

**1. `hold_interior_door_open` leaves the relay RELEASED, and re-enabling the automation whose job is to re-lock it does NOT re-lock anything.** An HA automation fires on its *trigger*, not on being switched on. So a probe built from "disable the guard rail → release the door → prove it stays open → put the guard rail back" returns with the building open. The fix is a third step nobody wrote: re-lock explicitly, and report it. Any "disable X, do Y, re-enable X" sequence has this hole — re-enabling restores the *rule*, not the *state* that the rule maintains. Ask of every such sequence: what does X do continuously, and who does it once, now?

**2. The plug's on-device OFF schedule is UNGATED, and it was armed whenever a cut was merely permitted.** The whole point of the door gate is that the software refuses the cut when it cannot prove the door is held — but the *device* fires on its own clock regardless. So the design's safety property ("being locked out is worse than the automation not running") was defeated by its own fallback: if the door check failed, the software safely did nothing and the plug cut power anyway, with the door locked. That is the 2026-09-11 lockout, reproduced by construction rather than by accident.
The rule this yields: **a fallback executor must carry the same preconditions as the primary, or it is not a fallback — it is a bypass.** Ask of every redundant path: what does the primary check before it acts, and what checks that on this path? Where the answer is "nothing", either gate it or drop it. Here the right answer was asymmetric and worth copying: the OFF is now software-only (gated), the ON stays on the device (ungated), because *restoring* power cannot lock anyone out while *cutting* it can.

**3. A test that patches `time.sleep` makes a wall-clock deadline unbounded.** I first wrote the power-up wait as `while time.time() < deadline: ... time.sleep(10)`. The suite mocks `time.sleep`, so the loop spun for its full 600 seconds of real time, doing real HTTP. Bound retry loops by **attempt count** (with the sleep as the pacer) rather than by reading the clock, and they stay bounded under mock and paced in production.

**Evidence.** 2026-09-19/20 on secratary: the probe's own report showed `persistent: true` while `switch.smart_switch_l4` still read `off` until an explicit relock; `_program_coming_weekend` wrote `program_off_raw` under `if allow_power_off` only; the first power-up patch hung `pytest` at 120 s with `SystemExit` from the outer timeout, and after switching to an attempt bound the same four suites ran in 0.80 s. Journal H605, L2074, P334.
