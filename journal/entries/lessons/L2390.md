**L2390 · A buying recommendation needs a spec threshold, not a ranked list - or the owner decides in the aisle**

Measured 2026-09-22, the same day, on myself.

At 17:57Z I texted the owner a CVS thermometer recommendation: a ranked list (1 temporal artery, 2 Braun ear, 3 digital stick) plus a skip-list ('skip anything that says only infrared forehead, temple touch, or no-name'). He went to CVS and came back with a **Braun Sensian Contact 5, BFH175USV4** - a contact **infrared forehead** thermometer with a documented +/-0.4 F lab accuracy (Braun BFH175 owner's manual: 'displays a calculated oral equivalent estimate').

By the literal wording of my text, the 'skip infrared forehead' clause pointed away from what he bought. Three transferable failures, in order of how much they cost:

1. **I gave taste, not a threshold.** A ranked list of ideal devices has no decision rule inside it. What was missing was the one sentence that makes the choice checkable: 'any of these is acceptable if the box states an accuracy of about +/-0.5 F or better and it is a real brand.' With that, the aisle decision is mechanical and I would have been indifferent between his pick and mine. Without it, every unbranded-looking option is a coin flip for him and I find out afterwards.
2. **I did not ask what he was actually constrained by.** He was going to CVS. The useful question was 'what does this CVS stock and what is the worst acceptable one of those', not 'what is the best thermometer'. A recommendation that ignores the buyer's actual shelf is a research artifact.
3. **The right artifact for a physical purchase is a decision rule plus a fast eligibility check**, which also survives contact with a product I have never seen: 'model stated on box -> brand real? -> accuracy printed? -> acceptable', rather than a closed list of three model names that will be discontinued.

Operational form for next time (any retail recommendation, not just thermometers): give (a) the one measurable threshold that makes an option acceptable, (b) 2-3 concrete examples at that threshold, (c) what to do when nothing on the shelf meets it. Then any device he brings home can be judged by the threshold instead of against my list.

What did NOT go wrong, and is worth separating from the lesson: the device he bought is genuinely adequate for the purpose on file (trending a fever that had never been measured, docs/personal/health/recurrent-febrile-episodes.md). Recorded the acquisition, the specs, the correction of my own advice, and the limits of the device in that file as S17; commit 705e11de on secratary.
