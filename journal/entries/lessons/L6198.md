## A reading quoted from one store is not a fault -- 21% in the HEALTHY store retracted my "140 chats damaged"

Third correction of the evening on the Kramer case, and the cheapest of the three, because I finally ran the
control instead of only the measurement.

I had listed, in the case file and to the owner, **"newest message is not at the highest `ZSORT`: 140 chats"** as
a third footprint of the merge. Then I ran the identical reading against **her own healthy store**, the one taken
off the source phone before we touched anything:

 HER HEALTHY STORE : 125 of 598 chats (21%) duplicate-ZSORT chats: 0
 THE STORE ON HER PHONE : 135 of 603 chats (22%) duplicate-ZSORT chats: 17

**21% versus 22%.** The reading is a normal property of a WhatsApp store - `ZSORT` has gaps where messages were
deleted, so the newest message by date frequently does not hold the highest sort key. It is not a defect and the
claim is withdrawn. The same control is what makes the neighbouring line **solid**: duplicate `ZSORT` slots are
**0** in her healthy store against **17** on her phone, so that one is real.

Two smaller lessons inside this one:

- **The number moved between my own two scripts** - 140 in one, 135 in the other - because messages sharing a
 timestamp have no tiebreak unless the query specifies one. If a reading is not stable to a tiebreak, it is not
 a reading to quote.
- **A control is the cheapest instrument there is and the one I keep skipping.** It cost one query. Both of
 tonight's earlier wrong mechanisms (a corrupt reply pointer, a replayed peace sign) died to one query each too.
 The pattern is consistent: measure the thing, then measure it somewhere it should NOT appear.

**Also now measured, and it is what sizes the job:** 76% of her 19,167 recovered messages sit inside the 10
conversations that will misplace what she sends (`#Pepperspray` 10,195 and `Coventry women` 2,201 dominate),
while the six conversations the allocator has already damaged hold 107 messages - 0.6%. Loud fault, huge
footprint, no data loss; silent fault, small footprint so far, and it deletes. **17,654 ids stand between the
allocator and the end of her imported rows, and each one it hands out takes a message.**

Rule to keep: before quoting a footprint, measure the same reading in a population where the fault cannot exist.
