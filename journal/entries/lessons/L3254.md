**L3254 · In this house kashrus decides the dish before nutrition does, and in a meat dish the dairy hides in the serving suggestion**

Asked for a meal idea on 2026-10-04, twice, and both times the answer turned on kashrus rather than
cooking. The constraint was already in this repository - ~/code/home/trips/ checks hashgacha before
recommending a restaurant across eight files - and I did not consult it before proposing food either
time.

Rule for future food work: establish the kashrus posture BEFORE proposing a dish, then filter the
source material by it. The American recipe corpus is built on meat+dairy - sausage in a cheese dip,
bacon in a cream sauce, chicken in a cream sauce - so a recipe found by search is more likely than not
to be unusable as written, and it is usually unusable in the exact place it gets its protein.

The second-order effect is the part worth remembering: in a MILCHIG dish, stripping the meat strips
the protein, so a kosher version is a LOWER-protein dish than the original unless the dairy base is
chosen deliberately. In a FLEISHIG dish the mirror applies and the dairy is normally in the garnish or
the drink, not the recipe.

Corollary on fat: traditional meat recipes assume a butcher's fat ratio. The Gaziantep lahmacun
specification requires 20-24% fat and Janjigian requires at least 15%; kosher ground beef is routinely
leaner, which is the most common reason a fleishig dish comes out dry. Extra virgin olive oil fixes it
and is also the one oil that needs no hechsher, so both constraints point the same way.
