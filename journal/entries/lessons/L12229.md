**L12229 · Non-Samsung anti-reset gap measured: Settings reset identical across brands, Knox Guard is a toll gate, EFRP inert today, price is published**

## D3 answered with evidence — the non-Samsung gap is not what our docs say it is

Read-only brief from the mesh (ZABZ-TECH, 570 s): docs/design/ANTI-RESET-ARCHITECTURE.md in full, plus
public documentation. Four findings change the decision, and two of them say our own architecture doc is
wrong.

1. **At Settings > Erase all data, every brand is identical.** `DISALLOW_FACTORY_RESET` via device owner
 removes that route brand-independently — Google's own wording is "whether factory resetting from
 settings is disabled". Our doc has this at ANTI-RESET-ARCHITECTURE.md:194, so the "Samsung can, others
 cannot" framing is wrong for this route.
2. **The real gap is recovery/bootloader.** Generic Android: the recovery wipe succeeds, then only FRP
 stands, and FRP is removable on a PC — mtkclient erases MediaTek flash free in BROM mode without
 disassembly; shops sell FRP removal (Chimera). Samsung with Knox Guard: reset restricted while locked,
 and a wiped phone re-locks on boot. So the difference is skill and cost — a teenager with YouTube
 versus a bench with test points and a paid licence — not "impossible versus pointless".
3. **Knox Guard is a toll gate, not a wall.** Chimera documents KG removal via EDL/test points on Qualcomm
 and EUB on Exynos (2026), and CVE-2026-20978 bypassed KnoxGuardManager persistence. Our doc says it
 "stops it; nothing else does" (ARCH:94) and concedes bypass tools twelve lines later (:147). Price is
 published: 6.99 dollars per device for 3 years (CDW retail; Samsung 2020 MSRP 4.80). ARCH:131 says the
 price is not published — stale — and the repo contradicts itself at ARCH:131 (7 dollars) versus
 ANTI-RESET-DECISIONS.md:54 (5 dollars). Both wrong; use the retail figure and mark it as retail.
4. **EFRP is inert in the current build (ARCH:61-63, :72).** The account string is never proven and the
 arming is coded-but-inert, so TODAY the generic tier has no after-wipe protection at all. This is the
 most important fact: the weakness is not hypothetical, it is the shipped state.
5. **Brand is not the real lock for re-provisioning.** Android zero-touch re-provisions after a reset on
 any GMS Android 9+ device regardless of OEM; the barrier is that only authorised resellers can add
 devices. KME is the Samsung equivalent and needs a KDP reseller. No other OEM sells a reset-lock a shop
 can buy (Motorola, Xiaomi, TCL, OnePlus publish none). Google Device Lock Controller is gated to
 approved financing partners.

Unverified and bench-only before any promise: whether the FRP account string shape is accepted at all
(ARCH:43, :345 — wrong shape bricks the phone against everyone including us); whether Knox Guard "block
factory reset" stops a recovery wipe or only Settings plus re-lock; whether DISALLOW_SAFE_BOOT stops
hardware-key safe mode per model (:348); whether BROM is open on our MediaTek models; whether zero-touch
fires reliably on Android 15/16.

Consequence for D3: Samsung-only lets the counter sign "he cannot reset it" for 6.99 per device. Any brand
drops it to "if he resets it, the phone is dead unless he owns a PC" — and only once EFRP actually arms.
Cost of Samsung-first: IMEI-checking used units for inherited locks.

Docs to correct (mine, not his): ANTI-RESET-ARCHITECTURE.md:94 (nothing else stops it), :131 (price not
published), :147 and ANTI-RESET-DECISIONS.md:53-54 (5 dollars).
