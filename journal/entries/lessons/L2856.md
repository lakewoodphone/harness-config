**For this household, an event whose seating is not published is excluded — check seating before recommending any concert**

Measured 2026-09-28 while planning Chol Hamoed. The owner's standing description of his family: *"they are
pretty yeshivish so obviously no movie options."* The operative rule extends past films to **concerts and
shows whose seating arrangement is not published**.

Three chol hamoed events came back in the current Voice of Lakewood guide and the Jewish Link activity guide
with **no published seating information at all**:
- *A Dream Chol Hamoed* at DreamLive PAC, American Dream, East Rutherford — Mon 9/28 and Tue 9/29, 12:30 and
 4:30 PM (8th Day, Eli Marcus, Itzik Dadya, Moshe Tischler).
- *Miami Boys Choir* at American Dream — Wed 9/30, 1:30 and 5:30 PM.
- *Eitan Katz* at Kings Theatre, Brooklyn — Tue 9/29, 8 PM.

All three are Jewish events with frum promoters and frum artists, which is exactly what makes them easy to
recommend by mistake. **Not published is not the same as mixed — but it is unverified, and an unverifiable
seating arrangement is not something to send this household to.** They were recorded in the trip record under
"looked at and rejected" with the reason stated as *seating not published*, not as an assumption about the
event.

The trap in the other direction: the guide's own film listings include a **"MEN ONLY"** screening. Men-only
does not rescue a film — the household rule is about the medium, not the seating. Two independent tests, and a
pass on one is not a pass.

GENERALISE: when an event's fitness depends on an attribute the listing does not state — seating, kashrus, a
sukkah, a minyan — treat it as unverified and say so, rather than assuming the favourable case because the
event "looks frum". Every kashrus claim got the same treatment the same day (see the Red Moon correction in
H2849): the restaurant's own page is not evidence of hashgacha.
