**L3395 · Our measured token shape: 99.5% cache hits, 217k avg prompt - the 100k cliff, not the cache rate, is what makes DeepSeek cheaper**

Measured 2026-10-08 on ZABZ-YOGA from DSH session logs: the 40 most recently modified
session files, every usage record carrying cacheReadTokens (n = 8,192 calls). This is the
fleet's real token shape, and it decides every model-cost question we have.

 cache-read input 1,769,675,008 tokens
 cache-MISS input 8,404,696 tokens -> CACHE HIT RATE 99.5%
 cache-write 0 tokens (DeepSeek charges no write premium)
 output 7,552,552 tokens -> 0.42% of input volume
 average prompt 217,051 tokens/call; average output 922 tokens/call
 6,364 of 8,192 calls (77.7%) have a prompt over 100,000 tokens, and those calls carry
 94.0% of all prompt tokens.

Cost of those 8,192 calls, at published list rates:
 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash off-peak 11.10 USD (per call 0.00136)
 DeepSeek V4.1 Flash peak 22.20 USD (per call 0.00271)
 Claude Haiku 5.5 106.4 USD (per call 0.0130)
Cross-check against measured reality: spend-guard ~/.dsh/spend-guard/day.json said 93
requests for 0.156062 USD on 2026-10-08; 93 calls at our shape predict 0.126 USD. Within
24%, so the model is sound.

THE DRIVER IS NOT THE CACHE RATE. Counter-intuitively the 99.5% hit rate is what makes
BOTH models cheap, and it nearly erases the gap on short prompts: at 95% hits with a 50k
prompt and 2k output, DeepSeek off-peak is 0.0017175 and Haiku 5.5 is 0.001725 per call
-- a 0.4% difference, i.e. a dead heat, and at PEAK rates Haiku is then 2x cheaper.
The gap comes from Haiku's 100k prompt cliff (rates go x5 above 100k: 0.50 in, 2.50 out,
0.05 cache read). Hold every other variable and remove only the cliff -- pretend all our
calls were under 100k -- and Haiku drops from 106.4 to 22.36 USD, turning a 9.6x gap into
2.0x off-peak and a dead heat at peak (22.20 vs 22.36).

So the durable rule: on this estate, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is ~2x cheaper than Haiku 5.5 on
short-prompt work off-peak and a tie at peak, and ~5-10x cheaper on our actual long-context
agent loops. Any future "is model X cheaper" question must be answered with our measured
217k-token average prompt, not with headline per-token prices.

Caveats that must travel:
 - The comparison assumes EQUIVALENT caching. DeepSeek caches automatically and charges no
 write premium; Anthropic requires explicit cache_control breakpoints and charges writes
 at 0.125 (0.625 over 100k), with a 5-minute TTL. A naive switch to Haiku 5.5 with no
 cache-breakpoint work would bill our 1.78B prompt tokens as misses at 0.50/1M -- about
 889 USD for those 8,192 calls, roughly 80x the DeepSeek off-peak figure. Caching is not
 a detail of this comparison; it is the comparison.
 - Haiku 5.5's tokenizer yields ~30% more tokens for the same text, so our 217k DeepSeek
 prompt is ~280k in Haiku tokens. That widens the gap and pushes more calls over the cliff.
 - This supersedes the short-prompt reading in L3394 where Haiku looked cheaper per task.
 That was Artificial Analysis's sub-100k benchmark shape, not ours.
