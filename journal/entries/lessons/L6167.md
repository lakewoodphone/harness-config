**L6167 · A buying recommendation with no throughput requirement in it: the handoff-media directive priced a drive on price per unit, and it was my recommendation the owner acted on**

**RECORDED 2026-10-13.** `lpt-hub/directives/active/2026-04-23-cheap-reliable-customer-data-handoff-media.md` recommended the **PNY Turbo Attaché 3** as the shop's bulk customer-handoff media, and the owner bought exactly that line (three orders: 59.99 + 64.99 + 79.99). On 2026-10-13 he asked: *"i thought you recommended i buy them but they are starting to seem like very junky pieces."* He is right on both counts.

**Every number in that directive is a price per unit.** Its success criteria ask for cheaper strategies, live-verified prices and a repeatable policy. **Nothing in it asks for a write speed** - and this shop's work is writing 100-200 GB per handoff, so write speed is the only spec that matters. The research did what it was asked and the criteria were the defect.

**What the omission cost, in the shop's own numbers:** PNY publishes **no write speed in MB/s** for the Turbo Attaché 3 or the Attaché X (the spec table says *"Write Speed - Up to 10x faster than USB 2.0"*, and the footnote defines that as 10 x **3 MB/s** then disclaims it: *"Write speed may be lower"*). Independent tests measure **8.7-24.9 MB/s** sustained. The shop's own four recorded stick copies ran **8.99 / 9.35 / 10.74 / 12.3 MB/s**. And the one **1.53 TiB** delivery it has ever made went onto a portable SSD at **285.8 MB/s**. Against the shop's 16-job size mix that is 427 bench hours per 100 handoffs at 10 MB/s versus 12-17 hours at 253-370 MB/s.

**FIXED, in the places that actually get read** (each committed):
- the directive carries a dated addendum - lpt-hub `745a37d9b`;
- **`lpt-hub/docs/procurement/README.md`** is new and is the first *standing rule* in that directory, which previously held only dated case records - lpt-hub `e201971a3`;
- the `lpt-production-case-work` skill §8 now says to read it before recommending or ordering anything - harness-config `0dda04c8`;
- the whole research is preserved with sources at `docs/customer-operations/research/handoff-media-2026-10-13/` - lpt-hub `aa98c0d33`.

**THE GENERAL SHAPE, which is why this is a lesson and not an incident:** *a purchase research that does not name the spec the work consumes is a price list, not research.* Such a document will always recommend the cheapest thing that satisfies the criteria it wrote down - and when every criterion is price, the answer is always "buy the cheapest". The second half of the shape: when the recommendation is implemented by someone else, nobody re-checks the omitted variable, and the omission only surfaces **when the thing is used** - here, six hours into a 207 GiB copy.

The rule that now covers it, in `docs/procurement/README.md`: **buy on the spec the work consumes; if the vendor does not publish it, the product is not a candidate; demand it over the working size, never a burst; and price the whole cost, including the hours the purchase costs.**
