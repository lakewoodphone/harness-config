**L3831 · Side-by-side E4610 data: the customer's SIM sent 5/5 in a factory handset after 0 in the customer's - our filter exonerated on data**

**L · 2026-10-15 (evening) · ZABZ-TECH · Side-by-side E4610 measurement: the same SIM that delivered 0 pictures in the customer's handset delivered 5/5 in a factory handset — and our filter is exonerated**

Full data record: `lpt-flip-phone/docs/incidents/MMS_FIELD_DATA_2026-10-15.md`.

**Three E4610s measured today with a fully automated harness** (`_scratch/mms-ab/Invoke-MmsAutoTest.ps1`, no human
in the loop, `-SelfTest` green, 15 assertions):

| handset | filter | carrier / SIM | outbound | median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 615387849 (customer's) | filtered, 27 disabled | US Mobile `+19086927704`, US Mobile `+18483267693`, direct Verizon | **0 delivered** | — |
| 616450266 | filtered, 27 disabled | Verizon direct | **10/10** | 10.05 s |
| 616574160 | **UNFILTERED, 1 disabled, zero LPT apps** | **US Mobile `+18483267693`** | **5/5** | 7.90 s |

**THE finding.** `+18483267693` is the SIM issued to the customer's handset and the one that failed there all day.
The same SIM in a factory handset produced **5 of 5**. Same SIM, same number, same carrier, different device — the
outcome inverts, which holds the line constant and varies only the handset.

**Confound, not glossed:** the failing run was ~13:20 local and the passing one ~15:15, so a carrier change in that
window is not excluded. Against it: US Mobile's forum thread had the fix due **end of 14 October**, and the
customer's handset failed on **three different SIMs including direct Verizon**.

**Our filter is exonerated on data, not argument.** A factory handset with ONE disabled package and a filtered
handset with 27 both send reliably. Filter state does not separate working from failing.

**Two more theories killed with evidence from a *working* phone:** the working handsets also show
`userAgent= / uaProfUrl=` **empty** and `WAP PUSH: no receiver app found` on every MMS notification. A handset that
sends 10/10 displays both anomalies. Neither can be the cause.

**The one protocol difference that still separates them:** the failing handset accumulated **10 orphaned `VZWAPP`
MMS connections** and released its MMS network request in **~1.05 s** (once *before* the APN finished coming up);
the working handsets hold **1–2** and about 4.4 s on a completed send. That is the thread to pull if it recurs.

**Device behaviours now documented and reusable** (each cost real time today):
- **The send is `input keyevent 23`.** Message+'s `Abc | Send | Options` bar is a *label for the physical keys*;
 tapping it does nothing and uiautomator exposes no send node. Detect a send by watching
 `requestNetwork.*legacyType=2`.
- **A factory handset shows Message+'s Terms & Conditions gate** (`WelcomeActivity`) and never composes until
 Accept is pressed; its soft keys are real views (`com.vzw.basicphone.view.SoftkeyView`) and the right one accepts.
- **`am force-stop` before a SEND intent**, or Android raises the existing task and the message is silently saved
 as a **Draft** ("Activity not started, its current task has been brought to the front").
- **`settings put global stay_on_while_plugged_in 7`** — a sleeping display makes `screencap` return 1024 black
 bytes and makes taps miss.
- **24-hour ADB:** `enable-tech-window --duration-minutes 1440 --test-mode` (`--test-mode` is load-bearing).

**Transferable rules:**
- **Hold the line constant, vary the device.** The same SIM moving from a failing handset to a working one is worth
 more than any ten log readings, and it is the cheapest experiment available.
- **A control converts an anecdote into a measurement.** Three handsets, one harness, and now every future phone has
 a baseline to be compared against.
- **Count the protocol symptoms, not just the outcome.** The orphaned-connection count and the request hold time are
 the only signals that still separate a working handset from a failing one.
- **Automate the operator out of the loop before you rely on repetition.** Every bug found today was found by
 running it — none by reading it.
