**L4705 · CORRECTS L4641: the LAN has no IPv6 because no prefix was delegated, not because the second router refuses to route it - the WAN IPv6 is Up with an empty delegatedPrefix**

CORRECTS L4641, written a few hours earlier the same day, which said the LAN has no IPv6 because the second router kills it. That was the right observation and the wrong mechanism, and the router's own API settles it.

WHAT I SAID IN L4641: "IPv6 dies... The gateway reports hasIPv6 true; on the Verizon's LAN no interface has a global v6 address and there is no v6 default route at all... Every device behind the second router is IPv4-only."

WHAT THE ROUTER ACTUALLY REPORTS (CHR30A, /CRSP/getRouterDetails and getIpv6Configuration, 2026-10-15 14:37 UTC):

 broadbandIpv6Status: "Up"
 ipv6AddressWan: "2607:fb90:2e04:4d42::1" <- the router HAS a global IPv6 address
 ipv6AddressingType: "DHCPv6-PD"
 wanIpv6Protocol: 1, wanIpv6Enable: 1, ipv6Enable: true
 wanIpv6DefGateway: "fe80::fa3e:b0ff:fe40:32c0"
 BUT: delegatedPrefix: "" ipv6Prefix: "" lanPrefix: "" lanHostIpv6Address: ""
 WANIPv6AssignedPrefix: "" lanChildPrefix: "0:0:0:0::/64"

The WAN's IPv6 is up. What is missing is a DELEGATED PREFIX: T-Mobile's CGNAT hands an address to its own gateway and does not delegate a prefix to a router standing behind it, so the CHR30A has an address of its own and nothing to give the LAN. The LAN therefore advertises no prefix, no client gets a global address, and the host has no v6 default route - all observed, all downstream of the empty prefix, none of it the router "refusing" to route IPv6.

WHY THE DISTINCTION MATTERS, beyond tidiness: it changes the fix. "The Verizon breaks IPv6" points at a setting on the Verizon. "No prefix was delegated" points at the topology - it is a consequence of the CHR30A being a router behind the T-Mobile gateway, and it would also be the consequence for any other router placed there. Put the CHR30A back on the Fios ONT and Fios delegates a prefix normally. Nothing on the Verizon's IPv6 page needs touching, and turning its IPv6 off to "fix" the situation would be the wrong move.

METHOD NOTE, and the general rule: two symptoms looked identical from the client - "no IPv6 on the LAN" - and the client could not tell me whether the router had an address and no prefix, or no IPv6 at all. The router's own status endpoint distinguishes them in one call. When a symptom is about what an upstream device did or did not hand out, read the upstream device; the endpoint only knows it received nothing.
