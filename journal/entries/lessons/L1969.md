# `secretary-cf` (Cloudflare tunnel) is unreliable; use `secretary-ts` (Tailscale)

Measured 2026-09-18 during owner-queue triage from ZABZ-YOGA (home network).

## The rule

For SSH to the authority from any machine, prefer **`secretary-ts`**
(`secratary.tail93e6e6.ts.net`, Tailscale) over **`secretary-cf`**
(`secretary-ssh.abletelsolutions.com`, Cloudflare Tunnel).

## The measurement

- `secretary-cf`: **~1 call in 3 failed** with `Connection timed out during banner
 exchange` / `Connection to UNKNOWN port 65535 timed out`. It failed serialised calls as
 well as parallel ones, so it is not my connection concurrency. A background job over it
 succeeded while, seconds later, two serialised calls failed.
- `secretary-ts`: **zero failures** across every command in the session, including scp and
 a long multi-command shell script.
- The tell: `banner exchange` is pre-authentication, so the failure is the tunnel/transport,
 not the remote command, not the database, not busy_timeout.

## Why this matters beyond the retry

The failure mode is *misleading*. `[exit code: 1]` with a stderr timeout looks, in a
transcript, like the command failed — and the cheap wrong response is to conclude the
authority is down, or that the queue write failed, or to abandon a read-only verification and
report "could not verify". None of that is true. Three separate verifications in this session
(#90's repo visibility, #89's divergence counts, the resolution-text check) would have been
dropped as unverifiable if I had accepted the first timeout instead of switching host.

## Corollary: "could not verify" must be a transport conclusion, not a first-failure conclusion

Before recording a fact as unverifiable, try the other path (`secretary` / `secretary-lan` on
the office LAN, `secretary-ts` on Tailscale, `secretary-cf` over the tunnel). An unreachable
transport is not evidence about the thing being measured.
