**L1969 · Before commissioning research on how to do a thing, check what our own repos already document - and read the SOP rather than its filename**

2026-09-17, the Bachrach Family Link case. The owner said "I think we already have documentation
and information on how to do this." The honest answer turned out to be NO, and it took reading the
actual files to be able to say so precisely instead of vaguely.

What I found by reading, not by guessing from filenames:
- TECHLOQ-FILTER-REFERENCE.md is 707 lines and is the shop's MAIN filter SOP - and it is
 WINDOWS-ONLY (kernel driver tpd.sys, WindowsFilterService, WFP callout filters, ~118
 dollars/device/year). Nothing in it applies to Android. Anyone who reasoned from the filename
 ("we have a filter reference") would have been confidently wrong.
- Livigent IS a real kosher Android filter and the shop has a directive about it FAILING
 (LG Exalt VN220 boot loop, 2026-01-15) - it blocks recovery-menu functions and survives into
 Safe Mode. So the tamper-resistant option exists in our records, but there is no install
 procedure.
- The AI-kosher-filter directive is scoped to a Pixel 2 and never shipped; line 124 lists Family
 Link / Knox / NetFree / Gentech as "prior art to survey" and nobody surveyed them.
- Conclusion: the flip fleet runs Livigent, the Windows fleet runs Techloq, and a Samsung Galaxy
 under Family Link is an UNOWNED GAP.

The rule: "do we already have documentation" is answered by reading the candidate documents, at
least their declaring sections, and naming what each one actually covers. A filename match is a
hypothesis about coverage, not evidence of it. What made this answer useful to the owner was not
"no" but "no, and here is exactly what we DO have and why none of it applies."

Cheap method that worked: grep the SOP for its own headings (^#{1,3} ) and read the first ~40
lines - enough to establish what the document is about, without reading 707 lines.
