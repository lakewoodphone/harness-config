**L3662 · The remaining 24 ambiguous orders should stay ambiguous, and here is the measurement that says so**

2026-10-13. Closed the marketplace-matching thread with a measured reason rather than with a fix, and
the reason is that the remaining cases are genuinely ambiguous.

WHERE CLAUSE 2 ENDS UP. 585 purchases with a total, 484 matched to a transaction, 490 itemised. Of the
96-109 that carry no evidence, the matcher's own verdicts are:
 no_candidate 44 amount_only 28 ambiguous 24-27
 by marketplace: amazon 52, ebay 44
and **zero are scored `matched` without an evidence row**, so there is no write gap between the decision
and the record. That check matters more than the counts: it is the difference between "the matcher
declined" and "the matcher decided and the write was lost".

WHY THE REMAINING `ambiguous` SHOULD STAY AMBIGUOUS. Order 111-4275432-3393006, 16.97 on 2026-09-10,
has exactly two candidates scoring identically:
 personal-amex-94045 16.97 'Amazon' <- the card charge
 personal-amazon-business 16.97 'Amazon Order - TOTAL' <- the Amazon history row
Both are real, both are about this purchase, and neither names the order number, so the tie-break I
added last round correctly does not fire. I went looking for the estate's own convention to break the
tie with, and measured it:
 lpt-chase-3929 (LPT Chase Card *3929) 147 links
 personal-amazon-business-transactions 130 links
 lpt-chase-7542 69 | personal-paypal 39 | escape101-amex 33 | personal-amex-94045 28 ...
 the mirror account holds 246 rows and 143 of them are linked
There is NO single convention: the matcher links to the card when it finds the card and to the mirror
when it finds the mirror, and both are legitimate representations of the same purchase. So a tie-break
here would not be applying a rule, it would be INVENTING one, and whichever way it went it would put a
claim in `transaction_evidence` that the evidence does not support. The matcher's own comment already
says why that is the wrong trade: "Writing that into transaction_evidence would put a claim in the books
that the ledger cannot support." Leaving 24 orders honestly unlinked is worth more than 24 links of
which half are guesses, because a wrong link drives CATEGORISATION, and a wrong category moves money.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY RESOLVE THEM, for whoever wants them: the Amazon history rows are a per-order
mirror, so the order number IS recoverable for them from Amazon's own order export rather than from the
bank description. That is a data-acquisition task, not a matcher change.

AND THE REST, honestly. `amount_only` 28 agree on amount and date and nothing else - a merchant string
that names neither the marketplace nor a card - and are refused by design. `no_candidate` 44 have no
debit within tolerance at all: 18 are ebay, where a PayPal settlement rather than a card charge is the
EXPECTED state and not a defect, and the amazon ones include charges that have not posted yet (3 are
from the last three days). None of these is evidence of missing money, and I am not going to describe
them as such without the ledger side examined one by one.
