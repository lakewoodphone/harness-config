**L3716 · A sync record kept but out of force must not donate an operational claim - lptcase ignored supersededBy and linkWarning**

MEASURED 2026-10-12, lpt-hub. `tools/case-index/lptcase.py` read `orderNumber`/`orderId`/`workOrderState` from EVERY sync record linked to a case, and harvested every `WO\d{8,}` from the case prose. Two documented ways of KEEPING a record out of force were invisible to it: root `supersededBy` (an older mirror of the same case) and `references.productionOrder.linkWarning` (a record that says in its own words "THE LINK IS THE DEFECT, NOT THE RECORD").

Consequence, seen in `lptcase show`: `wo WO2604210007 order_id 1393` on the Greenfeld Surface case and `wo WO2606090003 order_id 1663` on the Yanofsky MacBook Air case - in both, the WRONG order, reported as the live case's own work order. The Yamofsky false link also blocked the push tool permanently: `lpt-sync-to-production.py` SKIPped the case with "record already references live production order ... creating another order for this case is the duplicate defect", so the right order could never be created.

FIXED in lptcase.py (commit 018aaf0d9, pushed): operational CLAIMS come only from a record in force (`claim_bearing = [r for r in linked if not superseded_by and not link_warning]`), and an order that any linked record flags as a known-wrong link is removed from the case's work orders AND order_id whichever surface it was read from. That second half matters: a correction written into the case PROSE necessarily names the wrong order, so suppressing only the sync record re-injects the number from the body. Names, phones, device, pricing and nextAction may still be read from any mirror - a mirror's context is not a claim about production. `lptcase check` unchanged: 4 classes / 222 / 99 / 21.

RULE: when a record is kept but out of force, every reader must know it. An annotation that no reader understands is indistinguishable from a claim.
