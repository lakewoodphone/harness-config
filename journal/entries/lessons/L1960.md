**L1960 · A talking video beats a photo set: 90 seconds of the owner narrating answered what 83 frames and 15 agents could not - and it hands you his vocabulary**

**What happened (2026-09-17).** The owner shot 38 stills and a 90-second video of the same structure on the same afternoon. The stills went to seven vision agents; the video's audio was transcribed with per-segment timestamps and its frames to six more. **The 90 seconds of him talking outperformed everything else combined.**

What only the narration supplied:
- **The vendor identity of the enclosure** that four separate analysts had logged as "cannot tell whether this is the tarp or a rigid panel system". He said the sukkah was "already up"; crops then read the label `SUKKAH OUTLET` / `(973) 778-5624`. Two routes to one answer, and his was instant.
- **The state of the steel cable** - "we started doing the steel cable however we didn't do it very successful". Without that sentence the frames show stranded wire rope on a post and an analyst can only report hardware; with it, the missing turnbuckle/thimble/clip is a *finding* rather than an absence.
- **The meaning of the joint hardware** - "the metal frame is held on both ends by those types of brackets" told me what the U-bolt and saddle-plate clamp *is for*, which no single frame could.
- **The relationship between the two structures** - the metal frame above the sukkah, "wooden stuff that the bamboo goes on", which reconciled "overhead lumber" versus "overhead pipe" into two different layers instead of a contradiction.
- **His own vocabulary**, which is the index to the whole corpus: he calls the rails "poles", the sleeve "the UHMW with the thing around it", the clamps "adapters and screws". Every retrieval failure in this project's history has been a vocabulary failure - the archaeology found that `leading pole`, `webbing`, `rubber washer`, `cam buckle` and `grommet punch` all return zero hits *because he calls those things "the pole", "the pocket" and "straps"*.

**The rule.** When a human can narrate, ask for a talking video, not more photographs. A frame answers "what does it look like"; narration answers "what is it, what is it for, and what is unfinished" - and it hands you the vocabulary to search your own corpus with. Ten agents reading 83 frames took longer and answered less than 90 seconds of his own explanation. Time the transcript, quote it verbatim, and treat it as primary evidence alongside the images rather than as commentary on them.

**Corollary for cost.** Whisper transcription of 90 seconds cost one API call. The equivalent information from vision passes cost 15 agent runs. Reach for the audio first.
