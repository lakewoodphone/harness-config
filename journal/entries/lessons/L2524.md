## A render that succeeds is not a render that is right — and a door sign's dates belong to the phone system

Measured 2026-09-24 on ZABZ-TECH, making the Sukkos closure door sign.

**The render lie.** The first version of the sign set the headline at `font-size: 25vw`. The HTML
was valid, Chrome's `--print-to-pdf` exited 0 and wrote a 76 KB PDF, and the screenshot wrote a
PNG. All three artefacts existed and every command reported success — and the PNG showed the final
"D" of **CLOSED** sliced off by the right border. A headless render has no failure mode for
"the text did not fit": overflow is a perfectly well-formed page. The only check is to **open the
image and look at it**, and then to check the PDF's `/MediaBox` and `/Count` afterwards (612x792,
1 page, here) because print media can differ from screen media.

Rule: after generating anything a human will look at — a sign, a PDF, a chart, a screenshot
comparison — read the rendered pixels back before claiming it is done. "It generated" is a claim
about the process, not about the output.

**The convention.** `lpt-hub/docs/operations/store-signage/` holds door signs as an HTML **source**
plus a generated **PDF** (the thing you print), with a README carrying the exact regeneration
command, the PDF's sha256, and the print instruction (100% / "Actual size", not "Fit to page").
All type is sized in `vw` so one file fills US Letter and scales to 11x17. Black on white only, so
any printer works and no colour cartridge is spent on a temporary sign. `lpt-hub` commits PDFs
(26 of them) and no PNGs, so the PDF is committed and the preview PNG is not.

**Why the sign is not allowed to own its dates.** The sign says closed Sep 25 - Oct 4 2026,
reopening Mon Oct 5 — 14-23 Tishrei 5787. Those dates come from the generated Dialpad closure
calendar (D2582). A sign is a *copy* of that fact in a medium no query can reach, which is exactly
the trap in L1675 (the hours that lived only inside an uploaded audio file). So the README names
`scripts/dialpad-holidays.py verify` as the check that the phone system and the door agree, and
reusing the sign in a later year means re-running that, not editing the HTML by eye.

**Two omissions that are decisions, not oversights**, recorded so nobody "improves" them in:
no wording about the 24-hour hallway drop-off (this closure is ten days; inviting unattended
drop-off on a door sign would strand devices for a week and a half, and whether that stays open
over Sukkos is the owner's call), and no custom-hours block. The sign says one thing: closed, and
when we reopen.
