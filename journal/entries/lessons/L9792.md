## Correction to L9791 — the DeepSeek number, read from the bank instead of the card (2026-10-19)

**L9791 said** that `model_usage`'s $237.94/week overstates DeepSeek spend by **2.9×**, and that the
true rate was **≈ $355/month**, because `app/pricing.py` prices `deepseek-v4-flash` at the legacy
(0.44, 1.32) card rather than the current (0.15, 0.60) one.

**That conclusion was wrong and it is corrected here, not rewritten there.** I stopped at the price
list and never opened the bank. `accounting.db` → `transactions`, merchant *Hangzhou DeepSeek
Artificial Intelligence Co., Ltd.*, holds every auto-recharge debit:

```
last 7 days 2 debits $159.00
last 31 days 10 debits $636.00
since 2026-08-18 (first) 18 debits $892.52
individual: $53 (10-15), $106 (10-13), $79.50 (10-11), $53 (10-08), $79.50 (10-06),
 $53 (10-05), then $53 weekly back through September
```

So the money is **$636 in 31 days ≈ $615–640/month**, and the three numbers are:

| source | per week | vs the money |
|---|---|---|
| **the bank** (31-day debits) | **$143.6** | — |
| `model_usage` at the legacy card (0.44/1.32) | $237.94 | **1.66× too high** |
| the same tokens at the current card (0.15/0.60) | $81.76 | **1.76× too low** |

**The real finding is bigger than the mispricing.** The recorded tokens do not reconcile with the money
at *either* card — correct the price and the ledger under-states the bill by nearly half; leave the
price and it over-states by two thirds. That means **something is spending DeepSeek money without
landing in `model_usage`**, and the table is not a budget instrument until that is found. The implied
blended input rate (~$0.27/M) sits between the off-peak (0.15) and peak (0.30) cards, which is what a
mixed-time workload looks like — a hypothesis, not a measurement.

**The lesson, corrected and sharpened:** when a cost figure is going to drive a decision, reconcile it
against the money that actually left the account *before* publishing it. I had the bank one query away
and used the price list instead — the same mistake as trusting a list price over an invoice, which is
the other half of L9791 and the part that was right. Two internal numbers that agree with each other
are not evidence; they are evidence when they agree with the bank.

**Applies to:** any spend figure taken from `model_usage`, from a cost column, or from a rate card.
`docs/cloud-audit/2026-10-19-cloud-and-storage-audit.md` §4 carries the corrected arithmetic, and
`H9579` should be read with this entry beside it.
