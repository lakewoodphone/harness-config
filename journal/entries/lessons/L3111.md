**L3111 · Twilio DateSent is date-granular: a coarse remote filter feeds 100%-false alarms into our own timestamps**

**L — Twilio's `DateSent` filter is DATE-granular and its date-only upper bound means 00:00 of that day, so a ">= yesterday & <= today" query returns yesterday and NOTHING from today. Compare every returned message against its own timestamp instead.**

WHAT HAPPENED (measured 2026-09-30, secratary)

- The `family-chat-watchdog` cron (every 30 min, read-only instrument that answers "does our record of the family chat still match what the carrier sent?") wrote, at 05:30:02 UTC:
 `window 6h: carrier 179, ours 11, missing 179` -> `PROBLEM: 179 of 179 messages the carrier DELIVERED in the last 6h are absent from our database`.
 A 100 % miss rate is the signature of a broken comparison, not of a broken hub, and it was recorded as such in H2723 ("the watchdog alarm compares a carrier DATE to our TIMESTAMP and so reports delivered messages as missing").
- Verified false: the ten rows the log named as `missing` are all present in our own table with `status=delivered` (e.g. id 125833-125844, 2026-09-29 03:42:18-03:42:57).
- Cause: `_twilio_window()` built `?DateSent>={since:%Y-%m-%d}&DateSent<={until:%Y-%m-%d}`. For a run at 2026-09-30 05:30 with `--hours 6` that is `DateSent>=2026-09-29&DateSent<=2026-09-30`, which the carrier resolves to 2026-09-29 00:00 → 2026-09-30 00:00: a whole day of traffic, compared against six hours of our own rows. None of the 179 SIDs could match a 6-hour window, so every one was reported lost.
- Same cause, opposite symptom, same day: at 14:59 UTC the identical code returned `carrier 0` for a window that contained the 13:33 relay (11 rows in our table) - the coarse bound also made the alarm blind to the current day.
- Fix, deployed to secratary (`scripts/family_chat_watchdog.py`, backup `.py.bak`): ISO-8601 bounds in the request (`DateSent>=2026-09-29T23:30:00Z&DateSent<=2026-09-30T15:00:00Z`) **and** a local membership test on the carrier's own `date_sent` (`_within()`), so the answer never depends on the request filter's granularity.
- Proof after the fix, same instrument: `6h carrier 11, ours 11, missing 0`; `24h carrier 34, ours 34, missing 0`; `168h carrier 1190, ours 1191, missing 0`.

RULE

When an instrument compares a remote API's *filtered* list against our own timestamps, re-check every returned item against its own timestamp locally, and ask what the request filter's granularity does to the comparison. A filter coarser than the comparison it feeds generates 100 %-wrong alarms - and an alarm that is always wrong is worse than no alarm, because it is the thing that trains a real alarm to be ignored.
