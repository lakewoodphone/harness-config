**L3708 · Two ways a stock write goes to the wrong row: a substring selector, and a row name the export truncates with an ellipsis**

Two traps, both hit on 2026-10-12 while turning owner answers into stock rows. Each one silently wrote something wrong.

**1. Never choose the row a write lands on by a substring of its name.** Taking the QIN keypad phone out of stock, I matched rows on `"qin"` - fine - and then, for the Switch Lite donor, on `"switch lite"`. That second pattern also matched `Nintendo Switch / Switch OLED / Switch Lite Joy-Con controllers joystick`, a row I had *verified against its own bag label an hour earlier*, and zeroed it. Nothing in the output of the write said so; only reading the live stock back showed a correct row emptied. The read-back is what caught it, which is the only reason it is a minute of work instead of a wrong inventory.

Rule: match on the **id**, or on a string that can only describe one row. When a substring is unavoidable, print every row the pattern matched *before* writing and check the count is what you expect. This is the same failure shape as `git add -A` (P13) and `pkill -f` on a broad pattern: a fuzzy selector plus a destructive verb.

**2. `_name_for()` prefers `display_name`, and `display_name` is truncated with an ellipsis - so a hand-added row can never be matched by name.** Adding the broken Sony ZV-1F and the 13 mini by hand, I aligned the catalog record's `device_compatibility` to the row name, expecting `lptparts.py export` to see it as already on record. It did not: the export names rows from `display_name` (`Apple iPhone 13 mini - FULL PHONE for parts (damaged screen,…`, ellipsis included, 61 characters), which can never equal a hand-written name. The export added a second row for the same physical phone - one row per unit in the shop's stock list, doubled. It is zeroed now, and the zeroed row's name is the exact truncated string, so a re-export matches it and cannot grow a third.

Consequences worth carrying: (a) hand-added rows are invisible to the export's dedup unless their **part number** matches, so a hand-added row should always carry one; (b) the export's names are ugly and lossy for a human - the ellipsis drops a word out of the middle - which is a report problem, not just cosmetics, because it is what a person types into a search box.
