**L3423b · Owner attention was a fragmented pile of jobs with no single writer; it now has one**

WRITTEN 2026-10-13 on secratary, at the end of the session the owner opened with
"what needs my attention, especially all my emails".

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY WRONG, measured, not argued:

1. **Nothing had ever read his mail end to end.** `email_catalog` (the app's own sync) held 658
 rows with a ~100-character Gmail snippet and an `internal_ts` whose newest row was
 2026-10-11 19:07 while Gmail held mail to 2026-10-13 00:42. 121 rows were flagged
 `needs_owner=1` and **not one of them had been put in front of him**. A snippet cannot
 decide whether a message needs a person.
2. **Three separate subsystems each kept their own claim on his time** - the email triage, the
 `gv_daily_digest` work order, and the waiting-comms sweep - and none of them knew about the
 others. His own words on 2026-10-10: "This does not look like a unified brain. It looks
 scattered and split up."
3. **The owner decision queue had 156 live rows and he was seeing the same one three times.**
 Four separate rows asked about the prepaid AI balance. The queue is the right store; it just
 had no digest in front of it.

WHAT NOW EXISTS. `scripts/owner_attention.py` in `personal-secretary-mvp` is the single writer.
It gathers from five sources (owner-queue, the full-body email sweep, the LPT books
obligations, the waiting-comms index, and the other digests' own memory rows), dedupes,
sections by *what kind of decision it is*, and caps each section so a 156-row queue cannot
crowd out every deadline. It writes a numbered artefact to
`personal-secretary-mvp/data/owner-attention/latest.txt`, mirrors it into
`memories(category='owner_attention')` so the next session reads it instead of re-deriving it,
and can send it to the owner with `send` once a destination is named.

Scheduled as `lpt-owner-attention.timer` (00:10/06:10/12:10/18:10 UTC), fed twice daily by
`lpt-email-sweep.timer` writing to `/home/zabz/accounting-data/email-sweep/`.

TWO MEASURED TRAPS in building it, both worth carrying forward:
- `comms-search.py` in harness-config takes `--json` BEFORE the subcommand
 (`--json waiting`), not after. `waiting --json` exits 2 with a usage error, and the caller
 that swallows that into "no results" reports an empty world instead of a broken call.
- The books `obligations` table's date column is `due_on`, not `due_date` or `due`. A source
 adapter that guesses the column name returns "no obligations" - a silent zero that looks
 like health.
