**L3233 · ZABZ-YOGA's two USB-C ports are separate USB4 systems - and the Windows device tree lies about dark monitors**

## What is true

ZABZ-YOGA is a Lenovo Yoga 9 2-in-1 14IMH9 (type 83AC), BIOS NNCN37WW (2026-04-30). It has
**two Intel USB4/Thunderbolt 4 host routers**, one PCI function each:

| Port | Intel device | PCI fn | Host-router instance |
|---|---|---|---|
| left #1 (the dock works here) | 7EC3 | 0&6B | USB4\ROOT_DEVICE_ROUTER&VID_8086&PID_7EC3\4&1f2c478c&0&0 |
| left #2 (failed 2026-10-02) | 7EC2 | 0&6A | USB4\ROOT_DEVICE_ROUTER&VID_8086&PID_7EC2\4&1bae3532&0&0 |

The ports look identical and are **not** the same system: one can fail while the other is perfect,
so any report must name the port. Dock in question: Dell Thunderbolt 4 Dock (USB4 router
VID_8087&PID_0B26).

## The trap that would have made me wrong

With the dock on 7EC3 and all three Dell monitors dark, the readings said:

- Win32_VideoController: one adapter, mode **2880x1800** (the internal panel).
- Screen.AllScreens: **1** screen, 1440x900.
- WmiMonitorBasicDisplayParams: 3 instances Active=True, **two of them Dell and not lit**.
- Get-PnpDevice -Class Monitor: both Dell monitors **OK**.

Only one reading described reality: Get-PnpDevice's non-OK list, holding
`Unknown USB Device (Port Reset Failed)` USB\VID_0000&PID_0001\5&2F4067DA&0&4 (CM_PROB_PHANTOM, 45).

**Rule: presence in the device tree is never evidence a display is lit. Only absence is.** A
stale `DISPLAY\...` entry and a code-45 phantom of the failed hub are the honest trace. Device
tree and monitor-class status come from registry last-known state; they survive disconnection.

## Two-command diagnosis

```powershell
Get-PnpDevice | Where-Object { $_.FriendlyName -match 'USB4|Thunderbolt' } |
 Select-Object Status,FriendlyName,InstanceId
Get-CimInstance Win32_VideoController |
 Select-Object Name,CurrentHorizontalResolution,CurrentVerticalResolution
```

Internal-panel native mode + AllScreens == 1 means the dock's DP tunnels did not come up, whatever
the monitor entries claim.

## The positive signal to look for

`Microsoft-Windows-USB-USB4DeviceRouter-EventLogs` id **1**: "A monitor attached to your hub or
dock was successfully enumerated by the USB4 Connection Manager." 12 of them in the last 7 days.
That event, not the monitor list, is the confirmation after a replug.

## Not proven

Whether port 7EC2 is dead or merely stuck in a bad state. Only a full power drain (shutdown, hold
power 15-30 s, boot) distinguishes them, and the owner has not taken the machine down. Do not
claim either.
