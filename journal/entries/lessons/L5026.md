READ IT WITH THE CONFOUND STATED, because the top of the table is partly definitional. Closing the
task is often how a session RECORDS that it finished, so for update_task/close_task the arrow may run
from completion to the tool rather than the reverse, and neither is a safe trigger for a guard.

The informative half is the bottom: a session that reaches for repo_*, github_* or qb_* completes 12
to 50 per cent of the time, and github_read_file completes 2 of 16.

THE LEVER THIS POINTS AT IS THE OPPOSITE OF A GUARD: make the read-only tools sessions die on fail
FAST and CLEARLY when they cannot succeed. The absent-repo case was fixed exactly that way on
2026-10-15 (_NON_FATAL_CB_FAILURE_PATTERNS, /opt/company commit d43a75a37): a repo that is not on the
host is now non-fatal, so it is neither retried into a deadlock nor counted toward the action's
circuit breaker. Extend the same treatment to github_read_file (12.5%), qb_status (35.3%) and
qb_snapshot (31.2%) -- one clear "this cannot succeed here" answer, and no retry, instead of a session
that spends its whole step budget discovering it repeatedly.
