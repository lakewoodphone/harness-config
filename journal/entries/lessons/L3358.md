**L3358 · A permission is not a capability: 'total' was in the write whitelist all along, and 46 of 48 work orders could not be charged because no command wrote it**

**Recorded 2026-10-08 (ZABZ-YOGA), from pricing one work order during the LPT board walk.**

**The fact.** `app/services/website_db.py` has always carried `total` in its writable set:

```python
ORDER_WRITABLE_FIELDS = {
 "status", "deviceInfo", "needsAttention", "description", "paymentStatus",
 "pickup_notified_at",
 "total", # quoted repair total (numeric column exists in prod)
 "referredBy", "title",
}
```

`lpt-write` — the only sanctioned production writer for the shop's work orders — exposed exactly two
commands: `note add` and `status set`. So the field was permitted by the rule and **unreachable by any
tool**. Measured consequence: 46 of the 48 open LPT work orders carry no price, `lpt-ops check N9`
reported at 2026-10-07 14:38Z that *"46 of 48 open work orders cannot be charged or released"*, and
the owner's instruction for the session — *"knocking them out or updating them"* — had its second verb
impossible for 46 of 48 rows. The symptom read as a data problem ("the counter can't charge, the orders
have no totals") when it was a missing command.

**Fixed** in `lpt-ops` `bede1b0`: `lpt-write total set --order K --amount N [--reason S] [--apply]` —
dry run by default, written through `website_db.update_order` so there is still one writer, one line in
the hash-chained ledger, and a read-back through the shop's own reader.

**The transferable rule: a permission is not a capability.** When a system reports "X cannot do Y",
establish whether the constraint is the *rule* or the *tool* before accepting the limitation or working
around it. Here the whitelist said yes, the CLI said nothing, and the gap survived long enough to become
a standing fact in a handoff (H3308, H3310: "46 of 48 cannot be charged") that later sessions then
treated as a property of the data. **The tell is a whitelist, allow-list or permission set that names a
field nothing ever writes.** Grep for the fields a permission set grants and check each one has a caller.
Two commands were built and the third was assumed; the assumed one was the one the shop needed.

**Two deliberate strictnesses in the amount parser, both from this shop's own history:** a blank is
refused and so is more precision than a cent. The blank matters because a stripped amount has already
gone out to a customer in this business — `send-lpt-sms.py` carries a validator for exactly that reason,
where PowerShell ate a `$155` and sent an empty figure. And a **negative** amount is refused rather than
written, because `total` holds what the customer owes and this toolchain has no refund path: refusing is
the honest answer where guessing would be a second wrong number.
