## CORRECTION to P2856 — there is no ~15-minute CI wall; `postgres-runtime-tests` intermittently hangs

`P2856` says jobs crossing about 15 minutes are cancelled by some undeclared limit. **That is wrong, and
the evidence that kills it arrived on the very next run:**

 postgres-runtime-tests cancelled 0 steps 21:15:32 -> 21:30:35 (15m03s)
 server-tests success 14 steps 21:15:54 -> 21:31:05 (15m11s)

`server-tests` ran **longer than the cancelled job and passed**. A wall at fifteen minutes cannot
explain that, and I should have looked for this before writing the pain entry - I had already seen
`server-tests` pass once at 15m28s and explained it away as an outlier instead of letting it falsify
the theory.

**What the evidence actually supports.** `postgres-runtime-tests` normally takes about a minute. On
this run it had succeeded in 1m02s and 1m13s earlier the same evening, and it has also come back
cancelled at 15m0x more than once. A job whose duration is either ~1 minute or ~15 minutes is not
hitting a limit; it is **hanging, and being cancelled after a fixed wait**. It is also the only job of
the three that uses a **service container** (`Initialize containers` is one of its steps) - a Postgres
service it has to wait for. The thing to investigate is that wait, not a platform quota.

**What is NOT established, and must not be asserted next time:** why it hangs. Not checked: the
service-container health wait, the port binding, the pull of the Postgres image, and whether the
`cancelled` jobs' logs are reachable at all (they report `steps=0`, which may be an API artefact for
cancelled jobs rather than a fact about the run).

**Transferable rule, and it is the same one as `L3335` and `L3336`:** when a theory predicts a
threshold, look for the case that CROSSES it and does not fail. I had that case in hand - a
`server-tests` success at 15m28s - and treated it as noise because the story was already good.
