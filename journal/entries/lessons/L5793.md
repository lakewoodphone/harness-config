**Two instruments that lie quietly: an inner `ssh` on stdin, and a locale-collated `sort`**

Measured 2026-10-16 on ZABZ-YOGA against `secratary-cloud`, both while sizing the journal fork.

**1. `ssh` without `-n` READS THE OUTER SCRIPT'S STDIN.** A here-string piped to `ssh host 'bash
-s'` is the idiom this estate uses to run a remote script without quoting it. A bare `ssh otherhost
'cmd'` *inside* that script inherits the script on stdin and **consumes the remaining lines**. The
observed shape: a three-host loop printed two hosts, and every line after it — including the `echo`
sections that were supposed to report the answer — vanished. No error, no exit code, a truncated
script. That is the dangerous failure mode, because a missing section reads as "nothing to report".
The first line printed was `bash: line 17: $'head\r': command not found`, which was a *second*
cause (CRLF in a PowerShell here-string) and sent me looking in the wrong place. Two faults, one
symptom: **fix both — `ssh -n` for every inner call, and `-replace "`r`n","`n"` (or `tr -d '\r'`)
for every here-string that crosses to a POSIX host.**

**2. `sort` IS LOCALE-COLLATED; `comm` IS BYTE-ORDERED.** `comm -13 main-ids.txt x-ids.txt`, after
a plain `sort` on both, reported "42–130 entries not in main" for ELEVEN different trees — a
plausible-looking fork network. With `LC_ALL=C sort` on both files, ten of those trees had **zero**
unique ids and exactly one, `/root/work/wt-item221`, had **234**. The ten phantom forks were an
ordering artefact. `comm` prints the reason on stderr — `comm: file 1 is not in sorted order` —
120 lines of it, and the counts scrolled past while I read the numbers I wanted. **The rule: any
`comm`/`join`/`diff` over sorted input must set `LC_ALL=C` on BOTH the `sort` and the comparison,
and the stderr of the comparison is part of its output.**
