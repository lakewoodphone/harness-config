**L4836 · A budget pause is not a failure: counting 'paused' as a give-up strike abandons 38% of the tasks that pause three times**

Round 4 recorded, in its own words, that a `[BUDGET-PAUSED: agent_soft_cap]` session is "not a loop failure". Round 6 then amended the give-up ceiling to count `paused` as a strike, and the two statements were never put side by side until the post-deploy check.

THE RULE: a budget pause says the session ran out of ROOM. A failure says the work did not come out. Treating them as one thing makes "needs more room" indistinguishable from "cannot be done", and the cost is asymmetric in the direction that matters -- refusing a task that pausES costs the work it would have finished; refusing a task that keeps FAILING costs nothing.

THE NUMBERS, 30 days, per task: 13 tasks paused 3+ times and **5 completed** (38%). 482 tasks failed 3+ times and 9 completed (1.9%). The ceiling is evidence-based for `failed` and not for `paused`, and the ratio is the argument.

CHECK THE PREDICATE YOU ARE LOOSENING against what the field MEANS, not against the count it produces. `paused` was 153 rows in 30 days -- a plausible-looking 8% addition to a 1869-row base, which is how it got in.
