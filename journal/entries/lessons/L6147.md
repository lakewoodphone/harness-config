**1. A name the owner says out loud is not the name in the record — do not hand-roll the search.**
Asked to pull up "reitman cadence phone that isn't charging", I built my own regex families over `customers` (`reit|rait|reyt|ryt|ritm|eism…`) and got a real negative twice, in both stores. The customer is **reifman** (`yisroel reifman`, id 1665, +18483738293). `lpt-hub/tools/case-index/lptcase.py find "reifman"` answered in 84 ms with fuzzy + skeleton matching, phone normalisation to the last ten digits, and case-body search. **2. `open_work_order_reconciliation.py` rewrites `orders.status` from the TEXT of notes and comms — including a bare "works".** Measured: WO26100812732 went AWAITING_DIAGNOSIS → READY_FOR_PICKUP at 2026-10-11 17:53 and back at 18:02, both logged as "Auto-updated by secretary reconciliation (confidence: 0.95)", because `_message_signals`/`_record_signals` carry `works|working|working now` → ready/completed and `unable to fix|cannot fix|not fixable` → UNFIXABLE. Its `TERMINAL_STATUSES = {COMPLETED, CANCELLED, UNFIXABLE}` is declared and **never used** in that file, so nothing there protects a finished order; the protection is that `fetch_open_work_orders` only pulls the open set. So: writing a note like "charging is now working" on an open order can flip its status. Land the order in a terminal status when the work is over, and choose words for a note knowing a regex reads them.
