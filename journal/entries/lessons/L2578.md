**L2578 · Verify a transcription against another document in the same pile, not against itself**

### Verify a transcription against another document in the same pile, not against itself

The strongest check on a transcription is not re-reading it — it is finding a
*second, independent* place where the same fact is printed, or a structural
constraint the rows have to satisfy.

Worked example, 2026-09-25: two photographed pages of a neighbourhood directory
(126 households, dense 6pt print) were transcribed by two separate readers. What
made them trustworthy was not the effort, it was two cross-checks:

1. **Six mobile numbers matched a different document in the same pile** — the shul
 welcome sheet — exactly (917-539-8821, 908-910-7946, 646-326-3003,
 732-228-1054, 848-525-7877, 848-299-2417). Two documents, two readers, same
 digits: the numbers are right and the readers were awake.
2. **The two pages joined.** Page 1 ended at Flannery Ave number 6 and page 2 began
 at number 7 and ran to 39 with no gap and no overlap, because the directory is
 ordered by house number. A page transcribed twice would have shown a break; a
 page transcribed short would have shown a gap.

So: before declaring a transcription good, ask what *else* in the pile constrains
it. A join, a sequence, a checksum, an amount that has to add up, or the same fact
printed somewhere else — any of those beats a confident re-read. Where no such
constraint exists, say so, and leave the cell `?` rather than filling it.
