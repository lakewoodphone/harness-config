**L1572 · Measured: 10.4% of the family chat's carrier messages are absent from our own database, and 18 were recorded as dropped that Twilio delivered**

window 2026-08-15..2026-09-16:

 twilio on hub number : 1448
 our messages table : 1315

Classification of the 151:
 30 Chip generated replies, no row
 20 Chip canned-fallback replies, no row
 10 "[Family Chat] ..." admin notices (expected: _notify_admins never logs)
 ~85 forwards to one member, +18482407257
 6 unmatched

THE CONTRADICTION WORTH REMEMBERING
+18482407257 was muted/deactivated 2026-08-19 22:24:26. That evening 18 messages to her
could not be delivered, so they were queued to the outbox AND written as messages rows
with twilio_sid=NULL, status='queued' (ids 26949..27343). The outbox then set those 18
outbox rows to status='dropped', last_error='member no longer active or muted'.

Twilio shows all 18 of those bodies DELIVERED to that number. So our messages table says
"queued, never sent" and our outbox says "dropped, never sent" for eighteen messages the
carrier delivered, and finalize_outbox_message (which keys its UPDATE on outbox_id) never
reconciled them.

The general lesson: two independent local records can be wrong in the SAME direction and
agree with each other, so internal consistency is not evidence. Only the carrier (or the
far end) can settle what was actually sent. When a system keeps its own delivery ledger,
reconcile it against the transport on a schedule and report drift as a number, or the
ledger quietly becomes fiction. Every question answered from that ledger alone -- what
did we send, how much did we spend, did the reminder go out -- is wrong by at least the
drift.

Instrument (read-only, safe to rerun): measure_family_chat_drift.py, on the authority at
/tmp/measure_family_chat_drift.py; --since/--until or --days. Exits 1 and prints a
VERDICT line when drift exists.
