**L1679 · A count nobody sampled is a liability: my own view said 1,431 where the truth was 172, and its
own sample is what exposed it.**

Building axis C's canonical identity surface (2026-09-16), I created `v_identity_conflict` fairly
faithfully to C-005's SQL and it reported **1,431 phones carrying more than one name claim**. I printed
a sample because that is the habit, and the sample destroyed the number:

```
8482453600 claims=6 dialpad:(848) 245-3600, dialpad:+18482453600, dialpad:(848)2453600, ...
```

Six claims, all of them the same telephone number. Two faults, both inherited from the plan's own SQL:

1. **The join used the RAW phone value.** `+17324569956`, `(732) 456-9956` and `7324569956` are three
 different join keys for one human. Axis C normalised for its *analysis* and then wrote a view that
 does not — the classic gap between the measurement and the artifact.
2. **It counted Dialpad's `customer_name` as a name.** That column frequently holds the phone number
 rather than a name, so one meaningless claim in three formats counted as three claims. Axis C had
 *found this itself* (it is why the busiest correspondent once displayed as "Microsoft Word") and did
 not carry the finding into its own view.

Fixed — normalise both sides, and exclude any claim whose digits equal the phone's digits — the number
went **1,431 → 172**, and the top hit became something real: `7326745878` carrying `Mom Zabrowsky` /
`Mommy` / `Shmuel Avigdor Zabrowsky` / `Adeena Zabrowsky`. One line, four names, two humans, in the
owner's own hand-written book. That is axis C's W4, and it is now visible in a surface instead of buried
in a plan.

**The general rules, and they are cheap:**
- **Print a sample of anything that counts.** The row count is the least informative thing a query
 returns; the sample is where the lie is. A number that agrees with your expectation needs sampling
 *most*, because you will not otherwise look.
- **A view is a claim, and it is read as one.** A query used once for analysis can be sloppy; a view
 someone will `SELECT` from later cannot, because the sloppiness becomes the answer.
- **When two of your own numbers disagree, explain the gap rather than picking one.** Axis C said 673,
 the corrected view says 172. That difference is real and it is *informative*: C normalised the phone
 (fault 1) but still counted phone-as-name claims (fault 2), so **~501 of C's 673 "conflicts" were junk
 labelling, not disagreements about who a human is.** The honest output is both numbers and the reason,
 not whichever one looks better.
