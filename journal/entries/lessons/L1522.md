**L1522 · Chip-off labs: PC-3000 Flash vs Rusolut VNR, the three rebuild blockers (XOR key / ECC-LDPC / FTL translator), and the three questions to ask when a lab reports a partial rebuild**

When a chip-off lab says "the tool does not fully support your controller and NAND chip-type", it is naming a REBUILD failure, not a read failure. Know the two tools and the three things that can block a rebuild, then ask which one it is.

THE TOOLS
- **PC-3000 Flash** (ACE Lab, ACELab Europe s.r.o., Czech Republic). The industry-standard chip-off/NAND platform. It does not "recover files" on its own: it takes the NAND dumps (produced by the chip-off) and, for each known controller+NAND pairing, applies ECC correction + the XOR/descrambling key + the FTL/translator mapping to build a mountable image. Where the pairing is only partially covered, it rebuilds what matches and drops the rest -- classically short single-block chains (documents) survive while anything spanning blocks (photos, video, archives) comes out corrupt.
- **Rusolut VNR** (Visual NAND Reconstructor, with its own NAND Reader + adapters). The peer tool with its own controller/XOR database. It is the usual "different tool" in a second attempt, but a lab that says "a different tool" has not necessarily named it -- ask.

THE THREE BLOCKERS, and why the distinction is the whole answer
1. **XOR / descrambling key not derived** for that controller+NAND revision.
2. **ECC / LDPC decode** not implemented for that NAND generation.
3. **Translator / FTL mapping** unknown, so blocks cannot be ordered into files.
(1) and (3) are routinely solved by a second tool or manual analysis. (2) is often terminal: Desert Data Recovery's SanDisk Ultra 1 TB monolith case (DDR-2026-0002, 2026-07-17) read the raw NAND successfully via pinout + Rusolut VNR and still declared it **not recoverable, because the LDPC-encoded data could not be decoded with current tooling** -- source `desertdatarecovery.com/cases/sandisk-ultra-1tb-microsd-monolith-ldpc/`.

DURABLE DISTINCTIONS
- **"On the supported-controller list" does not mean the DATA is decodable.** ACE Lab lists controller 20-82-00388, and the chip-off read succeeded, while the rebuild still failed on the SanDisk Z4480104744 256G NAND. Read-success and rebuild-success are separate gates.
- **A partial result costs nothing extra to retry**: the dumps persist, so pass 2 is re-translation of the same dumps with another tool -- no second chip-off, no second shipping risk, and any lab can quote it.
- **NAND marking arithmetic**: on SanDisk/Toshiba parts a marking like `256G` is the PER-DIE density (256 Gbit ~ 32 GB), so a "single NAND chip" in a 256 GB drive is a package of ~8 stacked dies. It bills as one chip; it is why interleaving/FTL rebuild is hard.
- Ask three questions of any lab reporting a partial rebuild: which tool, which of the three blockers, and whether the existing dumps suffice. Otherwise you are waiting a week to learn nothing.
