**L2590 · A cache TTL shorter than the scan it caches is a bill paid on every call (list_agents, 2026-09-25)**

A cache whose TTL is SHORTER than the work it caches is not a cache - it is the
same bill paid again on every call, plus the illusion of protection.

Measured on ZABZ-YOGA 2026-09-25. `plugin-health` replaced the shipped
`list_agents` with a 3 s TTL + 8 s deadline cache specifically to stop repeated
full-corpus walks. The engine reports `listAgents` as mounted and running - and
with **0 cache hits, 3 deadline misses, lastScanMs=6623**.

The arithmetic is unforgiving: TTL 3000 ms, scan 6623 ms, callers spaced minutes
apart. Every call missed the cache and re-paid the walk. The cache was correct for
the corpus as measured on 2026-09-16 (389 dirs / ~320 MB, scans 600-4300 ms) and
silently stopped being correct as the corpus grew to 837 dirs / 440 MB. **A tuning
constant derived from a measurement is a claim with an expiry date; nothing was
watching it expire.**

Reproduced standalone, which is what makes this a fact rather than a story: a
probe that readdirs every session dir, opens every file, reads the first 64 KiB
(752 ms), then reads all 837 files in full (5,675 ms) finished in **6,437 ms** -
matching the engine's own 6,623 ms scan. The walk reads whole files because the
records are zstd frames and a frame cannot be decoded from its header alone, so
cost is proportional to total BYTES on disk, not to session count.

Consequence, and why it is felt rather than merely logged: that walk runs on the
shared event loop, so every other session in that engine freezes for its duration.
The engine's own max loop lag is 13,210 ms; the multi-window proxy measured
`POST /api/session/list` at 25-31 s cold and its origins refresh logged
"rows=837 in 12843 ms". One of my own diagnostic tool calls returned "outcome
unknown" mid-diagnosis - the same stall from the inside.

Fix landed (harness-config 0907823): TTL is derived from the measurement instead
of fixed - `max(floor, 5 x lastScanMs)` capped at 30 s, floor 0 still disables.
Verified: a 10 s-old entry now serves as a hit where the fixed 3 s TTL could only
miss.

Generalisable rule: whenever a cache TTL, deadline, budget or interval is chosen
from a measurement, either derive it from that measurement at runtime or attach a
tripwire that reports when the measurement has moved past it. "0 hits" next to
"lastScanMs=6623" was sitting in /healthz the whole time, and nobody was reading
it as a contradiction.
