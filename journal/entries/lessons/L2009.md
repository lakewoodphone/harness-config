**L2009 · iPad Pro 11 (A1980) USB-C port is modular - the one iPad port job with no soldering**

Asked 2026: 'A1980 charging port replacement, how doable, is it also soldering?' Answer: NO soldering - A1980 (iPad Pro 11-inch 1st gen, 2018) is the FIRST iPad whose charging port is a modular plug-in part.

EVIDENCE. iFixit iPad Pro 11-inch teardown (guide 115457): 'Last to come out is the USB-C port - which unlike previous iPads, is fully modular... previous iPads had Lightning ports for the same purpose, and those were soldered to the logic board.' iFixit called it 'a nice repair win'. Independently confirmed by a technician on an A1980: two screws holding the port, then it unplugs from the board ('this is the charging port connector where the charging port flex is plugged into' - VCC Board Repairs, youtube 6GXZpseKP6s, Feb 2026).

PART BUYING TRAP. MobileSentrix lists TWO SKUs for iPad Pro 11: an 'Aftermarket Plus Charging Port Flex Cable' (the complete modular assembly - what you want) and, separately, a 'Charging Port Only (Soldering Required)' SKU, which is just the bare connector for techs who re-solder onto the existing flex. Ordering the wrong one turns this into a soldering job by mistake. Same applies to the Pro 12.9 3rd gen (A1876/A2014) and 2nd-gen 11in (A2228/A2068).

HOW DOABLE. The part swap is the easy half; reaching it is not. iFixit scored the 2018 iPad Pro 3/10. The display is glued down hard, the port is literally the LAST item out of the device, so this is a near-full teardown: heat + de-adhesive the display, board out, battery. Risk on a still-valuable device is cracking the display or killing the TrueDepth assembly, not the port.

THE TRAP THAT DECIDES WHETHER TO QUOTE IT. On A1980 a new port often does NOT fix the fault. Documented A1980 case: known-good port fitted, still stuck at 5V/0.49A looping instead of 15V - the real fault was board-level: CD3215 (USB-C PD controller) plus 610A3C Tristar, with a 0-ohm resistor (R3882) and a diode on that line that commonly blow, and an 'Orion' chip as a further possibility. Red/red on the diode-mode tester = short, O = open circuit, and the readings are inversely symmetrical across the connector.

DIAGNOSTIC GATE, always before quoting: (1) USB-C PD meter with a USB-C-to-USB-C cable on a 15V-capable brick - an iPad Pro should pull 15V; 5V and a meter that loops/restarts means a short, not just a dirty port. (2) Diode-mode USB-C tester (Mechanic T824 or equivalent), comparing against known-good. (3) If a known-good port still reads bad, it is the board. Also: a flat battery below 3.0V will not charge at all - measure the battery before blaming the port (A1980 example measured 2.8V and had to be boosted on a DC supply).

SCOPE NOTE. D157 (no in-house soldered iPad charge-port work) does NOT automatically cover this model, because this port is not soldered. Port-only swap on an iPad Pro 11/12.9 USB-C unit is mechanically legitimate bench work; the CD3215/Tristar board case is not.
