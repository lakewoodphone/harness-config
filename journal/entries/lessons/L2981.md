**L2981 · Three fabricated values in one session - an invented price, an invented mechanic, and invented timestamps**

**Three fabricated values in one session — an invented price, an invented mechanic, and invented timestamps — one shape, and the only fix**

2026-09-28, a single session, three separate fabrications. Each filled a gap in a record that then looked
complete.

1. **An invented price.** A scrape summary returned "34 per hour" for a Topgolf bay; the number is not
 published anywhere retrievable — the pricing widget is client-side and the pricing URL 404s. I repeated it
 to the owner as *"the site quotes 34."* (L2850)
2. **An invented mechanic.** Activate's site says difficulty levels run 1–10 and unlock as you beat them.
 From that I wrote that one brother could sit at level 3 while another ground through level 9 in the same
 room. Impossible: the level is group-level and the competition between players is score. (L2976)
3. **Invented timestamps.** A document I wrote and *committed and pushed* said "researched 16:00–17:15 ET"
 and carried a per-source column of fetch times to the minute. The real window was **14:45–15:20 ET**, and
 no per-source times had been recorded live. I never read the clock; I generated times that looked right.

**What they have in common, and why it is worse than three mistakes.** A price, a mechanism and a timestamp
are precisely the fields a reader will trust. None of them announced itself as a guess, and two of the three
reached a written artefact before anyone caught them. The second-order cost is the real one: **once one number
in a document is invented, the reader cannot tell which numbers are measured.** Provenance is the product, and
this destroys it — in a system whose worst historical failure was reporting a crisis that did not exist
because a stale value was believed.

**What actually caught them.** Not care, and not a second read-through:
- the owner asking a question that had **no answer** under my description — *"how can 2 people play the same
 game at the same time at different levels?"*
- comparing the claim against a **second independent source** (the theater page, not the zip page);
- **reading the clock.** The timestamps were caught only when I ran `Get-Date` for an unrelated reason.

**THE RULE — three habits, cheapest first:**
1. **Read the clock. Never write a timestamp from memory or by estimation.** `Get-Date` costs nothing and
 invented times are indistinguishable from measured ones in prose.
2. **Every number in a written record is either traceable to a source read at a stated time, or labelled an
 estimate.** If a fetch time was not recorded live, write the honest window ("roughly 14:45–15:20 ET") and
 say so — never manufacture a per-row column to make the table look rigorous.
3. **When a question cannot be answered under your model of a system, the model is wrong.** That is
 falsification, not a request for elaboration.

**And when one is caught:** correct it **in the artefact**, keep the error visible beside the correction, and
never quietly rewrite. The record of having been wrong is what makes the rest of the record worth believing.
