**L3736 · A moved authority moves its producers: a stale reading is not a monitor**

# A moved authority moves its producers. A reading that says "stale" is not a monitor.

**Measured 2026-10-12, from the owner's own question** — *"why does it say stale? why is everything
stable?"* — and the answer was that "stable" meant **stopped**.

## What happened

The company's authority cut over from the office box `secratary` to the cloud box `secratary-cloud` at
2026-10-11T05:43:36Z. The cutover moved the database, the API, the dashboard and the phone gate. It
did **not** move the `ceo-kernel` sentinel — the job that computes every "Company findings" item the
attention badge shows. That job only ever ran on the office box, and the office box was then FROZEN as
the rollback anchor (`cron` `masked`+`inactive`).

So for six hours every badge in the fleet rendered `6 needing attention · stale 6h ago`, from a
`latest.json` whose last write was `2026-10-11T20:20:06Z` — and that write was itself inside the
accidental 19:04–20:26Z unfreeze window, i.e. the box's last act before it was re-frozen.

The badge was **not** broken. It was honest: it labelled a reading it could not prove current. The
estate failed, not the badge.

## Why nobody noticed

- The frozen box was **healthy**. `cron` masked *by decision* and `cron` masked *by fault* look
 identical from outside unless you know which box you are on and why (`AGENTS.md` §5).
- The badge's staleness flag is not an alarm. It requires a human to look, understand what stale
 means, and care.
- `run-sentinel.sh` **always exits 0** by design (a finding is the signal, not a scheduler failure),
 so an exit-code monitor cannot tell "nothing wrong" from "stopped sensing". 140 §A1 predicted this
 on 2026-10-11: *"what would notice its failure: nothing."* It was right, and it happened the next day.

## The second, quieter defect

`packages/plugin-attention-badge/lib/client.js` hardcoded `BADGE_AUTHORITY = the office box`. Client
bundles are served **immutable**, cached for a year. So even once the authority computed the findings,
every machine whose page was already loaded would keep asking the frozen box — silently, for a year.
A hostname in a client bundle is a year-long commitment made by accident.

## What to do instead

1. **When you move a host, enumerate what READS the thing you moved, not just what writes it.** The
 port list was organised by "jobs on the old box"; the badge's dependency is on the *output*, which
 nothing listed.
2. **A monitoring surface must be able to say "the thing I read has stopped" in a way someone acts
 on** — and that alarm must live outside the producer. The candidate here is an
 absence-reporting check: "no new `history.jsonl` row in 15 minutes", which works precisely because
 the wrapper exits 0.
3. **Never put a hostname in a client bundle.** Use a list, ordered by trust, and let the consumer
 prefer the freshest reading. Cost: one attribute. Benefit: an authority move stops being a
 year-long silent wrong-host read and becomes a self-correcting degradation.
4. **Re-check every check when a monitoring job moves hosts.** Three of nineteen arrived blind on the
 authority, and one of them (`app_voice`) was blind because it named `secretary-api` — the *office*
 unit name; the authority's is `company-api`.

## Evidence

- office `~/ceo-kernel-var/latest.json` mtime `2026-10-11T20:20:06.262586248 +0000`, `host: secratary`;
 `run.log` tail ends `2026-10-11T20:20:06+00:00 exit=1`; `systemctl is-enabled cron` → `masked`.
- `secratary.tail93e6e6.ts.net` resolved to `100.84.72.88` (office); its gate had answered **115,737**
 `/dsh-attention.json` requests.
- the authority answered `{"read": false, "error": "cannot read /root/ceo-kernel-var/latest.json: No
 such file or directory"}` — i.e. the gate was ready and the producer was missing.
- after the port: `{"read": true, "at": "2026-10-12T03:15:45Z", "host": "secratary-cloud"}`,
 `age_seconds 0.3`.
- `docs/mesh/168-ATTENTION-AUTHORITY.md` is the full record; `docs/badge/README.md` §10 is the badge's.
