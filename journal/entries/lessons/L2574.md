**L2574 · A payroll debit decomposes exactly: gross + employer taxes, and the employee's withholding never changes what the employer pays**

**L · 2026-09-25 · You can always split a payroll debit into the employee's money and the employer's, and the run structure never changes the employer's cost.**

Measured on 2026-09-25 while answering the owner's question *"how much would he actually get, not including all the
taxes I would pay the government for no reason."* He was right to want the split and the split is derivable from
figures already on the record.

**The identity:**
- `total debit = gross wages + employer taxes`
- `net to employee = gross wages − employee withholding`
- `Gusto's "taxes" line = employee withholding + employer taxes`

The reason the third line needs care: employee withholding appears on *both* sides (it lowers the net, and it is part
of the tax line), so **it cancels out of the total.** Change when or how you run payroll and the employer's cost is
identical; only the split between the employee's pocket and his withholding moves.

**Verified twice against Gusto's own numbers, and it closes to the cent:**
- Normal week: total 1,472.21 − gross 1,326.80 = 145.41 employer taxes (10.96%). Tax line 283.11 = employee
 withholding 137.70 (1,326.80 − net 1,189.10) + 145.41.
- The 1.5-week makeup: total 2,208.32 − gross 1,990.20 = 218.12 employer taxes (10.96%, same rate). Gusto's tax
 line 523.17 = employee withholding 305.05 (1,990.20 − net 1,685.15) + 218.12. Both Gusto figures — the total and
 the net — reproduce from the hours and the rate alone.

**What it settles, in one line each:**
1. **Employer taxes are the employer's real cost and are fixed.** 10.96% of gross here (FICA match + NJ SUTA and
 reemployment at this wage level), identical week over week. That is the part the owner pays and the employee
 never sees.
2. **Withholding is the employee's money, not a cost.** All 305.05 is credited to him on his return.
3. **Lumping periods together over-withholds the employee and saves the employer nothing.** One run for 1.5 weeks
 withheld 305.05; the same money as a full week plus a half week would have withheld 203.87. The ~101 difference
 is the employee's own income tax, recovered when he files. Withholding tables treat a lump as one oversized
 period — the classic effect, and it is the employee's cash-flow problem, not the employer's cost.
4. **Never quote a net figure from a reconstruction when a processor has stated one.** My own first pass gave
 1,780.91 net by chaining normal weekly nets; Gusto's stated net for the actual run was 1,685.15. Both summed to
 the same 2,208.32, which is exactly why the error was invisible until the pieces were separated.

**Where it applies beyond this case:** any owner question of the form "what does this actually cost me" resolves
with these three identities and two known figures. Ask for the gross and the total, and everything else falls out.
