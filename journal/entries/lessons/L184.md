**L184 · A concurrent session put my staged work in its commit - verify what landed, not what you staged**

*Learned:* the L33 hazard, observed live. I staged exactly my own paths with `git add <paths>`; a **second
session** in the same working tree committed with a message about Yocheved's laptop, and my staged journal
files went into *its* commit. My own commit then reported "no changes added to commit" — the work was safely
in history, but the log said the wrong thing about why.
*Rule:* in a shared working tree, staging precisely is necessary and not sufficient — **check what the last
commit actually contains** (`git log --name-only -1`) before assuming your work is where you put it. The
remedy is not to fight the other session: it is to keep commits atomic, verify, and make the next entry cite
the hash. Recorded as an extension of L33.
