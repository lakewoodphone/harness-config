**L3814 · CORRECTION of L3811: /v1/models is not defective - the never-closing response was a wedged process, and the probe's accept-and-hang path is UNVERIFIED**

`L3811` recorded a defective endpoint. The endpoint is not defective — it was a wedged process, and the finding expired when the process was restarted. Corrected here rather than edited, and the practical consequence is the interesting part.

**What `L3811` said**, measured 2026-10-14 ~12:40Z: `GET /v1/models` on `company-api` produced its **complete correct body** — `{"object":"list","secretary_catalog_version":1,"default_model":"secretary-auto", …}` — into the curl output file, while curl reported `http=000` at a **60-second** deadline with `size_download=0`; `Connection: close` did not help; `HEAD` returned 405 in 0.0019 s; no response headers arrived within 15 s; and for contrast `/health`, `/docs` and `/openapi.json` all answered. The lesson drawn was *"no answer" and "an answer that never closed" are different sentences*.

**What is true now**, measured 2026-10-14 13:08Z on the same host, after two restarts (13:04 and 13:05) and the timestamp-window patch:

 curl /v1/models http=200 t=0.046045 s
 the serving probe on it OK http=200 in 0.198091 s

So the route is fine and always was. **The 000-with-a-body was a symptom of the wedged process** — the same process whose cgroup was 80 % unable to run for memory, which I diagnosed and restarted at 12:33 and which had restarted again at 12:47. `L3811`'s *observation* stands (curl reported 000 while the body was on disk, and a deadline probe therefore reports "down" for something that has an answer); its *attribution* — an endpoint that never completes — does not. **A transient state of a broken process is not a property of the code**, and I recorded it as the latter.

**The measurement lesson survives and hardens.** A 000 at a deadline tells you the transfer did not complete. It does not tell you whether the data existed, whether the route is broken, or whether the *process* is broken. Those are three different diagnoses and only one of them was true here. The way to tell them apart is the thing I did next: read the service's state, not the endpoint's behaviour — `memory.current` against `memory.high`, `memory.pressure`, and `ExecMainStartTimestamp` — because an endpoint that hangs and a process that cannot run look identical from the client side.

**And the probe's most important path is now UNVERIFIED, which I am recording rather than papering over.** I set out to prove `company-api-serving-probe.sh` catches a service that **accepts the connection and never answers** — the exact wedge signature — by pointing it at `/v1/models` while that endpoint was hanging. **There was no hang to test against**, because the restarts had cleared it. So the probe is proven on three paths (live `OK` exit 0; dead port `curl rc=7` exit 1; wrong endpoint `http=404` exit 1) and **not proven on the one that matters most**. Its `--max-time` deadline is the right mechanism for it and it is the same mechanism already proven against a connection that never establishes; but *the accept-and-hang case is untested*, and saying otherwise would be exactly the confident-wrong-number failure this entry is about. The way to test it deliberately is a listener that accepts and sleeps — noted as work, not claimed as done.

**A second, smaller thing this exposed:** two consecutive `/v1/models` calls now take 0.046 s and 0.198 s, where on 2026-10-11 the first call after a start was documented as slow and the second took 10.2 s (`ai-gateway-health.py`'s own comment). Those numbers are stale. The slow first call may have been the same memory pressure, not the catalogue read.
