**L6850 · After finding fabricated attributions, audit your own NUMBERS with the same tool - five were stale or wrong and two were in the first paragraph**

Measured 2026-10-16 on the owner-question document, one round after section 5 was found to attribute five answers the owner never gave. I re-measured every figure in the document against the ledger, the per-entity statement, the park store and the balance snapshots, and found: Q1 called a 4,301.87 row 'a payment out' when it is a CREDIT with the bank line 'Payment Thank You - Web'; Q3 said 'eight monthly charges of 180, seventeen ledger rows' when it is 33 charges totalling 6,480.00 from 2 January to 9 August with a 360.00 maximum; Q4 said '75 days late' for a deadline that is 78 days late (the queue's own premise check flags the same drift); Q5 quoted 25,671.84 of gross receipts that reconciles with nothing I can read now (the ledger's Q3-2026 receipts band is 49,359.21 across 310 rows and September revenue alone is 7,905.22); Q6 quoted a park-store total of 20,765.82 from a state the store has since left (it is 35 clusters, 27,146.78, none ever asked); Q8 quoted a 1,694.25 reading I cannot reproduce. Two of the stale figures were in the intro block and the footer - the two places a reader looks first, and the ones I had not thought to check because the body edits felt like the whole job. What made the difference was mechanical: a script that scans the document for every superseded figure and reports any occurrence that is NOT inside the passport paragraph or a sentence naming it as a correction. It reports zero now, with one self-inflicted false positive on a correction sentence that does not use the word 'correction'. The rule: a numbers pass is not finished when the body is right - the summary, the headline block and the footer quote the same numbers and are checked last, which is exactly backwards; check the two places a reader looks first, first. And when a figure cannot be reproduced, drop it and say so rather than quoting it: 'unverified' is a complete answer and a stale number is not.
