## THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST FOR THE BENCH

The owner read **19.4 V / 1.25 A** lid-closed on **both** USB-C ports and called it healthy. The **voltage** is healthy (19.00-21.00 V is what a 20 V PD contract allows at the source). **The current is uninterpretable without one more datum**, and Lenovo's own C930 manual supplies it: *"Battery charging does not start until the Power Meter shows that less than 95% of the total power remains."* The same manual documents a **charge indicator — solid white = on AC at 91-100 %, solid amber = on AC at 1-90 %.** So ~24 W into a drained pack is charging hard, and **~24 W into a pack above 95 % with the machine closed should not exist.** **Read the indicator next time — it is free and it decides which reading this is.**

Second finding: the healthy twin-port reading proves both ports are **electrically alive** and does **not** prove either still **retains a plug**. iFixit 706941 has a C930 with **both** receptacles loose ("*the cable seems loose*"), Molex's spec shows retention force softening from 8-20 N to 6-20 N after 10,000 cycles, and Dell KB 000216384 maps *"Connector won't stay in port"* to **"damaged port OR cable"**. That is why the next test must use **the customer's own cable** and not another bench cable.

Third, recorded so it is not led with later: this board's (EYG70 NM-B741) dominant documented failure is the **CYPD4226 PD controller**, but every documented case is **no charging at all on either port** — which is **not** this machine.
