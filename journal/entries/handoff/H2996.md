## 2026-09-28 19:59 UTC - ZABZ-TECH - Round 20: two concurrency defects found by measuring, and a session's work recovered onto master

THIS ROUND'S FINDINGS, both created or exposed by raising concurrency to 5.

1. THE FAN-OUT OVERSHOT. Measured 19:54Z: SIX `wake-dispatch.sh` processes alive against
 WAKE_FANOUT_TARGET=5 - two running 15.5 minutes, four started in the previous 30 seconds - and
 `wake.py claim` answered **`database is locked`** while four dispatchers polled and heartbeated the
 same sqlite store every 60 s, with one heartbeat process sitting in uninterruptible sleep.
 CAUSE: the loop counted a row as in flight only once the STORE said `claimed`, so every pass landing
 inside the window between "process launched" and "claim landed" concluded there was room and launched
 another. Polling to close that window was not enough - a slow claim, a gated claim, or a claim taken
 by a different dispatcher all let it through.
 FIX: count live dispatch PROCESSES, take the larger of that and the store's count, and hold an
 absolute process ceiling. A store count alone can never again authorise an overshoot.
 It also fixed a counting bug of my own: `pgrep -f "wake-dispatch.sh" | wc -l` matched the PROBE'S OWN
 command line, so a dry run reported "processes 1" with none running and the live probe reported 2 with
 one - an inflated count is not harmless, it makes the loop believe shifts are in flight that are not,
 so it never reaches its target. It now matches the argument shape:
 `ps -eo args= | grep -c '^bash /home/zabz/bin/wake-dispatch.sh'`. Verified: 1 dispatcher -> reports 1;
 a real run with ceiling 5 ended at 1 process, at or under the ceiling.

2. THE CLAIM PATH COULD NOT EXPLAIN ITSELF. With 15 rows waiting, `wake.py claim` answered
 `{"ok": true, "row": null, "blocked_by": null}` - which reads as "the queue is empty". Every row was
 in fact gated: `_candidates()` refuses on a future `not_before`, on night-quiet for low priority, and
 on `WAKE_MAX_PER_SOURCE_PER_HOUR` (3) being spent for that row's SOURCE. The store's stats showed the
 cap; the claim path - what a dispatcher and a woken session actually call - said null. It now reports
 `blocked_by: "row-gates"` with a per-reason, per-source count of what was refused. The old
 `blocked_by` meanings (daily-cap, cost-cap, paused, night-quiet) are unchanged.
