THE DEFECT: SUPERSEDE checked that a `superseded-by` target EXISTS. Nothing checked that it was worth arriving at. Two documents pointed at a successor that was itself archived:
 docs/audits/android-client-capability-audit-2026-09-14.md -> docs/ENGINEERING_STATE_AUDIT_2026-09-14.md (archived)
 docs/HANDOFF_2026-09-10.md -> docs/HANDOFF_2026-09-11.md (archived)
A pointer into history is worse than no pointer, because it LOOKS answered. The new code SUPERSEDE-DEAD follows the chain and requires it to end at live / generated / draft / pointer; it fails on archive, on a superseded stop with no further successor, on leaving the estate, on cycles, and past 16 links.

MEASURED FIRST, AND THE MEASUREMENT CHANGED THE DESIGN. A throwaway script over all ten superseded documents found three shapes the first draft would have got wrong:
 `superseded-by: ADR-34` and `ADR-27` are decision IDS, not paths - the resolver has to consult the decision register, which is why follow_supersede_chain takes by_id and my first version silently reported them as broken;
 four chains end at a `draft`, which is legitimate (a draft names what is true and says it is unfinished) so draft is a terminus and the docstring says why;
 one chain ends at a GENERATED document (QUESTIONS_AND_CLARIFICATIONS -> decisions/REGISTER.md) which is also legitimate - a generated register is the live owner.
So of ten flagged chains, two were real. Measuring before writing the checker saved a check that would have cried wolf eight times and been disabled.

LESSON, generalisable: "points at something that exists" and "points at something true" are different checks, and the second one is the one a reader needs. The same distinction already existed for numbers (FACT-CONTRA) and for generated pages (GEN-STALE); it now exists for supersession.

ALSO: two of my patch scripts used a silent `t.replace(old, new)` with no assertion and did nothing because the target string was not there - the `updated:` date on HANDOFF_2026-09-10 stayed at 2026-10-18 and check_docs_index caught it as FM-STALE. Every replace in a patch script needs an assert; I have written that rule twice today and broken it twice.
