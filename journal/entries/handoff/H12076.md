## THE FAMILY: A CUT THAT LEAVES A SENTENCE LOOKING COMPLETE

Every defect this round is a truncation that produces a confident falsehood rather than an
obviously broken string. That is the dangerous kind -- it reads as finished.

1. **#868 began mid-thought.** Body: "starting with pt708 6c6693eb8 (which also lands item 710),
 or will you merge them yourself?" The question is complete in the store; my either/or clause
 loop overwrote the opening clause with the LAST clause that fit, so the sentence lost its
 subject. Fixed: take the longest fitting PREFIX of clauses, never a later one alone.

2. **#837 stated a false quantity.** Body: "The central search index holds 72." The real figure
 is 72,050 documents. The ceiling cut landed inside the number at its thousands separator, and
 the trailing-comma strip then removed the comma -- so a wrong number survived as a complete
 sentence. Fixed in three places: the ceiling cut walks back out of a number, the context
 comma-split ignores commas between digits (`(?<!\d),(?!\d)`), and a truncated clause drops an
 unbalanced parenthesis rather than leaving "(27,742 journal entries duplicated out of
 worktrees." hanging.

3. **#837 again, the other direction.** With no context fitting, the body became "May I delete
 them?" -- "them" pointing at nothing. Truncating the context to fit is ugly; dropping it makes
 the question unanswerable, which is worse, so the context is now truncated rather than dropped.

4. **#673 in the third person again.** "Want me to confirm the DeepSeek charge is HIS?" -- the
 message is to him. Added `(is|was) his` -> yours to the pronoun rules.
