### The two defects that mattered most, both found by measuring rather than thinking

1. **The update channel served the old app to every phone.** The broker sets
 `cache-control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` on the APK and the domain is behind Cloudflare, so
 a fixed filename means the first release a phone fetched is the only one it can fetch. Measured after
 publishing 0.1.1: manifest `sha256 86445b45…`, the URL returned `e29f489a…` (the 0.1.0 bytes),
 `cf-cache-status: HIT`, `age: 103838`. Every phone would fail its digest check and never install.
 **Fix:** `publish-update.ps1` names the artifact `app-<abi>-release-<versionCode>.apk`. Verified after:
 200, 58,236,103 bytes, sha256 equal to the manifest.
2. **The second broker was in the rotation with a read-only database.** `standby_read_only=True` on the
 twin → its SQLite handle is `O_RDONLY` (`fdinfo flags 02500000` vs `02500002`), and Caddy
 round-robined both (6 requests split 3/3), so half of every write reached a process that cannot write.
 **Fix:** the live Caddyfile now carries the repo's intended block (writes → primary, reads → both),
 and `deploy-broker.ps1` refuses to prove a deployment where a read-only twin has no `@write` matcher
 in front of it.

**My own error, recorded because it repeats:** the first version of that deploy guard measured
*appearance* (does the twin's name appear as an upstream) and refused the **correct** configuration.
A guard must measure what can be **sent** to an instance, not whether its name is present.
