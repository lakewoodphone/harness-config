## 2026-10-11 19:05 UTC · ZABZ-TECH · INCIDENT 2026-10-11: the company on secratary was shut down and cron masked for 15 hours; restored 19:04Z

Incident, 2026-10-11, found at 18:58Z only because the owner asked whether things were syncing.

WHAT WAS WRONG. The autonomous company on `secratary` had been **shut down for 15 hours** and nobody
had noticed. Measured state before the fix:

- `cron.service` **masked** (`/etc/systemd/system/cron.service -> /dev/null`) and dead since
 04:01:34Z. `systemctl is-enabled cron` = masked, `is-active` = inactive.
- `secretary-api`, `dialpad-sms-ws`, `secretary-dashboard`, `phone-engine` all inactive.
 (`mesh-broker` was still active.)
- Consequence: **not one scheduled job ran on the authority for 15 hours.** The two the estate
 itself measures both went red — `comms.dialpad_newest_age_h` = 18.48 h (the shop's texts were not
 being harvested) and `lpt.refresh_age_min` = 900 min. A third check,
 `lpt.refresh_fast_forward`, read `unknown`.

THE SEQUENCE, from the journal. 04:00:08Z `estate-monitor` logged two CRITs, one of them
`offsite_push_incomplete_run=1.0` — "run started 2026-10-11T01:40:08Z has NO 'push finished' line
and is not in progress (139 min)". The offsite push had hung. At 04:01:34Z systemd began stopping
cron. At 04:02-04:03Z something running as `zabz` with sudo was doing **database-lock surgery** —
`lsof -nP -- .../secretary.db`, `lsof -t -- .../secretary.db`, `kill 49047` — and then at 04:03:33Z
issued, in one burst:

 systemctl stop cron / cron.socket / cron.service
 systemctl mask cron.service cron.socket
 systemctl stop secretary-api dialpad-sms-ws secretary-dashboard phone-engine mesh-broker

**The mask is the part that matters.** Stopping a service is recoverable and anything watching would
restart it. Masking it means the estate could not heal itself, and for 15 hours nothing did.

THE FIX, 19:04Z: `systemctl unmask cron.service cron.socket`, `systemctl enable --now cron`,
then started the four stopped services. Verified rather than assumed: `cron` active with a new PID
and `enabled`; `curl /health` on the API returned **HTTP 200** and its apscheduler came up with 5
jobs; and a forced `lpt-hub-refresh.sh` ran clean afterwards — status ok, 412 records, `behind 0
ahead 0`, timestamp 19:04:48Z.

WHAT THIS COST AND WHAT IT TAUGHT.
1. **Nothing in the estate alerted on "the company is off".** Two freshness checks went red and sat
 red; the thing that surfaced it was the owner asking a question. A dead `cron.service` on the
 authority is a total outage of the company's scheduled work and should page, not wait.
2. **A self-inflicted outage was performed to break a database lock.** The lock contention is the
 real disease (the estate already records thousands of 'database is locked' events a week, and the
 Dialpad call harvest was logging `gave up after 5 attempt(s) ... database is locked`). Stopping and
 masking the whole company was the cure applied at 04:03.
3. **I have the sequence but not yet the actor.** The lsof/kill/stop burst was issued from repeated
 short sessions as `zabz`, which reads like a script looping rather than a person. Until the actor
 is identified, this can happen again at ~04:00 tomorrow, and it will mask cron again.
4. **Disk is at 92%** (411G of 467G, 37G free) — worth holding next to a hung offsite push.
