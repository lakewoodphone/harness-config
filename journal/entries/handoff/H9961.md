## 2026-10-19 21:33 UTC · ZABZ-YOGA · Shlock: the owner corrected fourteen facts, seven workstreams audited the awning, and the design is a fall plus a bar on the existing hangers

Owner walked his own 10-05 recordings and 10-11 photographs with me on 2026-10-19 and corrected the awning record on fourteen points. The corrections are the highest-authority shlock document now: `lpt-hub/research/miscellaneous/shlocks-redesign-2026-10-19/00-owner-corrections-2026-10-19.md` (repo path `shlock-redesign-2026-10-19/`), and `01-register-corrections` lists every defect-register row they overturn.

What was wrong in the record, in one line each: the blocked drain holes are a RED HERRING (most have no rope; water never reaches them); the leading-edge upturn is a MIX of rope, hem, pocket and fabric; "maybe we need more poles" means members UNDER the cover, not the pocket pole, so N-23 does NOT collide with D2588; the sleeves and hangers ARE fitted on every rail, so N-07 is withdrawn; the parked bundle does not merely hold water, it TEARS THE VINYL AT THE GROMMETS and can become impossible to pull out; down is the hard direction, up was easy (S3-70's 25/14 lb figures are the abandoned design); and the rope count is 12-14 (4 up, 4 down, 4-6 tie-downs), not four.

Seven mesh workstreams on ZABZ-TECH and zabz-tech-linux then audited it end to end. Files 10-16 and the synthesis 20-design.md. The finding that carries the design: the cover is a LEVEL MEMBRANE WITH NO LOW EDGE. Seven 42-in bays each brim at 2.26 in and hold ~39 gal; one inch across them is ~1,470 lb, 34x the cover's own weight. A membrane always sags and water always finds the sag, so it never spills — which is why it cannot be moved wet and why the parked bundle tears itself.

The recommended design (530-920, ~19 h, reuses every existing part): 2% fall to the yard (the root fix, ~60, turns basins into gutters); a rigid bar through the leading sleeve carried on the EXISTING eight hangers on 2-in drop-links so it sits below the rail plane (distributor + edge stiffener + tow point, and it is not a dam because the fabric slopes down onto it); a rigid 3-4 in drip lip on the bar instead of a fabric skirt (a skirt is 14 ft2 of new sail giving 65-130 lb of uplift at 50 mph); a 342 in aluminium Z wall lip with 1/4 in clearance; two lines from the bar ends meeting as one bight at one station on the centre house post, horn cleats, 3:1 on retract only, and a 150 lb orange sacrificial link at each mouth so the fuse parts instead of a non-returnable grommet. Plus a hood over the park position (it is under the eaves, ~4x the rain) and a 4-in positive stop so it satisfies the 3-in Yom Tov rule at zero cost.

The seven 21-in rails (1,270) are HELD BEHIND A HOSE TEST, not bought. The tarp is on a 21-in grommet module and the frame on 42 in, so the mismatch is real — but fall beats rails on price and the test decides.
