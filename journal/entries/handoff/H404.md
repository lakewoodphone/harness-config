## Ranking: the research found the real cost and it is now guarded
FTS5's expense is **ranking, not matching**, and `LIMIT` does not bound it. Measured on the real
273,108-chunk index: "customer" matched 17,551 chunks -- 0.5 ms unranked vs **535 ms ranked**; my
`search()` was taking 716-8,817 ms on common words. Now: an `fts5vocab` document-frequency estimate
(terms stemmed by FTS5 itself, because the content vocabulary holds porter stems) degrades a query
above 2% of the corpus to an unranked recency scan, and every such hit carries `degraded: true` with
`strategy: fts-unranked`. Result: 1,644 ms instead of 8,817 ms, and a gibberish query 34 ms instead
of 52 s.

I made two mistakes here worth remembering. First I used the estimate as PROOF that a query had no
answer, which broke every stemmed-word test and made a search for "customer" return nothing on an
index holding 8,784 matching chunks; it is a hint that may skip ranking, never a verdict. Then I used
`min()` over per-term counts including zeros, which drove the estimate to zero for the same reason.
Both were caught by the tests, not by reasoning.
