## THIS ROUND: the measurement that closes the autopilot question, and a designed-not-applied instrument

**Measured and recorded (L2139, D2093):**

 AUTOPILOT_INTERVAL_SEC = 300 a completed run ~480 s an abandoned run 600 s
 the 600 s timeout fired at load 1.4 -> NOT a load problem
 work sessions in 6 h = 110 ticks in 6 h = 237
 total tick time in 6 h = 8,422 s autopilot run_count in the same 6 h = 2

**`run_once` is a wrapper, not the engine.** 110 sessions of real work against 2 loop completions means
the work is driven by the reminder poll, the work-queue poll and APScheduler jobs on their own threads.
Two consequences: the configured interval is shorter than the run it schedules (an impossible cadence,
so every slow tick is abandoned), and scaling `run_once` would not scale the company.

**D2093 recommends raising the interval 300 -> 900 as a measurement step**, not a fix: it is the only
option immediately verifiable, it costs little because the work is not driven by the loop, and it is one
reversible env var. **Not applied**, because the interval governs how fast the company reacts to external
events and my reasoning that it does not (the event path has its own trigger and gap) is an inference
from reading the code, not a measurement. That is the owner''s call and it is queued.

**D336 designs the timing instrument** that D2093 is blocked on: `_subsystem_health` already records
`last_ran_at` per subsystem and only lacks a duration; add `last_duration_sec` at the `setdefault`
(autopilot.py:1191), a run-local `_stage_secs` dict, a `_timed` context manager, and wrap the eight
guarded subsystem blocks at their known start lines (world_context 4665, company_tick ~4821, ceo_tick
~5642, plus work_mode, healing, self_prog, evolution, llm_checkin). Persist into the returned dict and
the state file. **Deliberately not applied this round:** eight edits in the file whose failure mode is
"the company stops working", at the end of a long session, is the pattern that produced two near-misses
of mine today.
