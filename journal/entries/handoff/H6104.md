## 2026-10-15 15:00 UTC - ZABZ-YOGA - House internet: the stack is innocent, the phones are on 2.4 GHz, and the fiber is unplugged

Picks up from H4680 earlier the same morning. That entry ended with the stack measured and blamed for four config defects. This one corrects two of its conclusions and adds the finding that actually answers the owner's question.

OWNER ASK, 2026-10-15: "the Verizon router after the [T-Mobile] one causes problems and makes the Internet slow. Right now, the Internet slows. I want to know if it's related to that... take full measurements of the current state of the Internet, and then pull up all those sessions and analyze this and document it."

ANSWER, measured, in one line each:
- The router stack is NOT the slowdown: 0-1 ms to cross into the T-Mobile hop, 0% loss in ~250 pings, 269 Mbps aggregate, better than the night before.
- The owner's phone is on the 2.4 GHz radio at 11-26 Mbps PHY (RSSI -67/-69) while the same router serves 2268 Mbps on 6 GHz to the laptop. That is what "slow" means from his hand. Not the stack, not the uplink.
- The house's uplink is the T-Mobile cellular gateway (CHR30A WAN 192.168.12.245, gw 192.168.12.1) while Verizon Fios 300 was installed at this address on 2026-09-14 and bills 30 a month. Single-flow cellular swings 44-122 Mbps minute to minute; fiber would remove that, plus the missing IPv6 prefix, plus the 1436 MTU, plus the double NAT, in one cable.

Recorded as L4644-level correction (see the appended lesson citing L4641).
