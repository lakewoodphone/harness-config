## 2026-09-24 23:14 UTC · ZABZ-TECH · Shlock 2026-09-24 night: second fleet done - loose pocket rope dead, stays dead, the tied-rope drive is the design; the real water curb is the hanger stack; cover off before Friday sunset

 does the hem drain, the pocket rope as a pull member, the routing and equalisation, the cycle with a soft front
 edge, every job the pole had, what the trades do, and a kill-shot on the pocket rope. All seven delivered.
- Wrote `lpt-hub/research/miscellaneous/2026-09-24-shlock-drive-and-no-pole-audit.md`; journal D2587 (the pole-back
 decision) is superseded the same evening.

WHAT THE SECOND FLEET SETTLED
- The loose rope in the pocket is dead, twice over and independently: whole-length friction is 0.3-0.9 lb against
 ~17.5 lb per mouth, so it slides; the load then lands on stopper knots on an unreinforced pocket mouth (two layers of
 18 oz VCP, stitched hem, no grommet, no webbing, no patch) and the knot is smaller than the mouth slot.
- A STAY in the pocket is dead too - the owner's own argument and it is correct: any member stiff enough to straighten
 a 28.5-ft edge is a dam (the 1-3/8 in pipe = 17.7 in of backwater, 1/2 in PEX = 8 in). Stiffness makes a dam;
 tension makes a drive.
- The design that survives: a 3/8-in rope through the empty pocket, TIED to the pocket every 3 ft (ten zip ties or
 stitches), bolted through at both mouths, pulled from both ends with one line per direction and the bight in the
 operator's hand. The ties carry the tension into the cloth, so friction is not asked to do anything; the rope has no
 bending stiffness, so the hem still dips and the drainage is unchanged; the bolts stop it walking out. Water-neutral:
 the rope is a 0.375 in ridge, sitting below the hanger stack that already dams 16.5 in.
- Routing validated (Y3): all four legs within 1.6 deg of the rail axis, no line across the cover; two independent
 lines rack, one line with the bight equalises by construction; yard wheels 3 in outboard; a redirect at the house
 beam (the drop steepens to 72 deg at full retract); bolt terminals, not knots; ~40 lb dry on the scale.
- The pole's real loss (Y5, Y4): it was the equaliser for the 8 sleeves and the rigid member a tow pulls on. With it
 out, nothing keeps the 8 stations in phase; a cocked sleeve that jams sends the pull into the fabric and tears a
 grommet. Cheapest guard: thin UHMW liners (0.170 in of play down to 0.045 in).
- The water: the real curb is the HANGER STACK, 1.29 in = 16.5 in of backwater, not the pole - and the record says the
 poles were never in when the 0.79-in pool was measured. The hem's own sag (0.45-0.80 in) only shrinks the pool from
 ~50 lb to ~7-25 lb. The fix that matters is lowering the hanger stack (sockets to 6 o'clock, a 4.07 in swing, no
 parts) on a dry day with two people.
