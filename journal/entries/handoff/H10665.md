I SET OUT TO WRITE TESTS, AND THE TESTS IMMEDIATELY EXPOSED A BUG IN MY OWN INSTRUMENT.
 Six admin_actions routes had no test: GET/PUT/DELETE feature flags for a tenant and for a household. Wrote server/tests/test_admin_feature_flags_api.py - round trip through storage, second DELETE succeeds and leaves defaults, PUT {} clears rather than ignores, unknown flag refused and nothing stored, 401 without credentials, 404 for an unknown tenant. 6 passed.
 AND AFTERWARDS THE PAGE STILL SAID "never named" FOR ALL SIX. Cause: re.sub(r"\{[^}]*\}", "", path) left a DOUBLED SLASH, so /admin/api/tenants/{tenant_id}/feature_flags became //feature_flags and no test writes that. **Every parameterised route in the entire application was counted as named by nothing.**
 CORRECTED: 328 of 364 named, 36 not - not 239/125. The operator dashboard is 87 of 92, not 77 of 113. The 125 I quoted in STATUS and in four journal entries was a measurement artefact, and the "eleven capabilities with an untested endpoint" conclusion was built on it.

WHAT THIS COSTS AND WHAT IT TEACHES: a wrong instrument does not look wrong, it looks like a finding. I spent four rounds treating 125 as a real, actionable number and wrote a "by capability" table to make it more actionable. The tell was available the whole time - a test that names /health but not /health/db, a dozen routes at exactly 0% - and what finally surfaced it was writing a test and watching the instrument refuse to see it.
 THE GENERAL LESSON, added to the file of them: when you finally touch the thing a metric measures, check that the metric MOVES. A metric that cannot observe a change you just made is broken, and the cheapest test of any measurement is to perturb the thing it claims to measure.

The tests themselves found one more thing worth recording: an operator session left behind by a previous request legitimately authenticates the next one, so "no auth header" is not the same as "unauthenticated". The test clears cookies to assert the real case rather than assuming.
