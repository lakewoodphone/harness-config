### THE COMPANY WEDGES ITSELF — the round's most valuable finding (`L3930`)
`_note_run_once_overrun` (`autopilot.py:7650`) states it: a `run_once` exceeding **600 s** is **abandoned** (*"the worker thread cannot be killed"*) and **keeps holding `_db_lock`**, so the next run is slower and more likely to overrun; after **`_RUN_ONCE_OVERRUN_BREAKER = 3`** consecutive overruns the loop **disables itself and "needs an explicit re-enable"**. Measured: 3 `ABANDONING it` events, `Owner outbox flush failed: database is locked`, `Work step 11 failed: database is locked`, two `run_once timed out after 600s`; the breaker never tripped but the loop was **wedged anyway** (`last_run_at` frozen 44 min, `run_count: 6`).
- **A stuck loop is invisible**: `systemctl is-active` → `active`, `/health` → 200, `enabled` → `True`, and no work for 44 minutes. Only `last_run_at` not advancing shows it, and **nothing watches that**. Remedy today = a restart (two, at 14:17:59 and 14:41).
- **This corrects an earlier attribution**: the idle hours are **partly the budget cap and partly this wedge** — two different stoppages I had collapsed into one. The 71 `/health` probe timeouts (32 in the noon hour) and every `database is locked` are downstream of abandoned threads holding the lock.
- **Child 49 is building the fix** (cooperative cancellation + observability + bounded recovery). Check `/tmp/evolution-v3/wedge-progress.md` before assuming it ran.
