### The five things that matter, in order
1. **The page is sequenced behind the siren loop.** `initial_response` calls `script.phoenix_intrusion_loop`
 as a blocking service step and pages in the step *after* it, so the page cannot be attempted until the
 alarm ends. Measured: loop started 23:23:38, killed at the 10,000-iteration cap 02:14:22, and
 `evidence_and_page` started in that same second — a page attempt 2 h 51 m after the intrusion.
2. **My own condition fix silenced the page.** `:180-182` aborts before the page call at `:209`. On the exact
 case it was written for, the result is no siren AND no page. The page must be moved above the gate.
3. **The retry loop runs backwards**, and my verify fix is what let it execute for the first time:
 `repeat: count: 4` + `stop` on failure means max 2 attempts and the fail-loud block is dead code (verified
 in script.py: `_StopScript` → `finished`, re-raised from sub-scripts); on *success* there is no break, so
 up to 5 pages per recipient.
4. **The siren is a latch with no timeout**: `POST /alarm/on` to an unauthenticated listener, no expiry anywhere,
 and the only `/alarm/off` caller is `intrusion_reset` reached by the disarm. Between 23:23:38 and 11:25:25
 nothing asked it to stop — 12 h 01 m, of which 9 h 11 m had the loop already dead.
5. **Nothing pages in practice**: the backend journal (12 days, `company-api.service`) holds exactly two
 `security-alert` POSTs, both mine today. Zero intrusion pages ever.
