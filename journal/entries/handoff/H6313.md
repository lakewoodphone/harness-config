## 2026-10-16 03:27 UTC · ZABZ-YOGA · Data-plan audit: Telnyx's ladder is progressive per cycle (we really pay 0.072-0.077/MB), the plan turns positive at 4-5 devices not 20, a real driver uses 100-300 MB, and the cheapest legal option is a volume quote - committed as waze-fleet 30b7fae

## What this was

The owner asked for the data plan to be audited properly and redesigned around "the cheapest option that
works well and we still make money", then documented, committed and pushed.

## The four findings that matter (all measured this session)

1. **The billing ladder is PROGRESSIVE inside the billing cycle, not a flat pooled rate.** Telnyx's own
 page: *"the account starts at Tier 1 for its first 100 MB"*. The carrier's per-record data confirms it:
 `data_rate` is 0.078/MB from the 1st of the month until the account clears 100 MB, then 0.039/MB — and
 the rate changes **by date, not by SIM or network** (both rates appear across AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon
 records). **September 2026: 196.07 MB billed, 14.11 USD, effective 0.07195/MB. October to the 16th:
 118.15 MB, 9.05 USD, effective 0.07661/MB.** Our `pricing-billing.md` §3 assumed the flat version, which
 predicted 7.65 for September.
2. **"Profitable past roughly 20 devices" is wrong.** The boundaries are 100 MB / 500 MB / 2 GB / 5 GB of
 *account* volume, so a 100–300 MB user turns the account positive at **4–5 devices**. Cost per device:
 11.75 / 7.65 / 6.29 / 5.16 at 1 / 5 / 10 / 25 devices (progressive ladder + 2.00 SIM fee).
3. **A real driver uses 100–300 MB/month, not 0.5–1.5 GB.** Measured driver reports (~99–125 MB/month;
 a 400-van fleet with 240 vans under 500 KB/month and 160 outliers up to 14 GB) plus **our own DRN 2002
 at 194.88 MB in September and 107.70 MB in October**. The 20–40 MB/hour figure in our August research
 came from vendor marketing pages, not measurement. **So the 250 MB allowance is correctly sized** — it
 was the pilot-loss arithmetic and the ladder model that were wrong, not the plan.
4. **At the 100% cap the carrier DISABLES the SIM** (Telnyx's own support page). A capped device keeps
 guiding only on an already-started route; a new destination, search and traffic all stop, and Waze has
 no downloadable offline maps. So the cap is a silent mid-drive failure — **and it is plausibly how
 DRN 2001's SIM came to be stuck in `disabling` since 2026-10-13.** Telnyx supports 80% notifications by
 SMS/voice/email/**webhook**; that plus the portal's existing cap-raise path is the fix.

## The cheapest legal option

- **Hologram: flat 0.03/MB + 1.00/SIM = 5.50/device at 150 MB — cheaper than Telnyx at every fleet size
 we will reach this year** (vs 6.29 at ten, 5.16 at twenty-five). **1NCE** is cheaper still for a light
 user (14.00 per 500 MB valid ten years ≈ 0.028/MB) but is the wrong shape and its terms are unchecked.
- **Both are blocked on paperwork, not price:** Hologram's terms prohibit reselling connectivity without
 written consent; **Telnyx's §18.1 expressly contemplates a downstream reseller/integrator/end-user
 chain**, which is the only reason today's model is defensible. That is a one-email question.
- **Consumer plans are out on terms before price:** T-Mobile bans "unattended use … automated
 machine-to-machine connections" and reselling; US Mobile, Tello, Google Fi and Helium all ban resale or
 activating service for others; and every cheap one includes voice, which the product forbids.
- **The biggest lever available today is a written volume quote from Telnyx** — their published ladder
 bottoms at 0.0125/MB and the only commitment paths are "Custom" and a 5,000/month enterprise minimum.
 At 0.0125 the typical device costs **3.88 instead of 6.29** — about 38% off the largest variable cost.

## Traps worth carrying forward

- **`GET /v2/invoices` and `/v2/usage_reports` both 404 on this account** — `detail_records` is the *only*
 billing-grade source, and it carries `data_cost` and `data_rate` per record. The per-SIM MRC exists only
 on the console invoice. (This contradicts `2026-09-22-launch-plan.md` §2's "there is no billing-grade
 usage source" — there is, and nobody had paged it.)
- **The Telnyx API rate-limits hard**: after two big scans in a row, 26 of 41 pages returned HTTP 429.
 A month of records is ~41 pages; sleep between pages and expect to re-run.
- **Nested quoting keeps biting**: a PowerShell parse error aborts the *entire* command, so an `scp` in the
 same call silently never runs (it cost one un-landed cross-link here). Put remote work in a `.sh`/`.ps1`,
 scp it, run it.
- **A Python `%`-format on a URL containing `%5B`** raises `ValueError: unsupported format character` — use
 an f-string.
