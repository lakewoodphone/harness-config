## 2026-10-14 16:19 UTC · ZABZ-TECH · Portable SSDs for customer handoff media: answered - portables approved for customer media, fused-interface rule NARROWED not deleted; bare+enclosure loses on price at every capacity since D2085; SanDisk case is 3:23-cv-04152 with NO settlement

PORTABLE SSDs FOR CUSTOMER HANDOFF MEDIA — answered 2026-10-14, five mesh streams, ~55 min.

THE ANSWER. For CUSTOMER-facing media the portable wins on price AND the fused-interface risk is acceptable —
but the rule is NARROWED, not deleted: portables for customer handoff, bare-2.5in-in-enclosure stays for
shop-owned bench/lab media (D2085 unchanged).

PRICE HAS INVERTED SINCE D2085 (one month). D2085 bought 870 EVO 2TB at 499.99 from B&H on 2026-09-17; on
2026-10-14 the same drive is 879.99 direct from Samsung (+76%), against a Samsung T7 2TB portable at 488.99.
Bare+enclosure = 894.93 at 2TB (was the cheap route). At 1TB the portable wins by 73.04 (B&H) to 154.94
(Samsung direct). DIY loses at every capacity now; the bare-drive case is serviceability ONLY.

SPEED IS A WASH because the bench is the port: both routes saturate 10 Gbps. Best-evidenced portable for this
exact workload = Samsung T9 1TB, 560 MB/s FLAT over a 400 GB folder write, no cliff (everythingusb, re-verified
by me 2026-10-14).

THE FUSED-RISK ASYMMETRY IS THE WHOLE ANSWER. Bench drive dies = unrecoverable shop asset (D2085's fear).
Customer drive dies = a re-copy off the master we already keep, bounded by the existing verify step. So the risk
is priced differently for the two uses and should be.

NEW DESIGN RULE FOUND: prefer BRIDGED over NATIVE-USB. Bridged (Samsung T7/T7 Shield/T9, SanDisk Extreme V2)
= own SSD controller + discrete USB bridge = a lab has something to work with. Native-USB single-chip
(Kingston XS1000/XS2000, Crucial X9/X9 Pro/X10 Pro, Lexar SL500 — all SMI SM2320/SM2322) = chip-off yields
CIPHERTEXT and Rossmann states it does NOT offer in-lab SM2320 recovery at all. That single distinction
disposes of most of the cheap end.
