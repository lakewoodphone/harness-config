## 2026-10-21 13:20 UTC · SECRATARY-CLOUD · Addendum to H12457: the judge was reading snippets, not bodies — its corpus path never existed

## 2026-10-21 · secratary-cloud · Addendum to H12457: the corpus was never read, and that is why the LTRAP miss was even possible

Written after H12457 in the same session, because H12457 understated the damage. Read this with it.

**The fifth defect, and the worst one.** H12457 says the sweep's spam exclusion was a gate. It was.
But behind it sat a gate nobody had noticed: **the owner-attention judge was not reading the sweep
corpus at all.** `from_email_catalog()` resolved it as
`ART.parent.parent.parent / "email-sweep" / "accounts"`, and with
`ART = /opt/company/data/owner-attention` three parents up is `/opt` — so it looked in
`/opt/email-sweep/accounts`, which **does not exist**. The `/tmp/email-sweep/accounts` fallback does
not exist either. Both `is_dir()` checks therefore failed on **every run since the sweep was
installed**, and control fell silently through to `email_catalog` — the ~100-character snippet
table that the same function's docstring spends a paragraph explaining is not good enough.

Measured 2026-10-21: the corpus is real and populated — **1,772 rows across 5 mailboxes, 189 of them
rescued from spam** — and the judge was reading none of it. The 2026-10-13 "the body decides" fix was
written, documented, and never once took effect.

**Consequence for the lesson.** The spam exclusion still mattered (a message in spam is fetched by
neither source), but I should not have trusted a fallback I had not proved was the fallback. The
general shape: *two silent failures compose into a disappearance, and each one alone looks like
health.* When a function offers a degraded fallback, prove which branch ran — print the resolved path,
do not infer it from the fact that it returned something.

**What changed since H12457** (both committed in `/opt/company`, `cff057a05`):

1. The single path guess became a candidate chain: env override `LPT_EMAIL_SWEEP_ACCOUNTS`, then the
 old arithmetic, then `<app>/accounting-data/...`, then `/root/accounting-data/...`, then
 `/home/zabz/accounting-data/...`, then `/tmp`. First existing directory wins.
2. A precision guard for what the newly-read corpus brought with it. A generic deadline word
 (`expires`, `refund`, `legal`) may no longer promote a message the classifier has already called
 marketing noise — **unless** it states an application/enrollment window, which is the exact form
 the LTRAP notice took. Measured over 1,772 rows: 49 were high under the old rule, 12 of them
 marketing (car dealers, Rakuten, MGM Rewards); the guard demotes exactly those 12 and leaves the
 notice high.

**Verified after the change**, on the production corpus at the real path:
`owner_attention.py build --days 3 --top 15` renders the notice as item 18, first in
EMAIL THAT WANTS AN ANSWER, at `[high]`; and MONEY AND FILINGS is populated for the first time
(items 12-17, six overdue obligations).

**Known-open, measured, deliberately not fixed tonight:** the remaining EMAIL noise is the
classifier, not the deadline vocabulary. `classify_message()` returns
`category='review', needs_owner=1, confidence=0.4, reason='unclassified: no rule matched'` for
unknown commercial senders (Back Market, Betckey), so they arrive as `medium` regardless of content.
That is a classifier-quality problem worth its own pass; do not "fix" it by deleting deadline words,
which would demote real money and legal items.
