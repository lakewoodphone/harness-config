**THE DESIGN CHANGED THREE TIMES, each on evidence, and the record says so.** The first plan was
Bluelink + an OBD dongle. Research then found Hyundai **TSB 24-GI-011H** — aftermarket DLC devices cause
battery drain, CAN errors and false codes, and damage from them is not warranty-covered — which pushed
the design to strict listen-only. A second, sharper correction showed the bulletin blames a device that
**polls continuously and holds the bus awake**, not one sending a few frames per drive. Final design:
**silent while parked (transceiver power-cut, no electrical connection to the bus at all), and a small,
fixed, counted request set while the engine runs.**

**Two corrections that materially improved it:**
- **The odometer is standard OBD-II Mode 01 PID `0xA6`** — CARB-mandated on US cars from MY2019.
 `(A·2²⁴+B·2¹⁶+C·2⁸+D)/10` km. One frame, no ISO-TP, no DID guessing. Confirmed against real MY2022
 Elantra captures. Cross-checked by the passive broadcast on CAN `0x5B0` (`CLU12`, 0.1 km — the only
 zero-traffic route, and the one that works while parked) and by Mode 22 UDS. Speed-integration is
 demoted to a tagged fallback.
- **Per-cylinder misfire counters are readable, from Mode 21** (`21 0D`/`21 1D` on `7E0`), not Mode 06.
 Misfires appear before the P030x code, and one cylinder outrunning the others localises the fault.
