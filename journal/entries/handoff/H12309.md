### 1144 — the register could list states and not define them

`docs/BYPASS-TRIAGE.md` named `open` / `closed` / `cannot-close` in its field table and never said what
any of them required, so it could answer *"what have we closed?"* and not *"what are we knowingly living
with?"* — the second half of the reason the owner asked for the process.

Added a table that defines each state by the **evidence a row must carry**:

- **`open`** — the method as reported; a `pending` bench test is allowed; **time alone moves nothing**.
- **`closed`** — the named test exists **and failed before the fix**, plus the commit and the date the
 customer was told; a release that reopens the method returns the row to `open`.
- **`cannot-close`** — reason, residual risk in plain words, a test pinning the residual behaviour,
 **and a dated review**. Without all four it is `open`, not `cannot-close`.

**B-003 is the row that proves the distinction** and was already there: the screen recorder as an
accepted hole (A-P4-08 settlement c) sitting in `open` with a named test and an **open disclosure debt**.
The new rules say it belongs in `cannot-close` once it carries the one thing it still lacks — a dated
review — and the disclosure debt is why that review cannot be deferred forever. Also corrected a sentence
that said "the two rows above" when the register has carried three since B-003 landed.
