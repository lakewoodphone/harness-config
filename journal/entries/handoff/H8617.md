## The measurement that undoes most of the confidence in this program

Every acceptance question in these twenty-four rounds was chosen by me, and they were all
keyword-shaped: "invoice template", "water damage motherboard", "mesh broker move". They answered in
15–250 ms and the suite stayed green for weeks.

This round mined **the owner's own questions** out of his sessions — question-shaped `user/message`
events, run verbatim:

 17.5 s · 28.9 s · 31.1 s · 34.9 s · 36.5 s · 42.0 s · 46.9 s · 49.5 s

**The queries he actually types took up to fifty seconds.** A twenty-word sentence ANDs into a term set
that nothing satisfies; the core exhausts its FTS rungs, falls through to a scan, and the answer comes
back eventually. Nothing was broken in any way the health check or the acceptance suite could see,
because neither ever asked a question phrased like a person.

## The fix

The question is normalised before the index is asked: stopwords and contractions removed, reduced to
**at most three** significant terms, **original order preserved**. Measured after:

 1.2 s · 1.6 s · 2.0 s · 2.5 s · 3.1 s · 5.1 s · 6.5 s · 9.0 s (median 2.8 s, worst 9.0 s)

Two details that took a measurement each:

- **Three terms, not eight.** Normalising to eight still took 40 s: an eight-way AND is as unsatisfiable
 as a twenty-way one against 2.67 million event rows.
- **Order preserved, not sorted by length.** Sorting by length turned "water damage motherboard" into
 "motherboard damage water", lost phrase proximity, and made a plain keyword query three times slower.

Keyword acceptance is unchanged: 8/8, 186–649 ms.

## What this says about the last three weeks of green tests

A test set chosen by the author measures the author. The failure was not in the index, the timers, the
alarms or the freshness model — all of which were real work and all of which held. It was that **nobody
had ever asked the system a question in the shape the owner actually uses**, and every "8/8, worst
836 ms" I reported should be read with that in mind. The honest version of those reports is: *the
system is fast at the queries I chose.*

The response now carries `searched` and `query_normalised`, so no caller has to guess what was asked of
the index on their behalf.
