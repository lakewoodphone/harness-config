## 2026-10-20 19:18 UTC · ZABZ-TECH · round 29: three state-file checker designs falsified by measurement; the search is closed in writing

# H (2026-10-20, round 29) - the search for a state-file checker is closed on measurement, not on frustration

## The third design, and why it also fails
Traceability: "every `remaining` entry must name a ledger item that exists and is non-terminal". Measured both ways
before writing a line of it:

| variant | live fleet | defect backup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| loose (any entry names any non-terminal id) | 3 fires - lpt-website 15/23 entries untraceable, personality-system 1/4, housekeeping 3/9 | fires (6/11) |
| tight (`work_remaining` true => an entry names a SAME-PROJECT non-terminal id) | **0 fires** | **0 fires** |

The tight form is the one that would be sound, and it cannot see the defect, because the stale prose happens to
mention "Item 120", which is genuinely non-terminal. The defect was **prose about another document's state** - "section 7
still has 23 unchecked rows" - and no ledger-derived invariant can see that.

So: three designs, all falsified by measurement (row counts, file freshness, claim traceability). The search is closed,
and closed in a way a future session can trust rather than re-open: **written into the audit page and into ledger 820.**

## Merged: PR #85 -> `b1636e8`
The audit page said the state file "has no machine-checked owner" and called it "the next place to build one". That
invitation would have cost a future session a round, so it now carries the four measured results and says what actually
governs the file: the shift contract (PR #83) requires rebuilding `remaining` from the ledger, and the remaining lever is
the mechanism one - make the loop not depend on the file at all, which is the owner's decision.

## What this round actually bought
Not code. It bought the elimination of a plausible-looking line of work, with numbers, and the correction of a page that
was inviting somebody to redo it. That is worth more than a fourth monitor, because the fourth monitor would have been
written, tested against the example, shipped, and then misfired on the fleet - which is precisely what rounds 25 and 26
did twice.

## The rule I would carry from three failures
**Test a proposed detector against the cases you want it to ignore before the case you want it to catch.** Both of my
failures were caught by the fleet, not by the defect: the defect made each design look right.
