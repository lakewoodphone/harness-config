## THE BACKOFF-VS-ESCALATION DEFECT. FIXED AND PROVEN.

THE DEFECT. `due_only=True` selects rows whose `next_attempt_at <= now`, and every refusal
pushes `next_attempt_at` further out. So a row refused often enough to EARN an escalation was,
by that same history, excluded from every pass that could have GIVEN it one. The escalation was
decorative on exactly the rows it existed for -- 13 rows with 9-20 attempts each, all with a
future `next_attempt_at`, none ever escalated.

THE FIX. Two predicates instead of one filter: a row that is DUE is a retry; a row that is NOT
due but DESERVES escalation is a first real attempt at something that has already waited past
its threshold. `_owner_message_due()` is the retry half, `_owner_message_deserves_escalation()`
the other, and the selection takes `due or escalation`.

PROVEN AGAINST THE LIVE STORE with a planted row (70 h old, 12 refusals, next_attempt_at +6h):
 in the OLD selection (due_only): False <- the defect
 in the NEW selection: True
 escalation predicate: True
The row was removed afterwards. Separately proven: a row that is NOT escalatable and has a
future backoff stays excluded, so this did not become a way to bypass the gate.

THE OTHER HALF, VERIFIED RATHER THAN ASSUMED. A permanent refusal must TERMINATE, not be
re-held forever. Planted a machine-noise row with 14 refusals and a future backoff:
`reconcile_owner_message_queue` expired it with the written reason "held longer than the stated
maximum age of 72h". So the no-limbo guarantee holds, and it is a plant-and-observe proof, not
a docstring.
