**Two findings worth keeping.**

1. **The capacity margin was 42 MB, not the 1.34 GiB I predicted — my estimate was ~1.4 GB optimistic and I
 do not yet know why.** The arithmetic did cover cluster slack exactly (real per-file sizes against this
 medium's 128 KiB clusters), so something else costs ~1.4 GB across 64k files. **The transferable lesson
 is the margin, not the mechanism: do not plan a delivery to within 2% of a medium's capacity.** It fit,
 barely, and "barely" was luck rather than judgement.
2. **The house verify remounts the medium READ-ONLY (that is how it forces and proves the flush), and my
 own finisher then tried to copy `CHECKSUMS-SHA256.txt` onto that read-only mount.** It would have
 printed a PASS and left the stick with **no checksums file** — precisely the failure
 `recovered-data-delivery-to-customer-media.md` §8.8 names: *"a PASS in a log is not a finished delivery
 — look at the MEDIUM"*. Fixed by arming a **second, separate detached step**
 (`/tmp/finish-checksums.sh` → `logs/stick-checksums.log`) that waits for the first finisher to exit,
 **refuses to write checksums unless a PASS line is present**, remounts read-write, writes the file,
 syncs, remounts read-only, and then proves it by listing the medium.

**Verify timing, and a rate correction I made on myself.** A 60-second sample said the stick read at
5.3 MB/s and that the verify would need 12 hours. **Four minutes of measurement said 29.3 MB/s → about 2.2
hours** (source reading at 56.4 MB/s, load 2.7 on 12 CPUs, so not CPU-bound). The 60-second window was
unrepresentative — the same trap the delivery doc documents, demonstrated here.
