### THE THREE FINDINGS THAT MATTER MORE THAN THE 115 CHANGES

1. **`READY_FOR_PICKUP` is terminal, and `lpt audit` does not know it.** The live `OrderStatus` enum
 carries seven labels and **no `COMPLETED`** (`select 'COMPLETED'::"OrderStatus"` → invalid input).
 On 2026-10-07 the owner settled the seven-state design and the `order_status_seven_states`
 migration mapped `COMPLETED` → `READY_FOR_PICKUP`; the deployed router carries the alias table and
 the comment says it in his words, *"a device is paid for before it goes on the shelf and that IS
 the completed state."* So the **425 orders in `READY_FOR_PICKUP` are finished, not stuck**, and
 `lpt audit`'s N4 — which reports *"ready for pickup, never collected"* — is two days stale and
 accounts for **400 of its 660 N4 findings**. Measured: full audit = **1,077 findings, 725 high**,
 of which 600 are that rule plus *"open and untouched"* on terminal rows.
2. **`communications` stores every message about fourteen times.** Measured: **116,675 rows on
 customer files hold 8,361 distinct (customer, channel, direction, text)**; one customer has
 **10,049 rows for 44 messages**. From 2026-07-10 the duplicate blocks carry **import timestamps,
 not send times**. This is not cosmetic: the first pilot batch concluded a customer had been
 ignored — *"no reply is recorded"* — on an order whose log holds the reply verbatim
 (`we were not successful I don't think its fixable. you can come pick it up, sorry.`, message
 28133). Rule 9 of the analyst contract and the `hold` on any note that rests on ordering both
 come from that one error; a second misread (order 299, "still waiting for an answer") was caught
 the same way, by reading the deduplicated log by hand.
3. **A customer's full card number is in the log in plain text.** **187 message rows across 9
 customer files** match a card number as a customer types it (4×4 grouped, or 16-19 digits),
 first seen 2026-05-20, last 2026-09-20. Same exposure as queue row #309, with a fresh count. The
 tool **cannot remove it and does not claim to**; it scrubs every analyst verdict, refuses to write
 a note carrying one, and `lpt-sweep scan` counts without printing. Fifteen digits is deliberately
 **not** a match — that is an IMEI, and redacting one destroys real information.
