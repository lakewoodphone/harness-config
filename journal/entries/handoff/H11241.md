# Closed out 147 stale pickups: the rule was skipping the orders it was built for

Owner, this session: *"we just start going through work orders one at a time. Now, I want to close
out like the oldest ones."*

The work was already specified — he set the 50-day pickup rule today at 14:19Z — so the job was not
a manual walk of 314 orders but finding why his rule had stopped short. See `L11134`.

## What was wrong

The rule ran at 14:31 and closed 105. Every run after it said `no order qualifies` while 314 orders
still sat at `READY_FOR_PICKUP`, oldest 300 days. The selector's "no recent activity" condition
counted **any** `customer_notes` row, and our own 2026-10-14 reconciliation job had written notes on
243 of those orders (254 `PAYMENT RECONCILIATION` + 74 corrections + 16 `CLOSED OUT`, plus 63
imported payment rows). Each note restarted the 50-day clock. `authorId` could not separate them —
that job files under the owner's own account (user 100).

## What I changed and proved

- `backend/src/services/work-orders/auto-close.service.ts`: `BOOKKEEPING_NOTE_PREFIXES` names the
 opening markers of notes we write to ourselves; the selector gates on **customer-side** activity
 and returns both clocks (`lastActivityAt`, `lastCustomerActivityAt`, `heldOpenByBookkeeping`); the
 auto-close note now says when the newest record is our own bookkeeping.
- `scripts/prod/auto-close-stale-pickup.ts`: the console line prints the customer-activity date and
 flags a bookkeeping-held order, instead of printing our own note's date as "last activity".
- Tests: 6/6 pass, including the two new cases. `tsc --noEmit` clean for these files.
- Committed `e4dba19d1` and **pushed** to `lpt/auto-close-50-day-20261020`.
