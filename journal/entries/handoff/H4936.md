## 2026-10-15 · SECRATARY-CLOUD · The Waze device business now has one source of truth, and three of its own numbers were wrong

**Objective given:** *"I need one source of truth that contains all the wisdom, scripts, documents, info,
clearly organized, audited, researched in one place"* — for the DRN fleet, the LPT fleet, the shop fleet,
all the profiles, all the CLI, all the devices, and four research tracks (voice-to-text, the multi-network
SIMs, iOS 27 MDM exposure, and the camera device). He expected it to take the whole day; it did.

**Built:** `/root/code/waze-fleet/` — a git repo, commit `015d4b2`, 58 tracked files, ~1 MB.
Synced and verified on `ZABZ-TECH` at `C:\Users\ezabz\Code\waze-fleet` (57/57 markdown files).
Entry point `00-START-HERE.md`; the rule that makes it different from its predecessor is `AUTHORITY.md`.

### The four things that matter most, all measured

1. **The three fleets are not what their names suggest.** `fleet_devices.site` separates them. **DRN =
 the Waze Gemach** — a free-loan desk, 63 rows, no money changes hands. **LPT = 6 kiosks** (DRN 2002,
 2004–2008), the product we sell. **SHOP = 2 counter devices (DRN 2001 and 2003, an iPad)**, which live
 *inside* `site=lpt` and have no site of their own — which is why the shop fleet kept going missing.
 71 rows cover **70 physical phones**; DRN 21 and 41 are one iPhone.
2. **The DRN fleet is silent and the LPT fleet is not.** LPT 8/8 reporting within 7 days; `lakewood` (DRN)
 **3 of 59**, with **34 stale beyond 30 days**. This was known only as a series of alarms before.
3. **The lock-down profile on the fleet is not the one in git.** 28 profiles served by `waze-mdm-01`,
 13 in the repo, **15 in no repository at all**, **11 sharing one `PayloadIdentifier`**, and
 `waze-layered-kiosk.mobileconfig` itself deployed with different bytes than git holds. R2 then found a
 **third copy on Windows** holding content that exists nowhere else — including **the Telnyx APN profile
 in three versions**, the one that decides whether a SIM gets data.
4. **iOS 27 does not take our controls away.** 195-key union against Apple's schema at `Release-v27.0`:
 0 removed, 30 deprecated. The dated change is a different one (`legacy software update management`
 stopped at 27.0) and we carry none of those keys. `L3937`'s "every key is on Apple's deprecation path"
 overstates it — corrected in `L4769`.

### What I learned that I did not know at the start

- **`subagent` (mesh) and `subagent_local` are not interchangeable.** `subagent` ran on `ZABZ-TECH`; it
 reported `/root/authority.db` as **0 bytes** — a Windows path remap (`/root/x` → `C:\root\x`) that made
 a file it had itself created look like the company's truncated database. A silent fabrication with a
 byte count and a timestamp attached. Rule: partition work by where the data is, and **a mesh child's
 negative about an authority path is not evidence.** Written up in `L4769`.
- **The DSH corpus is 34 days deep, not months.** 8,586 sessions, oldest 2026-09-11. The months live in
 the journal and the Copilot archive — and the Copilot archive in `authority.db` holds **only the owner's
 half** (all 4,373 rows are `role='user'`), while 808 on-disk sessions were never ingested and the DB
 stops 2026-09-18. **The last month of chat from the primary machine is in neither place.**
- **A rule that produces a document is not a rule that produces a maintained document.** He asked for
 this once before: `lpt-master-knowledge-2026-06-29.md`, self-described as *"Single source of truth for
 all LPT operations"*, archived three weeks later and never updated — which is why the same questions were
 being re-derived in October. This tree supersedes it and says so.

### What is now different, and what is next

- Different: there is one place, it is audited, and it carries its sources. Every document in it names
 which fleet it is about, from live reads rather than memory.
- Next, in order of value: (1) **fold the served profiles into git before any rebuild** — a rebuild from
 `master` today silently reverts the live lock-down profile, and L3 found it would also revert the
 customer-billing route (`/waze/billing/usage`) and the data-roaming fix, which exist in production and
 in no branch; (2) persist installed-profile state, because `device_profiles` has **0 rows** and
 everything is reconstructed from a 30-day log a weekly cron deletes; (3) the one bench test that decides
 voice input.

### What still hurts, and what would fix it

- **Two adversarial audits were necessary and both found real errors** — 38 refuted claims in the
 fleet/profile set, 56 in the research and lessons set, including two in my own `MANAGER-CHECKS.md` and
 one in `00-START-HERE.md`. A third of what the workers wrote was confidently wrong in a detail. **The
 fix is that every number in this tree carries its predicate**, which is why the audits could settle
 them at all.
- **The audit is only as good as the reviewer.** Auditor A2's own "19 identifiers" was wrong (17); the
 fixer caught it. Blast radius: if the iOS 27 DoH exposure was ever sized on 21 devices it was sized on
 the wrong control — `sirifilterdns` reaches **57**.
- **A GET endpoint mutates state.** `GET /fleet/devices/{drn}/profiles` **enqueues a command**; an
 audit worker calling it pushed DRN 2004. Nothing installed, but a read-only brief was violated by a
 read. Any brief that says "read-only" must carry that exception.
- **`owner_decision_queue` row #449 was already stale when read** (it said 2 SIMs blocked; measured
 10,597 cents, 6 active, 0 blocked). Resolved with the evidence; the auto-recharge preference re-raised
 as **#519**.
- **Row #510 is the one that can end the product** and is still unanswered: Apple's ABM Agreement
 §2.5 appears to forbid reselling supervised devices, and we sell them to consumers. Documented at
 `15-business/apple-abm-risk.md`; nobody has yet read the clause at source.
