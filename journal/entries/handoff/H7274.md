## TWO THINGS I GOT WRONG, so they are not repeated

1. **I predicted a convenience fee of 37–40 and there was none.** I extrapolated from the four July 2026
 payments, which each carried one (6.67, 7.29, 9.34, 26.21 — 49.51 in total). This payment reads
 **Fee Amount 0.00**. So the fee depends on the payment route and must never be predicted as a fixed
 percentage of the tax. Tell the owner the tax figure and say the fee, if any, appears on the
 confirmation.
2. **The 2.76 residual was not paid** — `Payments and Credits 0.00`. I had told him it might be offered
 and could be added; it wasn't. **It is still outstanding** and needs a separate payment to leave the
 account clean. Small money, but it is the difference between "filed and paid" and "clean".

## STILL UNVERIFIED AT THE TIME OF WRITING — do not claim these

## THE LESSON WORTH KEEPING

The owner asked for a double and triple audit, and the audit changed the answer: **1,700.76 → 1,619.55**,
an 81.21 overstatement, from four differences that reconciled to the penny. The step that found it was
not re-reading the number — it was making the old figure *reconcile* to the new one with no residual.
When two figures disagree, do not pick the more recent or the more confident one; explain the gap exactly,
and if you cannot, trust neither.
