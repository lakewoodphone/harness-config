## 1. The form-submission notifier exists, and it is OFF

- Lives at `/opt/lpt/computer-request-notify/notify.py`, driven by `computer-request-notify.timer`
 (60 s). Branch `lpt/computer-request-notify-20261019`, commit `75f83601c`, **not pushed**.
- It is a poller, not a line in the request handler, and its marker is a new side table
 `computer_request_sms` keyed by `requestId` — one row per submission by construction. `computer_requests`
 itself is untouched: no column, no contract, no price.
- One text per submission to `+17326552355` (the shop's own office line). Claim-before-send; SENT is
 terminal; 4xx is FAILED and retried; 5xx or timeout is UNKNOWN and never auto-retried, because a
 missed text a person can resend beats a second text to a customer.
- **Sending is not enabled anywhere on the test backend**: `COMMUNICATION_PROVIDER` is absent and the
 factory defaults to `noop`, so the normal path would log a fake id and look successful. The notifier
 therefore calls Dialpad directly with the live key, under its own switch
 `COMPUTER_REQUEST_NOTIFY=off` in `/opt/lpt/computer-request-notify/notify.env`.
- **Two things must be settled before the first real send.** First, sender and recipient are the same
 number — whether Dialpad accepts a from==to SMS is untested, and it may thread on the office line or
 reject it. Second, the nine rows in the table are not customers: three browser walks ("DSH Deployed
 Walk", "DSH Final Walk", "Deploy check") and six rate-limit probes, all in the reserved 555-01xx
 range. The rendered bodies are printed and staged; nothing was sent.
- To turn it on: flip the switch to `on`, `systemctl daemon-reload`, `systemctl enable --now
 computer-request-notify.timer`. Backfill of the existing rows is `--backfill --send --only <refs>`
 and sends the stored body byte for byte, so what the owner approved is what goes out.

## 3. The card crash was real and is fixed

Root cause, proven on a copy of the database: `card` gathers the queue with deferred rows **excluded**
and `show` gathers it with them **included**; the read-time collapse then let **deferred decision #646**
("Brian at 300DDR") absorb pending attention **#809** because both titles carried the 300DDR case
reference. `card` selected #809, `show` could not find it, and printed "no pending item with id 809"
and exited 1 — a dead queue tool in front of the owner.

Four fixes, deployed atomically with backups and sha256s recorded in the child's report:
- a deferred or out-of-view row can never suppress a live one (rules A, B and C);
- a multi-member digest is never absorbed whole into a single decision;
- `show` falls back to rendering the pending row itself and says where it went; `card` and `knock`
 print `SKIPPED #id — reason` and move to the next item instead of dying.

And a data-loss fault found on the way: the mail ingest built a digest title from the **first three**
members while the count said N ("7 notices …: Techloq · NEXT · iCloud"). A row that names three of
seven can be declared covered by those three and disappear with the four it never named. It now names
every member up to twelve, then "+N more".
