The guard's author could only test the **fixed-offset fallback** (their host has no tz database)

### And through `_quiet_guard`, the function that actually decides

| condition | verdict |
|---|---|
| no inbound row | **SUPPRESS** — he is asleep |
| he texted 5 minutes ago | **SEND** — *"quiet hours open: he texted us 5 min ago"* |
| two hours later | **SUPPRESS** again |
| urgency `urgent` / `critical` | SEND |
| `class_hint="reply"` | SEND |
| a non-owner recipient | never subject to his hours |

### Is it inert? No — measured

Across 127 recorded attempts: **127 normal / 7 urgent** (5%), and 25 carry `class_hint="reply"`.
Verdicts so far `SEND 82 · DEDUPE 50 · SUPPRESS 2`. The bypass is not the default, which was the
real risk: a guard whose bypass always fires would look installed and do nothing — exactly the
shape of the descriptor rung sitting at 0.80 against a 0.85 bar.

### A correction to my own test, recorded

My first boundary check called `quiet_hours_now()` and reported the awake bypass as **broken**. It
is not. `quiet_hours_now` answers "is this instant inside the window" and nothing else, which is
the right separation; the bypasses live in `_quiet_guard(c, now, urgency, class_hint, to,
subsystem)`, which takes `now` explicitly and can therefore be driven at a simulated 00:35.
`submit()` reads the wall clock, so **no end-to-end test can put the channel at 03:00** — the
decision function is the right seam to test, and it is the seam a future session should use.
