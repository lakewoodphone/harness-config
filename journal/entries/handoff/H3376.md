### BROKEN — the latency, which is the owner's actual criterion
`usearch` cold answers in **15-112 s**; warm in **0.74-1.5 s**. The budget is 5 s.
**Eliminated by measurement, do not re-investigate these:**
- the SQL and its plan — the tool's exact query is **163 ms** warm (join, full projection, `c.text`,
 `bm25` all included); raw `rowid, rank` is **73 ms**;
- the vocabulary lookup in `match_estimate` — `term = ? OR term GLOB ?` is **18 ms**, the GLOB prefix
 **7.5 ms**, and `fts5vocab` seeks fine;
- `count(*) FROM chunks_docsize` — **224 ms**;
- orphaned processes holding the lock — **none present**.
What remains is the cost of cold reads across an **8.53 GB** index (2,082,691 pages x 4096), and the
split between disk I/O and machine contention is **not yet established**.
Two independently true defects make it worse and are still unfixed:
- `usearch.py:1247-1255`: `max_ranked = max(200, int(total_chunks * 0.02))` — **the guard threshold
 scales with the corpus**, so it can never protect a latency budget. At the audit's 273,108 chunks,
 2% was 5,460; at today's 2,884,529 it is 57,690.
- `journal_size_limit` reads **-1 (unbounded)** although `docs/AUDIT.md` states the WAL is bounded.

**Correction to an earlier claim in this session:** I said pruning the scratch trees would shrink the
index and therefore fix the speed. The walk disproves it — the *real* corpus is bigger than what is
indexed (176,679 files now yield, vs ~40,737 non-scratch documents today). Pruning fixes duplicate
**results**; it does **not** by itself fix the cold-query latency. Both are needed.
