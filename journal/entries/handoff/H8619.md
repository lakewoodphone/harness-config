The probe is **9–77 ms either way**. Letting the core discover the same absence by itself costs
seconds, because its ladder runs out of FTS rungs and falls through to a scan. That is the entire fix:
ask the cheap question first, and when the answer is no, say no.

## Why I had thrown it away

One round earlier I built this gate, measured it *together with* a loose fallback I had put behind it,
saw 10.3 s / 5.0 s / 8.4 s, and concluded the gate was a second slow path. It was not — **the fallback
was, and I measured the pair as if it were the gate.** I then wrote `H8553` about exactly this mistake
("run the candidate SQL directly against SQLite and time it, then decide") and walked into it again two
rounds later.

The difference this time was one script that timed each SQL on its own, in one table, across four
corpora. Five minutes of measurement would have saved two rounds of deploying and reverting.
