# 2026-10-14 · ZABZ-YOGA · Round 3: a false rule about money was sitting in the record, and two uncollected repairs are hiding behind it

Goal round 3. State: **`journal/state/work-order-remediation-program-2026-10-14.md`**.

## THE FINDING

`READY_FOR_PICKUP` **means "a device that is paid for and on the shelf"** — so it is an **assertion**,
not a reading, and it is exactly the state uncollected money hides behind.

`H3664` (a concurrent session, 2026-10-14) recorded the opposite as a discovered rule: *"a finished
order sitting in READY_FOR_PICKUP with no payment row is to be treated as collected."* It quoted note
2885 for support. **Note 2885's own action disproves it.** Verbatim:

> The order had been left in READY_FOR_PICKUP, which in this shop means paid and on the shelf. **Status
> moved to AWAITING_PAYMENT so the receivable stays visible.**

The note cites the convention as the reason the row was WRONG — the order looked paid, it was not, so it
was moved out. The session read the convention as licensing the opposite conclusion and stopping.

**The correction, recorded as `L3821`:** not chasing old unverifiable money and "it was paid" are
different statements. The owner's 2026-10-14 ruling (`D3151`) stands on its own reasons and needs no
fiction to support it. Where the record CONTRADICTS the state, the shop's own action is to move the
order to `AWAITING_PAYMENT` so the receivable stays visible.

## WHAT THAT EXPOSED

Scanned every order at `READY_FOR_PICKUP` carrying a note that indicates an unpaid balance: **5**.
Three are settled or dispositioned (one is order 430 itself, one has an owner decision not to pursue,
one was resolved from a Stripe record). **Two are genuinely uncollected and still sit at
`READY_FOR_PICKUP` today:**

* **`WO2608060002`** — a BlackBerry KEY2, screen fitted, order history reason reads *"did not pay
 waiting for pickup"*, roughly 160 uncollected, device on the shelf since 2026-08-21.
* **`WO2603290007`** — a Kyocera, workbook note *"did not pay"*, 105.

**Neither was given a further note.** Both are already documented at length by other sessions —
`WO2608060002` carries four notes from 9-13 October, including a correction that a null
`pickup_notified_at` is *not* evidence the customer was never told (he was, by phone, on the day). Adding
another would be the duplicate-note defect this whole program keeps finding. They are flagged instead,
and moving them out of the paid-looking state is a money action for the owner.

## ALSO CHECKED, AND HONESTLY NOT RESOLVED

customer on 9173917846). That is "not found with the surface I have", **not** "it does not exist" — the
invoice would be in the business mailbox body or the accounting books, neither of which I searched here.

## STATE UNCHANGED OTHERWISE

**34 rows remain** — 20 physical checks (one walk list) and 14 decisions. Ledger 121 writes. Nothing was
written to production this round: the correct action on both hidden receivables is the owner's, and
writing a fifth note on an already-documented order would have been noise.

## THE TRANSFERABLE RULE

**A convention tells you what a state MEANS; it is not a reading of what HAPPENED.** When a record
states its own convention, read what the record then *did* with it — the action is the evidence — and
here it pointed the opposite way from the inference drawn. A rule inferred from a phrase, with the
phrase's own example contradicting it, is precisely the shape of the `wrong-data` failure this estate
counts as its most common and most corrected.
