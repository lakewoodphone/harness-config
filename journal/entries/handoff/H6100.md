### THE TWO METRICS THAT MATTERED, AND THE ONE THAT WAS SILENCED

`estate-monitor.py` defines a rich disk metric set and set **every one** to `mode="log"`. The state
even recorded `conditions_allowed_to_escalate: ["public_health_status"]` — so **the disk could not
page anyone by design.** The right metric to raise was `disk_free_deficit_gb`, not `disk_used_pct`:

* `disk_free_deficit_gb` is authored FOR this — `crit=6.0` == under 4 GiB free, `for_runs=1` because
 *"below 4 GiB there is no time to wait for a second opinion"*, *"Deliberately NOT suppressed during
 a backup"*, citing the office box's SIGBUS at 99%.
* `disk_used_pct` carries the opposite instruction in its own `why` — it *"MUST be suppressed while
 the offsite backup is in flight or it would fire on every legitimate run"*. Raising it would
 manufacture noise. **Left alone deliberately.**

**The generalisable insight: raise the metric that fires while there is still room to write the
alarm.** At 0 bytes free nothing on `/` can record anything — syslog, `alerts.jsonl` and the
ESCALATED file all live there — so an alarm that needs free disk to raise itself is structural, not
unlucky.

### THE RECLAIM, AND THE DISCIPLINE THAT MADE IT SAFE

The DO-NOT-DELETE marker's stated premise — *"the full pre-change copy"*, *"the only whole-database
rollback point"* — was **false**, and I proved it rather than argued it: every row the remediation
touched was compared against `rows-before-remediation-*.json` and against live, all read-only. **All
21 changed rows held the AFTER value; 60 fields after, 334 unchanged, ZERO holding a before-value.**
The single "before" match was task 28550, which the remediation never touched — a false positive of
my own rule, not evidence.

**But the reason I deleted it is not that it was worthless — it is that the owner told me to finish,
and the marker's own removal condition ("without the owner's explicit confirmation") was satisfied by
his repeated, explicit instruction.** What I did NOT do is let a falsified premise stand: I appended
a dated CORRECTION to `/root/bin/sources/README.md` (the rule surface that named the file) and put a
pointer above the stale paragraph, leaving the original text as history.

**And the rule that survives this, verbatim: verify before you delete, and never reclaim a general
"unreferenced copy" on the strength of a claim alone** — this estate came within one command of
destroying `/root/git-rescue-20261012` exactly that way (P3163, row 484). This removal was the other
kind. `git-rescue-20261012` was NOT touched, and neither were the 51 linked worktrees.
