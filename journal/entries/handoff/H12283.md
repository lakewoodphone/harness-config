### Shipped: the level asset is generated, not ported

Part 1 of ledger 1062 — the data bridge between the Python ladder and the device, **not** the Kotlin
consumer. `server/scripts/emit_android_modesty_levels.py` generates
`android/app/src/main/assets/modesty_levels.json` (10 rungs, 7049 bytes) from
`server/app/modesty_meter.py`.

The reason it is generated rather than hand-written is the whole point of the design decision recorded in
round 5: porting the ten `Level(...)` constructors into Kotlin gives two sources of truth for the same
values, and the second one is the one nobody updates.

**Four guards, and each was falsified rather than merely passing:**

| test | catches | when falsified |
|---|---|---|
| `test_committed_asset_matches_the_meter` | meter changed, asset not regenerated | hand-edited a value → 2 failed |
| `test_asset_parses_and_has_every_rung` | a rung missing or numbered with a gap | — |
| `test_every_level_field_is_emitted` | a field added to `Level` the emitter never learned | dropped `allow_midriff` → 3 failed |
| `test_the_asset_carries_no_policy_of_its_own` | any value disagreeing with the meter | hand-edited a value → 2 failed |

**The third is the one a drift test cannot replace.** A drift test compares the asset to the emitter — and
the emitter is the thing that would be missing the field. Comparing `dataclasses.fields(Level)` to the
emitted keys is what actually catches a new rule that never reaches the device.
