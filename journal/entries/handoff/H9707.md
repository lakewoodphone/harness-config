## Addendum 16 to H9513 (round 17-18) — an outage I caused, and a lever that would have broken the channel

### Round 17: the maintenance window I mistook for an outage

`company-api` was inactive on the authority. I called it an outage and restarted it. It was inactive
again eight minutes later and I restarted it again. **Both restarts landed inside another session's
34 GB `VACUUM INTO` compaction**, which had stopped the API deliberately so the copy could be taken
with no writers. The script's integrity gate detected that the copy no longer matched the database,
refused to swap, cleaned up, and restarted the units.

Outcome verified: the database was **never replaced** (still 34,096,103,424 bytes), `pragma
quick_check` **ok**, `sms_log` 4,285 rows, no leftover files, `/health` 200 at 0.006 s. Nothing was
lost or corrupted. What was lost was ten minutes of a 34 GB job — by me.

The tell was on the same screen as the wrong conclusion: `sqlite3 /root/authority.db VACUUM INTO
'/root/authority.db.compact-tmp'`, started the same minute as the stop.
- **P9099 retracted**; **P9227** files the correction; **L9918** is the rule: *a service that is
 stopped and comes back stopped is a maintenance window, not an outage — read `ps` for
 VACUUM/backup before restarting, and a quiet monitor is evidence.*
- The compaction did **not** complete; the authority database is still 34 GB and that retry belongs
 to the session that started it, with nobody else touching the host.

### Round 18: the crowd has no queueing cure

The largest behavioural defect left is the crowd — 271 of 298 replies follow more than one text,
median 9, max 76. The obvious lever is "hold everything after N unanswered". Measured against the
merged channel it would withhold **N=2 → 88.9%**, N=3 → 87.3%, N=5 → 85.0% of all outbound texts.

Because **he does not reply to most of what is sent**: 3,541 outbound against 294 replies arriving
after any burst, so the counter almost never resets. A cap on it does not tame the crowd — it
silences the channel and starts dropping the few texts that do work.

Two medians that look contradictory and are not:
- **median 9 before a reply** counts texts inside the 6-hour reply window — crowding around a reply,
 which is the health measure;
- **median 1, p90 6, max 845** counts everything since his previous reply, however long ago —
 accumulation across silences, which is why a cap fails.

**The crowd is a volume problem with a per-message cause, not a queueing problem with a queueing
cure.** The real levers are already in place: do not send what is not worth sending (guards, topic
cooldown, composers writing one item instead of thirty-six), the per-class mutes, the machine-per-day
cap, and the 20-minute floor.

Recorded so a future session does not implement the cap, watch the channel go quiet, and call it a
success until someone notices the reminders stopped.
