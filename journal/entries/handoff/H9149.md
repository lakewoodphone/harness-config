## 2026-10-19 01:30 UTC · ZABZ-YOGA · Anti-reset architecture: seven-stream design, the FRP string-format risk, and a reversal from shop-anchored to parent-anchored accounts

Owner: all hands on deck for a robust multilayer anti-reset system with failovers and fail-saves, and keep
documenting/organising. Seven streams ran in parallel (attack tree, detection, recovery/custody, Knox Guard
ops, unused device-owner powers, red team, own-image feasibility). Master document:
kosher-filter-ai docs/design/ANTI-RESET-ARCHITECTURE.md (310 lines), pushed da43265. Research evidence in
docs/research/ANDROID-ENROLMENT-NO-WIPE-2026-10-18.md (Part 1 no-wipe sealing, Part 2 the bootloader hole).

FINDINGS THAT CHANGE THE PLAN:
1. Enhanced FRP, once armed, DISABLES the consumer unlock flow ("Once set, the consumer unlock flow will
 be disabled and only accounts in this list can unlock"). So the screen-lock/PIN hole I described to the
 owner does not exist - arming the policy IS the way to require the account only. My earlier statement
 that the API has no such control was wrong.
2. The DANGEROUS unknown is the account-string FORMAT: "It's up to the FRP management agent to interpret
 the String as account it supports". If the shape we pass matches nothing, nobody can unlock, including
 the shop. One device test before any customer phone.
3. REVERSAL: anchor the PARENT's account, not the shop's. The custody stream argues one shop credential
 over hundreds of devices is a fleet-wide single point of failure and makes resale impossible. Shop
 account only for stock and loaners; use Google Workspace if we hold one at all (2 super admins on
 hardware keys, printed backup codes, domain registrar under two-person control, 20-day user restore).
4. The strongest route is a PURCHASING decision: Knox Mobile Enrollment (free) and Google zero-touch both
 re-enrol a device on the next factory reset, on a CERTIFIED image so banking keeps working. Building a
 custom image is rejected: Play Integrity DEVICE_INTEGRITY fails on self-signed images (no Wallet, no
 banking), 136 GiB build, twice-yearly AOSP drops, and the whole support burden lands on the shop.
 KosherCell's "exclusive technology available from the phone manufacturers themselves" is this channel.
5. Knox Guard runbook: seats are per-device and NOT transferable; activation must happen within 7 days of
 the device booting; offline lock 30-60 days domestic (never 3); no offline webhook (must poll); licence
 expiry returns the device to Pending, it does NOT brick it; bypass tools and CVE-2026-20978 exist.
6. Five unused device-owner calls worth adding tomorrow: setUserControlDisabledPackages (kills Force
 stop/Clear data on our app), setPackagesSuspended (Play/installer/browser/Settings), accessibility +
 input-method allowlists, DISALLOW_GRANT_ADMIN + FUN + SYSTEM_ERROR_DIALOGS, and the branding trio.
7. RED TEAM: the stack is strong against removal and weak against substitution and consent. The cheapest
 attack is a second-hand phone and a borrowed SIM; the second is a parent who agrees with the teenager.
 The one attack we cannot stop.

FAIL-SAFES documented per layer, including: detector dead-man (a silent detector beats a silent phone),
idempotency key hash(phone_id, level, silence_started_ts) so restarts cannot duplicate alerts, FRP release
via setFactoryResetProtectionPolicy(admin, null) or wipeData(WIPE_RESET_PROTECTION_DATA), and the
Play-policy fact that an unremovable app cannot ship as a consumer Play app.

FIVE TESTS that turn the design into fact are listed in §11; the first is the FRP string format.
