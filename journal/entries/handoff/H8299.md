### What this round actually established

**The session corpus is complete.** The raw archive holds **14,753 DSH session files** across six
machines against **8,637 sessions** in `authority.db` — which reads as thousands of unsearchable
conversations. Compared **by ID** (every session id the manifest names, against `dsh_sessions`):
**8,609 distinct ids, only 14 absent**. There was no backlog, and the importer I was ten minutes into
designing would have imported nothing.

The 14 absent ids are recorded here rather than chased: they are almost certainly torn sessions the
archive kept and the parser refused, and 14 of 8,623 is not a corpus gap.

### Kept, with the honest reason

`push-dsh-sessions.mjs` now reads `sessions` **and** `sessions-archive`, live copy winning on a
duplicate id. Not for a backlog — for the **race** where a session is archived before the shipper
reads it, and so lands in neither the live store nor the database. The first version of that code
comment asserted the backlog I had just disproven; it was corrected in a follow-up commit rather than
left to mislead the next reader.
