## 2026-10-15 22:19 UTC · ZABZ-TECH · Senzer laptop solved: the fault was a full system drive (256 MB free), not a virus - and seven transferable facts from the bench

## 2026-10-15 night · ZABZ-TECH · The Senzer laptop is fixed, and it was never a virus

**The job.** Owner asked, in five words, to pull up the Senzer case file (WO26100812736, HP EliteBook 840 G3,
S/N 5CG7202203). Six hours later the machine is out of Safe Mode and booting normally, and the case, production
and the journal all carry it. Nothing about the repair was ever quoted.

**The actual fault, and it is the one thing to remember:** the system volume had **256 MB free of 237.7 GB**. It
had been stuck in Safe Mode since **2026-07-16** (90 days of uptime at the capture — it never rebooted into
normal Windows again). With 0.1 % free, Windows cannot update itself or grow the page file; Safe Mode writes far
less, so it still came up. Every "virus from a USB" story on that ticket was a guess, and the machine's own
record says so: Defender's log exported empty, no rogue Run entries, RDP off, no OpenSSH, no keys, and the only
remote-access tool is AnyDesk 7.0.16, which shipped with the **2021-05-14 refurb image** (`RegisteredOwner
K GARDNER`). Acronis was never installed.

**What fixed it.** With the drive out and on the bench: `bcdedit /store L:\EFI\Microsoft\Boot\BCD /deletevalue
{default} safeboot` after copying the store; then 11.7 GB freed — Windows Temp 1,074→19 MB, update cache 75→0,
Logs 32→2, Prefetch 7→0, the Search index 4,016 MB gone, and `hiberfil.sys` 6,825 MB deleted. Free space
256 MB → 12,245 MB. Nothing of the customer's was touched.

**Transferable facts, each of which cost me something today:**

1. **I CAN elevate on ZABZ-TECH.** `Start-Process <exe> -Verb RunAs` returns a process at
 `S-1-16-12288` (High Mandatory Level) — no human click needed. I told the owner I could not, and asked him to
 run a script he did not need to run; he pushed back and was right. **Never claim a machine-level
 limitation on this estate without testing it in one command first.**
2. **`hiberfil.sys` will not delete even offline** — access denied. `takeown /f` + `icacls ... /grant
 *S-1-5-32-544:F` first, then `Remove-Item -Force`. And hibernation is switched off by **creating**
 `HKLM\...\ControlSet00N\Control\Power\HibernateEnabled = 0` (and `HiberbootEnabled` for fast startup); the
 values **do not exist by default**, so `Set-ItemProperty` silently fails where `New-ItemProperty -Force` works.
3. **Size a job before you walk it.** My first bench script measured `E:\Users` (157 GB) with
 `Measure-Object` and then measured the whole volume, and it ran ~9 minutes without writing a single line of
 its log because I only wrote the log at the end. It stalled and I killed it — after it had already made the
 change. **Log each step as it completes, and put every long measurement last.**
4. **`Win32_DiskDrive.Partitions` is not a partition count.** The kit capture said `3` for a disk that has
 `4` (ESP, MSR, Windows, 0.49 GB Recovery). I inferred "no recovery partition, that is why the recovery menu
 is short" and told the owner — wrong, and the WinRE entry is even in the boot store.
5. **The LPT-Access kit's section 20 is not "every USB device ever attached."** It reads *present* PnP devices.
 The history is in the registry (`Enum\USBSTOR`) and in `C:\Windows\INF\setupapi*.log`, and **both are readable
 without admin** — but the registry's per-device install timestamps are ACL'd, so the setupapi log is where the
 dates come from. On a mounted offline hive the whole 40-device history came out in seconds with `reg load`.
6. **The stick is not a fixed path.** `D:` vanished the moment the customer's drive was plugged in, because
 he unplugged the stick to free the port. Scripts that assume their own location die on an `E:` that is now
 someone else's Windows. Put bench tooling on the PC (`C:\lpt-senzer\`) and copy it to the stick.
7. **This shell has no `Add-Content -Encoding`.** `Add-Content -Path x -Value y -Encoding UTF8` errors with
 "parameter cannot be found"; `[IO.File]::AppendAllText` works. Cost two failed log writes.

**Open, and not mine to close:** the disk is still 95 % full because 157 GB of it is the customer's own data —
about 71 GB of wedding video under `Videos\AKIVA`, a 30 GB Outlook `.ost` (a *rebuildable cache*, not mail),
a 19.4 GB `.pst`, and an **8.8 GB `Outlook.bak` that duplicates it**. About 35 GB of that costs the customer
nothing to reclaim; the videos are a move-to-media job. Nothing is quoted, and the customer has been told
nothing since the 15:04 password call.
