## 2026-10-21 13:15 UTC · SECRATARY-CLOUD · Spam was a blind folder, and 'Applications Accepted Nov 2-10' matched no deadline: the LTRAP notice reached the owner from someone else

## 2026-10-21 · secratary-cloud · A deadline that reached the owner from another person, not from me

**The owner's correction, verbatim, and it is the requirement:** *"If it was in my emails, I don't
know why it was not brought to my attention earlier and someone else had to tell me about it. This
is exactly the sort of thing that the owner Q is supposed to inform me about."* He was right. The
notice was in his mailbox for ~17 hours before he heard about it from someone else.

### The trigger

LRRC newsletter, 2026-10-20 20:39 UTC, "Section 8 Housing Voucher (HUD) Applications Open for a
Limited Time": Lakewood's LTRAP Section 8 waiting list reopens 2026-11-02 09:00 and closes
**2026-11-10 23:59** (he had said "the 9th" — the official banner on ltrap.org says the 10th; both
sources read 2026-10-21), 3,000 applications drawn by lottery, apply at
`https://www.pha-web.com/portals/onlineApplication/1641` — **not** ltrap.org, which is only the
front door. Full brief, with every source, at
`/root/accounting-data/household/hud-section8-ltrap-2026.md` (private; harness-config is a public
remote, so the household detail stays off it).

### Why nobody told him — three independent gates, each measured, not inferred

1. **Gmail filed it as spam.** `labelIds: ['UNREAD','CATEGORY_UPDATES','SPAM']`, read from the raw
 archive payload `/root/mail-archive/eliyahuzabrowsky/raw/19/1a15b0a4b0613519.json.gz`. The two
 2022 LRRC notices from the same sender were `INBOX`; this one was not.
2. **Every pipeline reads `in:inbox`, and the sweep excluded spam explicitly.**
 `lpt-email-sweep.py` line 113 read `in:anywhere -in:inbox -in:sent -in:spam -in:trash`, and
 `email_intake.sync_email_catalog` uses `is:inbox newer_than:3d`. Consequence measured:
 `email_catalog` 0 rows for that message id, `owner_mail_verdict` 0 rows. It could not have
 reached the queue, because it never entered the system the queue reads.
3. **The deadline vocabulary only knew the literal word.** `_HARD_DEADLINE` in `owner_attention.py`
 matches `deadline|expires|past due|final notice|...` — the notice says "Applications Accepted
 November 2-10", "Opens:", "Closes:", "Don't Miss the Application Window", and matched **nothing**.
 It scored `low` and was dropped. Independently, `classify_message()` returned
 `category=noise, priority=p3, needs_owner=0, reason='marketing/noise'`.

A fourth, different defect found on the way: **the digest had no MONEY AND FILINGS section at all.**
`_SECTION_OF` mapped `"books"` but `from_obligations()` emits `source="obligations"`, so every
money/filing row fell to OTHER. Read on the 2026-10-20 14:20 digest: sections were DECISIONS /
EMAIL / PEOPLE WAITING / OTHER, and six overdue obligations sat at positions 29-34 in OTHER.

### What changed (all verified, none of it "configured and assumed")

- `lpt-email-sweep.py` (repo: `harness-config/scripts/owner-attention/`, installed `/root/bin`):
 added a fourth Gmail pass `in:spam newer_than:{min(days,14)}d`, capped at 250. Rows keep `labels`,
 so a rescued message stays identifiable as having come from spam. **Verified end to end:** ran the
 real sweep for one mailbox (`--days 10`, exit 0, 195 rows); the notice is now in the corpus at
 `id=1a15b0a4b0613519` with its full 4,165-char body, and 17 spam rows came through where zero did.
- `owner_attention.py` (repo: `/opt/company`, the live git repo): added `_APPLICATION_WINDOW`
 (application/enrollment/waiting-list language) alongside `_HARD_DEADLINE`, and mapped
 `"obligations"` to MONEY AND FILINGS. **Verified:** the notice now scores `[high]` through
 `from_email_catalog()` run over the new corpus.
- **Precision measured, because a louder feed he ignores is worse than a quiet one:** over 195
 corpus rows the old regex matched 16, and the new one adds **exactly 1** — the Section 8 notice.
 The two marketing mails that also scored high (Twin Pine Ford, MGM Rewards) match the OLD
 `expires` pattern; that noise is pre-existing, not new, and is known-open below.
