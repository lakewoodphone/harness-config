**3. This corrects `175` section 4.1 a second time, and the correction is the useful part.** I first said
"49 documents are single-copy" (wrong - 34 of them were on the authority, unversioned). Then I said
"16 are truly single-copy" and rescued them. **The real taxonomy has three grades, and I had conflated
two of them:**

| grade | what it means | n this session |
|---|---|---|
| single-copy | one host, not in git anywhere - **the only true emergency** | 16, now rescued |
| versioned elsewhere but absent here | in git on another machine - findable, but invisible to a reader on this host | the original 49-set remainder |
| **present here but in NO git** | safe from a crash, **invisible to every future session and to every other machine** | **72**, now promoted |

**The third grade was the one I kept missing**, and it is the one that matters most for an estate whose
entire continuity mechanism is "the next session reads the record". A file that exists on one disk in no
repository is not "backed up" by that disk; it is one `rm` from gone, and no search will find it because
nothing indexes a staging directory.

**The lesson about method, third time.** Three passes, three answers: a two-way diff found 49; looking
for the files anywhere on the host found 16; and only asking "what is on this host that is in no git at
all" found the 72. **Each question I asked was narrower than the last, and each one missed a grade. The
right question for a durability audit is not "which copies are missing" but "for every artefact, how
many independent copies exist and is each one discoverable by the next reader".**
