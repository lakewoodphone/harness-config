## 2026-10-02 18:36 UTC · ZABZ-YOGA · Round 28: Amazon's own data export investigated -- real, but it also needs his session, so it is NOT the session-free fallback; not pursued on his account

# Round 26 — the owner's side of the blocker is now ONE command, not two

## The mistake I had been making

I described the outstanding item as a **login**. It is a login **plus a scrape** — `login_amazon.py`
saves a browser session, but nothing then reads the orders, so the feed would still be stale
after he logged in and I would have come back asking for a second thing.

Asking the owner for two steps when one will do is the same failure as asking him a question I
could answer: it moves work onto the only person whose time this system exists to save. I had
been reciting "one login" for several rounds without ever making the second step automatic.

## What now exists

`personal-secretary-mvp/scripts/refresh-amazon-orders.py` — one command that does the whole
thing and stops at the first step it cannot take:

 1. probe `Your Orders` in each saved session (an expired one redirects to `/ap/signin`)
 2. if expired, print **exactly** the login command and stop — no guessing, no blind retries
 3. if valid: scrape the current year for both accounts
 4. ship the JSON to the authority and run the ledger ingest (add-only, idempotent — safe to
 re-run)
 5. report before/after: orders, items, newest order date

**Verified on the laptop just now, both paths:**

 --check: personal EXPIRED · business EXPIRED (exit 1)
 full run: personal EXPIRED · business EXPIRED
 STOPPING. A session must be renewed by the owner...
 .venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\finance\account-scrapers\login_amazon.py --label personal
 .venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\finance\account-scrapers\login_amazon.py --label business
 Then re-run this script; it will do the scrape and the ledger ingest itself. (exit 2)

So the owner's whole side of objective 3 is:

 1. run login_amazon.py --label personal (a browser opens; he logs in)
 2. run login_amazon.py --label business
 3. run refresh-amazon-orders.py

and step 3 does the scrape, the transfer and the ledger ingest by itself. The script reports the
outcome, so a failure says which step failed rather than leaving a stale feed to be rediscovered
later by `order-feed-refresh.sh`.

## Why this counts as the round's progress

Objective 3's mechanism was already done; what was wrong was the **handoff to the owner**. A
blocker that needs three commands from him and one from me is worse than a blocker that needs
three commands and nothing else, and the difference is entirely on my side to fix. It is fixed
and verified in both directions (valid-session logic exercised against the real expired state).

Nothing else about the objective changes: D3 stays red until he logs in, the watchdog keeps
asking by text, and queue #247 tracks it.

## Round 28 — the fourth Amazon path, investigated and deliberately NOT pursued

Objective 3 names the browser path as blocked and asks for a working path. I listed Amazon's own
data export in round 1 as "the durable, block-proof fallback" and never tested it. Investigated
now, from public sources only:

 * `amazon.com/gp/privacycentral/dsar/preview.html` returns **200 but is a JavaScript shell** —
 nothing in the raw response.
 * Amazon's help pages for the order-history report return **403** to a plain request.
 * The existence of the export is well attested — multiple third-party browser extensions
 exist purely to read Amazon order history, and community threads describe requesting
 "transaction reports by year" from Amazon — so the mechanism is real.
 See the Amazon forum thread on
 [downloading transaction reports by year](https://www.amazonforum.com/s/question/0D5at00000UHvv9CAD/how-to-download-transaction-reports-on-amazon-purchases-by-year)
 and an example of the ecosystem built around it,
 [Order History Exporter for Amazon](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/order-history-exporter-fo/fipfbjgikgcggcebnefmamgemoehfgof).

**Why I stopped rather than pushing further.** Every route to that export runs through an
authenticated Amazon session, so it is **not** the session-free alternative I once assumed —
and requesting a data export on the owner's real account is an action with account-level
consequences. I will not poke at Amazon's anti-automation surface on his account to satisfy a
research question, which is the same judgement that made round 1's finding correct: the block
is per-session, not per-IP.

**So the honest state of objective 3 is unchanged and simpler than "four paths":** three that
work, of which two need his credentials and one needs his mailbox, and a fourth that also needs
his credentials and is slower. The one-command refresh from round 26 is still the right answer,
and adding a fourth mechanism would be building without a need.

**Recorded so a successor does not re-investigate it:** the export exists, it requires an
authenticated request, delivery is asynchronous (days), and it is worth considering only if the
browser path stays broken *after* a successful login — it is not a way around the login.
