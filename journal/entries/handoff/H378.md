- **Three of the six are old and already on the record as this exact defect.** `L221` and `L221a` are

- **The mechanism, as far as I have measured it:** the footer is `entry_hash(heading, body)` computed at
 WRITE time over the body the tool held, while `check` recomputes it over the body as it is READ back
 through `norm_body` (which collapses runs of spaces and tabs and strips trailing blank lines). Any body
 whose written form does not survive that round trip byte-identically gets a footer that never matches.
 Three of my six (H355, H366, P200) were recent appends and one of them — H366 — *deliberately contains
 the mojibake sequences as examples*, which is exactly the kind of body that trips it.
- **My own process was part of the cause and is the part I can fix:** I have been writing entry bodies
 with PowerShell `Out-File -Encoding utf8` into a temp file and passing `--body-file`. Measured just
 now: those files carry no BOM (`first bytes = b'## 202'`) and CRLF is handled by `norm_body`, so the
 damage is not a BOM — but routing non-ASCII text through a PowerShell encoder is precisely what
 L221/L223 warn about, and it is the one variable I control. **From here, journal bodies are written
 with the file-writing tool (UTF-8, LF, no shell in the path) and only the path is passed to
 `journal.py append`.**
- Final state: **`check` -> 0 errors**, `recon` -> `16 ok · 0 FAIL · 0 could not measure`, 4 clocks in
