## P3145 — A re-encoded duplicate defeats weekly.py's content-hash guard, so a zip of a customer's photos became a bin batch

A re-encoded duplicate defeats a content-hash guard, so a zip of a customer's photos became a bin batch.

Measured 2026-10-12 in `lpt-hub/tools/parts-catalog`: batch `data/bins/2026-10-11` holds three photographs (`IMG_1116-1118`) that are the **Pearl EliteBook 840 G5 label shots** — the same three frames already on that customer's case at intake positions 2-4. The Pearl case had established it on 2026-10-11 by downscaling both copies and differencing them: mean absolute pixel difference 0.39-0.42 of 255, **0.00% of pixels differing by more than 16**. So the frames are the same; only the JPEG encode differs, which is why the sha256 differ.

`weekly.py`'s guard is exactly that hash — "it refuses to re-ingest a zip it has already catalogued, by content, so the owner's old export sitting in Downloads is not mistaken for a new one". It works on identical bytes and cannot see a re-encode. Result: a batch was born out of a customer's laptop photographs, and it sat at 0 items because nobody could honestly catalogue a laptop bottom cover as a bin part.

**Cost as it stands:** small — one empty batch, and a `status` line that says three photos are waiting to be read when nothing about them belongs in the catalog. **The cost it points at is not small:** the weekly loop's input is "whatever the owner drops in a folder", and the owner drops whatever the phone gave him, which routinely includes customer case photographs. An unguarded loop turns those into inventory.

**Fix direction (not made):**
1. **Perceptual identity, not byte identity** — an image hash that survives a re-encode (dHash/pHash, or a downscale-and-difference check at the ingestion boundary) alongside the sha256, so the same frame cannot enter twice.
2. **Cross-check the batch against the case corpus** — a new batch photo that matches a `case-media`/intake photograph is customer evidence, not stock, and belongs in a decision rather than a catalog row.
3. **A batch is not stock until a human says it is.** `apply` already only runs with results; the missing step is that the batch itself was created from an arbitrary drop without anyone asserting "these are bin items".
