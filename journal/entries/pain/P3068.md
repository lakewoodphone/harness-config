## P3068 — A customer's device left at the counter has no custody record at all - no intake case, no photograph, no bin row, so a lost card cannot be traced or reconciled

**Measured 2026-10-11, Fisher SD card (WO26101112743), number 914-570-2075.**

The owner reported that the customer's card is **currently lost** — not found in the shop. Nothing in the
shop's systems can say when it came in, which bin it went into, or where it went, because **nothing
records that it was ever received.**

Searched read-only in the `operational` store on 2026-10-11 — every one of these is zero:

- `repair_intake_cases` for this customer: **0 rows**.
- `repair_intake_case_photos` / `repair_intake_case_events`: nothing (there is no case to hang them on).
- `kiosk_intake_payments` mentioning his number: **0 rows**.
- `payment_transactions` for customer 2840: **0 rows**.

The only traces of the whole matter were **a Dialpad call** and the **work order created today**. Yet the
customer had left the card with the shop earlier (*"I left my card before"*), the shop had written **his
name and number on a note with it**, and the owner's own account is that he has *"your name in one bin
and the SD card in another"* — so the shop knowingly holds customer cards in bins keyed by a handwritten
note, and there is no system behind that note.

**Why this is worse than the loss itself:**
- a device accepted into a bin is customer property with **no receipt, no identifier, no photograph and
 no storage location** recorded anywhere a computer can read it;
- so there is no list to reconcile the bins against, and "lost" cannot become "last seen in bin N on
 date D";
- when a note separates from its card, nobody can say which card was whose — which is exactly what
 happened: two unlabelled cards were read on the bench that day and **both belonged to other people**;
- money can be taken for a device that cannot be produced, and the payment is invisible too.

**What would fix it:** one custody row per device at counter intake — work order id, customer, a one-line
description, a **photograph of the item together with its label**, and the physical bin — written the
moment the item is taken in. `repair_intake_cases` and `repair_intake_case_photos` already exist and
already hold photographs in R2 for kiosk intake; the gap is that a **counter** intake writes nothing to
them.
