## P2936b — The shop's SMS auto-responder answers every customer text with opening hours, so real questions are never read

MEASURED 2026-10-13 on secratary, reading the shop's whole text history.

Every inbound SMS to the business line gets an instant reply:

 "Thanks for texting Lakewood Phone & Tech. Our walk-in hours are Sunday 12-4, and
 Tuesday and Thursday 10:30-5:30. You can drop your device off any time at our secure
 drop-off and we'll get started on it. We'll text you back when we're in."

In the comms database the reply lands **one second** after the customer's message, and then
nothing else happens. The customer's actual question is never answered by a person.

The proof case is (848) 333-6341. Three separate messages asking about a laptop:

 2026-10-09 Wheres my laptop? -> canned hours reply, 1.1 s later
 2026-10-09 Whats witg my laptop? -> canned hours reply, 0.9 s later
 2026-10-12 ? -> canned hours reply, 0.6 s later

At 2026-10-13 the same number is still texting "Following up on weitman laptop". The customer
was never told anything about the laptop, in three days.

WHY THIS IS WORSE THAN SILENCE. A queue that is visibly unanswered gets worked. An
auto-responder makes the system look healthy from every dashboard: the thread has an outbound
reply, `waiting_on_us` was closed by acknowledgement, and the "we'll text you back when we're
in" sentence is a promise the system then breaks. It also destroys the signal, because the
owner's own read of the thread shows "we replied".

SCALE, same measurement: 30 parties waiting on a reply, 48 missed calls in 14 days. The
auto-responder is not the only cause, but it is the one that hides the rest.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT. Either remove the auto-reply, or gate it: hours and drop-off are legitimate
first-contact facts, but a second message from the same party must NOT get the canned reply -
it must open or bump a task against a real person. The current reply is unconditional.

WHAT IT IS NOT: this is not a reason to stop reading texts. It is the reason the texts must be
read by something that is not the auto-responder.
