## P223 — /tmp on secratary is 5.9 GB of stale scratch on a 12 GiB tmpfs, and it is not safe to delete in a batch

`/tmp` on secratary is 5.9 GB of scratch on a 12 GiB tmpfs, and no one may safely delete it.

MEASURED 2026-09-17 03:47Z: `df -h /tmp` = 12G size, 5.9G used (52%); `du -sh /tmp` = 5.9G;
23,706 files older than 2026-09-16T00:00Z and 8,841 newer than 2026-09-17T00:00Z. So it is BOTH in
active use and full of stale scratch, and the two are not separable in a batch.

WHY IT HURTS. tmpfs pages are swappable. This directory alone filled the host's entire 4 GiB swap
area with cold files (89% of it, measured with mincore), which made the broker halve the authority's
slots from 3 to 1 and made the machine look like it could not absorb a spike when the real problem
was an oversized scratch filesystem. It also holds `/tmp/mob/prof1` and
`/tmp/phone-layout-check-profile` — live Chrome profiles the phone gate is using THIS MINUTE — and
`/tmp/wt-green` is a git worktree belonging to another stream, so a `find -delete` is a very good
way to break someone mid-write.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT (all need a per-action yes; `rm -rf`-class destruction is not a call this seat
makes at 04:00Z on the company's authoritative server):
 1. delete /tmp scratch older than ~2 days that NO PROCESS HAS OPEN (`lsof +D /tmp` first);
 2. lower the tmpfiles.d age for /tmp from 10 d to 2 d so it stays clean;
 3. decide whether /tmp should be a 12 GiB tmpfs at all (systemd's default `size=50%` of RAM).
Order 1 then 2; 3 is a deliberate attempt, not an opportunistic one, because a 5.9 GB directory
under a smaller cap starts failing writes with ENOSPC for whoever is mid-write.

WHAT WAS DONE INSTEAD (no data touched): the swapped pages were faulted back in, reclaiming
4,023.6 MiB of swap (100.0% -> 1.8%), and `vm.swappiness` went 60 -> 10 so tmpfs is no longer the
kernel's first eviction target while 12.6 GB of clean page cache sits idle. That fixes the symptom
durably enough to be worth having; it does not shrink /tmp.

Refs: docs/mesh/86-authority.md §2.5.
