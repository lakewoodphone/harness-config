## P2803 — The owner queue carries work that is not the owner's, and the badge counts rows instead of decisions - 31 was really 16

**The owner queue accumulates work that is not the owner's, and the badge counts rows instead of decisions. An instance of P1, with a specified fix.**

Measured 2026-09-30. The owner saw the kernel badge read **"31 owner decision(s) queued"** and asked the
obvious question: *"so why does it say 31 owner decisions"*. He asked because it was wrong. Of the 31:

- **16 were genuinely his** (money, a state filing, family, his own car, his own health record, a
 customer/partner reply, his machine).
- **14 were engineering, data hygiene and deploy authorisations the seat should have taken itself** -
 including **two pairs that were the same question filed twice** (#194 and #195, #184 and #186).
- **1 was a stale question** asking him to choose dinner for the night of 2026-09-29, asked on the 29th
 and unreachable by the time he read it.

After the triage the queue reads **16**, and all 16 are his. Nothing about the work changed; only who was
carrying it.

**Why it matters more than tidiness.** This number is the single surface that tells the owner how much
of his attention the system is asking for. A number that is 48% not-his teaches him to ignore it, and the
rows he ignores are the ones about his money and his family. It also contradicts, in public, the rule
this system is built on: 383 of 595 historical escalations were engineering faults that were never his,
and the queue is still manufacturing the same class.

**Why it happened.** `owner-queue.py add` accepts any text with no field naming *which* owner thing it
is. Nothing in the write path knows the difference between "should I pay 597.29 in cash" and "land this
commit on master", so both look the same to the queue, to the badge, and to the kernel.

**The fix, specified rather than proposed.**

1. **Make the category mandatory on `add`** - one of `money`, `customer`, `legal`, `family`,
 `irreversible`, `taste`. Refuse the write without it, the way the P265 guard already refuses suspect
 text. A row that cannot name its owner category is, by definition, not an owner decision.
2. **Make the kernel report distinct owner items, not rows** - it should count rows *and* say how many
 collapse into one question, since four rows here are one decision.
3. **Have the sentinel name the oldest row that carries no category**, so the backlog of mis-filed rows
 is visible rather than sitting inside a total.
4. **On every read, triage.** A queue read is not a report; it is a triage. Anything solvable by the seat
 comes out of the queue and into a task in the same pass, which is what was done here.

**Status: open, not yet fixed.** Applied today: the 14 rows removed and tracked in task 27217, the stale
row closed, the count read back at 16. Not applied: items 1-3, which are code changes to
`~/bin/owner-queue.py` on `secratary` and to the kernel's `owner_queue` check and should be made
deliberately rather than at the tail of a long session.

**Evidence.** `owner-queue.py stats` before and after (pending 31 -> 16; resolved 96 -> 111); the row
list read back; task 27217; the owner's own question, verbatim, above.
