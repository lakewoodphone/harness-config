## P3144 — Nothing compares a case file's money to production, so payment_state: paid with zero payment rows survived five weeks

No verifier compares a case file's money to production, so a case read as settled for five weeks while it was neither settled nor correctly priced.

Measured 2026-10-12, `lg-old-phone-shwartz-2026-08-12.md` front-matter vs production order 2631:
- `payment_state: paid` — production had **zero** rows in `payment_transactions` for that order, of any status, ever. Nothing was collected.
- `price: 175` — the ultrasonic tier the owner superseded on 2026-08-24; the operative figure was 50. Production's `orders.total` said 75, a third number.
- `deliverable` — held a copy-pasted caveat from a **battery listing** ("possible older manufacture date (2017 date code) — capacity may be slightly worn; fine as bench"), not a deliverable at all.
- `updated: 2026-09-03` — and the case genuinely had not been touched since, while the job ended unsuccessfully on 2026-09-20 and the customer was told so.

`lptcase check` raised nothing for this case; `money --uncollected` cannot see it either, because the file claims `paid`. The four-surface audit's checks are about links, schema and status regressions — none look at the money claim.

Fix direction: a `lptcase check` rule that, for every case with a `work_order`, compares front-matter `payment_state`/`price` with the order's `total` and its SUCCEEDED `payment_transactions` sum, and flags (a) `paid` with no SUCCEEDED row, (b) `price` disagreeing with `orders.total`, (c) `deliverable` that is empty, or suspiciously long, while `payment_state` is not `paid`. Each is a one-line comparison and every one of them was wrong here at the same time.
