## P327 — Nothing excludes the machine the owner is using, so when the biggest node fills the laptop is next in line

**P · The one thing that would make offloading real is missing: nothing excludes the machine the owner is using, and when the biggest node fills up the laptop is simply next in line**

MEASURED 2026-09-18 on ZABZ-YOGA, while four fan-out children were running on `zabz-tech`.

WHAT HAPPENED. Three separate dispatches, each of which believed it was aimed somewhere else, produced a
child that ran on the owner's laptop:

| what was asked for | what the child reported |
|---|---|
| `mesh-run -Node lakewooechsmini` | `MESH-HOST: zabz-yoga` |
| `mesh-run -Node secratary` | `MESH-HOST: zabz-yoga` |
| `--profile mesh` with `MESH_TARGET_NODE=secratary-ts` **set in the real process environment** | `MESH-HOST: zabz-yoga` |

`mesh-run`'s own verifier caught all three (`location disagreement: zabz-yoga not on <node>`, exit 1) —
the tool is honest, and that is the only reason these were not written down as successes.

TWO SEPARATE DEFECTS, AND THEY MUST NOT BE CONFLATED.

**(1) The fixed target does not take effect — root cause NOT established.** The installed
`~/.dsh/profiles/mesh/cordis.patch.yml:76` carries
`target: !!js "process.env.MESH_TARGET_NODE ?? 'laptop-ts'"`, and every observed result is exactly the
**fallback** `'laptop-ts'`, which is the laptop's own ssh alias. So either the `!!js` expression is not
being evaluated in a context that sees `process.env`, or `--profile mesh` is not the profile that
actually mounts and the default (broker) profile runs instead. The second would make `-Node` decorative
for a duller reason. **Not distinguished yet**; the discriminator is whether the child's own boot log
names the mesh profile. What IS established: `mesh-run -Node <node>` does not constrain placement, in
any of the three forms tried.

**(2) The policy gap, which is the answer to the owner's complaint and is independent of (1).** Even in
pure broker mode the placement was *correct by its own rules*: `pressure.js` excludes the local node only
at or above **85 % of physical commit**, and the laptop measures ~40 GB of 46 GB commit limit — nowhere
near it. With `zabz-tech` carrying four children, the broker balanced onto the next node with room, and
that node was the laptop. **So there is no mechanism at all for "the owner is using this machine right
now".** The single signal that should exclude it — he is typing on it — is not modelled anywhere in the
placement path.

WHY THIS IS THE WHOLE COMPLAINT. His sentence is *"when I use DSH it is speedy without any lagging …
because it offloads properly"*. Offloading works, and it stops working exactly when the mesh is busy —
which is precisely when he is most likely to notice. A policy that treats his laptop as an ordinary
worker below the 85 % line will keep putting agents on the machine he is looking at, and no amount of
capacity on the desktop fixes that, because the desktop fills first.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT, in order:
1. **A presence signal.** The harness already knows when a session is active and what the owner's own
 loop-lag is; `pressure.js` already has the vocabulary (`routeAwayFromLocal`). "The owner is present"
 should raise the local machine into the high band regardless of commit, and the reason line must say
 so, because a placement the model cannot explain is a placement nobody can debug.
2. **Say where it went, in the collapsed row.** Covered in `110`: the node is in the job's output but
 not in its label, and `dsh-jobs` has no relabel.
3. **Make `-Node` honest.** Either fix the fixed-target path or make `mesh-run` refuse `-Node` with a
 message saying it cannot constrain placement until the profile honours it. A flag that silently does
 nothing is worse than no flag: it is how three dispatches were believed to be aimed elsewhere.
