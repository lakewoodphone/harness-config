## P2187 — P266 - A queue row is a days-old photograph of the world, and nothing re-checks it before the owner sees it

**What hurts.** A row in the owner decision queue is a photograph of the world taken when it was
written, and it is presented to the owner days later with the same recommendation and no re-check.
Row #100 was written 2026-09-18 about a choice between two machines; when it was raised on 2026-09-22
the machine the customer had chosen (the $390 Newegg Latitude 5400) had gone **out of stock**, and the
other machine's source price had moved **$130 → $169**. The recommendation happened to survive, but the
facts it rested on did not, and nothing in the queue or in the badge noticed — the badge printed
"4.2d" and the same sentence.

**Why it is structural, not a one-off.** Anything the queue holds about the outside world (a listing
price, a listing's existence, a vendor's deadline, a loan offer's expiry) decays at a rate no one
measures. The queue has no staleness re-check, and `owner-queue.py list` does not even populate the
`age_days` field it displays — every row in the JSON came back `age_days: null` while the badge
computed 4.2 days for the same row from `asked_at`. So neither the tool nor the reader can see how old
the world inside a row is.

**What would fix it.** (1) A `revalidate` step: before a row is raised, re-read its declared external
facts and stamp `revalidated_at` + what changed, refusing to present the row stale after N days. (2)
Give each row a machine-checkable `expires` or `deadline` where it has one — #123's Stripe Capital
offer expires 2026-10-03, #127's free cancellation window closes the moment the chip-off starts — and
let the badge sort on *that* rather than on `asked_at`. (3) Populate `age_days` in `list --json`.

Recorded 2026-09-22 from the Weitman re-check (case file section "LIVE RE-CHECK 2026-09-22", commit
`12bc18a5a`).
