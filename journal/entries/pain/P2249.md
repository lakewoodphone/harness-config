## P2249 — 319 of 391 sync records fail their own schema, so the schema cannot be a gate

**Measured 2026-09-22: 319 of 391 sync records under
`lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/sync-records/` fail
`CUSTOMER_CASE_SYNC_RECORD.schema.json`. 72 pass.** The schema is closed at the root and at
most sub-blocks (`additionalProperties: false`), while the records carry real keys nobody
declared. Top signatures, counted: `'' is too short` 19, `contactId/taskId` 15+9+5,
`filename` 13, a whole legacy key-set (`case_file`, `case_id`, `created_at`, `issue`,
`legacy`, `lpt_contact_id`, `lpt_task_id`, `next_actions`, `status`, `type`) 11, `parts` 10,
`None is not of type 'string'` 8, `partnerReferral` 8, `'partnerName' is a required
property` 6, `'oo-week' is not one of ['claims','confirmed',None]` 6.

**Why it matters.** The schema is the only mechanical statement of what a sync record IS, and
`lptcase.py check` is what other sessions trust. A validation that fails for 82% of its own
population cannot be used as a gate: it trains everyone to ignore the one field that would
catch a real error, exactly the way `error_log` did (`docs/... CUSTOMER-DATA-PLANE-DESIGN.md`).
The same pattern already has two precedent fixes: the root `$schema` key "the schema
previously forbade, so no record could validate", and `pricing`/`labEscalation` were opened to
`additionalProperties` because records were already carrying those blocks.

**What would fix it.** A three-way reconciliation, done once, in one pass, not record by
record:
1. Classify every failure into (a) a real field the schema should declare, (b) a legacy
 duplicate of a field that now exists under another name, (c) a genuinely invalid value.
2. For (a) declare it — a closed schema that rejects the majority of its own corpus is
 wrong about the corpus or about itself, and here the records describe reality. For (b)
 migrate the value onto the canonical key and drop the legacy one, recording the move. For
 (c) fix the record.
3. Make the count a tracked number: `0 invalid` or a named, shrinking list. Never re-measure
 from scratch — the first honest count is the expensive part and it is now done.

**Not urgent, genuinely bounded, and mine.** It is roughly 320 records over one schema, which
is a fleet job (partition by file) rather than a session job. It is recorded here so the
number is not rediscovered.

**Blast radius today is small** — nothing consumes the schema as a gate, so no bad data is
flowing because of it. The cost is the missing detector, not a live error. That is why it did
not block the Heichal work.
