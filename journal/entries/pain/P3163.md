## P3163 — The archive pull had no free-space check (fixed), and the queued reclaim would have deleted the git rescue

MEASURED 2026-10-13, read-only, on the authority. Two things that change the plan.

## 1. The free-space guard the archive pull never had

`scripts/transcript-archive-pull.py` stages every machine's tar in
`SPOOL = <archive root>/incoming` -- the SAME filesystem as the company database -- and
`scripts/transcript-archive-run.sh` runs up to ROUNDS=3 of MAX_BYTES=3,000,000,000 each.
**One pass could stage 9 GB, and neither file contained a single free-space check.**

That is the arithmetic behind the 2026-10-13 disk swing: 92% / 6.1 GB free measured by me at
13:2xZ, and `disk-guard` CRITICAL at **98.7% / 0.0 GB free** at 13:00:03Z, with `prune-backups`
logging `FLOOR BREACHED ... Pruned NOTHING; investigate what filled the disk.` The box that fills
is the sole writer of the company database.

FIXED: `transcript-archive-run.sh` now computes a floor (max-bytes + a 2 GB reserve) and REFUSES to
start when free space is below it, re-checking before every round so rounds cannot stack into a
full disk. It also now resolves SCRIPTS across the three plausible checkouts -- the old default
`/home/zabz/code/harness-config/scripts` is right on the office box and wrong on the authority,
where the checkout is `/root/code/harness-config`, so a cron entry inheriting it would fail with
"no such file" and record rc=127 in a ledger nobody reads.

VERIFIED with stub scripts in a temp tree: absurd budget -> refuse, exit 3, full diagnostic
printed; small budget -> runs the pull, writes status.json, exit 0.

HONEST LIMIT: the guard bounds the pull; it does not by itself explain the 0.0 GB reading, which
needs a pass with a larger effective budget or another writer at that minute. It removes the
mechanism by which this job can be that writer.

## 2. The queued reclaim recommendation would destroy irreplaceable work

Owner-queue row **#484** asks to "reclaim about 5 GiB of shift-created scratch on the authority"
and recommends "reclaim only the dated, already-on-origin scratch (/root/git-rescue-2026101...)".

**`/root/git-rescue-20261012` IS NOT ON ORIGIN. That is the whole point of it.** Its own manifest:
*"Created by the session that read docs/mesh/189-GIT-AUDIT-EVERY-MACHINE.md, which found 24 items
whose work existed in exactly one place. NOTHING was merged, rebased, pruned or pushed: these are
read-only copies made with `git bundle create`."* 21 bundles, **21/21 verified by cloning**, and it
captures `refs/stash` explicitly because `--all` omits it. 2.4 GB.

**If the owner had said yes and an agent followed that recommendation, 2.4 GB of uniquely-held work
would have been deleted** -- the exact failure this estate has already paid for twice, and the same
shape as the archive stall: a rescue created because work lived in one place, proposed for deletion
by someone who did not read what it was.

## 3. And what that means for the archive

The archive is 5.5 GB and growing (measured on the frozen box). The authority has 7.0 GB free.
**Even reclaiming everything plausible does not fit it.** So the archive cannot simply be re-hosted
on the authority: it needs a bigger disk, an offsite store (the estate already holds Cloudflare R2
with a bucket-scoped token and a restic repo), or another host. Row #473 already asks the disk
question with the recommendation "enlarge the disk".

**Do not run a pull that writes 5.5 GB onto this disk.** The guard will now refuse it, which is the
correct behaviour and not a bug to work around.
