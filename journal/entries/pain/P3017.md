## P3017 — Cash money cannot be recorded in production: payment set requires a Stripe charge id, so a paid cash deposit reads as collected 0.00 on the work order

**Measured 2026-10-11, order WO26101112743 (Fisher SD card).** A customer paid a **50 non-refundable
cash deposit at the counter**, and production cannot be made to say so.

`lpt-write payment set` **requires** `--stripe-charge ch_...` and validates the amount against that
Stripe charge before writing. There is no cash, Zelle or manual path on the command. So after a correct,
canonical write, `lpt ticket WO26101112743` prints:

```
payment PENDING
collected USD 0.00 in SUCCEEDED transactions
```

while 50 dollars is physically in the drawer. The mismatch is invisible to any staff member reading the
work order, and `lpt ticket`'s "collected" line is exactly the field the case skill tells agents to trust
for money received ("money received is `sum(payment_transactions.amount) WHERE status='SUCCEEDED'`").

**Mitigation used, and it is only a mitigation:** the deposit is stated in a work-order note (note 3119),
worded so the next reader does not mistake `collected 0.00` for "no money taken". A note is not a money
record — it does not reach the payment field, the ledger, or any revenue total.

**What would fix it:** a `payment set` path for non-card money that goes through the same single writer
(`website_db`), records the amount and the method (cash / Zelle / check), and still refuses an amount
with no evidence. Until then every cash intake in the shop is understated in production, and the
direction of the error is the dangerous one: the books say less money came in than did.

**Related, same session:** `orders` has no `phone` column, and `customers` has no `name` column —
`firstName`/`lastName`. Quoted camelCase identifiers are stripped by PowerShell when passed as a literal
argument to `lpt sql` ("column firstname does not exist"); pass the SQL through a variable, or use
`to_jsonb(t)->>'camelKey'`.
