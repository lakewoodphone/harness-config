## P6340 — The automated payment reconciliation wrote 62 FALSE 'could not be reconciled' verdicts onto live orders

# The automated payment reconciliation wrote 62 FALSE "could not be reconciled" verdicts onto live orders

Measured 2026-10-14. This is the estate's most-corrected failure — `wrong-data` — committed by an
automated pass, onto real customer orders, and it is invisible unless someone re-checks the arithmetic.

## What happened

At **12:14:14 - 12:14:22 UTC on 2026-10-14**, an automated pass wrote **254** notes onto customer
orders in **eight seconds**. Each is headed `PAYMENT RECONCILIATION 2026-10-14` and reviews a closing
note's card claim against the shop's Stripe account. **78** of them concluded `COULD NOT BE RECONCILED`.
**None** concluded `reconciled`.

## The defect

The pass searched Stripe for the **untaxed** amount. Card charges in this shop are taken **with New
Jersey sales tax at 6.625 %**, so a job quoted at 290 is charged **309.21**, one at 150 is charged
**159.94**, one at 45 is charged **47.98**. A search for `290.00` therefore cannot find a real charge
of `309.21`, and the pass reported an order as unreconciled for the exact reason that the money *was*
collected.

The excuse does not survive the record: the shop's own total set, and its Stripe amounts, carry the
multiplier on nearly every charge. The signature is unmistakable once seen — 5331 = 50 x tax,
7997 = 75 x tax, 11729 = 110 x tax, 12795 = 120 x tax, 15994 = 150 x tax.

## The measured scale

Re-checking all 78 against both the untaxed and the tax-inclusive figure within one day of each claim:

| verdict | n |
|---|---|
| **FALSE NEGATIVES** — a tax-inclusive charge exists anyway | **62** |
| genuinely unmatched at either figure | 11 |
| could not be parsed | 5 |

So **62 live orders carry a note telling the next reader the card payment could not be reconciled,
when the payment is present in Stripe at the taxed amount.** The affected order ids are written to
`_lpt-wo-sweep/closeout/false_unreconciled_orders.json` with the matching charge id and date for each,
so a correction pass does not have to re-derive them.

## The one that was caught in the act

`WO2609040001` (order 2981, Yehuda Pinter). Note 2791 of 2026-09-04 claims *"paid $290 via card"*.
The automated note 3950 on the same order says *"COULD NOT BE RECONCILED. No successful Stripe charge
of $290.00 was found"*. The charge exists: **`ch_3UBzHiLnlgYj1gmT1omeKAN3`, 309.21 USD, created
2026-09-04 ~15:55 UTC, succeeded, not refunded, terminal** — and it was taken about **ten minutes
after** the closing note was written. 309.21 is 290 plus tax. A correction note was written to that
order on 2026-10-14 documenting the match and its limit (the charge's own description is only
"sale", so it is matched on amount and timestamp, not on a name).

## The rules that follow

1. **When matching a "paid X by card" claim against Stripe, always search `X x 1.06625` as well as
 `X`.** A search for the untaxed figure reports paid orders as unreconciled, and **that false
 negative is indistinguishable from a real one** — which is what makes it dangerous.
2. **A false negative is worse than no answer.** "No charge found" reads as "the customer did not
 pay"; 62 orders were left one human glance away from being chased for money they had already paid.
3. **An automated note is a claim, not evidence.** The note itself cites its source and its charge
 count and still got this wrong, because the *method* was wrong. Cite the arithmetic, not the tool.
4. Anything that writes 254 notes in eight seconds onto customer records needs the arithmetic of its
 matching re-derived from the data before it runs, not after.
