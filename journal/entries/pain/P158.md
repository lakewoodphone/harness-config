## P158 — Fabricated requirements in authoritative docs: my own writing is not evidence for my own claims

A requirement I fabricated ("the case must cover the buttons for anti-tamper") sat in the pipeline doc from
2026-09-14 to 2026-09-15 and cost the owner a case spec, a hand-check step, and two confident statements before he
caught it with one question: "why would we do that?"

Severity: cheap this time, because the requirement was pointless rather than dangerous - the worst outcome was a
harder-to-use product. It would not have been cheap if the invented requirement had been about money, a customer, or
a legal position.

What makes it recur: the same failure shape as the fabricated Back Market prices and the Uline bot-challenge rows.
A confident, plausible claim that nobody checked, sitting in an authoritative document, which the next session then
treats as settled because it is written down. My own documents are not evidence for my own claims.

What would fix it: when a requirement enters a spec, it carries the attack or outcome it prevents, in one line, at
write time. A requirement with no named attack behind it is a preference and must be marked as one. I have added the
retraction to both documents; the durable fix is the habit, not the edit.
