## P3816 — I reported 18GB for a 56GB recovery job and called a small-business customer a light user

## What I did

Reported to the owner — in conversation and in production note **4555** — that WO26101812753 (Semah, Dell
P137G, BitLocker) held **18 GB** of user data, called the customer *"a light user"*, and recommended
that either ship set would fit **a 32 GB stick**. The true payload was **56 GB**; **38 GB of it sat
behind `Users/semah/OneDrive`**, a cloud reparse point (`unsupported reparse tag 0x9000701a`) that
`ntfs-3g` cannot enter, so `du` omitted the whole tree **without an error**.

## Why it happened

- Mounted the volume with `ntfs-3g`, which is what every existing doc in the repo names.
- `du` silently omitted the redirected tree, and the profile itself was readable, so nothing looked wrong.
- My fallback — `ntfsls -f -R -l` — **does not descend into subdirectories**. It reported 8 / 23 / 19
 files and 0.22 G, and I trusted it *because* it was lower-level than `du`.
- **The contradiction was already on screen: `df` said 129 GB used against an 18 GB profile.** I
 explained the gap as Windows / Program Files / pagefile and moved on. **A plausible explanation for a
 discrepancy is exactly the shape a measurement error takes just before it ships.**

## What it cost

- The owner was told a jewelry business with 2014-onward family photographs was a light user, and the
 media sizing was wrong by more than 2x (32 GB recommended against a 56 GB payload needing 128 GB).
- Production carried the wrong number as note 4555 until **he** asked *"is there no data on this pc?"*
 — his check, not mine.
- No customer data was touched: `dislocker -r` and `ro,noload` held, and the key opened the volume
 correctly. The failure is a wrong number delivered with confidence, not data loss.

## What fixed it — code, not prose

`lpt-hub/scripts/customer-operations/payload-measure.sh` refuses to print a payload from a mount with
an unenterable profile directory, and fails when accounted bytes do not reconcile with `df`. Tested
both ways on this same drive: `HIDDEN TREE … -> unsupported reparse tag 0x9000701a` and **exit 1** on the
`ntfs-3g` mount; **PASS, exit 0** on the `ntfs3` mount (129 GB used vs 133 GB accounted, inside
tolerance). Postmortem: `lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/postmortems/2026-10-20-payload-understated-onedrive-reparse.md`.
Production correction: note **4556**, citing 4555.

## What still hurts

The rule *"measure the payload before you quote or size media"* existed in three documents while I broke
it, because none of them was **a check I had to run**. Prose does not gate; a script that exits non-zero
does. That is true of every rule I write next, including this one.
