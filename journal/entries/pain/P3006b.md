## P3006b — The integrity check cannot run on the authority, and D2655 already decided what not to do about it

2026-10-13. Chased the one clock condition left open, and the journal stopped me from proposing the
thing the owner had already refused.

THE CONDITION. `guard-registry` reported `db-integrity status file does not exist:
/root/.db-integrity/status.json`, 178 times. Cause: `db-integrity-check` is scheduled WEEKLY
(`17 4 * * 0`) and `guards.yaml` documents that deliberately with a 7-day max age to match - but the
box came up at the 2026-10-11 05:43 cutover, AFTER that Sunday's 04:17 slot, so it has never had a
first run. Measured: nothing for it in `~/.cron-status/runs.tsv`.
Ran it by hand, which is what the next Sunday would have done. The status file now EXISTS, and the
guard moved from "does not exist" to `status ok=False` - an honest condition instead of a missing
file. What the check said:
 ok: false, source: "refused"
 "not enough free space for a consistent copy: 8.9 GiB free, 33.6 GiB required ... The live
 database was not read."

WHY, MEASURED. `/root/authority.db` is **23.66 GiB** on a 75 GB disk that is **88 per cent full,
9.0 GB free**. By `dbstat`:
 dsh_session_events 8.56 GiB
 dsh_session_events_fts_content 8.47 GiB
 dsh_session_events_fts_data 3.49 GiB
 dsh_session_events_fts_docsize/idx 0.03 GiB
 TOTAL 20.55 GiB (of which FTS01 alone 11.99 GiB)
2,027,027 rows spanning 2026-09-11 to 2026-10-13, about 63,000 a day, from 8 machines and 6,804
sessions. The FTS5 shadow tables are DERIVABLE from `dsh_session_events.raw`.

THE JOURNAL STOPPED ME, and this is the part worth recording. My own first instinct was to prune the
table by age. `journal/entries/decisions/D2655.md`, 2026-09-28, records that this seat did exactly
that analysis two weeks ago and that the OWNER then said **"VS Code and DSH session data is the most
valuable data the business has or will have"** - a training corpus he intends to keep for years. The
pruning proposal was WITHDRAWN. The decision: **keep every row; reduce the cost of the COPY, not the
size of the record**, by excluding the FTS5 shadow tables from snapshots and rebuilding them on
restore. Had I not read D2655 I would have proposed deleting the single thing he has said matters
most, in a session where I had already been told twice that the record beats my inference.

D2655 IS STILL OPEN AND UNIMPLEMENTED, which is the actionable finding.
`/usr/local/bin/authority-offsite-backup.sh` (592 lines) streams the whole database page-by-page
through `sqlite_dbpage` to Cloudflare R2 and contains no mention of `fts`, `shadow` or `exclude` -
so every one of the 11.99 GiB of derivable index is uploaded on every run.

WHAT IS HEALTHY, verified rather than assumed: the offsite backup WORKS.
`authority-offsite-backup.service` ran 2026-10-12 at 03:13 (36 min) and 05:23 (27 min), finishing
successfully both times, and it verifies each object by reading it back from R2, gunzipping in one
streaming pass and checking the sha256 AND the SQLite file magic. So the authority is backed up, and
"no local snapshot" is a design change, not a failure - it just removed the input the integrity check
used to read.

STILL TO DECIDE, and it is genuinely the owner's because every route costs either money or data:
the check cannot run locally at 9 GB free with a 23.66 GiB database, and D2655 forbids shrinking the
data. The options are a bigger disk, D2655's own fix (exclude the derivable FTS so the copy shrinks by
half), or accepting the offsite backup's read-back verification in place of `PRAGMA quick_check`.
