## P245 — A deposit smaller than the lab's own non-refundable charge turns every failed job into a loss - the per-job margin rule does not catch it

**A non-refundable customer deposit that is smaller than the lab's own non-refundable charge converts every failed job into a cash loss, and the margin rule does not catch it.**

Found 2026-09-17 while pricing the Joe Fishman drives to the owner's rules (/drive deposit, >= net per drive after donor cost). The margin rule is satisfied (the drives net -495) while the failure case is still a loss, because the two are computed on different branches:

| | 2TB | 1TB |
|---|---|---|
| Failure: keep the deposit | +\ | +\ |
| 300DDR charges us anyway | -\ | -\ |
| Shipping both ways | -\ | -\ |
| **Position** | **-\** | **-\** |

At 85%/75% recovery odds the failures are expected (15% and 25%; ~36% of two-drive jobs have at least one), so this recurs. **A "profit per job" rule silently permits a loss per failed job** - the two numbers must be checked separately, and the deposit should be set against the *lab's* non-refundable exposure, not picked as a round customer-facing number.

Same trap exists on any lab route where the lab charges on failure: 300DDR charges a labour fee when sector recovery is 99-100% but nothing is readable, and shipping is never refunded.

**How to catch it next time:** for any success-fee job, write both branches down - success (quote + deposit - lab - donor - shipping) and failure (deposit - lab - shipping). A job can pass the success branch and fail the failure branch, and only the second one shows up as a loss.
