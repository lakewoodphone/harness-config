## P2943 — Six ported monitors can only alert a file nobody reads, and the external dead-man switch is not rebuilt yet

## WHAT HURTS

Six of the estate's freshness monitors, as ported to the new authority on 2026-10-11, can only tell a
file that nobody reads. `site-uptime-check-cron`, `disk-guard`, `lpt-freshness-watchdog`,
`production-comms-freshness`, `ai-gateway-health` and `lpt-deploy-drift` each write a line to
`/root/.cron-status/runs.tsv` and/or a log file under `/var/log`. There is no SMS, no owner-queue row,
no external dead-man switch, and no reader on the new authority. This is the exact shape of the
failure the estate has already paid for three times: a job that reports success while producing nothing
anyone sees.

## THE EVIDENCE

Measured by the porting workstream 2026-10-11 06:30-06:35Z on `secratary-cloud`: `disk-guard` printed
`SELFTEST OK ... / at 62.3%, warn 90% crit 95%` and, with the threshold lowered to 60 to prove it
fires, printed `"findings": ["/ at 62.3% (25.1 GB free, 25711 MB)"]` - and then wrote it to a log.
`production-comms-freshness` exits 1 from ~10:10Z every 15 minutes (`dialpad_sms: DEAD FEED`) and that
exit code reaches nothing outside its own ledger row. The external dead-man switch that used to be
the backstop stopped on 2026-10-10T22:45Z, so the 3 h 29 min authority outage on 11 October raised no
alarm anywhere (journal H3416, docs/mesh/135).

## WHAT WOULD FIX IT

One of these, in order of cost:
1. A per-run escalation path that already exists on the new authority: the app's own owner-notification
 endpoint, called by any monitor whose exit code is non-zero. The monitors already know they failed;
 they need somewhere to say so that a human reads.
2. A single "monitor ledger reader" that runs hourly, reads `/root/.cron-status/runs.tsv`, and raises
 ONE owner-queue row when any watched job has a non-zero rate over a window or has stopped running
 entirely. One reader, every monitor - rather than each monitor inventing a channel.
3. At minimum, an external dead-man ping per critical monitor, so that silence itself is a signal.
 This depends on the dead-man switch being rebuilt (in flight as this is filed).

The recommendation is 1 plus 2: the endpoint is a one-line curl from a job that already has the
failure in its hand, and the ledger reader converts every existing monitor into something that can
actually reach a person without touching any of them.

## RELATED

 it, together with a second pain: `owner-queue.py` does not exist on the cloud box, so monitors that

 collides with the single-writer rule.
- `~/.cron-status/runs.tsv` is the right ledger - it records every run's rc and duration - but nothing
 reads it on the new box. On the office box the readers were `pain-check` and `cron-target-guard`,
 both of which are among the jobs still being ported.
