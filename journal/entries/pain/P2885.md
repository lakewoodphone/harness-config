## P2885 — The mesh placement broker is one process on one node - a burst of dispatches takes the whole wave down

## The mesh placement broker is one process on one node, and a burst of dispatches takes the whole wave down

Measured 2026-10-09 over 32 dispatches in three waves. The broker runs at
`secratary`:`localhost:3091` and every `subagent` call goes through it over ssh. When that ssh hit a
banner-exchange timeout, dispatch failed outright:

```
remote-ssh: broker-unreachable: the placement broker could not be reached at
secratary-ts:http://localhost:3091/place — ssh exited 255: banner exchange: Connection to
UNKNOWN port -1: Connection timed out
```

Three batches failed, were re-dispatched in a fresh wave, failed again, and all succeeded on the
third attempt about ten minutes later — while `ssh secratary-ts hostname` answered in 0.9 s both
before and after. So the broker is the single point of failure for **every** subagent on the mesh,
and its failure looks identical to a dead node.

Contributing: the broker host is also a placement target, and at load ~14.7 with ten node processes
the ssh handshakes contend. Waves of ten (the background-job cap) rather than twenty-four, and
pacing the dispatches, is what worked.

**What would fix it:** a second broker, or a health probe that distinguishes "the broker did not
answer" from "the node did not answer" and retries the placement without counting it as a failed
child. Until then the rule is: **a batch that fails at the broker returns to the queue** — check the
job list, re-dispatch, and never assume a failed dispatch means the work was done.
