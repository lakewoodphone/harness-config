## P3002 — eBay sends ONE confirmation for several order numbers, and the store models one row per order

2026-10-13. The reason the 125 marketplace orders have no total is not a parser bug and not a
scraping difficulty. It is an EMAIL SHAPE the store does not model, and getting it wrong would have
triple-counted money. Recorded so the next session implements the real fix instead of re-deriving
this.

THE PROOF. One email, `1a0ab8f2597ad7`, subject "Your order is confirmed", mentions:
 21-15155-16873 4 times
 21-15155-16874 2 times
 21-15155-16875 2 times
 "The Apartment" 1 time
 "This Is Not a" 1 time
 "Gamera" 1 time
So a SINGLE eBay confirmation covers several seller order numbers, and the figure it prints -
"Order total: $82.42" - is the total for the WHOLE email, not for any one of them.

WHY THAT MATTERS. The store is one row per order_id. Writing 82.42 onto each of the three rows
records 247.26 for one 82.42 purchase, and the matcher would then have three orders each claiming to
be a card charge that only exists once. That is not a rounding error; it is the books inventing
money. Two of those rows were written during this round and have been reverted.

HOW I ALMOST SHIPPED IT, and what caught it. Two orders with different items and different order
ids both resolved to 82.42. I read that as "scraping a shared template block" and reverted; the
truth turned out to be "one email, correctly parsed, covering both". The alarm was RIGHT for a
reason I had not yet found - which is the useful part. A cross-subject control does not tell you
WHY two records agree; it tells you not to write until you know.

THE ACTUAL FIX, for whoever picks this up. The email carries ITEM-level prices - sampled figures in
the body include 20.97 and 24.47 - so each order's total is derivable from ITS OWN items, which
`marketplace_order_sync._total_from_items()` already does correctly (W2746). What is missing is ITEM
EXTRACTION from this email shape: these orders hold zero items, which is why there is nothing to sum.
Two pieces of work, in this order:
 1. teach the parser that one confirmation may carry several order numbers and must attribute each
 ITEM to the order number it sits beside in the email
 2. only then will `_total_from_items` produce a per-order total, and 144 orders stop being a
 special case
Until step 1 exists, "Order total" from a multi-order confirmation must be REFUSED, not recorded.
