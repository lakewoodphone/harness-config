## P2882 — lpt audit's N4 predates the seven-state design and reports 600 finished orders as problems

## `lpt audit`'s N4 predates the seven-state design by two days and reports 600 finished orders as problems

`lpt_ops/audit.py` classifies `READY_FOR_PICKUP` as **open**, so its N4 check fires:

* `ready for pickup, never collected` — **400 findings**, every one a terminal order with a
 `completedDate`;
* `open and untouched` — **200 findings** on terminal rows.

Measured 2026-10-09: the whole audit is **1,077 findings, 725 high, 292 medium, 60 low** over 641
orders, and those two rules are **600 of them**. The design they contradict was settled by the owner
on 2026-10-07 — `order_status_seven_states` maps `COMPLETED` → `READY_FOR_PICKUP`, and the deployed
router still carries that alias table with the reason.

It matters beyond tidiness: a weekly audit that shouts about 600 non-problems is an audit nobody
reads, and the 111 real problems found on 2026-10-09 were found by the sweep, not by the audit.

**What would fix it:** the classifier's own stated principle is *"a status whose own rows mostly
carry a `completedDate` is closed"*. 372 of the 425 `READY_FOR_PICKUP` orders carry one (87%), so
the rule already implies the answer — it is simply not applied to that status. One change to the
classifier, plus a test that a status whose rows mostly carry a completion date classifies as
closed. `lpt-audit` is another workstream's entry point but `lpt_ops/audit.py` is shared; the fix is
small and the doc block at the top of `docs/audit.md` needs the same correction.
