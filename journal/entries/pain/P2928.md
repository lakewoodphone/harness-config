## The live production store lost a month of card receipts, and neither store is trustworthy alone

MEASURED 2026-10-08 on secratary, read-only, both stores named:

`operational` (the LIVE AWS RDS store, what api.lakewoodphoneandtech.com writes) holds **ZERO**
`payment_transactions` rows whose `createdAt` falls anywhere in September 2026. `app` (the frozen
Hetzner archive) holds 35. The missing set is exactly ids **3243-3267**, spanning 2026-09-04 to
2026-09-24, totalling **2,652.32**.

It is not a quiet month. In `operational` the same September holds 41 orders, 12,708
`communications` rows and 49 status-history rows. The independent check settles it: the accounting
ledger (`~/accounting-data/live/accounting.db`) records **18 Stripe payouts to the shop's bank
totalling 7,256.22 in September 2026**. The money moved; the live store does not show it.

The fork is wider than one table, measured by comparing both stores:

- 13,162 `communications` rows exist only in `operational` (11,889 of them September) — `app` is short.
- 12 payment ids (3273-3284, October, 871.34) exist only in `operational`.
- **257 of the 337 shared payment ids carry a DIFFERENT STATUS in each store.**
- 46 order numbers exist only in `app` (37 native, quoted 1,103.00).

Classified: the payment gap is **a genuine loss in `operational`**, not merely a cutover artifact —
the cutover row/order maps (`reconcile_row_map`, `reconcile_order_map`, applied 2026-10-06
18:04-19:29) explain the communications, note and id-collision differences, but they do not explain
why September payments never arrived.

WHY IT MATTERS: every "how much money came in" answer is understated by an unknown amount for
September 2026, and the standard advice — "read the live store" — is wrong here. Two sessions have
now independently hit this: the growth-report repair found September money received = 0.00 against
8,565.22 of accounting-verified revenue, and the integrity audit found the same hole from the other
direction.

NOT DETERMINED: the exact September total `operational` lost (the ledger's 61 September charges sum
to 7,347.69 while `app` can only match 4,018.49, leaving 3,329.20 in neither store); row-level
ledger-to-store matching (the ledger stores `ch_` ids, the stores store `pi_`); and which store is
right for the 13 colliding ids.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT: a decision about which record is authoritative for money — the bank/ledger, or the
shop's own order system — and then a one-time repair from `app` plus a guard that compares the two
stores' payment rows by day and fails loudly when one is short. Neither has been done. Nothing was
written; this is read-only throughout.
