## P263 — dialpad_sms_cache rewrites created_at on every re-sync, so old outbound texts read as if sent today

MEASURED 2026-09-18 02:20Z on the authority. The Weitman thread (8483336341) shows five outbound/inbound rows stamped 2026-09-17T11:44:52Z-11:46:53Z with fetched_at 11:54:09Z, bodies from 2026-09-09 ('I will check and get back to you'), 2026-09-10 ('temporary fix ... put out for pickup') and 2026-09-15 ('We do not have cheaper than 390'). Every row in that block shares the same fetched_at to the millisecond, which no live capture does. Contrast the genuinely live rows from the same day: sends to 5163100303, 7325036369, 8455707067, 3473069402, 7329791377, 8483291230 all have fetched_at = created_at + 1-2 seconds. So created_at in this table is the IMPORT time for re-imported rows and the send time only for rows captured live at the moment they happened.

WHY IT MATTERS: the owner looked at this thread, saw 11:46 today, and said 'I think we texted her today'. He had not - today's real outbound went to six other numbers, none hers. The only new thing in that thread is her inbound at 2026-09-18T01:34:57Z ('Would go for 390 one'). A record that makes old outbound traffic look like fresh contact will make a session tell the owner a customer was answered when she was not. Same class as the stale-database crisis: reading a wrong timestamp and believing it.

RULE: never order dialpad_sms_cache by created_at and never quote a created_at as a send time. For 'did we contact X, and when', trust fetched_at only where it is within ~5 s of created_at (live capture); treat the rest as undated. The authority's /api/v1/comms/by-phone/<n> endpoint carries the same flaw - it reads this table's created_at into its 'when' field.

FIX, not yet done, and ours: preserve Dialpad's own message timestamp on re-import instead of overwriting it. The importer is discarding the provider field. Add provider_created_at, write it on every sync, and make every reader use it. Until then the comms record has no trustworthy send time older than the last live capture.
