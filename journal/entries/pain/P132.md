## P132 — Two sessions can migrate and audit this journal at once, and the lock does not cross machines

Symptom: for the two hours of this rebuild, a second agent session was migrating the same journal to the same new format, on the same checkout, at the same time. Its tree ended up with 1,238 files, 563 of them with a file name, marker id and heading id that disagreed, a cache missing the pain and decisions rows, and ids allocated from a ceiling that ignored `log/**`. One session's `append` also held the lock while the other read.

Cost: the whole session, and a rebuild of the derived tree from the frozen sources. Nothing was lost - `log/**` and the six flat files were never touched - but the damage was invisible until it was measured.

Why it happened: the mutation lock (`.lock`) lives in the working tree, so it excludes two processes in ONE checkout and nothing across machines or across two sessions that each have their own checkout on one machine.

Fix, done: one allocation ceiling over every source; the whole claim set built before any id is assigned; identity-based absorption; `check` that re-parses the files and alarms on a name/marker/heading disagreement.

Fix, NOT done: a cross-machine lock or central id allocation is still missing. Candidate: `owner-queue`-style locking on the authority (a lock file plus a monotonic id reservation served over ssh), with the local ceiling kept as the offline fallback. Until then two machines can still pick the same number, and only `check` will say so.
