## P6309 — The sync-record contract is strict, and nothing has ever enforced it: 300 of 423 records fail their own schema

PAIN - the sync-record contract exists, is strict, and nothing has ever enforced it.
Measured 2026-10-13 from ZABZ-TECH, all 423 files in lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/sync-records
validated with jsonschema against docs/customer-operations/CUSTOMER_CASE_SYNC_RECORD.schema.json:

 123 PASS 300 FAIL (71% invalid)

and lptcase check reports, separately, 201 sync-records-without-case and 37 cases-without-sync-record.

WHY THIS HURTS. The skill that governs this work (lpt-production-case-work, section 7) tells a
session to validate the record against that schema as part of the write-back duty. Nothing in the
estate does it automatically - not audit.py, not sync-health-audit.py, not the case index - so the
only time a record is ever validated is when a human or an agent chooses to. The two records
touched today (Fishman 3475346208, Weissman 8482993187) were both invalid, and both had been since
the day they were written. The Fishman one had a top-level `payment` key the root schema rejects,
four pricing keys inside the closed `operational` block, AND two fields carrying numbers that were
simply wrong (remainingOnFullSuccess 1600 where the truth is 740; depositIsCreditAgainstPrice false
where the owner's own recorded words say true) plus a syncHoldReason that was false.

THE SHAPE OF THE DEFECT. The schema is additionalProperties:false at the root and on
customer/device/workflow/referral/operational; only references/pricing/labEscalation/production are
open. The most common violations: operational.internalSummary empty (18); extra keys at the root -
case_file/case_id/status/date/notes/issue/filename, and a top-level `source` which 17 records carry
and every one of them fails on; referral.sheetTab outside its enum (16); workflow.smsConsentSource
null where the schema types it string (8). So a strict contract was written, and the records have
never conformed to it, and nothing said so.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT. One decision, then one migration. The decision is whether the schema should grow
(many of these keys are recorded reasoning that a reader genuinely needs and has nowhere legal to
live) or the records should shrink. Do NOT answer it by loosening the schema to make the count go
green, and do not answer it by deleting evidence. The convention adopted today is reversible and
loses nothing: park a key the closed blocks reject under
references.parkedBySchemaGap['<original path>'] with a note saying it was parked and why.
Then add the validation to whatever runs on these records, so a new record cannot be born invalid
and the 300 existing ones get a burn-down rather than another silent year.
