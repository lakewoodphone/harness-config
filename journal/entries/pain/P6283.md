## P6283 — The tax calendar was computed daily and read by nobody: six obligations past due, three never queued

2026-10-13. A tax calendar computed every morning and read by nobody. Six obligations past due, three
of them never once put to the owner.

THE FINDING. `/root/bin/tax-deadlines.py` is on the clock at 06:17, it ran today at 06:17 with exit 0,
and it writes `/root/.cfo/tax-deadlines.md`. Grepping for its own name across /root/bin found ONLY the
tool itself - **nothing reads that file.** What it was reporting:
 2026-09-15 28 day(s) PAST Federal estimated tax (1040-ES)
 2026-09-15 28 day(s) PAST Q3 Estimated Tax Due (federal + NJ)
 2026-10-15 2 day(s) 2025 Tax Return Due - EXTENSION DEADLINE
and a search of the ENTIRE `owner_decision_queue`, every status, for 1040 / estimated tax / 2025 tax
return / extension returned **ZERO rows**. Only #465 (NJ ST-50, 7 days) and #466 (NJ-927, 17 days) had
ever reached him, and both were filed today. **A green cron exit 0 while the most time-critical
obligations in the business never become a question is the failure shape this estate keeps
rediscovering, and tax is where it costs the most.**

FIXED IN TWO PARTS. Queued #475 [critical] for the 2025 return - two days away - and #476 [high] for the
28-days-past Q3 estimate, each with options and a recommendation and the measurement behind it. Then the
durable part: `/root/bin/tax-deadlines-to-queue.py`, which imports the deadline tool's own `build()` -
one calendar, no second implementation - and files ONE owner row per obligation that is past due or
inside 21 days, deduped by a stable key `taxdl:<name>:<due-date>` so a deadline is never asked twice and
next year's same-named deadline is a different key. Wired as `tax-queue` at 06:23, six minutes after the
calendar that feeds it. 54 cron-wrap jobs now.

AND THE DRY RUN SHOWED THE SCALE, which is worse than the digest's DUE NOW section suggested: TEN
obligations inside 21 days and SIX of them past - Federal 1040-ES 120 days, NJ ST-50 85 days, NJ-927 +
WR-30 75 days, the Gusto employer-number action 59 days, Federal 1040-ES 28 days, Q3 estimate 28 days.
Some are certainly filed already; the tool cannot know that and does not claim to. The point is that
they had never been asked.

AND MY OWN NEW TOOL DID THE THING I HAVE SPENT THIS SESSION DOCUMENTING. Its first `--apply` printed
"FILED 10" while `owner-queue.py`'s own similarity guard was answering "Nothing was written" for seven
of them - the pending count rose by FIVE, not ten, and the script counted the refusal string as success.
Fixed by reading the outcome from the WRITER'S OWN OUTPUT rather than assuming it: it now reports
`FILED 0, refused-by-queue 7, already-pending 3` and is idempotent on a second run. **The lesson is
mine as much as the estate's: a tool that reports success while a downstream guard refuses is the same
defect as the cron that exited 0 into a file nobody read - and I wrote one an hour after diagnosing
one.**
