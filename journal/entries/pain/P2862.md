## P2862 — Production's comms ingest duplicates messages: four real SMS became 168 rows, and the duplication reads as a two-month conversation that never happened

**Recorded 2026-10-08 (ZABZ-YOGA), found while pulling one customer's comms for the LPT work-orders
walk. Ranked by what it costs: it silently destroys the answer to "what did we tell this customer",
which is the question the whole archive exists to answer.**

**The measurement.** mrs vahab, customer 2214, in production `communications`:

| content | copies | first | last |
|---|---|---|---|
| IN "Hi my name is sori vahab, I dropped off my laptop for repair last week and was just wondering if there are any updates? Thanks" | **36** | 2026-08-05 15:08 | 2026-08-24 12:06 |
| OUT "Hi. The latest update is that the laptop is looking good. We will confirm tomorrow beh" | **44** | 2026-08-05 18:35 | 2026-09-02 19:04 |
| IN "Ok ty" | **44** | 2026-08-05 18:38 | 2026-09-02 19:04 |
| OUT "Sure" | **44** | 2026-08-05 18:40 | 2026-09-02 19:04 |

**Four real messages. 168 rows.** The customer's actual contact with this business is one exchange on
5 August; the rest is re-ingest. Each copy carries its own `createdAt`, so the duplicated sequence
*reads as a two-month conversation* — a reader sees 44 days of "we'll confirm tomorrow" and "Ok ty",
and concludes the customer was being kept informed. Nobody has texted her since 5 August. The copies
are identifiable: they carry `ptf-comm-*` refs beside the original Dialpad message ids, and they
cluster seconds apart in the same order on each day.

**Not a one-off.** The same shape is already visible through `lpt ticket`'s own folding: it reports
"110 for the customer; showing 9 distinct; 101 duplicate row(s) folded away" for klugman, "275 for the
customer; showing 17 distinct; 258 duplicate row(s)" for bornstein, "180 ... 12 distinct ... 168
duplicate" for vahab. So production's comms tables carry roughly an order of magnitude more rows than
messages.

**Why it matters beyond tidiness.** Three concrete failures, all already observed:

1. **`count` is not contact.** Any reading that treats a row count (or a "last contacted" date) as
 evidence of how much we have spoken to a customer is wrong by ~10x, and wrong in the direction
 that looks healthy. The last-activity date this produces is the last *ingest*, not the last
 message. `WO2607160004` and `WO2607240002` both had their "recent activity" produced this way.
2. **The archive answers the wrong question confidently.** `ps_comms_search` / `ps_comms_person` read
 this lineage; a query for "what did we quote them" returns the same sentence dozens of times and
 buries the one real message.
3. **It hid a live business failure.** Vahab's case looked like a customer in steady contact for two
 months. It is a customer who was quoted 340 on 6 August, said she would ask her husband, and was
 then not contacted again for 55 days.

**What would fix it.** Identify the writer that inserts with a `ptf-comm-*` ref for content already
present, and give it an idempotency key (the Dialpad message id it already has in hand) instead of a
fresh one per poll — the same class of fix as the PBP migration's "one writer" rule. Then dedupe the
existing rows. Until either happens, **dedupe by content before reporting anything about how much
contact a customer has had**, and never read a comms row count as a measure of contact. The `lpt`
reader already folds duplicates for display, which is why the problem stayed invisible for so long:
the tool that people look at was hiding it rather than surfacing it.
