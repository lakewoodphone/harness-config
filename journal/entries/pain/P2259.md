## P2259 — The Shlock cover's middle 6 and side 14 hold-downs are unsolved: every scheme jams, grips, or holds the bunch open, and the owner is unhappy

**P-SH1 - The Shlock rain cover's middle six and side fourteen attachment points cannot be held down without either jamming, gripping, or refusing to let the cover gather. Unsolved, 2026-09-23, and the owner is unhappy about it.**

**Symptom.** A retractable 28.5 x 12 ft cover on eight rails has 20 attachment grommets - 14 along its two short side
edges and 6 in the middle over rails 3 and 6. None of them can be secured. Each attempt failed on one of three
grounds: it **jams** (a short, loose bearing cocks and bites the pipe), it **grips** (a rope loop is a capstan, so
its drag equals the load it holds - ~74 lb per point at the design load), or it **holds the cover open** (any rigid
part's length along a rail, times the number of points on that rail, becomes the tightest the cover can ever gather
to - three middle points per rail at 2 in = 6 in that still projects past the house when the cover is fully in).

**Owner's constraint, in his words in substance.** Nothing rigid on any rail, because of that multiplication. He
rejected a 1-in collar (7 points x 1 in = 7 in) and a 2-in collar (3 x 2 in = 6 in) on exactly this ground.

**Consequence while it is unsolved.** The cover is held on 16 points, so the deploy rule is capped at 25 mph instead
of 40; the two side edges and the middle are free to lift and billow between the leading-edge hangers and the house
straps; and the 20 holes stay open, which is at least right for drainage (they are the only interior drains).

**Evidence.** Nine independent adversarial audits on 2026-09-23 (journal H2529); the owner's own on-site finding that
the bought 2.00-in hitching rings "just fit" the 1.660-in rail and friction-lock when pulled; and
`lpt-hub/research/miscellaneous/2026-09-23-shlock-decisions.md` §6, which lists the ten schemes tried and the exact
ground each failed on.

**What would fix it, honestly, and still unexplored:** (1) a **continuous guide or channel** along each side and the
two middle lines - the approach every real sliding cover uses (awning, greenhouse screen, pool cover): the edge runs
captured for its whole length, so there is no discrete part on a rail and therefore no bundle floor at all. The
obstacle to solve is the leading-edge pole crossing the channel line once per cycle. (2) **A shallower cover**
(e.g. 342 x 120 in) so the gathered strip sits over the eave instead of over the schach, which also removes the
halachic and drainage questions left over from the 36-in gather. Both are off-season design work.

**Why it matters.** This is the difference between a cover that is safe to leave up at 40 mph and one that must be
furled at 25, and it is the one place in the project where the owner's own constraint and the physics are in direct
conflict - which is exactly why it should not be quietly marked "done".
