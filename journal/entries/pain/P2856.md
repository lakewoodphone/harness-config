## P2856 — CI cancels whichever job crosses about 15 minutes, and nothing declares a 15-minute limit

## PAIN — CI cancels whichever job crosses ~15 minutes, and nothing declares a 15-minute limit

**Measured, repeatedly, on `lakewoodphone/kosher-filter-ai` (a user-owned PRIVATE repo):**

| job | duration | conclusion | steps recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| server-tests | 14m10s | success | 14 |
| build-image | 15m03s | **cancelled** | **0** |
| postgres-runtime-tests | 15m02s | **cancelled** | **0** |
| server-tests | 15m28s | success | (one outlier) |

`cancelled` with ZERO steps recorded, at almost exactly the same wall clock, on a run nobody re-ran -
so it is not a test failure, not a merge conflict, and not a re-run artefact. `gh pr checks` renders
it as **"fail"**, which is how it gets misread as a code problem: I reported it that way myself, then
talked myself out of it as "probably my own re-runs", and then a fresh run reproduced it.

**No workflow declares `timeout-minutes`.** Grepped every file in `.github/workflows/`. So the limit is
not coming from the repository.

**Verified by reading the log rather than the status:** the job that PASSED ends with
`Post job cleanup.` and `Cleaning up orphan processes.` The cancelled ones have no steps at all.

**Why it hurts.** The trunk gate is the thing every other claim rests on. A gate that silently
cancels long jobs is worse than a slow one: it reports failure for work that may be fine, it trains
people to trust `gh pr checks` less, and as the suite grows more of it falls past the wall. The suite
is at ~1543 tests and takes 10-14 minutes; the two Docker/Postgres jobs sit right on the line.

**What would fix it, in order:**
1. Make the two long jobs fit. They are long because they build the image and run the Postgres parity
 suite; sharding or caching the image would put them under the wall regardless of its cause.
2. Find the actual limit. The plausible candidate is GitHub Actions minutes for a user-owned private
 repository, but `/users/lakewoodphone/settings/billing/actions` answers 404 with this token, so the
 quota is **not verifiable from here** and I will not assert it. Whoever has the account can read it
 in Settings -> Billing.
3. Until then, read `conclusion` from the API, never the `gh pr checks` word "fail". The distinction
 between cancelled and failed is the difference between a platform limit and broken code.
