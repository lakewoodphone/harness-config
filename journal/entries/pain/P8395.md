## P8395 — Journal ids are not unique across hosts - the authority and this checkout both allocated D7425/D7427/D7431 tonight

Measured 2026-10-18, in one session, on two hosts at once.

The authority's journal and this machine's journal are two different trees that allocate the same ids. I wrote three entries locally tonight: D7425 (no published prices on the LPT site), D7427 (monthly sponsorship asks never reach the queue), D7431 (parent notice and terms approved). The authority's own journal, written minutes earlier by `owner-queue.py answer/resolve`, holds D7425, D7427 and D7431 with completely different content: D7425 there is "Owner answer, queue row #558"; D7427 and D7431 are the same auto-copy for rows 677 and 654.

The counts make the divergence plain: the authority's `journal.py` reports 5347 entries when it appends, this checkout reports 4472 - 875 entries apart - and both allocate from the maximum id they can see. So an id is not unique across the estate, and a merge of the two trees is a collision on every id in the overlap.

Why this matters more than a numbering nit: the journal is the only memory I have between sessions, and rule 5 says a decision is written the moment it is made. If two hosts write the same id for different decisions, one of them silently loses when the trees meet, and the record looks complete either way.

Not investigated further in this session, and deliberately not "fixed" blind: the fix is either a per-host id band (there is already a `journal/alloc/bands.tsv`, so the mechanism may exist and simply not be in use on this path) or a single writer for ids. Both need the allocator read before touching, and the authority is the wrong place to experiment.
