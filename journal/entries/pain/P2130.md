## P2130 — P2137 - run_once exceeds its own 600s ceiling under load, so ticks are abandoned rather than completed

Measured 2026-09-20 on the authority.

 ERROR app.autopilot: run_once timed out after 600s â€” ABANDONING it (the thread keeps running;
 it cannot be killed) and applying backoff
 WARNING app.autopilot: run_once failed, backoff 300.0s (x1.0)

The latch fix (W2144/W2146) makes this survivable - the loop continues instead of wedging - but the
underlying fact is that **a `run_once` tick takes more than ten minutes on this box**, and 5 of 6 hours
produced no completed run earlier today.

Contributing measurements from the same window:
 - load average reached **7.88 on 4 cores** while the full test suite ran;
 - `1.8 GB` was already in swap and free memory fell under 1 GB during that period;
 - `app.database: _get_conn: _db_lock contended for key sqlite:///data/secretary.db` appears repeatedly
 during the run - the connection lock is being held long enough to be logged as contention;
 - `/api/v1/next-stage/systems-custody/node-report` returned a 500 with `database is locked` at 18:00.

This is the same root as P2127/P2132: **six writers on one 9.9 GB SQLite file with no scheduler and
4 cores**, now with an added load source (a full test suite) that is itself part of the problem. Note
that the suite was created by me for verification and it slowed the company while it ran - a real
cost that belongs in the record, not a footnote.

**What I did NOT do:** raise the 600 s ceiling. That would hide an over-long tick rather than shorten
it, and it would make a wedged tick less visible. The fix is to make `run_once` faster or to stop it
running unserialised against batch writers - i.e. the scheduling work, which is a design decision for
the next round rather than a constant to nudge.
