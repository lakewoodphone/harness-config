## P2550 — The engine crashed and the only witness was a stack trace at the tail of a log file

RANKED, 2026-09-25, ZABZ-YOGA.

P1 - THE ENGINE DIES ON AN UNHANDLED ERROR AND NOTHING SAYS SO.
Root cause found and fixed (crash on ENOENT when its %TEMP% spill dir is deleted
underneath it), but the DETECTION gap remains: at 15:32 the health loop printed
"healthy: all 1 enabled engine(s) listening", the engine crashed at ~15:36, and
the only record is a stack trace at the tail of a 400-line stderr log plus two
lines in engine-recovery.log. Nothing surfaces "the engine died and restarted" as
a fact - not to the owner, not to the next session, not to a dashboard.
FIX: a restart detector. Compare the engine pid/port against the last recorded
one on every `dshw health` pass and, when it changed, read the stderr tail for an
unhandled exception and surface it loudly (journal + console). An engine that
dies should cost one line, not a forensic session.

P2 - LIVE STATE IS KEPT IN THE OS TMPDIR.
dsh-subprocess-local mkdtemps under `os.tmpdir()` and Windows' cleanup deletes
that tree without asking any process. Fixed for the engine by routing TEMP/TMP
into DSH_HOME/tmp, but the same pattern will bite anything else that spills to
%TEMP% and expects it to persist. Test: any directory the harness expects to
survive an hour must not be under %TEMP% or %LOCALAPPDATA%\Temp.

P3 - DISK FELL TO 8% FREE AND THAT IS WHAT PULLED THE TRIGGER.
36 GB free of 476 GB. That is what made Storage Sense run aggressively, which is
what deleted the spill directory. Now 61 GB after its cleanup. Nothing watches
free space as a first-class signal on this machine, though it is the upstream
cause of this entire incident.

P4 - THE HEALTH LOOP WRITES ONE LOG FILE EVERY 5 MINUTES FOREVER.
900 files in ~/.dsh/multi-window/logs, one per pass, never pruned. 4 MB today so
this is hygiene, not a crisis - but it grows without bound and it is the same
shape as the corpus that was blamed tonight.

P5 - THE OVERLOAD GUARD IS NOT IN THE PATH.
admission_governor reports 0 of 24 slots leased while agent loops execute. It
could not have helped tonight (this was never a resource-saturation problem), but
a guard that nothing calls will not help the next time either.
