## P235 — P230 — The WAZE MDM renumber trail is nearly unrecoverable: health history keeps only 2 bulk snapshots and commands has no device column

**The WAZE MDM renumber trail is nearly unrecoverable, because `fleet_device_health_history` retains only two bulk snapshots and `commands` retains only ~30 wallpaper command rows.**

Measured 2026-09-17 while reconciling the owner's inventory sheet against the live registry:

- `fleet_device_health_history` holds **two bulk inserts and nothing else**: 35 rows stamped `2026-07-05 22:58:02.695927` (covering only 28 distinct devices) and 275 rows stamped `2026-09-11 18:51:11.x` (the whole current fleet). Both share a single identical microsecond timestamp, so neither is a live check-in series. Between those two instants there is **no per-device DRN history at all**.
- Consequence: 18 serials demonstrably changed DRN between those dates, but **intermediate renumber steps are invisible**. Of the 17 disputed sheet rows, the 2026-07-05 snapshot covers only 8; the other 9 have no pre-renumber record, and 6 of the covered ones match *neither* snapshot — i.e. they passed through a numbering state that was never recorded anywhere I can read.
- `commands` (nanomdm) retains only **30** `Settings-drn%` rows, all dated 2026-08-31 or later (drn 45, 2001, 2002). `commands` also has **no device column** (`command_uuid, request_type, command, created_at, updated_at`) and `command_results` has no device linkage either, so even the retained rows cannot be attributed to a physical device.
- `fleet_deployments.drn` was rewritten for **some** renumbered serials and not others (e.g. `drn 6 / J7RPQRYFW5` survives while `DD6WQ5YH12` shows at both 9 and 34 across tables), so it is not a trustworthy historical source either.

**What would fix it:** an append-only `drn_change_log` (old_drn, new_drn, serial, changed_at, actor, endpoint) written by `/renumber`, `/renumber-v2` and `/fleet/batch/renumber`; and a per-check-in DRN series in `fleet_device_health_history` keyed on serial rather than on the DRN, since the DRN is exactly the field that moves. Without one of those, any future "the numbers don't match" question costs a full manual reconstruction and still cannot be answered.

**Cheap interim mitigation:** make `/renumber` re-render the wallpaper and enqueue the Settings command itself, instead of relying on a follow-up `/lockdown`. That closes the drift at the source rather than recording it.
