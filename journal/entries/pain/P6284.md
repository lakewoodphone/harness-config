## P6284 — A fifth Chase card (9680) has statement mail and no account: four unusable balances

2026-10-13. A fifth Chase card's statements have been arriving and landing nowhere.

THE MEASUREMENT. Matching every Chase statement anchor to a ledger account by the card's last four:
 ...022005 46 anchors -> personal-amex-22005
 ...861006 26 -> lpt-amex-blue-business-1006
 ...9650 21 -> personal-chase-9650
 ...7542 17 -> lpt-chase-7542
 ...3929 11 -> lpt-chase-3929
 ...5578 9 -> lpt-chase-5578
 ...22005 6 -> personal-amex-22005
 ...9680 4 -> *** NO ACCOUNT ***
 ...4258 1 -> personal-chase-4258
So every statement-bearing card maps cleanly EXCEPT one. `...9680` has four "Your credit card statement
is available" messages - 2025-06-14, 2025-07-14, 2025-08-14, 2025-10-14 - each carrying a Statement
balance of 2349.79, 2561.85, 8579.39 and 5379.86, and the ledger has no account whose id or name contains
9680 and no reading anywhere carrying it. **Those four balances are unusable and any spending they
describe is absent from the books** - between two and eight and a half thousand dollars a month for four
months, for a card that stopped sending statements after October 2025.

I CHECKED THE ONE THING THAT WOULD HAVE MADE THIS A FALSE ALARM. A single transaction mentions 9680, and
it is an AMAZON ORDER NUMBER - `111-5968029-5429056` - that happens to contain the digits. So the finding
is real rather than a substring match. And the rule was not loosened to produce it: the reader matches
strictly on the account's last four, which is why 22005 and 22005-without-the-leading-zero both land on
personal-amex-22005 rather than creating two accounts.

QUEUED AS #482, ONE ROW, MEDIUM. The question is narrow on purpose - "was a card ending 9680 yours?" -
and the recommendation names the fork that matters: if it was real, a fifth card's 2025 spending is
missing and I will size the gap; if it was NOT, then the statement reader is matching something it should
not, and a reader that invents a card is worse than one that misses a statement.

AND A SECOND, SMALLER OBSERVATION FROM THE SAME SWEEP. `personal-chase-4258`'s newest statement anchor is
2025-05-17 while its transactions run 2026-09-25 to 2026-10-11 - 47 rows, and all 25 of its months are
unknown. So it is a recently-populated account whose statements stopped long before it had any activity.
Not filed as a question: it has an account, the gap is visible in the reconciliation, and the sweep's
one-row-per-subject rule exists precisely so a quiet oddity does not spend the owner's attention that
#482 needs.
