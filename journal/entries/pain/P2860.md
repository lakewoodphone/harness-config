## P2860 — No offsite copy of the authority exists - one disk holds the only copy of the company's memory

**What hurts.** The authoritative database, the 805,275-row session corpus the owner has called one of
the most important data he has, the OAuth tokens and every secret exist in exactly two places: the live
disk at the shop, and snapshots **on that same disk**. There is no offsite copy.

**How I know (four independent readings, 2026-10-06).** The only restic jobs in the estate are
`deploy/hosting-migration/scripts/hetzner-backup.sh` and `lpt-apps-backup.sh`, and each backs up its
own box; the R2 object map in `docs/security/r2-access-review-2026-09-16.md` puts the only repo at
`psm-backups/lpt-apps` ("Box 2 restic repo — nightly DB dumps + /opt configs"); the intended offsite
directory `C:\Users\ezabz\secretary-backups-offsite` exists (created 2026-09-30) and holds 0 files; and
`secretary-evolution-plan/DECISION-LOG.md` F1 "Offsite crash-consistent backups (restic)" is
**DEFERRED 2026-08-30** ("Owner deferred together with C1 — wants to think about hosting/backup
together"), with `secretary-evolution-plan/README.md` stating *"Backup/DR | Naive | 261GB same-disk
snapshots, no offsite, no restore drills."* The 2026-07-10 weakness list said the same thing.

**What it costs.** One disk failure, one theft, one fire, or one ransomware event at the shop ends the
company's memory permanently — and the 6-hourly job makes that feel safe while it is not.

**What would fix it, concretely.** A restic job on a Hetzner box (both are always-on, both already have
`restic` installed, up 35 and 146 days) pulling `~/secretary-backups` from the shop into the existing
R2 account under its own prefix, with its own bucket-scoped credential, plus a freshness alert that is
not hosted on the secretary. One snapshot is 16 GB, so the first copy must not stage locally (neither
Hetzner box has 16 GB spare today: 20 GB and 21 GB free) — it can go straight to R2.

**Priority.** The largest exposure in the resilience package and the cheapest to close; it outranks the
survivor host, the DNS switch and the lease work.
