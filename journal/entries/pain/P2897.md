## P2897 — subagent_local children cannot fan out (depth 2 > maxDepth 1), so a local fallback fleet is flat and the briefs were written for a capability it does not have

# A local fallback fleet cannot fan out, and the engine says so with a confusing error

MEASURED 2026-10-09, ZABZ-YOGA, mid-audit. The mesh was down (P2887), so six read-only audit
streams were dispatched on `subagent_local`. One of those children tried to fan its own brief out
further and was refused:

 SubagentDepthError: subagent depth 2 exceeds maxDepth 1

The child reported this honestly in its own brief, which is why I know. But the shape of the failure
matters: **the fallback path silently has half the capability of the path it replaces.** A remote
`subagent` on another node can spawn its own children; a `subagent_local` child cannot. So when the
mesh is down and I fan out locally, the fleet is flat - a child that would have partitioned its work
must instead do it serially, and its brief is the only evidence that it could not.

WHAT MAKES THIS DANGEROUS
Nothing in the tool contract says depth is limited. The brief I wrote said "partition and delegate
where it helps" - advice that is correct on the mesh and impossible locally. The child lost time
discovering that, and a less careful child would have silently produced a thinner report.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT
1. Either raise maxDepth for `subagent_local`, or
2. make the tool description state the depth limit per provider, so a brief can be written to the
 capability actually present, or
3. at minimum, have `subagent_local` refuse the *tool* at dispatch with the same one-line diagnostic
 it already produces at depth 2 - which it does. (2) is the cheapest.

UNTIL THEN
When the mesh is down, write local briefs for a FLAT fleet: state the depth limit in the brief
("you cannot spawn subagents; do this serially and say so"), do not ask a child to partition, and
expect each child to be slower. And label the results LOCAL, because where the work ran is a claim
that has to be true.
