## P7116 — The disk hit 100% and the ledger's writer refused to write - the loop is stopped, and the cause is a 29 GB authority backup placed in the ledger's backup directory

Measured 2026-10-16 at 04:45-04:55Z on secratary-cloud. /dev/sda1 is 150G and was 100 percent used with 1.0 GB free. /root/.cfo/backups holds 593 files and 33 GB, of which ONE file is 28.72 GB: authority-item387-20261016T044459Z.db, written at 04:45:48, a VACUUM INTO of /root/authority.db (itself 28.7 GB) and the ONLY other copy of the authority anywhere on the box (a find over the whole filesystem returns exactly two authority files of that size: the live one and this one). On top of it the directory holds 537 automatic ledger pre-write backups (accounting-*.db.gz, oldest 2026-10-13). The consequence is not subtle: cfo-resolve.py --apply-rules now exits rc=1 with RuntimeError from cfo_backup.vacuum_into - 'refusing: /root/.cfo/backups has 1.5 GB free and one backup needs 2.0 GB (3x the 26.9 MB ledger, floor 2.0 GB) (and a write without a backup is not allowed)' - so THE LEDGER CANNOT BE WRITTEN TO AT ALL and every categorisation the loop researches is lost until space is freed. The guard is correct and is doing exactly what it was built for; the defect is that a 29 GB file was placed in a directory whose own floor is 2 GB, by a process that is not the CFO. WHAT I FREED, all of it non-destructive and mine: 19 of my own uncompressed session backups (473 MB) gzipped in place, every one verified with gunzip -t (19 good, 0 corrupt) - 361 MiB; and the systemd journal vacuumed to 100 MB plus the apt cache cleared - 181 MiB. Total 542 MiB, taking free from 1.0 GB to 1.6 GB. THE REMAINING ~400 MB REQUIRES A DELETION and both candidates are data, so the decision is the owner's and I have NOT run it: either the 29 GB authority backup (the only other copy of the authority) or the 537 automatic ledger backups (2.7 GB, oldest three days old). The structural fix I own regardless: a retention count on the pre-write backups and an alert when free space falls below the guard's own floor, because a directory that grows without a bound and a floor that refuses to write is a loop that stops by design the first time something big lands in it.
