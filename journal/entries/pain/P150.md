## P150 — The apartment door lock has been silent 22h and my do-not-investigate note on its battery 0 is unsafe

**P (2026-09-15) - The apartment door lock has been silent for ~22 hours and I filed it earlier as "do not fix this, it is a device quirk". That note is now unsafe.**

Correcting myself. In `docs/AUDIT-2026-09-14-devices-and-integrations.md` I wrote:

> The Kwikset lock genuinely publishes `"battery":0` on its own MQTT topic while reporting
> `lock_state: locked` and a link quality of 109. **Do not report that as an empty battery** --
> the converter is the stock modernExtend `battery()`, so the 0 comes from the device.

The observation was right; the conclusion was wrong. `battery: 0` was read from cache at the
2026-09-13 21:51 ET restart, and **the device has published nothing since** -- it was not
"reporting 0", it was not reporting at all.

**Corrected reading, measured 2026-09-15:** `lock.0x002446fffd0a4705` shows `locked` with
`last_changed` **~16 hours** ago, and that is a **cached** value. Home Assistant will not mark it
unavailable until z2m's passive availability timeout (1500 min = 25 h) elapses, so for another
stretch it will look healthy while it is not. `lock.boltchecked_apartmentdoor` (the template lock
over it) has been `unavailable` for ~950 minutes and is the honest signal.

**Why the "quirk" note is now dangerous:** it tells a future reader not to investigate. In this same
session **all three** SmartThings door contacts turned out to be flat-cell deaths that looked like
network faults, and the whole estate is running on old batteries. A lock that has gone quiet with a
last reading of 0 is far more likely to be the fourth flat battery than a converter artifact. The
note as written could stop someone checking the apartment door lock -- access control -- at exactly
the moment it should be checked.

**What would settle it:** read the lock's battery on the device itself, or force a fresh report.
A Kwikset SmartCode deadbolt takes **4× AA** (verify against the unit, not from memory). If it
answers, the 0 was a quirk and the original note was right; if it does not, the lock is dead and
that is a real access-control fault, not a reporting curiosity.

**Lesson:** "the device genuinely reports X" is only true while the device is reporting. A cached
value plus a stale timestamp is not a report, and a note that says "do not investigate this" needs a
date and a re-check attached, or it becomes a blindfold.
