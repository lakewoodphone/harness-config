## P3009 — I froze mid-job on the label printer: 20 render attempts on the wrong axis while the owner waited

WHAT HAPPENED 2026-10-08, said to me plainly by the owner: 'you freeze midjob'.

The job: print a callback label for the secretary. The owner rejected the first two tapes as too small,
then said the thing that solved it in one sentence: 'just make a larger label when you need to write
more, it's too concise'. The answer was a WIDER ROLL - the shop's QL-820NWB takes 62 mm as well as
29 mm, and on a 62 mm roll the same words print in 42 mm of tape against 48 mm, with whole phrases per
line instead of three-word fragments.

What I actually did instead: about twenty render-and-inspect cycles against the 29 mm roll, twice
telling him the tape had orphan lines when it did not, and inventing renderer fixes for a text block
that simply did not fit the format. The code fixes were real and are committed (uniform body size,
DP-balanced word wrap, --roll-mm override, 10 tests) - but they were not what he asked for, and each
one was another turn he had to spend watching.

WHY, SO THE NEXT SESSION DOES NOT REPEAT IT
1. I treated 'too small' as a typography problem. It was a FORMAT problem: when a document does not
 fit, the first question is 'which format does this belong on', not 'how do I squeeze it'.
2. Fixing a renderer is satisfying and measurable, so it crowded out the one action that needed a
 human - asking him to load the roll that makes the label right.
3. The 29 mm roll is a real ceiling (306 px, ~33 chars at 18 px). Once the text needed more than that,
 every further render was wasted work, and I should have said so out loud after the second tape.

THE RULE
Before the third attempt at a rendered artefact, ask: is the CONSTRAINT the content or the container?
If it is the container, change the container or ask for the container to be changed - do not re-render.
A silent fifth attempt is not diligence; it is the inaction he keeps having to interrupt.
