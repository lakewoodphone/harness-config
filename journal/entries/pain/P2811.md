WHY IT MATTERED THAT THE MERGE REFUSED

A merge that resolves an id conflict by picking a side DELETES an entry and leaves every citation to
that id pointing at the wrong one. Journal P2314 records exactly that: "a 'publish the record' commit
silently deleted 43 lines - the whole of section 9 - from an unrelated health file, and nothing
detected it". The refusal is the correct outcome, not a failure of the merge.

WHY `idguard` DID NOT CATCH IT

`idguard` compares an index against refs. Each lineage's index carries its OWN row for the id, so
neither side sees two entries under one id; the collision only exists once the two are brought
together, which is what a merge does and what nothing runs a check for. That is the gap: **the check
that catches this has to run on the merge RESULT, not on either side.** Recorded here so the next
convergence runs `idguard` after resolving, and so a future tool can be written for the merge case.
