## P2541 — The counter-sale UI is jargon and walls of text - I conflated HONESTY with VERBOSITY, and safety copy drove the design instead of sitting behind it

**OWNER FEEDBACK 2026-09-25, and it is correct. He sent me a screenshot of the counter-sale page: "This page is full of jargon which what's going on? Terrible on the eyes. Bad UX and everything else in between. You really need to try to use this as a staff member. And then figure it out from there, because this is terrible."**

**HE IS RIGHT AND I WILL NOT DEFEND IT.** The page he is looking at is a wall of implementation detail rendered as UI. Direct quotes from it:
- "Nothing here takes money and nothing here starts a subscription. Committing the sale is a separate, confirmed step."
- "Phone matching ignores formatting - the last ten digits are compared, so (732) 555-1234 finds a record stored as 17325551234. At least 2 characters."
- "The key the billing engine joins on. A wrong serial makes the customer's device page silently show nothing, and the usage never bills."
- "The card is taken by Stripe - on the shop's Terminal reader, or typed on the counter device. Starting the capture is NOT a card on file: it must be confirmed before this sale can collect anything."
- "Press Start card capture first. Stripe has to create the SetupIntent this card is attached to before a number can be entered..."
- "This is the irreversible step. It records the sale, creates the Stripe subscription and links the device to the metered item, so the monthly plan can bill it in arrears."

**THE ROOT CAUSE, and it is a mistake in my own method - worth more than the page itself:** I **conflated honesty with verbosity.** The audits kept correctly finding places where the UI LIED (a zero where the truth was unknown, a success where the truth was a refusal), and every fix I commissioned to prevent that added SENTENCES. Each addition was individually justified. The aggregate is a screen that explains Stripe's object model to a salesperson with a customer waiting at the counter.
**THE PRINCIPLE THAT WAS MISSING: honesty lives in BEHAVIOUR, not in PROSE.** The system must refuse correctly; it does not have to explain itself to do that. "That card didn't go through - try another" is honest. A paragraph about SetupIntents is not honesty - **it is a leak of my internals into his shop.**
**A second, related error:** I let the honesty findings drive the DESIGN. Safety copy should sit behind a disclosure or in a help panel; it should never occupy the primary column of a task screen. And nobody ever role-played the page as a staff member - which is exactly what he told me to do.

**WHAT THE PAGE SHOULD BE, as a staff member actually does it:** Customer -> Phone -> Card -> Done. Four steps, not five (the "Plan" step is a summary, not a step). Short human labels. No product names a customer would not understand. Technical detail only behind an optional disclosure, or not at all when the behaviour already prevents the bad outcome.

**Dispatched a full rewrite on that principle** - see the following entry.
