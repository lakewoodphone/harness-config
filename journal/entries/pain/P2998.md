## P2998 — No order status means 'work is in flight' - so WO2608060003 sat on a false AWAITING_PARTS for 62 days and its 0.00 total stayed invisible

**Evidence, 2026-10-08, work order `WO2608060003` (Kogel, 300DDR ticket 73459).** The order sat at status `AWAITING_PARTS` from **2026-08-12 to 2026-10-08 - 62 days - with a total of 0.00**. Nothing was ever awaiting a part: the lab did a chip-off, returned >99% of sectors, and shipped the set back on 2026-10-05. The only thing the shop was waiting for was its own mail.

**The two defects compound.** The status vocabulary available on `orders` is seven values - `AWAITING_DIAGNOSIS`, `AWAITING_APPROVAL`, `AWAITING_PARTS`, `AWAITING_PAYMENT`, `READY_FOR_PICKUP`, `CANCELLED`, `UNFIXABLE` - and **none of them describes work that is in flight**: not awaiting the customer, not awaiting a part, not finished, not dead. So there is no honest label to move to, and the row stays on a wrong one.

**What that cost, concretely:** the false status is *why nobody re-read the row*. The 0.00 total was found only by walking the open work-order board one row at a time on 2026-10-08; with no total, the counter could never have charged this customer - so a finished, collected-nothing, 520.01-out-of-pocket job was invisible in exactly the view a person would use to find it.

**What would fix it:** add a status that means *in flight / at the lab / with the vendor* (something like `AT_LAB` or `IN_PROGRESS`), so an order that is genuinely moving has somewhere true to sit. Until then the failure repeats on every lab case - and there are at least six live 300DDR tickets alone.

**Second, independent guard worth having:** a total of 0.00 on an order older than N days with a non-terminal status is a pricing defect, not a zero-value job. That check would have caught this on day 3 instead of day 62.
