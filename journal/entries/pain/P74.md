**~1,741 rows are the machine managing itself. ~80 rows are anything external — about 4.5%.** In two
days the company ticked 389 times, diagnosed itself 155 times, skipped its own self-healing 158 times,
ran 88 self-audits, and **created eight tasks**.

**Why this matters more than the session-failure question I have been chasing for eight rounds.** The
failure rate measures the loop that runs work. This measures whether there is any work worth running.
`self_heal_skip` at 158 is the system deciding *not* to act, 199 times counting sweeps — activity that
looks like motion and produces nothing. `self_audit` at 88 is the company inspecting itself more often
than it creates tasks (8). That is the shape of a machine that is busy and idle at the same time, and it
is the honest answer to what the owner's ~$13/day is buying.

**It also reframes every "the work didn't complete" finding.** A work session that runs five steps of
`system_health` and `list_tasks` — which is what session 75338 did, and 2 of the first 3 sessions the new
`[ACTIONS:]` marker captured were read-only *entirely* — is a session with nothing to do and no
authority to invent something. Fixing completion signalling does not fix that. The goal generator
producing eight tasks in two days while the ticker runs 389 times is the thing to look at.

**A second signal, unflagged until now: `startup` = 70 in under two days**, roughly one every 41
minutes, of which about 8 are my own deploys this session. The watchdog restarts a dead service
(`Restart=always`), so this is either a crash loop or a watchdog fighting something. It has not been
investigated, and it is cheap to investigate — `journalctl -u secretary-api.service` and the restart
count over the same window. Recording it here rather than guessing.

**Honest limits of this measurement.** `activity_log` records what the *code chooses to log*, so a
mutation that logs nothing is invisible here — the same trap as the session log field in W46. It is
strong evidence about the mixture of logged activity and weak evidence about absolute output. The
`[ACTIONS:]` marker, now that it exists, is the direct measure and it needs a day of sessions before it
can carry this conclusion on its own; three sessions is not a sample.
