## P3182 — Background-job cap (10) is the hard ceiling on fan-out width, and it is not memory

WHAT HURTS: the background-job cap of 10 per owner is the real ceiling on fleet size - not memory,
not the CPU, not the mesh broker.

EVIDENCE: 2026-10-14, a 14-analyst fan-out over the chevedy email corpus. Ten launched; the remaining
four returned "background job limit reached for this owner (limit: 10)". Four of the ten then died on
ssh transport and had to be re-dispatched, and those re-dispatches again consumed slots. At no point
was any host under pressure: the authority sat at load 0.89 with 11 GB available and 71 GB free.

DAMAGE: it serialises the fan-out into waves and forces the manager to poll, wait for a slot and
re-dispatch instead of launching the fleet once. That is precisely the "agent stops after every
increment" pattern the parallel-orchestration rule exists to kill, reintroduced by the harness rather
than by the agent. A half-failed fleet then recovers slowly, because the retries queue behind the
survivors.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT: either a documented way to raise maxParallelJobs for an owner, or a fleet-launch
primitive that occupies ONE slot regardless of how many children it starts. The `workflow` tool may
already be that primitive, but it was not verified to dispatch to the mesh rather than run locally,
and this job could not afford an unverified placement on the authority - so measure that before the
next large fleet rather than assuming it.
