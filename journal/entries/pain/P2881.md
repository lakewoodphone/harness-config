## The shop's own message log holds customers' full card numbers, and nothing in the system removes them

Measured 2026-10-09 with the pattern the sweep uses (`dddd dddd dddd dddd`, or a 16-19 digit run):
**187 message rows across 9 customer files** in `operational.communications` contain what a customer
types as a payment card number, first seen 2026-05-20 and last 2026-09-20. One customer (id 1823)
accounts for 102 rows; another (1392) for 34. Rows 15418/15661/16716/… hold a number with its expiry
and CVV in plain text, repeated by the same duplication that affects every other message.

Already on the owner queue as **#309** with an older count of 64. The count is now 187, and the
exposure is **live**, not historical: order 2082's customer sent the card on 2026-07-15 and again in
August.

**What would fix it, in order, and why it has not been done:**

1. **Mask the digits in place** (keep the message, replace the PAN) — needs one `UPDATE` on
 `communications`, which is customer message history and irreversible, so it needs the owner's
 explicit per-action yes. He has the question in `owner-questions.md` as S1.
2. **Stop the harvest storing raw card text** — the Dialpad webhook in `phone-and-tech-full`
 (`dialpad-sms-webhook.service.ts`) writes `content` verbatim; a scrubber before the insert ends
 the inflow. This part is ordinary engineering and nobody needs to be asked.
3. **Decide on notification** — legal posture, his.

Until (1) happens, anything that reads the message log can pick a PAN up: the sweep's analysts did
exactly that on 2026-10-09 and `lpt_ops/sweep.py`'s scrubber caught it before it reached a note.
