## PAIN (new) — `transcript-archive-pull.service` is OOM-killed by its own cgroup once an hour and nobody has noticed

**Measured** 2026-10-14 on secratary-cloud, `journalctl -k --since -24h | grep 'Killed process'` — 4
OOM kills in 24 h, 3 of them the same job:
- 02:30:43Z `CONSTRAINT_MEMCG`, `oom_memcg=/system.slice/transcript-archive-pull.service`, pid 125148, **anon-rss 6,264,248 kB**
- 04:29:28Z same cgroup, pid 269052, anon-rss 6,266,180 kB
- 05:29:40Z same cgroup, pid 343887, anon-rss 6,259,692 kB
- 05:35:26Z `CONSTRAINT_NONE` / `global_oom`, `company-api.service`, pid 266723, anon-rss **14,301,752 kB**

Three kills at ~6.26 GB against its own `MemoryMax=6G`, on a roughly hourly retry, means the job
**cannot complete inside its own cap** — it dies, is retried, and dies again. This is the only
reason I read the log at all: `estate-monitor`'s `oom_kills_24h=4.0 (crit>=3)`.

**What would fix it:** find why a transcript archiver needs 6.26 GB — almost certainly a whole-file or
whole-table read that should stream (the estate has now hit this shape in `company-api` too). Owned by
nobody; recorded so it stops being rediscovered.

**Residual risk it creates for the fix I shipped tonight:** `company-api` is now capped at 10 GiB and
this job at 6 GiB; if both peak at once the sum (16 GiB) exceeds the box (15.6 GiB), so a GLOBAL OOM
is still possible. It is materially better than before — the 14.3 GiB unbounded consumer now has a
ceiling, so the kernel picks a victim *within* a cgroup — but it is not eliminated, and that sentence
should not be softened by whoever reads this next.
