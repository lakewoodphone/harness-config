## P — secretary-api is OOM-killed roughly every 20–60 minutes, at ~20 GB RSS

**Measured 2026-09-15 (kernel log on secratary), six kills in one day, every one of them this process:**

| killed at (UTC) | pid | total-vm | anon-rss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13:34:33 | 3985542 | 26.5 GB | 22.0 GB |
| 20:12:14 | 183696 | 24.8 GB | 20.3 GB |
| 20:41:04 | 248840 | 24.0 GB | 20.0 GB |
| 21:01:35 | 268781 | 24.0 GB | 20.0 GB |
| 22:06:31 | 275330 | 25.2 GB | 20.2 GB |
| 23:00:46 | 306180 | 25.6 GB | 20.3 GB |

systemd's own accounting for the 23:00 death: *"Consumed 18min 12.931s CPU time over 54min 11.938s wall clock time, **19.4G
memory peak**"* — so the app reaches ~20 GB in under an hour, i.e. on the order of 5–10 MB/s of retained memory.

**The log immediately before the 23:00 kill** shows the department-agent workers running hard and being abandoned rather than
reaped: `agent_worker: Agent dept_* step … round N recovered via gateway fallback model google/gemini-3-pro` (many) and
`app.workforce: Worker tick for dept_engineering exceeded 150.0s — abandoned (continues in background)` 5 s before the kill.
That is the current best lead, not a conclusion.

**Second-order damage (fixed tonight, but it is why an OOM became an outage):** after the kill, systemd restarted the unit at
23:00:48, and `secretary-access-watchdog` restarted it *again* at 23:02:47 after 3 consecutive failed `/health` probes —
before the app had ever finished starting (activated 23:04:17, first HTTP 200 at 23:06:14; a cold start is minutes, and the
90 s stop-sigterm timeout SIGKILLed the boot it had just asked for). Fixed in `71547526`
(`scripts/server/access-watchdog.sh`): `STARTUP_GRACE_SECONDS` (default 600) measured from the unit's own
`ActiveEnterTimestamp`, so a service that just (re)started is never HTTP-health-restarted mid-boot. Verified by dry run
(api/dashboard uptime 164 s → IN-GRACE; cloudflared 357179 s → past-grace) and by a live watchdog run reporting ok.

**What would fix the leak itself:** identify the accumulating structure in the department-worker path
(`app/workforce.py`, `app/services/agent_worker.py`) or whatever else retains per-tick state, and bound it. A `MemoryMax=` on
the unit would confine the kill to its own cgroup (today's kills are `global_oom`, which risks taking other services with
them) but that is containment, not a fix. A subagent is producing a ranked, evidence-backed diagnosis at
`~/phone/api-oom-report-2026-09-15.md`.
