## P2930 — Every day in the money/customer reporting is a UTC day, and the shop lives in America/New_York

## Every "day" in this reporting system is a UTC day, and the shop lives in America/New_York

MEASURED 2026-10-09 00:54 UTC on secratary, whose timezone is `Etc/UTC`:

 host : Fri 2026-10-09 00:54 UTC
 shop : 2026-10-08 20:54 (America/New_York, EDT, UTC-4)

So the reporting system called it **Friday** while the shop was still in **Thursday evening**, and
every day boundary in every report is four hours out of step with the business it describes.

HOW BAD, measured rather than assumed — and it is bounded, which is why this was recorded rather
than changed in a hurry:

- The shop trades **12:30–17:00 EDT**, which is **16:30–21:00 UTC** — wholly inside one UTC day. So
 **daily totals are correct** for business activity, and only the day *LABEL* drifts.
- The drift is real where it bites: a sale between **20:00 and midnight EDT** lands in the NEXT UTC
 day's figures, and the weekly window reported as `Mon–Sun` is really `Sun 20:00 EDT → Sun 20:00 EDT`.
- The `today` view printed "Today 2026-10-09 (Friday)" on Thursday night. A human reading that at the
 counter would be reading about a day that had not started for them.

WHY IT WAS NOT FIXED IN THE SESSION THAT FOUND IT: the sources disagree on what a timestamp is —
Dialpad carries **epoch millis**, Stripe carries **epoch seconds**, Plaid carries a **posted DATE**
(a label with no instant at all), and production carries naive local timestamps. The engine converts
some of these with an explicit `timezone.utc` and others with a naive local `astimezone()`. Setting
`TZ=America/New_York` on the runner would move the naive ones and leave the explicit-UTC ones where
they are — i.e. it would make the mixture **less** coherent while looking more correct. Doing it
properly means one canonical civil day applied per source with every metric declaring whether it is
an `instant` or a `date`. That is a deliberate piece of work, not a tail-of-session edit.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT: add a documented `LPT_BUSINESS_TZ` (default `America/New_York`); give every metric
a `date_kind` of `instant|date`; convert epoch-valued sources **to the shop's civil day**; assign
date-valued sources (Plaid `posted_date`) to the date they NAME, never through a timezone
conversion; use half-open `[start, end)` periods so a boundary moment is counted once and never
twice; and store both the civil date and the UTC instant. Then re-run the pipeline and diff — the
only figures that should move are those near the 20:00 EDT boundary.

CHEAPEST GUARD until then: the `today` view and the trend both name the day they are describing, so a
reader can see the drift — but nothing yet states the timezone, and it should.

NOT URGENT, BUT NOT NOTHING: a shop that closes at 5pm will not see wrong daily totals. It will see a
report that calls Thursday evening "Friday", and a weekly comparison whose weeks start four hours
early. Both are worth fixing for a record the owner intends to keep for years.
