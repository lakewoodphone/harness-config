## P332 — Fan-out concentrated on one node costs children: five on zabz-tech pushed its sshd to 8s and one was lost

**P · Fan-out concentrated on one node costs children: five children on `zabz-tech` pushed its sshd to 8 s and one was lost to a timeout**

MEASURED 2026-09-18, during a five-workstream fan-out.

WHAT HAPPENED. Five children were launched together to measure the mesh. The broker placed **all five on
`zabz-tech`** — correct by its own ranking, since that is the biggest node. One of them
(the mesh-wide-load workstream) then died with:

```
ssh_dispatch_run_fatal: Connection to 100.85.153.96 port 22: Connection timed out
exited 255 and produced no final message
```

and immediately afterwards a single `ssh desktop-ts hostname` from this laptop took **8,041 ms**. Earlier
in the same session the same hop answered in roughly 1.5-3 s. So the node was answering, slowly, and the
dispatch's `-o ConnectTimeout=10` was not enough headroom for a node whose sshd handshake had stretched to
eight seconds under the load I had just put on it. **The load that caused the timeout was mine.**

THIS IS THE THIRD CHILD LOST TO A TRANSPORT TIMEOUT TODAY (`subagent-1`, `subagent-11`, `subagent-17`).
The first two were unexplained; this one has a cause, and the cause is a real property of the mesh rather
than bad luck: **there is no cap on how many children one node may be given, and nothing measures the
node's sshd latency before more are added.** The broker ranks by capacity and slots; the local admission
governor bounds THIS host's processes; neither one sees that a remote node's ssh handshake is degrading
because it is already carrying four other children.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT, in order of cost:
1. **Raise the dispatch hop's `ConnectTimeout`** from 10 s for a hop to a node that is already carrying
 work, or make it proportional to the node's measured sshd latency. An 8 s handshake against a 10 s
 limit is a coin flip, and the coin lands badly once per fleet.
2. **Cap concurrent children per node** in the fan-out, which is a policy of the caller and does not exist
 anywhere today. Five was my own number and it was too many for one node.
3. **Spread a fan-out deliberately across nodes** when the workstreams are independent of the node, rather
 than letting the broker concentrate them — which the broker will do, because concentrating is what its
 ranking is for.

AND ONE MORE DISCRIMINATOR ELIMINATED ON AN OLDER DEFECT, recorded here so the next session does not
repeat it: the question was why `MESH_TARGET_NODE` / `mesh-run -Node` never constrains where a subagent
goes (P331(a)). Two candidates are now excluded by measurement — the environment DOES reach the child
(`echo $env:MESH_TARGET_NODE` inside a `--profile mesh` child printed `[secratary-ts]`), and the profile
flag IS honoured (a bogus `--profile definitely-not-a-real-profile` fails loudly with exit 1 rather than
falling back). What remains is inside `resolvePlacementMode`/placement itself. **The cause is still open**,
and that is what is known rather than guessed.
