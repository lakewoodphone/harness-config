## P3001 — The office box's "do not run heavy DB work on it" rule is written as a list, and I obeyed the letters of it and broke the machine anyway

MEASURED 2026-10-11 02:17Z. A 14.2 GB / ~200,000-file `rsync` of `data/` minus the database, run to
pre-stage the authority cutover, took the office box's load average to 52 on 4 threads, put 5,000-
22,000 blocks/s through the disk queue, filled 3.1 GB of 4 GB swap, stopped sshd answering on the
tailnet while `tailscale ping` still answered, and took `api.abletelsolutions.com` to HTTP 502 for
20+ minutes before it recovered.

The rule as recorded (`docs/mesh/000-SYSTEMS-REGISTER.md` trap 2, `128-HANDOFF` §7.2, and the
runbook) says: NEVER run `dbstat`, a recursive `du`, a full-table scan, or `.backup` of the large DB
on the OFFICE box. I ran none of those four things. The rule's real content is the SENTENCE AFTER
the list: this box's only path to the world is a disk-starved sshd, so anything that queues work in
front of its disk takes the company down. A four-item list invites the reading that item five is
fine.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT
 1. Restate the rule by its property, not by its examples: "any operation on the office box that
 keeps the disk queue busy for more than a few seconds is forbidden while it is the authority -
 reads included, and the test is not whether it touches the database."
 2. Add the signal that says it is happening: `cut -d' ' -f1 /proc/loadavg` above ~8, or `wa` above
 ~10 % in `vmstat`, on that box means stop and wait, whatever the command was.
 3. Keep the corollary next to it: sshd silent + `tailscale ping` answering == I/O starvation, NOT a
 powered-off box. Retry; do not escalate to a drive to Lakewood.
 4. Do the non-database data move AFTER the switch, in bounded batches, from the box that no longer
 has anything depending on it.
