## P2830 — The owner was asked the same charge question twice by two different askers with disjoint key spaces and no shared record of the budget

# P — the owner was asked the same charge question twice, by two different askers

**Measured 2026-10-02 on the authority. Two rows, two stores, one charge:**

 owner-ask #28 2026-10-01T20:30Z source=m:amazon order total sent=1
 "[q1790886601] what was 173.55 at amazon order total (7 charges)? I'd file it ..."

 cfo-ask #13 2026-10-02T12:30Z merchant='amazon order total' sent=1
 "CFO [ask-1790944221]: what was 173.55 at amazon order total on 20 Aug (Personal ..."

Both reached his phone. He answered the second — the one he had not already answered. That
is one charge, two questions, sixteen hours apart, which is precisely what he asked to be
spared: *"you only should text me when you have like a question one at a time"*, and
`cfo-ask.py`'s own design note, *"never the same merchant twice"*.

**Why neither could see it.** `cfo-ask.py` (cron `*/30`, store `~/.cfo-asks/asks.db`)
asks about unclarified CHARGES, keyed by merchant string from the ledger.
`owner-ask.py` (cron `*/30`, store `~/.owner-ask/asks.db`) asks about owner-DECISION rows
**and** about purchases — `source=m:<merchant>`, the same ledger. Their key spaces are
disjoint *by construction*: `cfo-ask` tracks a merchant, `owner-ask` tracks
`source:source_id`. Each is internally consistent and neither can detect the other.

I had earlier reported these two as complementary, on the strength of a first look that
only saw decision rows. The purchase rows were in the store the whole time; the claim was
wrong and the measurement that corrected it was one query over `source LIKE 'purchase%'`.

**The fix is a read, not a write** — `owner-ask.py` now reads `cfo-ask.py`'s sent
merchants and drops any purchase the other asker has already put to him, in both the text
path and the sitting document. Verified after the change: the four merchants `cfo-ask` has
genuinely asked (`amazon order total`, `the home depot`, `bh photography`,
`esti's world of fashion`) are all FILTERED, while `ls vineyard express` — asked by
neither — survives.

**And the fix's first version was itself the bug it was fixing.** I normalised with
`[^a-z0-9]+` → strip, so `"newegg"` → `newegg` but `"newegg.com"` → `neweggcom`: two keys
for one merchant, the exact miss the dedupe exists to prevent. `_merchant_key` is now
byte-identical to `cfo-ask.py::merchant_key` (`[^a-z0-9 ]+` → space, collapse, `[:48]`), and
the two functions are compared against eight real merchant strings in the test I ran.

**The general rule.** When two components share a *user-visible budget* — here, one text
per charge and a `3 + 2` daily question allowance — they must share the record of what has
been spent. Two independent ledgers of the same budget is not a design tension to be
reported; it is a duplicate question waiting for the owner, and the owner cannot tell it
from a system that ignores him.
