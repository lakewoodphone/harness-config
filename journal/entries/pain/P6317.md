## P6317 — Our completion signal for a data-landing job is a row count, so a store that loses the customer's messages still reports LANDED

# Our completion signal for a data-landing job is a ROW COUNT, so a store that loses the customer's messages still reports LANDED

Open pain, found 2026-10-13 on the Kramer WhatsApp case. The 2026-10-08 handoff said, with evidence, "19,167
messages + media live on the iPhone 16, verified on the device" and marked the job complete. The counts were
right and the app was writing to the store. Ten days later the customer reports she cannot use it properly,
and the store has a broken object-id allocator and 39 chats whose ordering counter points into the middle of
their own history. Five of the imported messages are already gone from the device.

**The pattern, stated so it can be caught next time:** when the target of a migration is an application's own
database, "how many rows are there" is the wrong completion test. It cannot see the allocator, the ordering
keys, the per-chat counters, or the app's ability to write after we finish. Every one of those was wrong here
while every count passed.

**What would fix it:** a landing job is not complete until (a) the allocator and every per-chat ordering
counter are asserted against the real maxima, and (b) one live write is observed landing where a person would
expect it. Both are cheap. Neither was in anyone's definition of done.
