## P2807 — An ordinary --why with newlines broke the journal status tier, and a one-field line killed every reader

I broke the journal's status tier with an ordinary argument, and the way it broke is the more useful half of the lesson.

WHAT I DID, 2026-09-30 15:55Z

Closing the 42 entries the pain-corpus audit had proved fixed, I called `journal.py resolve <id>
--status done --why "<reason>"` 42 times in one batch. Each `--why` carried newlines, because the
reason I wanted to record had a WHY / COMMAND / OBSERVED structure and newlines were the obvious way
to write it.

`cmd_resolve` folded tabs out of the field but not newlines. So every reason was written as several
physical lines and `journal/state/status.tsv` stopped being a TSV.

HOW IT FAILED, AND WHY THAT IS THE REAL FINDING

The next reader died with:

 ValueError: not enough values to unpack (expected 6, got 4)
 journal.py:851 load_status_events
 parts = (ln.split("\t") + ["", "", ""])[:6]

That padding is the bug. It appends THREE empty fields and then truncates to six, so a line with one
field becomes four and can never reach six. Any line in an append-only record with fewer than three
tabs killed the read of the whole file -- and `load_status_events` is called by `rebuild_cache`, so a
single bad line made the journal unreadable for every command. A record that can be corrupted by an
ordinary argument, read by a parser that dies instead of skipping, is two defects wearing one coat.

WHAT I FIXED

1. `_one_line()` folds tabs AND newlines at the WRITER, so a row is a row by construction. The
 invariant belongs where the data enters, not in the discipline of every caller.
2. The reader pads properly (`[""] * 6`) and already skipped rows without an id, so a malformed line
 is now skipped rather than fatal. A tolerant reader is not a licence to write a broken row -- both
 halves were wrong and both are fixed.
3. `journal/state/status.tsv` was repaired by joining continuation lines back onto the row they
 belong to, joined with " / ", nothing dropped and nothing reordered. Backup:
 `state/status.tsv.bak-continuation-repair-20260930T155750Z`. 253 physical lines became 126 rows,
 0 malformed, and `journal.py check` returned 0 errors afterwards.

WHAT I SHOULD HAVE KNOWN

The journal's own entry P200 records the identical class from 2026-09-15: a concurrent session's
mojibake rewrite of `journal.py` produced 1,065 false errors and corrupted the one entry appended in
that window. And P204: `check`'s verdict swung from 1 to ~1090 errors when unrelated code was added
to the file. The tree already knew that this tool's behaviour is not a function of its data. I read
both entries earlier in this same session, classified one of them as FIXED, and then wrote a
multi-line field into an append-only TSV twenty minutes later.

THE RULE THAT FOLLOWS

Before writing to a record that other processes parse, ask what the WRITER guarantees about the
format, not what the caller intends. Intentions do not survive a `\n`.
