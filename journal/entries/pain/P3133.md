## P3133 — Nothing alarms when the kernel sentinel dies, and the badge is not that alarm

# P — nothing alarms when the kernel sentinel dies, and the badge is not that alarm

**Ranked first of tonight's residue, because it is the property that let a six-hour outage look like
stability.**

## What hurts

The attention badge was, for six hours, a *stopped clock that told the truth*: `6 needing attention ·
stale 6h ago`, growing. Everything about that rendering is correct and none of it is an alarm. The
failure mode the estate keeps paying for is **silence about a missing reading**, and a staleness flag
on a surface nobody is watching is silence with a label.

`run-sentinel.sh` **always exits 0 on purpose** (a finding is the signal, not a scheduler failure), so
`cron-wrap.sh`'s ledger records `0` for a run that senses nothing wrong *and* for a sentinel that never
ran. The ledger cannot tell them apart, and nobody reads the ledger anyway.

`docs/mesh/140-PORT-CORE-AND-GAPS.md` §A1 wrote this down on 2026-10-11 verbatim: *"what would notice
its failure: nothing — and that is a defect I am filing."* It arrived the next day.

## What would fix it

An **absence-reporting** check, not an exit-code check: *"no new `history.jsonl` row in 15 minutes"*,
which is the only formulation that works on a wrapper that exits 0 by design. It belongs **outside the
producer** — the producer cannot report its own death — and it has somewhere to go already: the
authority's `run-sentinel` writes `/root/ceo-kernel-var/history.jsonl` every 5 minutes, and
`cron-wrap.sh` + `~/.cron-status/runs.tsv` is the estate's existing "one row per run, absence is the
signal" shape.

## Related residue from the same move (ranked below it)

1. **Three of nineteen checks are blind on the authority and the kernel exits 2 ("cannot see") on
 every run.** `app_voice` names the office unit `secretary-api` while the authority's is
 `company-api` — a porting error, not a real blindness. `config_sync` has no status writer on the
 cloud (the authority's `authority-repo-push` cron job was found refusing every run:
 `cron-wrap REFUSED: payload … is not valid base64`). `model_failover` has no model-watch record
 there. A check that can never see teaches its reader to ignore `unknown`.
2. **`db_writable` was HIGH and true**: the authority's `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` probe failed 4 times out of 4
 at 5 s each, *"database is locked — nothing can be recorded until this clears"*. The company is
 demonstrably writing something (WAL moving, ticks present), so this is either a long-lived write
 transaction or genuine write-lock starvation. Being investigated; it is the one finding tonight that
 could mean the company is not recording its work.
3. **The office box still serves a v3 badge and a six-hour-stale reading** to anything that asks it
 (`115,737` requests in its log). Harmless now that the badge prefers the freshest source, but it is
 a second answer to the same question and should be retired with the box.
