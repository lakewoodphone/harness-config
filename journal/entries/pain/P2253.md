## P2253 — Remote subagent children fail 2-in-5 with no usable output - a whole research strand can vanish behind a bare 'failed'

**Remote `subagent` children fail non-deterministically and silently enough to matter — 2 of 5 in one fleet,
2026-09-23.**

Dispatching a 5-child research fleet from ZABZ-YOGA to the mesh, three children completed cleanly on
**ZABZ-TECH** with full `MESH-HOST` / placement / released-lease records. **Two did not:**

- child (duty + freight) — `[status: failed, error; diagnostic: the remote one-shot exited 1]`, **no final
 message at all**, stderr tail showing an unhandled rejection inside
 `dsh-subagent/lib/index.js:712` at `ContinuableActivationRegistry.settlementState`.
- its replacement (freight + customs fees) — `exited 255 and produced no final message`; the stderr tail
 carried a **half-written report** and then `Read from remote host zabz-tech.tail93e6e6.ts.net: Connection
 timed out` / `send disconnect: Connection reset`.

**Why this hurts:** a failed child returns **nothing useful except the stderr tail**, and the job status
alone does not say how far it got. The first failure produced **zero** recoverable content; the second
produced a **truncated fragment** that was still partly usable (the LCL cost stack), which is luck, not
design. A parent that only reads `[status: failed]` will silently drop a whole workstream and report a
smaller answer as if it were the complete one — the exact "confident wrong answer" failure this system
keeps paying for.

**What to do (adopted this session):**
1. **Run the critical-path strand yourself in parallel with the fleet, not only inside it.** I had already
 secured the duty classification and the incumbent benchmark locally before dispatching; that is why the
 Mesh dying did not cost the answer.
2. **When a child fails, harvest its stderr tail before re-dispatching** — the second failure leaked a real
 cost breakdown, and `job_output` on a failed job still returns it.
3. **Re-dispatch at most once, with a narrower brief**, then fall back to local and **mark every affected
 number as LOCAL and ESTIMATED** in the deliverable. A fallback nobody can see is a claim about where work
 ran that is not true.
4. **Prefer several small single-purpose children over one big multi-part child** — a part-failure then costs
 one strand instead of the whole brief.

**What would fix it properly:** the provider surfacing *partial* agent output on a non-zero exit as a
first-class field, rather than leaving it to a stderr tail that may or may not be captured. Until then this
is a known tax on every fleet, not an anomaly.
