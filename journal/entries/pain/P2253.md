## P2253 — A customer text arriving during shop hours is answered by nobody until a human looks; there is no unanswered-inbound surface

A customer text that arrives during shop hours sits with nobody alerted until a human
happens to look at the Dialpad thread. Measured on the Weitman case
(848-333-6341), which is now on its THIRD unanswered stretch: 2026-09-10 16:46 and
2026-09-13 21:10 "Can I order a laptop asap" both answered only by the out-of-hours
auto-reply; then the $265 text of 2026-09-17 with no delivery witness; and now
2026-09-22 09:44 "Change i wld like to purchase the $500 one. When can you have it
in?" with no reply as of 13:2x ET, hours into the open day.

This is not a one-customer problem. Every one of these messages was live in
dialpad_sms_cache within a second of being sent, and every one was found only because
a session went looking. There is no surface that says 'an inbound customer text is
open and unanswered'.

WHAT WOULD FIX IT:
An unanswered-inbound detector over dialpad_sms_cache: any inbound where the newest
message in that phone_normalized thread is inbound, older than N minutes, and now is
inside shop hours (Mon-Thu 10:30-17:30, Fri 10:30-13:00) - push it to the owner once,
and to the shop's own ops channel every 30 min until answered. The table already has
everything needed (phone_normalized, direction, created_at, fetched_at) and live
capture timestamps are reliable for rows where fetched_at is within a few seconds of
created_at. Do NOT use created_at for backfilled rows - the importer rewrites it to
import time (that is case-recorded and is journal pain P263).

Not built this session: it is a real build with its own design, and this session's
job was one customer's open sale. Recording it so it stops being rediscovered as
'anecdotal slowness' rather than as the structural gap it is.
