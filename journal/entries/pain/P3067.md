## P3067 — Four inbound calls from a real customer reached the bot and left no trace - no transfer, no task, no contact, no customer record

**Measured 2026-10-11, number 914-570-2075.** A real customer called the shop four times before
anyone spoke to him, and the shop's systems recorded nothing a human could act on.

- `dialpad_call_full` holds **4 inbound calls**: 2026-10-09 15:15:21 and 15:15:53 UTC, and
 2026-10-11 15:51:39 and 18:35:14 UTC. All `state=hangup`, 0 s.
- `dialpad_digital_session` holds **4 matching `voice` sessions**, `chat_type=bot`, every one
 `agent_handled=0` and `agent_transfer=0`, `cause=inactivity-timeout-customer`.
- `dialpad_transcript_cache`: `has_transcript=0`, `has_recap=0` on both calls it covers.
- `tasks`: 0. `memories`: 0. `contacts`: 0. No SMS (`dialpad_sms_cache` 0 rows) and no voicemail.
- Every communications row carried `customerId: null` and `orderId: null`, and Dialpad holds **no
 name** for the number — it displays as "(914) 570-2075".

**Consequence:** when the owner called him back and booked the job, the shop's records could not
answer "who is this, and what did he call about". The whole history had to be reconstructed from
call rows and one recording — and a deposit was taken and a work order created before any of it was
visible. He was also invisible to every case search: `lpt ticket 914-570-2075` said "no work order
matches", and `lptcase find` had nothing, because nothing had ever been written down.

**What would fix it:** a bot-handled inbound call from a number with no customer record should leave
something behind — a task, an unattributed contact, or at least a flag on the number — instead of
vanishing into a session row. `agent_handled=0` on a short inbound call is exactly the signal that
means "a human never got this", and nothing reads it.
