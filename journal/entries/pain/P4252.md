# The shop line is not being handled, and the owner is counted as a waiting customer

**Measured 2026-10-15T~15:0xZ**, `ps_comms_waiting` (index age 0.47 h, not stale): **25 conversations
waiting, 24 missed calls, and 12 `reopened_by_machine_ack`.**

Two findings, both live.

**1. The owner is the single longest-waiting "customer" on his own line.** Party `+18483897895`, waiting
**13.5 hours**, last message from him:

> *"Didn't we already go through that it's my wife's professional liability insurance for doing therapy
> in college to younger students?"*

He is telling the machine the question was already answered, and the machine has still not answered him.
This is owner-queue rows #455 and #458 happening again in real time — the same defect the audit described,
still open while the audit was being written. `ps_comms_waiting` counts him as a party like any other
because nothing in that path knows the owner's number.

**2. The shop's own customers are waiting 13-24 hours, and some are urgent:**
- `(732) 664-4776`, 2.7h: *"I dropped off a laptop and would appreciate if you can take a look as soon as
 possible as I need it early Sunday morning."*
- `(848) 245-7112` (Shlesinger), 22.3h: *"Hi was there today to pick up laptop didn't find it"* — a
 customer who came to collect and could not find their device.
- Bayla Stein, 13.1h: *"How long does it take because we can't not have the phone at this time."*
- Nine voicemails 16-25h old, including two asking for callbacks.

**Why this matters for the objective.** The tiering rule the owner set is: personal channels are his;
**the shop's business is handled by the company, escalating only when it is urgent or has sat
unanswered.** By that rule these 24 should have been handled and never reach him. They are not handled —
so the third clause of the objective ("everything else is handled or suppressed") is **false today**, and
the fault is not noise reaching him, it is *work not being done*.

**What the mechanism would be, and why it is not built.** Replying to a customer is an outbound message
and needs a per-message yes (the outbound hard stop). So the lane has to be **draft-and-approve**: the
machine drafts a reply for each waiting party, the owner approves in a batch, and the send happens then.
Nothing in the estate does this today — `/root/bin/sms-responder.py` is per-inbound and is not even
scheduled (row #455), and `ps_comms_waiting` only reports.

**What is not claimed here.** I did not message anyone, and I did not change any reply behaviour.
This entry is a measurement and a finding; the lane is work, filed separately.
