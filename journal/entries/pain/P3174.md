## P3006 — P3005 addendum: the API recovers ~13x/day on its own; a wedge at 20:04 would delay the door relock 5-40 min, not lose it

**Addendum to P3005, measured 2026-10-09 (round 12).** Frequency and duration,
and what it means for the Shabbat power-up specifically.

## The measurement

From the health watchdog's own log
(`/home/zabz/.api-watchdog-degraded.log`, 174 lines over 10 days):

```
distinct recovery events ("/health is HEALTHY again"): 127
```

So the API reaches a **degraded/DOWN health state roughly 13 times a day**, and
recovers from it **autonomously** — the log records `recovered ... after N
observation(s)` with N between 1 and 8, i.e. 5 to 40 minutes at the watchdog's
5-minute probe cadence.

Today's explicit DOWN verdicts: `18:15:51Z`, `18:31:01Z`, plus the `20:21:24Z`
recovery I hit by hand-shaped timing. And the 21:11:39Z entry — the one I first
attributed to my own restart — reads
`recovered: /health is HEALTHY again after 1 observation(s)`, i.e. the watchdog's
own next probe saw it healthy again.

**A caveat on reading this log:** some lines are `DEGRADED RECOVERY: DOWN -> after
restart DOWN` followed by a bare `DOWN` on the NEXT line. So `grep -c DOWN`
overcounts (38) by counting continuation lines. The recovery-event count (127) is
the trustworthy figure.

## What this means for the 20:04 power-up — the part that matters

The API is unhealthy a meaningful fraction of the time, so there is a real chance
it is degraded **at** 20:04. If it is:

1. the plug still turns ON from its own device schedule — no API needed;
2. HA boots and its own assert-on-start automation may relock the interior door;
3. the secretary's `run_power_up` cannot run, so the door relock, the guard-rail
 restore and the ceiling-light re-assert are skipped **for that tick**;
4. `recover_incomplete_power_up` retries **every minute** and completes them as
 soon as the API answers.

**So a wedge delays the door relock by up to 5-40 minutes. It does not lose it.**
That is a materially better outcome than before this work — before, a single
failed power-up left the door released and the owner's tamper automation disabled
for a WEEK — and it is the strongest argument that the round-6 retry was not
gold-plating.

It is not a clean outcome either: a door released for up to 40 minutes after
Shabbat is a real, if modest, exposure, and the ceiling light does not come back
until the same moment.

## What I did NOT do, and why

I did not shorten the api-watchdog's fuse. It is threshold-based (6 readings /
~30 min) by explicit design, it has a one-restart-per-hour cooldown to avoid a
restart loop on a genuinely failing service, and shortening it in the last round
of a session — on the company's own substrate, without a soak test — risks
converting a slow recovery into a restart loop. The right fix is to reduce the
frequency of the underlying condition (23 GB SQLite, persistent lock contention,
81% filesystem) or to make the watchdog escalate on *responsiveness* with its own
measured fuse. That is a session of its own, and P3005 carries it.

## The residual risk in one line

The Shabbat automation now tolerates a wedged API, but does not eliminate its
cost: worst case, the interior door is relocked and the ceiling light restored
up to ~40 minutes after motzei rather than at it.
