**D9365 · 2026-10-22 · Correction to D9363: 39 GB freed, and the disk runway is December not January.**

CORRECTION to D9363, same day, after two read-only fleet children measured what I had estimated.

D9363 said the disk work bought "to roughly the end of January". That was wrong in its mechanism and too optimistic in its date.

What the children measured, which I had not:
- The database FILE is not growing: +7.9 MB in 3.4 days. What is being consumed is the FREELIST - 3,352,593 pages on 2026-10-19 down to 2,639,454 on 2026-10-22 = **942 MB/day**. So the 10.07 GiB of free pages runs out about **2026-11-03**, and only then does the file start growing at ~0.94 GB/day.
- Growth that exists TODAY, independent of the database: `/opt/company-archive/lpt-transcripts/objects` **0.66 GiB/day** (only copy, not regenerable) and chroma 0.21 GB/day.
- With 62 GB free, 12 days to the flex point and ~1.9 GB/day afterwards, the runway is **late November to the first days of December**, not January.

ALSO CORRECTED: "1 GiB/day of session events" is the right order of magnitude for the ROWS (174k/day) but the wrong clock for the file, and the earlier "10 months of buffer" claim in the estate's disk doc is out by ~30x (12.8 GiB at 1.28 GB/day is 10 days).

WHAT ELSE THE FLEET CHANGED:
- `dbstat`, measured (3 m 21 s): `dsh_session_events` 13,916 MiB + FTS data 4,655 MiB + indexes 421 MiB = **19.96 GB = 85.7% of allocated space**. Everything else is 3.3 GB and flat.
- **Two archive tools already exist**: `dsh_session_events_retention.py` (planner) and `dsh_session_events_archive.py` (per-day zstd JSONL, re-read verified, "ARCHIVE, NEVER DELETE", prune owner-gated). The gap is that JSONL is not SQL-queryable, so esearch cannot see archived days. Do not write a third tool.
- The offsite backup keeps **one version of each key, no rotation** - a corrupt run overwrites the last good copy. The DB leg streams 32 GB at 7.1 GB RSS on a box that OOM-killed twice today.
- `auto_vacuum=0`; `VACUUM INTO` would return 10.07 GiB once, and now has the 34 GB of scratch it needs.

RECLAIMED IN TOTAL TODAY: **39 GB** - `df -h /` from `225G 194G 23G 90%` to `225G 155G 62G 72%`. The second tranche (44 more trees, 18.6 GB) lived outside the first pass's globs; the reaper's glob list was widened to cover them, so the nightly job sees what I missed.

STILL TRUE from D9363: reaping protects against worktrees, not against the database. The only permanent lever is the archive-and-prune, which is his call because it deletes rows after export.
