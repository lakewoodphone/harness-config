**D2907 · 2026-09-30 · Owner's rule: capability is never reduced for safety - credentials get moved, encrypted, brokered or canaried, never withdrawn.**

## 2026-09-30 · The owner's answer on credentials: capability stays, exposure goes

Asked whether the QuickBooks automation should keep its live banking and card logins — Chase, three
Bank of America accounts, two Amex cards plus the two IMAP passwords it uses to fetch one-time codes,
Amazon, eBay, PayPal — given that bundle is plaintext on three machines. His answer, verbatim:

> **"i don't want to limit your autonomy or access at all, just make it safer"**

**That is now the standing rule for every credential decision in this program, and it settles the
design in a way worth writing down:**

1. **Capability is never the thing that gets reduced.** A credential is moved, encrypted, brokered or
 canaried — never withdrawn in the name of safety. If a security measure would make an agent or the
 owner unable to do something they can do today, the measure is wrong and must be redesigned, not
 shipped. This is the same constraint as `D1` (allow-by-identity) applied to secrets.
2. **The broker must inject on demand with no approval step.** The point of holding keys centrally is
 to remove plaintext copies, not to become a gatekeeper. A prompt, a queue or a human approval
 anywhere on the credential path is a regression against this instruction.
3. **What actually shrinks is the number and readability of copies**: one canonical store at `0600`
 instead of three plaintext files in working directories; encrypted artefacts instead of `.env`
 inside tarballs; canaries so a copy that walks is detected; and a scrubber so a value cannot reach
 a transcript or a log in the first place.
4. **Corollary for backups:** the same rule applies — an encrypted artefact on the Hetzner box is
 acceptable *because* it is encrypted, and no restore path may require the owner to be present or
 awake.

Decision `D2906` recorded the architecture before this answer; this entry is the constraint that any
later credential work must be tested against. A change that makes the owner or an agent say "I can't
do that any more" fails this rule.
