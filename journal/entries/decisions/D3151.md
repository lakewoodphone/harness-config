# Closing out the old LPT work-order backlog: the approach, and the numbers that decide it

Owner's instruction, 2026-10-14, verbatim: *"time to knockout the old work order backlog and close
them out together let's do this"*.

## What "the backlog" actually is — measured, not assumed

Not the standing work orders (34 in the secretary system; those are hygiene, and mostly already
retired). **The backlog is the 2026-10-09 sweep's leftovers**: that sweep read all 641 orders
oldest→newest, changed 115 in production, and filed **108 questions to the owner**. As of today,
**80 of those are still `pending` in `owner_decision_queue`** (`source='lpt-work-order-sweep'`;
11 resolved), and 26 orders were held rather than changed. The previous session's own next-step was
"take his answers one at a time"; he has now said *together*.

The questions map to **91 distinct work orders** and 78 of them carry a work-order number in the row.

## The ruling that decides most of them — already made, 2026-10-14 03:26 UTC

On `WO26082712689` the owner settled exactly this class: the payment state is UNKNOWN and too long
ago to establish, **close the case out rather than chase it**, is neither owed nor settled, and it
is **NOT to be chased**. `payment_state` is 'unknown' *deliberately*. That ruling is applied here.

## Two keystone questions, settled from the record rather than sent to him

* **#428 — should finished orders resting in "out for pickup" be released to a collected state?**
 No action is needed, and the question answers itself: 420 orders rest at READY_FOR_PICKUP,
 **370 of them carry a `completedDate`**, the live status vocabulary has **no `COLLECTED` value**
 (7 statuses: AWAITING_DIAGNOSIS, AWAITING_APPROVAL, AWAITING_PARTS, AWAITING_PAYMENT,
 READY_FOR_PICKUP, CANCELLED, UNFIXABLE), and the owner settled the seven-state design on
 2026-10-07 with `COMPLETED` mapped to `READY_FOR_PICKUP` — his words: *"a device is paid for
 before it goes on the shelf and that IS the completed state."* **READY_FOR_PICKUP is terminal.**
 Measured 2026-10-14: READY_FOR_PICKUP 420 (370 with completedDate), UNFIXABLE 100 (95),
 CANCELLED 88 (83).

* **#427 — ~30 orders closed with "$X paid by card/cash/zelle" in a note and no payment row.**
 It is not 30, it is **222 orders**, and the real defect is bigger than the question:
 **222 of 222** orders whose notes claim a payment have **no `SUCCEEDED` payment row**, and
 **301 of the 369 rows in `payment_transactions` have no `orderId` at all** (81.6% unattached).
 So the ledger can neither confirm nor deny a payment claim. The note is the only record. The
 correct handling is therefore: **record the claim as a claim, never fabricate a payment row, and
 do not chase** (the §ruling above). A fabricated payment row would be worse than an absent one.

## The money picture across the 91 orders, measured

`comms_succeeded_payments` totals **489.99** across the 91 orders, and **only 4 of them** have any
`SUCCEEDED` payment at all (`WO2512300001` 80, `WO2601150002` 280, `WO2602180002` 80,
`WO2602190001` 49.99). Two PENDING rows exist and are **not** money received. 17 of the 91 orders
already carry duplicate notes — do not add a third.

## The store divergence, and why it must never be merged

73 of the 91 orders exist in both stores, 18 only in `operational`, and **47 disagree on status** —
the retired `app` archive still says `COMPLETED` where the live store says `READY_FOR_PICKUP`, which
is the pre-2026-10-07 vocabulary showing through. Read `operational`; never merge the two views.

## Method

Partitioned by question into 5 disjoint workstreams (80 questions, 91 orders, **zero shared work
orders** — checked programmatically). Evidence fetched once, by the parent, to files
(`closeout/orders/<WO>.json`, 91/91 found, 0 failed, ~800-1000 ms each at 6 workers) rather than
having each child reach the database, because the mesh fan-out died in the transport hop. Writes are
applied **serially, one order per invocation**, through `lpt-write`, with the note text passed as an
argv element so no shell can mangle it, and every write read back.

## And the thing this job surfaced that nobody was watching

**Every `REPAIR_COMPLETED` notification the shop has ever sent is FAILED — 300 of 300, newest
2026-10-12**, and `pickupNotifiedAt` is null on **all 420** READY_FOR_PICKUP orders. So the
dashboard has been showing "customer told" for finished repairs whose owners may never have been
told, and the shop may be holding devices nobody knows to collect. That is customer-facing, live,
and was in no queue. It is reachable and countable; it belongs on the next session's list before
any more bookkeeping.
