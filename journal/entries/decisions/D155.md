**D155 · 2026-09-15 · In-app reminders are held and age in 24h; the badge line is freshness-first; journald keeps the measured default cap.**

**Decision: the in-app reminder channel, and how the badge's one line is ordered.**

- In-app reminder channels (`dashboard`, `app`, `in_app`, or absent) enqueue into
 `owner_message_queue` with `status='held'` and `reason='reminder:<id>'`. **Held deliberately:**
 a reminder the owner scheduled must never become an outbound SMS on its own, whatever later
 happens to the owner-SMS kill switch. `sms`/`both` keep going through `send_owner_update`, which
 already falls back to the queue when the switch is on.
- A reminder ages out of the queue after **24 h** (`("reminder", 24.0)` in the prefix horizons):
 a reminder is time-bound by nature, and the item itself stays open in `reminders` either way.
- `process_due_reminders` reports `queued` beside `delivered` and `sms_sent`, because "the poll
 handled it" and "the owner can see it" are different facts and were conflated.
- The badge's waiting line is ordered **freshness first** (48 h window on the observation clock),
 then reason class (mail and reminders above briefings, sync breakers last), then urgency, then
 recency. A stale critical is still counted and dated in the summary; it no longer owns the line.
- **journald retention: keep the systemd default cap, and record why.** Measured with INFO
 logging on: the service writes **~11 MB/day** (253 KB per 30 min), the journal holds 916 MB, the
 filesystem has 209 GB free, and the default cap is 10% of the filesystem (~47.8 GB) -- years at
 this rate. An explicit `SystemMaxUse` would be a number chosen for no reason; P147 is resolved
 with this measurement instead, to be revisited only if the rate changes.
