## Owner directive 2026-10-14 ~23:47 UTC: the company must operate INSIDE its AI budget. No more top-ups.

**Verbatim, in answer to my request to top up 100 of prepaid AI credit:** *"i won't keep rasing the money, it should learn to work within it's budget"*

**This reverses the ask, and it is an engineering instruction, not a refusal to decide.** I had asked for money. The answer is that the system must be made to scale its own spend. So the deliverable is a control change, not a payment.

### What was actually wrong (measured 2026-10-14 23:40–23:50 UTC, `/root/bin/wake-balance.py`)

The company had **stopped**, and it was stuck in a way no top-up threshold explains:

1. **A dead zone that is structural.** `--floor 10.0` writes the pause below 10; auto-resume requires `bal >= floor + hysteresis` = **15.0**. Between 10 and 15 — and anywhere below 10 — the gate is a one-way door. The balance was **7.84**.
2. **The pause file cannot ever be cleared by the guard that wrote it.** `/root/.sms-inbox/WAKE_PAUSED` is **0 bytes** with mtime `2026-10-14T22:41:02Z`. The guard wrote it during the previous session's disk-full incident and **crashed mid-write** (`OSError: [Errno 28] No space left on device`, traceback at `wake-balance.py:250`) — `open(PAUSE,"w")` created the file and truncated it, and the write never landed. The resume branch is gated on `body.startswith(MARK)` where `MARK = "low-balance"`, so an empty file fails the guard's **own ownership test** and lands in the `else`: *"pause file left alone (not ours, or balance not recovered)"*. **Auto-resume was therefore impossible at any balance.**
3. **The ceiling is a fixed absolute dollar figure, which the script's own docstring already condemns.** `ceiling()` returns a constant (25.00), and the log prints the contradiction on every run: *"WARNING: the daily ceiling (25.00) is larger than the balance (8.97) - it cannot bind."* The file's opening lines say exactly this: *"A ceiling larger than the balance is not a ceiling: a runaway day can spend past the money and then die mid-work."* The guard knows the right shape and does not implement it.

### The correct shape, and it is what the owner asked for

The control must be **runway-aware, not threshold-binary**: the daily ceiling should be a function of the balance and a target horizon, so the company keeps working and simply spends more slowly as the balance falls, instead of halting at an arbitrary point and requiring a human to restart it.

- `cap = clamp(balance / runway_days, min_daily, configured_max)` — with balance 7.84 and a 30-day horizon that is **0.26/day**, which is a real ceiling that can bind, unlike 25.00.
- The hard pause should not be a fixed `10.00`; it must never leave the system in a state it cannot exit without a human.
- The pause write must be **atomic** (`tempfile` + `os.replace`) so a crash cannot leave a marker-less file that defeats the guard's own resume test.
- The resume test must not depend on the file's content in a way an interrupted write can defeat.
- Low balance should also steer **model tier and tick cadence**, not just a spend cap: `smart_router.py` already has tiers and a `premium_cost_threshold`, so a low-balance mode should force the cheapest lane.

**Burn for calibration**, from `/root/.sms-inbox/wake-balance.log`: 120.73 → 7.84, about **1.1–2.1 per hour (~25–40/day)**. So a balance of 7.84 is roughly **4–7 hours** of normal operation, or about **a month** at a 0.26/day runway ceiling. That spread is the whole argument for scaling rather than stopping.

### Not to be done

**Do not delete `WAKE_PAUSED` as a "fix".** That spends the owner's remaining credit with no control in place and the guard re-pauses on its next hourly read. The pause must be replaced by a runwayscaled ceiling, and the empty-file defect fixed, in one change with a test.
