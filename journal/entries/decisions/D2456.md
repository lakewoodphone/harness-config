**D2456 · 2026-09-23 · Shlock 2026-09-23, all decisions: pinned poles, through-bolted tow to the pole ends, one line, 16 points at 25 mph, sides and middle left open.**

**Decision record for 2026-09-23, the day the Shlock material arrived and the cover went up. The owner asked for every decision taken today to be written down, including his dissatisfaction with the hold-down outcome.**

**State.** HD order in hand (20 hitching rings, 6 x 10-ft top-rail sticks, 2 pulleys). Tarp up on the frame. Poles
not yet cut and not yet in the pockets. Sukkos sundown Fri 2026-09-25.

**Material.** Do NOT cut the third stick - three full 10-ft sticks per pole (30 ft) is what leaves bare pipe at the
ends for the tow. Pin both swaged joints on each pole (2 x 1/4-in bolts per seam, second turned 90 deg) BEFORE the
poles go into the pockets: the July mechanism document says swaged joints hinge under lateral load, and after the pole
is in the pocket the seams are buried inside the fabric. The internal-ferrule-plus-4-pins splice is superseded (the
sticks are swaged; and both ferrule sizes ever named are dimensionally impossible).

**Drive.** Hand-pulling at the leading edge is impossible (9 ft up, 28.5 ft wide, schach in between), so the cover
can only be moved by rope. Tow the two protruding ends of the leading pole - not the fabric, no grommet in the load
path; the four-rope bridle is deleted. Four fixes are mandatory: pinned joints; a 1/4-in cross-bolt through each pipe
end with the rope on a shackle (the rope-eye-plus-hose-clamps detail is deleted - it was resisting 3 lb by friction);
a pull point at mid-edge (the pole's horizontal bending capacity is only 19.6-50.7 lb per end against 21-150 lb of tow
load); and ONE continuous line to both ends (a rigid bar transmits force, not equality; two ropes rack it). The
pulley goes at the fabric plane on the house-side post, never low (a low pulley loads the tip at 78% of allowable).
Measure with the 300 lb scale before choosing a ratio; 2:1 on the hard direction. Operator stands behind the turn.

**Hold-downs.** The 20 hitching rings do not ride the rails. Rope loops on the rails are dead (capstan: drag = the
load it carries x mu x pi). The 14 side-edge grommets and the 6 middle grommets are NOT secured this season; they stay
open as the cover's only interior drains. The owner's hard constraint: nothing rigid on any rail, because each part's
length times the number of points on that rail becomes the minimum the cover can gather to - his example, 3 middle
points per rail at 2 in = 6 in that would still stick out past the house when pulled fully in.

**Operating rules.** 16 points (8 leading-edge hangers + 8 rope ties on the fixed house edge, which does not travel
and so escapes every finding above) gives 129 lb per point at 40 mph and ~43 mph grommet tear-out, so: deploy below
25 mph, furl at 25 mph forecast gusts. The 8 cam-buckle house straps were never ordered. Retract only when dry - a
gather dams the only fall the design has and sends water into the sukkah. The retract stop is set by hand watching the
fabric pack; the stroke is 108 in, not 144.

**Open.** Four measurements gate everything (rail count and one gap; grommet bore #4 vs #5; in-service point count;
pocket ends open or stitched). Queue #79 (permit) is still the owner's. The middle-and-sides hold-down is unresolved
and mine.

**Owner-visible dissatisfaction, recorded as stated:** he is very unhappy that no good solution was found for securing
the middle and the sides. Everything tried failed on one of three grounds - it jams, it grips, or it holds the cover
open. See `2026-09-23-shlock-decisions.md` §6 for the whole solution space so it is not searched twice.
