**D9289 · 2026-10-21 · Never sold or shared, kept forever, and identity separated from behaviour.**

## Decision 2026-10-21 · Never sold or shared, kept forever, and identity separated from behaviour (Q18)

aliases: Q18 · D-20261021-17 · docs/decisions/D-20261021-17-pseudonymous-data.md

Owner's answer, verbatim: "Never sold or shared, used for product improvement, kept forever, but personal identifying details should be stripped in the logs or something like that. We anyways have to build a way to abstract the actual personal user from his phone and data."

1. Never sold, never shared — no advertising, brokers, partners or analytics vendor that keeps a copy. Flat promise, written on the page in those words.
2. Used for product improvement, by us, read by nobody else.
3. Kept forever — no expiry that quietly throws away the corpus the second year builds on.
4. Identity separated from behaviour: person and phone are one record, and everything the product learns is stored against a pseudonymous subject — never a name, email, phone number or IMEI. The mapping lives in one place, under its own access rule.
5. Logs carry the pseudonym only; a log line naming a customer or a phone is a defect.

Now required: a subject id on every behavioural row, the binding table encrypted at rest, backed up separately and never joined into an analytical query; no third-party analytics SDK; a deletion right beside "kept forever" that keeps only the billing record; audit retention and encryption, both off today, so this corpus is stored in the clear; a scrubber that fails a test when a name, email, number or IMEI reaches a log line or a complaint body. Images keep their own thirty-day rule (D-03), not this one.
