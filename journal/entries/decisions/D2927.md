**D2927 · 2026-10-06 · Yitzchak car insurance: his own NY policy at the real Brooklyn address, not the parents' NJ policy.**

DECIDED 2026-10-06 by Zabz on evidence, not routed to the owner except for one question (whose name is
on the title, which is a fact about his family, not an engineering parameter).

DECISION. Yitzchak Zabrowsky (23, DOB 2003-07-28), who will live in Brooklyn NY, insures his newly
bought car on HIS OWN New York policy, rated at his true Brooklyn garaging address. He does not go on
his parents' Lakewood NJ policy.

WHY (three independent grounds, all sourced in the case file)
1. It is not a preference, it is a registration prerequisite. NY DMV: "The New York State DMV does not
 accept out-of-state insurance"; the coverage "must be issued in the name of the vehicle registrant";
 "Your vehicle's insurance and registration must always show the exact same name"; "If your vehicle is
 registered in New York, it must have New York State auto liability insurance coverage."
2. The workaround is rate evasion with a measured enforcement trail. NJ territorial rating is defined
 as a ZIP map OF New Jersey priced to "vehicle garage location" (N.J.A.C. 11:3-16A). NY DFS issued a
 308 data call on 2026-01-23 explicitly measuring out-of-state garaging misrepresentation, and
 insurers reported the out-of-state share of that loss more than doubling to 6.5 million by 2025, with
 New Jersey one of the top three states used.
3. It fails asymmetrically and in the wrong direction. V&T 313 protects the innocent third party and
 permits prospective cancellation only (Peak v Mulverhill, 2025), and State Farm v Able Chiropractic
 (2025) requires the carrier to produce its underwriting manual to prove materiality - so the
 STRANGER usually gets paid. The INSURED is the one who loses: his own collision claim, his own
 no-fault/PIP claim. That is the bill for the car landing on him personally.

THE ONE LEGITIMATE FAMILY ROUTE, RECORDED SO IT IS NOT RELITIGATED. A parent holds a NEW YORK policy
rated at the true Brooklyn address, with the car titled and registered to that parent, and Yitz is a
covered resident relative on it - expressly contemplated by 11 NYCRR 67.1(b) ("related ... by blood,
marriage or adoption ... who regularly resides in the named insured's household, including any such
person temporarily living elsewhere"). Recommendation is still to leave the title in Yitz's name:
ownership is worth keeping clear, and the household saving is bought with a parent's exposure and a
parent's rating record. NOT routed to the owner as a menu - it is recorded as the alternative if the
family asks, with the recommendation stated.

ALSO DECIDED, IN THE SAME PASS. Do not recommend NY minimum limits as the cheap option. In Brooklyn the
gap between minimum and full coverage collapses to about 16 percent (215 vs 255 a month, Insurify,
2026-10-05) because the mandatory PIP and UM loads are large and fixed, so minimum limits save almost
nothing and give up the coverage protecting a car bought for 20,325.
