**D3915 · 2026-10-16 · The journal single writer is the authority canonical checkout; the fork is bigger than five trees.**

DECISION — the journal's single writer is the authority's canonical checkout, and the repair is the
lossless union. Written 2026-10-16 01:1xZ from a ZABZ-YOGA session, ON the authority.

WHERE THE SINGLE WRITER IS. `/root/code/harness-config` on `secratary-cloud`. This is not a
preference argued from first principles: the authority's own `journal-commit.sh` states it in its
header, and has since it was ported on 2026-10-11 — "The canonical journal checkout on this estate
is `/root/code/harness-config` on the authority, not `~/code/harness-config` on a laptop that is
shut at night." Read in this session, and its cron line is live: `*/15 * * * *
/usr/local/bin/cron-wrap.sh journal-commit` in the authority's crontab, so that tree's journal is
committed and published to `vault` on a clock.

PROVEN A WRITER, NOT A MIRROR. The same tree holds entries whose own marker carries host
`secratary-cloud`: `pain/P4569` (mtime 2026-10-15 23:59:58Z), `lessons/L5729` (23:59:39Z),
`L5728` (23:58:30Z). An authority-local session wrote them. The `journal-commit.sh` reflog agrees:
`journal: autocommit 3 path(s) on main from secratary-cloud at 2026-10-16T01:00:05Z`.

THE FORK IS BIGGER THAN FIVE TREES — this is the round's new measurement, and it changes the source
set the union must be built from. Rounds 5 and 6 sized the fork as five machine checkouts. Measured
tonight:
 * 21 directories on the authority contain a `journal/entries` (`find /root -maxdepth 6 -type d
 -path '*/journal/entries'`), including `/root/wt-*`, `/root/_lpt*_wt` and `/root/work/*`.
 * Exactly ONE of them holds ids the canonical checkout lacks: `/root/work/wt-item221`, the
 worktree of branch `hk/221-live-wake-reconcile` (HEAD `dd2fc442`), with **234 ids absent from
 main** — `decisions/D3111` and up among them, present in that worktree's HEAD and absent from
 the canonical one. Measured with `comm -13` over filename lists, `LC_ALL=C` on both sides.
 * ZABZ-YOGA holds ~40 directories containing `journal/entries` (~74,000 entry files) under
 `C:\Users\ezabz\code`, most of them worktrees and clones of the one repo.
A five-source union would have reported "0 bodies missing" while blind to those 234. That is why
round 8 added `/root/work/wt-item221` as a source and re-ran the collection.

THE REPAIR IS THE LOSSLESS UNION, NOT A VERDICT ABOUT WHICH BODY WINS. Round 6 built and verified it
(4,894 distinct bodies, `BODIES MISSING 0`, `BODIES INVENTED 0`). Where two trees hold one id with
two different bodies, BOTH bodies are kept and the loser is renumbered by `journal.py repair-ids` —
which reproduces the intent of the 2026-10-11 item-163 cross-machine repair mechanically instead of
re-deciding it by hand. The alternative was priced by `docs/mesh/117-journal-divergence.md`
(2026-09-18, ten colliding ids on ZABZ-TECH): per-id human adjudication, requiring a human authority
on both machines, per collision.

WHAT THIS DECISION DOES NOT SETTLE, AND WHY IT GOES TO THE OWNER. Whether the other machines stop
writing their own checkouts and become consumers. The union itself is safe and reversible — it is a
new tree under `/root/work/`, and no live journal has been touched. The cutover is not: it changes
how every session on every machine records the estate's only memory.

NOT VERIFIED HERE: whether the cross-host reservation ledger (`refs/heads/alloc/<host>`, `bands.tsv`,
`ceiling.json`) already prevents NEW collisions once each host's bands are visible to the others.
`journal.py`'s own code and comments describe that design in detail, while §2.0 of the round-6
handoff reports two trees holding the same window. The two have not been reconciled in this session,
and the answer decides whether the switchover is a one-time repair or needs a mechanism fix as well.
