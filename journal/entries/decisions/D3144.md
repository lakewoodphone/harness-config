Why it was wrong — three independent findings that all point the same way:
1. VALUE. Kate Farms is 1.29 USD per 100 kcal against his old Soylent at 0.875 — 47% more per calorie —
 and roughly 14-22x the cost of supermarket food. The owner pushed back on the price himself.
2. FULLNESS. Kate Farms is the LEAST filling of the three candidates per bottle: smallest volume
 (325 mL), least protein (16 g), and its 6 g fibre advantage is worth nothing for satiety. Korczak &
 Slavin 2018 and Liber & Szajewska 2013: fructan doses below ~10 g/day do not affect satiety or intake.
 Fat is a weak satiety signal anyway (Blundell 1993).
3. HEALTH. Its sugar is the worst on the label at 18 g (agave syrup + brown rice syrup solids) against
 Soylent's 1 g and OWYN's 4 g. And the claim I used to justify it - "cleaner ingredients" - collapsed:
 Kate Farms has its OWN earlier Prop 65 record (AG 2017-02692, cadmium and lead, settled 2018-05-18),
 publishes no batch heavy-metal data, and had a 2022 Class I microbial recall (Lyons Magnus). It is
 absent from both the Clean Label Project study and the Consumer Reports investigation. Neither brand
 is clean.
