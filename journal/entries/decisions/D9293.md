## Decision 2026-10-21 · The floor is adult content and gambling; the product protects against impulse, not against sin (Q22)

aliases: Q22 · D-20261021-21 · docs/decisions/D-20261021-21-impulse-not-morals.md

Owner's answer, verbatim: "Adult content, gambling; illegal material is not our job as a filter. Our filter protects users from current impulse against rational thinking, not decides morals for them."

1. Level one still blocks adult content and gambling. Those two are the floor at every level, not negotiable at any price or setting.
2. We take on no duty to detect or report illegal material: it is not a category we promise to police and there is no reporting pipeline. Where it falls inside adult content it is blocked for the ordinary reason, not because we are the enforcement.
3. The thesis of the whole product, first stated in the owner's own words: it protects a person from his own impulse in the moment, against what he would choose if he were thinking. It does not decide what is moral for him.
4. That thesis is the standard for copy, warnings and defaults: a sentence telling a customer what is good for him is off-brand, one showing him what he is about to trade away is on-brand.

Now required: the advisory layer's voice changes (D-08's benefits, costs and red marks now read "you will want this at eleven at night, and this is what it costs you then"); the delays become the centre of gravity, since a delay is the mechanism that interposes thought between impulse and act; the floor is two categories, and any addition needs an impulse-related reason, not a taste; no copy claims we prevent sin or illegality; the legal posture is recorded rather than assumed.
