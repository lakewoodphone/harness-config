**D9372 · 2026-10-27 · Deletions of customer data are the owner's act, never a session's (2026-10-27).**

Owner, 2026-10-27, verbatim, in answer to "Delete sarah levi (1.2 GB, 33 days, job completed and paid)?": **"no, i do deletions not you"**.

WHAT THAT BINDS, AND IT IS BROADER THAN THE ONE FOLDER

Deletion of customer data is the owner's act. Not "gated behind a per-action yes" (which is what the 2026-09-30
retention policy said) - HIS HANDS. A tool I write that CAN delete customer data is wrong by construction, because
the switch exists only to be reached for by some later session that reads the policy and not the man.

SO, CONCRETELY:

- `harness-config/scripts/windows/sweep-transfer-staging.ps1` NO LONGER HAS ANY DELETE PATH. `-Delete` (staging
 folders) and `-DeleteCustomerData` (the customer-data folder) are both removed, and the header says why. It
 reports age and size and prints, per folder past the window, the exact `rd /s /q "<path>"` line he can run.
 Verified after the change: the sweep ran, printed the sarah levi line, and left the folder in place.
- `docs/shop/customer-data-retention.md` says the same in words, so the next session reads the rule where it is
 meant to be read rather than inferring a capability from a script's parameter list.
- The wrapper `run-customer-data-sweep.ps1` reports only, and always did.

WHAT I DID NOT DO, DELIBERATELY: delete `sarah levi` (1.2 GB, 2,779 files, landed 2026-09-24, 33 days, job
WO2609030001 completed 2026-09-08 with 159.94 CAPTURED). It is eligible and the command is printed for him. It is
still on disk as of this entry.

THE GENERAL RULE FOR ANY FUTURE TOOL: if a job needs a deletion, the deliverable is the exact command and the
evidence that it is safe to run - never an execution path. Same shape as the outbound-comms rule: showing the body
is the work, sending is his.

Related and separate: the same session built the Shande customer pack (`FOR-CUSTOMER`, 577 files / 1,963,816,038
bytes, PASS) - her library only, no recovered stills, no cache, no phone logs. Detail is in the case file; the
relevant point here is that it was built by COPYING, never by pruning the source, which is why nothing needed
deleting to produce it.
