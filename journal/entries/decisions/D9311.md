**D9311 · 2026-10-21 · One device-side modesty policy: keep the loop's meter (220-case parity) and my staging, delete the duplicate levels reader.**

Made 2026-10-22 01:1xZ, while two implementations of the same thing were both in flight. Recorded here because a
decision that lives only in a session is thrown away with it.

## The contradiction

Two device-side modesty implementations exist for ledger 1062:

**A. `origin/agent/kf-1062-kotlin-meter` (pushed by the wake loop, 6 commits, 0 days idle).**
Package `com.lakewoodphoneandtech.filterai.modesty`:
- `ModestyLevel` — the 19-field rung table, as generated from `server/app/modesty_meter.py`.
- `ModestyLevels` — reads `assets/modesty_levels.json`, version-checked.
- `ModestyMeter` — `ModestyObservations` (17 nullable fields) -> `ModestyVerdict(level, action, reasons,
 unknowns)`, action in allow/warn/blur/block. **Pinned to the Python meter by a 220-case generated
 fixture** (`server/scripts/emit_modesty_verdict_fixture.py` + `ModestyMeterFixtureTest`).

**B. `main` (merged by me tonight as PR #111).** Package `...filterai.ml`:
- `ModestyLevels` — a SECOND asset reader, inside `LadderRungLevels.kt:30`.
- `LadderRung` / `LadderRungCascade` — staging: rung 1 from the floor model alone, rungs 2-8 sharing one
 pose pass and one skin pass, short-circuit at the first failing rung, three answers
 (known / absent / unmeasurable), cover-on-uncertainty.
- `LadderRungDeviceEvidence`, `SkinFraction`, `PoseSkinRegions` — the observation producers.
- 38 tests including a parity test, but the assertions are hand-written, not generated from the server.

**Two `internal object ModestyLevels` in two packages, reading the same asset.** They compile because the
packages differ; they are still two sources of truth for the same numbers, which is the defect the repo
files as its own ("a second source of truth for the same numbers, and the second one is the one nobody
updates").

## The decision

**One policy, one asset reader; the staging layer stays.**

1. The asset reader that survives is A's `modesty/ModestyLevels.kt`, because the generator's drift test
 already keeps it honest. B's copy inside `LadderRungLevels.kt` is deleted.
2. The policy evaluator that survives is A's `ModestyMeter`, because 220 generated cases is a stronger
 parity guarantee than 38 hand-written assertions, **and** because it carries four actions
 (allow/warn/blur/block) where B carries two. The product needs warn and blur to honour "cover it, then
 correct it"; a binary evaluator cannot express them.
3. B's staging layer stays and changes job: `LadderRungCascade` becomes the thing that produces
 `ModestyObservations` as cheaply as it can (floor model first, one pose pass, skin fraction over pose
 regions, tier/battery/thermal refusal) and then **asks `ModestyMeter` for the verdict** instead of
 re-deriving the rules itself.
4. The `SkinFraction` dark-skin blind spot its author documented stays documented and stays a known gap:
 the classical colour rule returns 0.0 on a fully exposed dark-skinned torso, and the class doc says the
 learned classifier is the shippable one.

## Why not the other way round

Deleting A and keeping B would throw away the only generated parity fixture in the tree and would keep a
device evaluator that can only say allow or block. Deleting B and keeping A would throw away the staging,
which is what makes the filter fast on a cheap phone - and speed is part of the promise the owner wrote
into D-20261021-01.

## What this costs, stated honestly

A refactor of `LadderRungCascade`, not a merge: two suites must both stay green afterwards (the 220-case
fixture and the 38 ladder tests), and the ladder's `ladderVerified`/`ladderAbsent` reporting has to be fed
from `ModestyVerdict.unknowns` rather than from its own three-answer enum. It is the right work, and it is
not a five-minute job.
