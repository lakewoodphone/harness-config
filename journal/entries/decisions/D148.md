**D148 · 2026-09-14 · The parent-facing claim step does not exist, and the right fix was to delete it from the plan rather than build an anti-abuse endpoint.**

**Found while trying to make onboarding stage 3 act, and it removed work rather than adding it.**

I assumed `POST /owner/api/phones/bootstrap` let a parent attach a phone by entering the activation code from their setup sheet. It does not. Read from source (`server/app/routers/account_owner_dashboard.py:2352`), that route **generates a pairing code for a `phone_id` the caller already has** — `phone_id` is a required form field, which is precisely what a parent does not know. It is the shop's side of the flow.

What exists, in full: the shop attaches the phone (`POST /admin/households/{id}/phones`), the shop issues a code (`POST /pairing/bootstrap/create`), and **the phone** redeems it (`POST /pairing/bootstrap/redeem`). A human never redeems one, and that is deliberate — the dealer runbook says the handset redeeming the code is the anti-abuse proof of physical possession.

**Decision: the parent-facing claim step is DROPPED, not deferred.** The product is shop-provisioned; the attach already happened correctly at the bench. Asking a parent to re-attach online would duplicate that step while adding an anti-abuse-sensitive endpoint (single-use, expiring, household-scoped, rate-limited, inert until the device redeems) — and getting any of those wrong would let a paper-code holder attach another household's phone to their own account. The cheapest correct move was to not build it.

**What this changes for W-BACK-052**, now stages 4–6 rather than 2–7:
- stage 4 (choose the level) needs a **new owner-scoped route** — the rules endpoints are admin-scoped today, so a parent cannot change their own level. Unlike the claim step this is worth building, and it must clamp to the recorded hard floor rather than accept whatever it is handed.
- stage 5 (verify) is read-only and already has its endpoints.
- stage 6 (billing) is now genuinely reachable, because the live Stripe price and `/billing/subscribe` exist.

*The general rule:* before wiring a UI step to a backend call, read the call's **required parameters** and ask who actually holds them. A route whose identity is the shop's can look like the parent's from outside — same owner-scoped session, same household — and the difference only appears when you ask what a parent could possibly put in the field.
