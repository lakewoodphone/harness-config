**D8579 · 2026-10-19 · Bench refuses asleep/locked handsets; screen state is recorded not scored; bars calibrated from five units; key window is an option.**

Four decisions taken while the first five handsets were on the cable, each because a measurement contradicted an assumption.

1. **The bench refuses to run on a sleeping or locked handset.** Not a warning - a refusal (`bench.py::_ensure_awake`). Rationale: on Android 9 a non-visible app is denied the camera and silenced on the mic, and that produced a NOT_SELLABLE verdict on two healthy E4810s. A tool that reports a hardware fault it caused itself is worse than a tool that stops.
2. **"Screen state" is recorded, not scored.** The audit's own probe runs before the bench wakes the phone, so scoring it would contradict the result three lines later. It is `n/a` with the reading attached; the enforcement lives where it belongs, in the bench.
3. **Pass bars are calibrated from measurement, and the calibration is recorded beside the constant.** Loopback bar \u221240 -> \u221230 dBFS, margin 9 -> 20 dB, from a measured healthy spread of \u221213.6 to \u221213.9 dBFS and 33.1 to 56.2 dB across five units.
4. **The key window is a CLI option (`--key-window-seconds`, default 15).** One 60-second window across a row of five handsets lets the owner close five keypad checks in one pass instead of five separate visits.

Open question deliberately left open: whether an audit should be allowed to toggle radios to read the Wi-Fi MAC. For now it does not mutate anything, and reports `n/a` with the reason when Wi-Fi is off.
