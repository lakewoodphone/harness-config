**D64 · 2026-09-14 · Two independent studies disagreed about whether COPPA applies, and the reconciliation — not either finding — is the decision.**

The COPPA stream assumed the amended rule governs (a photograph containing a child's image is personal information) and designed conservatively from there. The state-law stream found the FTC's own FAQ A.8 — *"COPPA only applies to personal information collected online **from** children"*, so information from parents is outside it — plus FAQ F.4, and concluded the rule does not apply on these facts. Both read primary sources. The reconciliation is what matters: the **baseline** on-device mode is outside COPPA on either reading (nothing is collected online), while the **escalation** mode loses the shield because the definition of "collects" includes *"passive tracking of a child online"* and our capture is automatic — which is exactly the conservative path already chosen. So both streams land on the same architecture, the disagreement became evidence for it, and the record shows the reasoning rather than a smoothed-over single answer (`L57`).
