**D265 · 2026-09-17 · Shlock as-built, from the owner's own 83 frames: joints are clamped, nothing is tied to a footing, the house tie is a hand lashing, the sukkah is commercial and independent, and the rail count is the open unknown.**

**From the owner's own 2026-09-17 photographs (44 frames) and his 90-second narrated 4K video, analysed by 15 agents and verified by me on the native pixels in three places. Every claim below is tagged by its source in `2026-09-17-shlock-as-built-site-record.md`.**

1. **Pipe-to-pipe joints ARE positively clamped - "the rails just rest on the beams" is dead where it was photographed.** The node he narrates at 74.8-83.2 s as *"the metal frame is held on both ends by those types of brackets"* carries **two opposed U-bolt and formed-saddle-plate assemblies, one either side of the crossing pipe, with a nut provably engaged on a visible thread** (video f033, t=80 s; corroborated by native crops at IMG_0896, IMG_0885, IMG_0887, IMG_0895). Consequence: the "roof panel leaves the post tops at about 40 mph" failure mode is **removed at the joints shown and nowhere else** - the exact role of that node (beam-to-post, rail-to-beam, or a crossing at mid-span) is not established, so this does not prove the frame as a whole is restrained.

2. **No post is positively tied to its footing, anywhere, in any of the 83 frames.** Post bases are consistently a rough unformed grey collar spilling into gravel, with no base plate, anchor bolt, cage or hold-down. The house-side grey patches are utility patching around the downspout and cable penetrations - a torn mortar bag is still lying beside them - not footings. The uplift case therefore stands exactly as the wind check has it (net pull-out 56-85 mph depending on the depth that cannot be seen), and the cheap fix that makes the depth question moot is a rod epoxied into each existing pier and tied up to the post.

3. **"The steel cable" is an untensioned hand lashing, not a tie.** At t=10.0 s the house-side post carries stranded wire rope coiled by hand around the pipe with a loop standing off toward a wall-mounted slotted strut channel - **and no turnbuckle, thimble, swage, wire-rope clip, eye bolt or shackle anywhere in frame, with no visible termination at either end.** A hand coil with free ends cannot be tensioned and carries no load. Today the house-side posts are tied to nothing.

4. **The sukkah on the deck is commercial** - its own label reads `SUKKAH OUTLET` / `(973) 778-56..` - and it is standing with its walls up and its s'chach supports in place. **The metal tarp frame sits above it with visible daylight between them and nothing bearing or bracketing between the two**, so the sukkah's timber carries none of the cover's load. Four analysts had independently logged that panel as "cannot tell whether this is the tarp"; the label settled it.

5. **The tarp is folded on the deck floor, not installed.** One deck-level frame (t=62.5 s) shows **two grommets and a doubled hem** - the only sight of the tarp's own hardware in the entire corpus. The 342 x 144 in finished-size inspection against ALCO's locked spec is still outstanding and still time-limited, because the order is custom and non-returnable.

6. **The white collar is a UHMW sleeve with a worm-drive band clamp over it** (video f028, t=67.5 s). His account is "each pole has one of these guys on it"; the images confirm **one**, once, so the distribution is his word, not a count. Where the load path runs through a band clamp gripping plastic by friction, it is clamped by friction rather than bolted through steel - stated as a local observation, not generalised to the eight rails' end fixings, which no frame shows.

7. **THE OPEN UNKNOWN THAT MATTERS MOST: how many of the eight rails are installed.** 6-8 long galvanised pipes are visible lying loose on the bare sand in the yard (t=37.5 s, two with blue tape near an end) and a grid of pipe lies across the deck in the stills. The wind numbers are sized per rail at 42 in on centre and assume all eight; if fewer are up, each carries more area and every threshold drops. Asked of the owner directly; not derivable from the images.
