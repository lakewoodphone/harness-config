**D3210 · 2026-10-14 · Owner answer, queue row #510: keep selling, his devices, users not buyers.**

**D · 2026-10-14 · Owner answer on the Apple Business Manager Agreement §2.5 question. His words, verbatim.**

**Asked** (queue row **#510**, critical): Apple's Apple Business Manager Agreement §2.5 says the
Institution *"will not resell any Authorized Devices with Device Enrollment Settings enabled"*, and its
definitions add that devices *"personally-owned by an individual"* are not permitted in supervised device
management without Apple's written agreement. LPT NAVIGATION already sells supervised, MDM-locked iPhones
to consumers at 299, and the camera product would do the same. Keep selling, or change the model?

**His answer, verbatim:** *"The answer is basically three. We're not being busy with this now, and I didn't
ask you about that. And also, technically speaking, we're not truly selling them, they're our devices that
we're letting them use."*

Option three was **carry on as-is, no legal spend now**.

**The standing position, recorded so no future session re-raises it:**
- **No legal review, no change to the sales model, no pause.** LPT continues.
- **The basis he states is ownership, not resale:** the devices remain LPT's; customers are being *allowed
 to use* them rather than buying them. That is a real distinction under the clause, and it is his call to
 make.

**Two things I am recording without arguing them further,** because burying a known counter-consideration
would be dishonest even inside an answer I accept:
1. The same agreement defines **"Authorized Users" as *"employees and Contract Employees … of Your
 Institution"*.** A retained-ownership model speaks to the "resell" half of §2.5 but not obviously to
 the Authorized-Users half, which is about *who* the device is for rather than who owns it.
2. Apple's remedy is not a lawsuit — §2.3 reserves the right to *"revoke or disable Server Tokens at any
 time in its sole discretion"* — so if it ever acted, the effect would land on every enrolled device at
 once, including ones already in customers' hands. That is why I raised it, and it is why the *affected
 surface* is worth remembering even though the decision is not to act.

**He also said plainly: *"I didn't ask you about that."*** That is fair and it is recorded as feedback —
he commissioned camera research, and I ended that piece of work by putting a legal question about a
different, already-running product in front of him. The finding came out of the legal work he *did*
commission, and it does bear on the camera, so reporting it was right; **making it the closing question of
the turn was not.** It belonged in the file and on the queue, which is where it now is.
