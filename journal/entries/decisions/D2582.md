## The LPT closure calendar is generated from the Hebrew calendar, not hand-entered — and the shop closes the whole festival

**Decided 2026-09-24 on ZABZ-TECH.** Two decisions, both now enforced in code rather than in
someone's head.

**1. Closure policy: the whole festival period.** The shop is closed for erev, both days of Yom
Tov, *and* Chol HaMoed, for Rosh Hashana, Yom Kippur, Sukkos, Pesach and Shavuos. That is 10 days
for Sukkos (14-23 Tishrei), 9 for Pesach (14-22 Nisan) and 3 for Shavuos (5-7 Sivan).

Basis, in order of strength:
- The owner's own words on 2026-09-24: closed for Sukkos starting the 25th, reopening the 5th.
 That window is exactly 14-23 Tishrei — it is not open to interpretation.
- Dialpad's pre-existing LPT holidays agree on the anchor dates (Sukkot II = 16 Tishrei = Sep 27;
 Simchat Torah = 23 Tishrei = Oct 4).

**Do not "reconcile" this with the Shabbos power automation.** `shabbat_power_down_holidays=false`
and the 2026-09-11 note calling Chol HaMoed "an ordinary working day" are statements about
*melacha*, which is forbidden only on Shabbos and Yom Tov. The shop-closure question is a business
question and has a different answer. Both are correct; they are not the same fact. The power
automation keeps HA/cameras **up** on Chol HaMoed and the phone system says the shop is **closed**.

**2. Generate it, never hand-enter it.** `scripts/dialpad-holidays.py` computes the calendar from
`pyluach` and writes both Dialpad objects. Hand-entering is what produced the previous state: 7
ad-hoc 2026 rows covering only the Yom Tov days that happened to fall on a weekday, with Sukkos I,
all of Chol HaMoed, Shmini Atzeret, Shavuot I/II and all of Pesach 2027-2028 simply absent. The
entries are `no_repeat` **on purpose** — Jewish dates drift against the Gregorian calendar, so a
repeating holiday becomes wrong within a few years. Re-run `apply --live-write` yearly; `--until`
defaults to 2028-12-31.
