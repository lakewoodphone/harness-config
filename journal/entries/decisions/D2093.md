**D2093 · 2026-09-20 · D335 - recommendation on the autopilot cadence: raise the interval to 900s first, then measure per-subsystem cost.**

The measurement (L2153) leaves three genuinely different options for the autopilot cadence. This records
my recommendation and the reasoning, so the next session does not re-derive it.

**Recommendation: raise `AUTOPILOT_INTERVAL_SEC` from 300 to 900, as a measurement step rather than a
fix.** Reasons:

1. **It is the only change that is immediately verifiable.** With the interval longer than a run, the
 abandon/backoff churn should stop and `run_count` should advance roughly once per interval. That
 gives a clean before/after, which the other two options do not.
2. **It costs less than it looks.** The work is NOT driven by `run_once` - 110 work sessions in 6 hours
 against 2 loop completions (L2153). The queues and APScheduler jobs keep running on their own
 threads, so slowing the wrapper slows the *scanning* cadence, not the work.
3. **It is trivially reversible.** One environment variable, and the previous value is in the record.

**Then instrument before choosing further.** The system records no per-subsystem duration - 37 subsystem
entries in `autopilot_state.json`, every one with `dur=None`. Without that, "make each subsystem cheaper"
cannot be targeted, and I would be guessing. The instrument is small: time each subsystem in `run_once`
and store it, then the next session can see which one consumes the 480 s.

**What I am NOT doing unilaterally.** The interval controls how fast the company reacts to external
events (a webhook, a customer message). The event-driven path has its own trigger and its own minimum
gap, so a longer *interval* should not slow event response - but that is an inference from reading
`run_once`, not a measurement, and if it is wrong the owner loses responsiveness. That is his call to
make, so it is queued rather than applied.

**Explicitly rejected for now:** letting `run_once` delegate entirely to the queues. It is probably the
right long-term shape, since the queues demonstrably do the work, but it is a refactor of the top-level
loop and should follow the measurement, not precede it.
