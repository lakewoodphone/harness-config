**D6721 · 2026-10-18 · The sign is an advertisement: the product name and the price go at the top, and no invented benefit lines.**

Owner, 2026-10-18, on the sign: "The sign is like more of like an advertisement about our device, so it doesn't
even see anywhere you need like the top saying ... buy your Waze Device for $285 ... or maybe like a new
product." Two things are settled by that:

1. **The sign is an advertisement, so the top must announce the product and the price.** A benefit headline
 ("Waze in your car.") is not an ad headline - it never says what is being sold or that it is for sale. The
 hero is now: a "New product" tag, the product name at 94 px, a one-line identity under it ("An iPhone 11
 that runs Waze on your dash."), and the price card beside it. Cold read after the change: "it's unmistakably
 an ad: price, phone number, warranty/returns, new product" - which is the first time a cold reader has said
 the sign reads as an ad at all.

2. **Also settled in the same message:** drop "No phone in your hand. No mount." (he called it "a weird
 statement"); the cap must be explained with its premise or not shown at all ("if it has 8 a month for data,
 then why are you setting a cap? You're not explaining that whole concept"); and "Only the data renews" is
 "weird" - he wants his original three benefits and nothing invented; and the last benefit row he could not
 parse ("A shop, not a website") is deleted. **Rule for future sign copy: do not invent benefit lines to solve
 a layout problem. He reads invented lines as noise.**

The cap sentence that finally worked, and the reason it works: "Billed monthly, on top of the device. Some months
use more, so you set a cap on the bill and it can't run past it." Cold read of that: "a guard against a surprise
bill, not a claim about how much data you'll need." The premise ("some months use more") is the part that was
missing for four rounds; without it a cap on a flat monthly price reads as nonsense.

Still open and named twice by cold readers: **there is no photograph of the device.** That is the one defect no
typography fixes.
