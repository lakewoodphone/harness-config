**D9291 · 2026-10-21 · A parent must be present at the counter to sign for a child's phone.**

## Decision 2026-10-21 · A parent must be present at the counter to sign for a child's phone (Q20)

aliases: Q20 · D-20261021-19 · docs/decisions/D-20261021-19-parent-present.md

Owner's answer, verbatim: "Parent must be present."

1. A child's phone is never sealed without the parent physically at the counter — not a link, not a signature emailed afterwards, not a grandparent acting on a parent's word. If the parent is not there, the install does not happen and the phone goes back in the bag.
2. The parent signs the notice at the counter, and the shop checks an ID against the name on it.
3. The parent is the consenting party of record for that phone, matching what D-02 already gives him: the floor, the band, the chaver's powers and the right to delete the household's data.

Now required: the counter gets a refusal it does not have — "I need the parent here to sign for this phone", said before the seal is attempted; the signed notice is a record (name, ID checked, date, phone id, the level the phone was left at, the signature) filed with the household; the seal is gated on the signature for a child's phone, so the product enforces the policy rather than the tech's memory; a gift phone stays unsealed until the parent brings it in; for an adult nothing changes; the child is told in the app what was agreed and by whom.
