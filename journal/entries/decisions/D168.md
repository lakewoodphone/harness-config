**D168 · 2026-09-15 · The /api/v1/* blanket exemption is the root cause of 141 unauthenticated routes; deny-by-default with an allowlist is the real fix.**

DECIDED 2026-09-15 (manager, on measured evidence). _api_access_guard (app/main.py:361-366) exempts everything under /api/v1/ before any key check, so each of ~600 handlers must authenticate itself and 141 do not. I applied an ADDITIVE hotfix (middleware consults the existing _dashboard_path_requires_api_key() helper; nine sensitive prefixes named) because it closes the dangerous routes without risking the ~600 legitimate callers, and loopback stays trusted so internal callers are unaffected. This is a mitigation, not the fix.

THE REAL FIX, deferred to a workstream with tests: invert the /api/v1/ exemption to deny-by-default with an explicit allowlist of the paths that are genuinely public, and prove each allowlisted path authenticates. Same shape needed for /webhook/*: verification there is if secret: = FAIL-OPEN and GITHUB_WEBHOOK_SECRET, EVENT_WEBHOOK_SECRET, HA_PTT_KEY and SECURITY_API_KEY are all UNSET.

WHY THE HOTFIX AND NOT THE FULL INVERSION NOW: inverting the guard could break the dashboard proxy, the VS Code extension (/v1/* with a gateway key), the phone badge, MCP and every cron, and I could not enumerate those callers safely inside this session. A wrong inversion takes the company down; a named-prefix denylist cannot.

COST OF WAITING, stated honestly: the other ~130 guard-exempt /api/v1/ routes remain reachable uncredentialed. Named as a top round-3 item.
