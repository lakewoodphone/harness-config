**D228 · 2026-09-16 · Mesh inventory measured: Tailscale is the only transport from this laptop (it is on CGNAT, LAN fails), the authority is on a DERP relay, and my own reaper would race a fleet scheduler.**

Measured from the ZABZ-YOGA laptop, 2026-09-16 (docs/mesh/10-inventory.md). Three findings that change the design.

1. THIS LAPTOP IS ON NEITHER LAN. It is on 192.168.12.104 (T-Mobile CGNAT; public 172.59.215.73), not
its documented 192.168.50.244. LAN reachability FAILS for all four office nodes (Connection timed out,
6.0-6.1 s each); Tailscale WORKS for all four; the two `-cf` Cloudflare paths work. MagicDNS resolves
all five mesh names to 100.x and the Tailscale backend is `Running` (the NoState incident is over).
CONSEQUENCE: any design that assumes LAN between home and office is wrong for this machine on this
network, and every placement/transport decision must be made on the tailnet path.

2. THE AUTHORITY IS THE WORST-CONNECTED NODE. `secratary` is the only node reached **via the NYC DERP
relay rather than directly** ("direct connection not established"), 35-170 ms of jitter against 39-62 ms
direct for the others. The node holding the authoritative database is the one with a relay in the path.
CONSEQUENCE: worth fixing on its own merits (relay paths are slower and can fall back further), and it
argues for keeping high-frequency traffic OFF the authority rather than making it the hub.

3. A FLEET SCHEDULER PLACED ON THIS LAPTOP WOULD RACE MY OWN REAPER. This machine runs 7 `\DSH *`
scheduled tasks, including an Engine Watchdog (1 min) and the Process Reaper I added today, whose job
is to kill processes it judges to be orphaned harness leftovers. Any scheduler that starts workers here
must (a) be excluded from the reaper's rules, (b) run under a recognisable command line, and (c) hold a
governor lease so the reaper and the scheduler agree on what is in flight. This is a self-inflicted
constraint and it is the kind of thing that would have shown up as random worker deaths.
ALSO FOUND, and it is a security matter rather than a performance one: a **PostgreSQL server is
listening on 0.0.0.0:5432 on this laptop**, which is currently on a carrier-grade NAT network. Inbound
is unlikely from there, but on any shared network (a cafe, a hotel, the office LAN) that port is offered
to every device on the link. It should bind loopback or be firewalled, and that is the owner's call.

RETRACTION, MINE: I inferred a retry loop from a session log whose size had not changed across five
rounds. The agent was writing its document. A frozen log size with a fresh timestamp is NOT sufficient
evidence of a hang - the correct statement was "I cannot tell from the outside". The hung-ssh case
earlier WAS real (23 minutes at 0.1 s CPU); this was not, and I stated it as fact.
