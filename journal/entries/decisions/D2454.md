**D2454 · 2026-09-23 · Shlock: nothing rides the rails this season; the tow goes through-bolted with pinned joints, a mid-edge point and one continuous line.**

**Decision, 2026-09-23 (Shlock sukkah rain cover), after a nine-angle adversarial mesh audit.**

**1. Nothing rides the rails this season.** The 20 bought hitching rings, the UHMW sleeves-as-hold-downs and the
proposed rope loops are all out of the travel path. Five independent findings: a rope loop is a capstan whose drag
equals the load it holds (~74 lb per point at design load); a rope wicks water so a knot on a rubber washer cannot
seal a grommet; 14 of the 20 attachment grommets are 22-24 in outboard of any rail; a UHMW sleeve cannot pass a
rope loop on its own rail (the loop sits inside the sleeve's wall); and the knot is as thick as the 1 in collar the
owner rejected for holding the bundle open. The cover is held on **16 points** (8 leading-edge hangers + 8 ties on
the fixed house edge) and the deploy rule drops to **25 mph** - the trade `2026-09-18-buy-now.md` §G already priced
at 129 lb/point and ~43 mph grommet tear-out. Ties on the **house** edge are exempt from the capstan finding,
because nothing on the fixed edge travels, and the 8 cam-buckle straps that edge needed were never ordered.

**2. The tow goes to the pole, through-bolted, with pinned joints and a mid-edge point.** Six fixes, four
mandatory: (i) pin the swaged joints, 2 x 1/4 in pins per joint - the project's own July document says swaged
joints "act as hinges under lateral load", and unpinned the pole is three links carrying no moment; (ii)
through-bolt each pipe end, 1/4 in stainless 1.0-1.5 in from the tip with the rope in a shackle (957 lb, 19x a
50 lb load) and delete the rope-eye-plus-two-hose-clamps detail, which was only ever resisting 0.9-3.4 lb; (iii) add
a pull point at x = 171, because the pole's horizontal bending capacity is only **19.6-50.7 lb per end** against
21-150 lb of tow load, and the rope package said from the start that the pole "must never be relied on as [a
spreader]"; (iv) **one continuous line to both ends**, because a rigid bar transmits force, not equality, and two
ropes rack the cover. Then: pulley at the fabric plane and at the pole end's own x (a low pulley puts a 45 lb
downward load on the tip = 78% of allowable), and a stop on the fabric side of the pocket mouth (the 4 in pocket
gives 1.0-2.6 in of dead-band before the pipe bears on cloth).

**3. Retraction is accepted as it is, with two rules.** The stroke is 108 in (9 ft), not 144; the design's own
36-in gathered target is what is built to; and a gather dams the only drainage the system has, so water lands
inside the sukkah. Therefore: retract only when dry, and never leave it gathered through rain. The off-season
alternatives are named, not bought: a 342 x 120 tarp so the stow strip sits over the eave, or a roll-up on a
one-piece tube.

**Open, and each is his or a measurement:** the four gates (rail count/spacing, grommet bore #4 vs #5, in-service
point count, pocket ends open or stitched); queue #79 (the permit); and whether the cover is moved by hand this
season or the corrected tow is built tomorrow.
