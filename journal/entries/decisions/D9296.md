**D9296 · 2026-10-21 · The child knows, sees it on his own phone, and can read who is looking.**

aliases: Q25

Owner, verbatim (2026-10-21):

> "1"

Which is: told at setup, shown on his own phone, every time.

What it decides, condensed:

1. The phone's holder is told at setup, in plain words, what the phone does and who can see what — a screen
 he has to read before the phone is handed over, not a policy link.
2. The app shows him the state, not a memory of it: whether a parent may keep frames, who the chaver is and
 what he is told, which level the phone is on and whether it has drifted from the bundle.
3. When something is kept, he is shown that it was kept. A retention setting that silently fills up is the
 covert product this decision rejects.
4. The child's screen and the parent's screen are generated from one source, so they cannot disagree — the
 failure mode is a child being told one thing and a parent another.
5. A covert mode is not offered at any price; if a customer asks for one the answer is written down, not
 improvised at the counter.
6. Enforcement is unchanged: knowing the filter exists has never made one removable, and the seal, the
 delays and the anchor are untouched by this.
