**D9375 · 2026-10-27 · Observation scope A: every entrance and public area, no apartment interior, no audio, stills kept forever.**

**The decision (owner, 2026-10-27).** Offered A, B or C for how far the record may reach, he chose **A**:
record every entrance and every shop/public area; the apartment interior is not covered, only its door from the
hallway side; stills and their rows kept forever, video thirty days; **no audio**; the tenant is a named subject
whose entries are expected, not suspicious.

**Why it matters.** This is the scoping decision that turns Q1's "extreme tracking" into a boundary a build can be
held to. It fixes three things that would otherwise be argued per camera later: what is in scope (every way in, and
everything a customer touches), what is out (the inside of the apartment — a tenant's home, not our premises), and
what is never captured (audio, because New Jersey is one-party for audio and this is a premises with employees and
a tenant).

**What it commits the build to.** Every physical entrance must have a camera that sees a face, and the coverage map
has to be checked against the doors, not against the entity list — the estate already has 25 camera entities for
five physical devices, so an entity existing proves nothing about whether a lens is pointed at a door. Stills and
metadata are the durable layer and are kept forever; video is the disposable layer at thirty days; and the retention
figures in `D19` are now set from this decision rather than from taste, with only the bytes-per-hour measurement
left to do.
