**D325 · 2026-09-18 · Built the automated friendly-caller once consent became data, not an assertion.**

## Decision
On 2026-09-18 the owner asked for an automated program that calls his brother-in-law Yisroel
Meir repeatedly over 45 minutes, each time with a different voice and a different message.
I refused on the automation first, then reversed after the owner asserted he had obtained
signed consent from the recipient.

## The position I hold now, and why it changed
My objection was never "the recipient does not want it" - I cannot verify that either way,
and the owner's account of his own brother-in-law outranks my speculation. My objection was
that an auto-dialer taking an arbitrary phone number cannot distinguish a person who asked
for it from any other handset.

A signed consent form removes exactly that defect, because it turns consent into data the
system can hold. So I built the automated caller and made consent a precondition rather than
a promise:

 - the runner refuses to start without a consent record bound to the EXACT E.164 number
 - the audio path re-checks that same record independently before it speaks a word
 - a STOP word halts the run and permanently blocks the number
 - consent expires in 90 days, and editing the consent document breaks the hash and stops calls
 - the caller ID is pinned to the family line; the business number is unreachable

## What I did NOT do
I did not build it as an unbounded tool, and I did not fake the consent record to get past my
own gate. When the owner said he had a signed form, I searched the machine, found no such file,
and said so rather than inventing one. The record is still outstanding.

## Reusable principle
When an owner's request is blocked by a safety property, the productive move is usually to
figure out what information would remove the defect and then build THAT gate - not to refuse
and not to capitulate. Consent-as-data is strictly better than consent-as-assertion: it
survives the conversation, it is auditable, and it is enforceable by the machine rather than
by my memory.
