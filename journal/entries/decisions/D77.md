**D77 · 2026-09-14 · The scrub belongs on the phone, and the pixel test found the bug the geometry test could not.**

C-9 is built: `FrameScrubber` paints every detected face to a constant colour, crops to the person, downscales and re-encodes, and `ScreenshotService.enqueueUpload` runs it before the base64 payload exists. It replaces the four-step server-side preparation as the *privacy* control — that stays as the cost/latency lever — because the pixels are only ours before transmission.
*Two design points that are easy to get wrong:* **bare arms are not painted.** Painting "face and skin" as the research phrase puts it would delete the evidence for the sleeve attribute, which is measured against the elbow; only the face is identity-bearing and answer-free. And **the head is kept while the face is painted** — my first version cropped shoulders-to-knees, which is stronger privacy and a broken product, because *"is her hair covered"* is one of the five attributes.
*How the second one was found is the point of the entry:* the geometry test passed (the box arithmetic was right) and the **pixel test failed** — it decoded the payload and sampled the face region, which came back grey because the translated face box had a negative coordinate, i.e. the face was outside the crop entirely. A test that asserts "we called paint" would have passed. A test that reads the pixels back after the whole pipeline failed. The lesson generalises: **assert on the artefact that ships, not on the call that was supposed to produce it** — and in Robolectric that means `@GraphicsMode(NATIVE)`, because the default legacy mode does not rasterise and would have turned the assertion into a no-op.
*And the rule the notice now states is enforced rather than hoped for:* no person, no face landmarks, no decode — the frame is **refused**, not sent. A frame we cannot scrub does not go.
