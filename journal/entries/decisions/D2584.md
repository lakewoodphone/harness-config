**D2584 · 2026-09-24 · Correction: do the rope sling under the sleeve, NOT the socket re-clock - the stub only takes a bolt from its outer end, so the tarp cannot reach it turned down.**

**Correction to the fix decided earlier today (D2583), found while explaining it to the owner on 2026-09-24.**

The hanger's threaded stub takes the bolt from its **outer end only** - it is welded to the ring, it is not a
through-hole. So a stub turned to 6 o'clock can only hold the tarp's grommet BELOW the rail if the tarp is pulled
down *past* the rail to reach it. That is possible, but it drags the cloth under the rail at 8 discrete points,
pinches it on the rail's edge and on the sleeve, and puts the load into the rail rather than into the sleeve. The
owner caught this in one line: *"the stub has to point up at the tarp."* He is right.

**The variant that does not have that defect is the rope sling**, which D2583 already listed as the equivalent: a
loop of 3/8-in rope passed **under the sleeve**, up through the tarp's grommet, tied above so the grommet sits
about 2.8 in below the rail crown. A rope has no orientation and can be dressed around the cloth; it rides the
sleeve and not the rail, so no capstan, jamming or sleeve-collision finding applies; and it is free from the 200 ft
already on site.

**So: do the sling, not the re-clock.** Same geometry, same 4.1-in swing, no part forced through the cloth, and the
"pocket and pole hang lower" consequence is unchanged (guard the yard beam with a UHMW offcut, and about 3 in comes
off the retract envelope, which is set by hand anyway).
