## What the owner decided, and what I told him

The owner's question about the dismissed worker, Weinberg: *"do I still owe him money? If so, how much and what's the
breakdown?"* Then, on the final day: Weinberg *"came in really late"*, said he would leave after *"an hour or two or
maybe three hours"*, the owner told him *"you're not really working and you clearly don't want to be here so you
just don't have to come back"*, and asked whether he has to pay for the day at all, *"being that he didn't really
work even when he was there and he came really late."*

**Answer given: he has to pay for the hours he was on duty — use 3.**
- Compensable time is time **suffered or permitted to work**, not time productively worked. DOL Fact Sheet #22:
 *"Work not requested but suffered or permitted to be performed is work time that must be paid for by the employer
 ... The reason is immaterial."* A workweek includes time the employee is required to be on the premises or on duty.
- Lateness and poor effort are grounds to **dismiss** — which is exactly what happened on 2026-09-14 — not grounds to
 withhold pay for hours actually on the clock. There is no written agreement permitting a deduction for the day.
- 3 hours, not 2.5 or 2: top of the owner's own estimate, about $10 more than 2.5, and it removes the only thing the
 employee could argue about. Gross $99.51 at $33.17/hr, roughly $110.41 all-in.
- **The reason it is not worth disputing is the size of the other number:** the $2,208.32 August arrears are overdue
 and undisputed, and NJ's Wage Payment Law lets an employee recover unpaid wages plus liquidated damages and legal
 fees. The exposure on a contested claim is the $2,208.32, not the $100. Paying the day closes the wage claim.
