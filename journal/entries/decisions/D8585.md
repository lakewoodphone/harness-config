**D8585 · 2026-10-19 · Schedule policy and the single delta threshold are built; the DeepInfra lane points at a retired model.**

## 2026-10-19T22:55Z · ZABZ-YOGA · rounds 4–6: the scheduler, the delta threshold, and two findings that block the key

### Built and green (63 tests across two suites)

- `app/services/schedule_policy.py` — `decide(kind, at)` returns run-now or wait-for-cheap. Exemptions
 are a hard list (customer reply, payment, owner message, voicemail, P0/P1, reminders, alerts,
 healing) and win in **every** mode including `cheap-only`. `SCHEDULE_MODE=legacy` is the kill switch.
 `SCHEDULE_CLOCK` accepts only UTC: any other value is refused, because converting a published window
 to a local clock reintroduces DST into the price.
- `app/services/cost_routing.py` — one number, `ROUTING_CHEAP_DELTA_PCT` (default 0.35), reproduces the
 owner's entire instruction. Off-peak DeepInfra is 25.3 % cheaper and *loses*; at peak it is 62.7 %
 cheaper and *wins*. "Usually DeepSeek direct" is the output of the threshold, so it cannot drift out
 of step with the price table the way a second hand-written rule would.
- `smart_router._apply_hour_aware_costs` — reprices candidates from the book before the budget caps and
 the scoring, so every downstream cost comparison is on numbers for the same hour. Declines anything
 the book does not list; inert today, correct when a same-model lane exists.

### The starvation bound, measured not assumed

Peak is 01:00–04:00 and 06:00–10:00 Mon–Fri, so the longest wait from any peak instant to the next
cheap instant is **240 minutes** — enter at 06:00, out at 10:00. A test scans both peak blocks and
asserts the maximum. Starvation is not a risk to design around here; the window forbids it. Anything
that cannot wait four hours is exempt by kind.

### Two findings that change what the owner should do with a DeepInfra key

1. **`_CAPABILITY_DB` has no DeepSeek-direct entry.** `ai_gateway._estimate_cost` only falls back to
 `app/pricing.py` when the static entry's prices are all zero — which for DeepSeek they are, because
 there is no entry. So `pricing.py` **is** the production cost path for every DeepSeek call. The
 earlier fix is not a backfill fix; it is the live one.
2. **The router's DeepInfra lane points at a RETIRED model**: `deepinfra/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731`
 at 0.08/0.18, i.e. the V4-Flash-0731 generation, while DeepSeek direct now serves V4.1-Flash. A key
 minted today would silently move the company onto older weights, and the price book would decline to
 price it because it lists V4.1-Flash. **Repoint the lane to `deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash` before
 the key is used** — same generation, so it is a fair price comparison rather than a capability change.

### Not done, deliberately

- The yield-to-cheaper-provider *action* is not wired. It needs the same-model candidate lane from
 finding 2, and doing it before that would compare DeepSeek V4.1 against an older model and call the
 difference a saving.
- Nothing deployed. `PRICING_MODE` defaults to `book`, so the first restart picks up hour-aware pricing;
 that restart, and reading a written row back, is the next deliberate step.
