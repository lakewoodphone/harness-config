**D218 · 2026-09-16 · The customer record is lpt_prod; the Heroku store is retired and merged into it.**

EVIDENCE AND REASONING: 2026-09-16, ZABZ-YOGA. The owner was asked which of two live databases should be the customer record going forward and answered: "this is your job, analyze this well figure it out and fix it robustly." So it is a manager decision, recorded here with its evidence.

DECIDED: **The container Postgres `lpt_prod` on the Hetzner box is the record. The Heroku store is retired, and its genuinely-new rows are merged into `lpt_prod` — not the other way round.**

WHY, in order of weight:
1. **It is the store the running backend actually writes.** `lpt-backend` (container IP 172.18.0.11) connects to `lpt_prod`. A census of writers proved the Heroku store has exactly ONE non-local backend — the office — and that **109,702 of its 116,893 communications (93.8%) are our own push**. The Heroku dyno holds no connection to its own database. It is a write-only ghost, not a live store. (E1, `lpt-merge/E1/WHO-WRITES.md`)
2. **It is the only store with the identity schema.** `lpt_prod` carries `google_id`/`google_email`/`google_linked_at` (applied 2026-09-10 18:49:31Z); the retired store does not. The LPT login cannot work anywhere else.
3. **It is ahead on every business table.** Measured independently by me: customers 550 vs 542, orders 595 vs 588, tickets 624 vs 617, customer_notes 1023 vs 960, payment_transactions 351 vs 332.
4. **The lone dimension the retired store "leads" is communications, and only because we put them there.** Our push is dedup-keyed, so those rows merge cleanly rather than confusing the record.

CORRECTION TO MY OWN EARLIER READING: I described this as a split-brain — two live stores diverging in both directions — and drew a symmetric reconciliation from row counts. That framing was wrong in the direction that matters. The row-level fork is real (111,749 shared `communications.uuid`, 621 container-only, 5,144 retired-only), but asking **who writes each store** reversed the plan. The real defect is an **unfinished migration**: the website moved off Heroku on 2026-08-31, the backend's `DATABASE_URL` was repointed, and **the secretary's `PHONE_TECH_FULL_DATABASE_URL` never was**. Sixteen days of faithful pushing into an abandoned target.

THE MERGE IS SMALLER THAN IT LOOKED: both stores carry the same partial unique index `idx_communications_unique_external_ref` on `(customerId, metadata->>'external_ref')`, so the comms merge is an idempotent insert-or-skip, and E1 estimates only ~5,092 rows are net-new on the retired store.

THE HARDER HALF IS THE READERS, NOT THE WRITER: the writer repoint is one host and one `.env` key. But **31 scripts, units and jobs plus 11 documents** read the retired store, and three of them matter commercially: `scripts/daily-partner-sync.ps1` (partner PAYOUT orders exported from a database frozen at 2026-08-31, on a Windows node not yet reached), the report family that produced the cited commercial "August growth" figures, and `production-comms-freshness.py` which **reports `ok:true` while measuring the retired store**.

ORDERING RULE, BINDING: **do not switch the retired store off before its readers are repointed.** If it goes dark first, partner payouts and commercial reports silently keep computing from a frozen database, or start failing, and nobody will connect the two events.

NEXT: E2 is re-scoped from "which store wins" to a **CUTOVER-PLAN** — writer repoint, the exact dedup-insert, the reader repoint list ranked by damage, `pg_dump` snapshots of BOTH stores before anything is applied, and a freeze procedure. Applied serially by the manager after the snapshots, never before.
