**D2326 · 2026-09-22 · A deferral the owner states must be recorded in the queue, or the kernel counts it as a missed deadline.**

Decided 2026-09-22 while clearing two false HIGH findings off the kernel badge.

## D1 — A health check judges a scheduled job by the job's own heartbeat

`comms_freshness` tested email triage on `MAX(email_triage_log.created_at)` and
raised HIGH "the Gmail poller has stopped classifying new mail" at 14:51Z, while
the poller's own state file showed it had polled 19 minutes earlier, `ok`, with 0
consecutive failures and a 900s interval. Replaced with the poller's persisted
`last_gmail_poll_at` plus its `last_status` and `consecutive_failures`. The log age
survives as the metric `email_stale_hours` so the change is auditable. A missing
or unparseable state file is a problem, never a pass. See lesson L2348.

## D2 — A heartbeat is necessary but not sufficient; add the thing that is still not done

A poller can run on schedule and never complete its classification. The new
backlog test counts messages the poller itself has held longer than the window
that have **no** `email_triage_log` row. The absent row is the evidence — no
subject line is ever inspected or filtered to guess whether triage "really"
happened, because that would be the same mistake in the other direction:
inferring a fact about the system from a fact about the text.

## D3 — `owner_decision_queue` records a deferral, and a deferral is not a missed deadline

The check reported "2 blocking item(s) past 3d" on 2026-09-22. Both were items he
had already seen and put off in his own words — #100 "i'll look into this
tomorrow", #127 "deferred by the owner until after Yom Kippur" — and the table had
no field for that, so the row could only report "unanswered and old": true of the
row, false of him. No answer could have cleared the finding; it would have stayed
high for the whole length of his own deferral, which is how a real item gets
buried under a stuck one.

Added `deferred_until` / `deferred_at` / `defer_note` and
`owner-queue.py defer <id> --until <date|tomorrow|+3d> [--note] | --clear`.

The boundaries, chosen deliberately:

- A deferral **does not close a row.** It stays `pending`, stays visible in
 `list` (marked `D`), and the journal mirror still counts it open. Nothing was
 answered, resolved or dismissed to make a number go green.
- The exclusion from the overdue count covers `blocking_over` and `over_age`
 only. `critical` and `blocking_pending` are untouched — a critical item stays
 critical whether or not it is deferred.
- A deferral that has **expired** is its own reason to raise, ranked ahead of the
 others, because that is precisely when it is genuinely his again.
- A deferred row sorts behind every live one, so his own "later" does not push
 live work off the front of the queue.

## D4 — Engineering decision, not an owner question

All four are development and queue-hygiene decisions, which are mine. None was
routed to him. The two things the queue legitimately holds were left untouched:
the customer-facing decisions in #128/#142 and the money/legal rows.

`owner-queue.py` was previously a bare deployed file at `~/bin/` in no repo. It is
now versioned at `harness-config/scripts/owner-queue.py`, with the deployment path
and the defer examples written into its module docstring, so the next machine gets
the deferral support instead of re-deriving it.
