**D63 · 2026-09-14 · Being the lawyer meant reading the contracts, and the contract killed the cheapest vendor.**

Acting as the owner's counsel-of-record (D54), four primary-source streams were read: the amended COPPA rule text and the FTC's full FAQ, the New Jersey and Connecticut statutes, the GDPR articles with the CJEU authority, and the vendors' own terms. The finding that changed a decision was a **contract**, not a statute: Google Cloud Service Terms §20(d) forbid using a Generative AI Service in any application *"likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18"*, the Gemini terms repeat it, and §20(f) lets Google suspend on a **suspected** violation. A filter on a child's phone fits no reading. So Gemini Flash-Lite — the cost winner at $0.11 per 1,000 images — is contractually unavailable, and the vendor order is Bedrock, then Azure, then OpenAI direct with ZDR (Fireworks is out: it warrants that inputs contain no under-13 personal information, which is false for us by construction, and caps liability at $100).
*Three more results that were not reachable by reasoning:* **content moderation is not in COPPA's internal-operations exception** and the FTC has never said it is (zero occurrences of "moderation" or "filtering" in 1.1 MB of FAQ and 0.6 MB of rule text — a negative finding proved by searching, not assumed); **Connecticut reaches this shop with no size threshold at all** (PA 25-113, effective 2026-07-01, with limbs for processing sensitive data and offering data for sale that have no numeric floor, while the CGA's own chapter page still prints the repealed thresholds); and **one of our own code paths was already sending a child's browsing history to Google as prompt text**, because `/classify/gemini` interpolated caller-supplied context into the request.
*The rule this entry exists to record:* a legal read belongs in the loop **before** the cost read, and the answer to "is this allowed" is often a sentence in a contract nobody thinks to open. It is now `decisions/37` (D37-01…D37-14) with the four code fixes that followed, each with tests.
