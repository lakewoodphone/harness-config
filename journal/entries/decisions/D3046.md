**D3046 · 2026-10-11 · 137: verdicts for the office box's five systemd timers - estate-monitor and company-outcome-monitor PORT-ADAPT, tmp-reaper/fa-index/trunk-check RETIRE, timers not cron, and disable --now never mask.**

DECIDED 2026-10-11, workstream 137, implementing the migration decision the 132 inventory had already
taken for the office box's systemd-timer family. Evidence and the full argument: `docs/mesh/
137-PORT-SYSTEMD-TIMERS.md`.

THE FIVE TIMERS, one verdict each.
 `estate-monitor` → PORT-ADAPT, unit name kept. Rewritten for the authority, because every
 office collector measures the office box's diseases (a 2013 SATA SSD at
 92 %, PSI thresholds tuned to its 2026-10-07 starvation, sshd penalty
 exemptions for the office LAN, `secretary-backup.service`) and none of
 them are this box's diseases. The three design rules, the `for_runs`
 hysteresis, the `max_age` staleness pass and the "only an outside
 reachability failure may escalate" rule are kept verbatim.
 `company-outcome-monitor` → PORT-ADAPT, paths translated (repo `/opt/company`, DB `/root/
 authority.db`, out `/root/company-reports`), PLUS the hardcoded
 `journalctl -u secretary-api` fixed to resolve at runtime.
 `tmp-reaper` → RETIRE the `--apply` deleter. Not because the office rationale was wrong
 but because it does not exclude `*.lock` and the ported cron jobs guard
 themselves with `flock /tmp/<name>.lock`; a reaper whose failure mode is
 admitting a second writer against the company database is not worth its
 benefit on the box holding the only copy. Visibility ported as
 `tmp_used_pct`.
 `fa-index` → RETIRE. No consumer installed (`command -v fa` → nothing), every code
 root absent, and its first action would be `apt-get install` as root on a
 15-minute timer. A desk tool on a box with no interactive agent.
 `trunk-check` → RETIRE. All four NODES unreachable from Ashburn. The **trunk** is
 reachable from there; the **peers** are not, so the check belongs on a
 machine that has both.

MECHANISM: systemd timers, not cron, for the two that were ported — they already exist as timer units,
the office box chose timers over cron deliberately for the monitor, and `Persistent=true` plus
`systemctl list-timers` LAST/NEXT plus journald timestamps is strictly better evidence than a `runs.tsv`
row for a job whose whole purpose is to report absence.

THE OPERATING RULE THIS WORKSTREAM ADOPTED, worth keeping: **a job whose dependencies are absent is left
OFF and named, never enabled** — *"a job enabled with a missing dependency is worse than one that is off,
because it manufactures failures"* (133-PORT-BRIEF Definition of Done #2). `trunk-check` and `fa-index`
would each have manufactured a failure every 15 minutes for ever, and both would have looked scheduled.

NO office unit was stopped, disabled, masked or restarted, and no file on the office box was written. The
cold-store retirement list is stated with exact commands in 137 §6 and deliberately **not run**: the
rollback anchor is the owner's, and stopping a unit there is a change to it. When it is ever retired, use
`disable --now` and never `mask` — all five fragment paths are `/etc/systemd/system/<name>.timer`, where
`mask` fails (`135` §6.5).
