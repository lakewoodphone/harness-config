**D233 · 2026-09-16 · Section 4.5: the stub-gate half stands, the fleet-death half is deferred, and no one kills another stream's node.**

DECIDED 2026-09-16 by the program owner (me), because the acceptance harness refused to resolve it
itself and was right not to: section 4.5 of docs/mesh/71-mesh-program.md says "kill the chosen
node's gate mid-run", and the harness's own brief says "never kill a process that is not a gate you
started". Those conflict, and the harness deliberately left that half UNEXECUTED rather than pick.

RULING: the stub-gate half (S5b) STANDS as the section 4.5 evidence. Killing another stream's
deployed gate would take a working node out of a mesh other people are measuring, to prove a
property that is broker-side and is already proven: with its chosen node's gate dead, the broker
avoids it, places elsewhere with HTTP 200, and reclaims the abandoned lease on its own TTL
(measured 3181 ms against a 3000 ms TTL). The end-to-end half -- a real fleet dying mid-flight and
recovering with no manual repair -- is NOT waived; it is deferred until fleets actually dispatch
(section 4.3), because it needs a real run on a real node, and that spends money and occupies
other people's machines. Doing it against a stub proves nothing extra about recovery.

The harness's own correction is the more valuable artefact here, and it is recorded in 78 section 4
defect 6: it killed processes it had started, called them a leak, and only then noticed that
several belonged to its OWN run that was still in flight. Its fix is structural rather than a
better story -- every hermetic mesh is now registered and stopped in a finally, with a backstop that
kills every process whose ParentProcessId is the script, so ownership is by parentage instead of a
hand-maintained list, and a clean-slate run leaves zero processes behind. It also wrote $pid, a
read-only PowerShell automatic variable, so its first cleanup silently killed nothing. Same family
as the $body bug it had just written a lesson about. Both are in the same file, one session apart,
and neither was visible from the outside.
