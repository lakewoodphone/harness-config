## THE FACT THAT ANSWERS "IS THERE A BETTER PRODUCT" — the phone is narrowband

AOSP at tag `android-7.1.1_r1`, `system/bt/include/bt_target.h`, ships **`#define BTM_WBS_INCLUDED FALSE`**. The mSBC wideband machinery exists in `bta/ag/bta_ag_sco.c` but every line is behind that flag, and the same file states *"If SCO open was initiated from HS, it must be CVSD"* — **the headset cannot upgrade the link; the phone is the audio gateway and it is compiled narrowband.** So call audio on an E4610 is 8 kHz CVSD: his voice leaves through roughly a 4 kHz pipe no matter what he wears. (Kyocera's own `bdroid_buildcfg.h` could override the flag — high confidence, not certainty. One real call would settle it.)

**What therefore survives the cap and is worth money:** mic-side noise suppression and beamforming, which run on the earbud *before* the CVSD encode; ANC on his own end; fit, comfort, battery, IP.
**What is wasted at any price:** LDAC, aptX, AAC, LHDC, Hi-Res, spatial audio. Music is SBC; calls are CVSD. A 328-dollar Sony exits the same pipe as a 35-dollar bud.

Rejected with reasons: Sony WF-1000XM6 (328 — over the cap, and its superior mic cannot be heard through the same 8 kHz pipe); Soundcore Liberty 5 Pro (169.99 — over the cap; its case touchscreen and 20 on-device voice commands are the one genuinely clever answer to a no-app phone, so it is the honest upgrade if the cap ever moves); Nothing Ear (3a) and Ear (3) ($99/$129.99, no independent mic verdict); Technics EAH-AZ80 (179 — rtings demoted it behind the Anker on 2026-08-12); Samsung Galaxy Buds FE (rtings: voice sounds *"muddier and less clear in real life"*).
