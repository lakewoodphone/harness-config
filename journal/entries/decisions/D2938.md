## DECISION — leave the remaining two audit fsyncs in place; the next latency budget is not worth a durability trade

**Context.** The decision path now costs **11.65–14.05 ms p50** (my own measurement on this machine:
p50 10.884, p95 13.319) after taking one durable commit out of three. The product's own documents set
two levels:

- `decisions/24-testing-quality.md:68` — "decision latency <50 ms", revised at `:69` to mean the
 **short-circuit/rule path**;
- `decisions/28-performance-scaling.md:20` — **cached/short-circuit <10 ms**, local cascade ≤150 ms to
 blur, and a server arbiter of 1–4 s that is explicitly *not* in the interactive path.

Measured honestly: **inside the <50 ms rule-path budget, and outside the <10 ms cached class both
before and after the change this round.** The broker was never near the ceiling that matters, because
the decision itself is 0.6–0.7 % of its own latency.

**What is left, and what it costs.** Two `COMMIT`s per decision remain — the `access_requests` row and
the `decisions` + entity-graph pair — each an fsync at ~1.8–2.1 ms under `journal_mode=WAL` with
`synchronous=FULL`. That is ~3.5 ms of the ~11 ms.

The two ways to remove it, both named by the performance lane and both correctly left to me:

1. `PRAGMA synchronous=NORMAL` — removes the remaining fsyncs. In WAL mode this survives an
 application crash but **not a power loss**: the last committed transactions can be lost.
2. Batch/async the audit writes — flushes every N records or N milliseconds. Keeps per-batch
 durability, changes the audit contract from "durable when the decision is answered" to "durable
 shortly after", so a hard crash can drop the most recent decisions.

Both trade audit durability — the evidence trail a parent is shown — for latency.

**Decision: do not take either, now.** Reasons, in order:

- The measured number is comfortably inside the budget the product actually committed to for this
 path (<50 ms), and the remaining milliseconds are below what a person can perceive in page load,
 let alone attribute to the filter.
- The expensive direction is the irreversible one. Audit rows are the product's evidence; a lost
 decision row is unfalsifiable after the fact, while a latency number is measurable again any time.
- It is cheap to revisit and expensive to un-lose. If a real-device measurement (see below) shows a
 felt problem, take the tradeoff *then*, with a number in front of it.

**What would change this decision:** a measurement on a real phone, through the tunnel, showing the
end-to-end time is dominated by the remaining write barrier. Everything measured so far is loopback
on one socket, and the lane named what it did not measure — concurrency (the tails under several
phones, which `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` governs), the real phone-to-broker network, and the `policy="ai"`
cache arm.

**Not a question for the owner.** Nothing irreversible is happening by leaving it; the arithmetic is
above and the recommendation is to wait for a device number. Recorded so the next session does not
relitigate it or "optimise" it away by reflex.
