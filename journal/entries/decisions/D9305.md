**D9305 · 2026-10-21 · We do not promise the filter cannot be bypassed - we promise it is built to be, and that we keep chasing.**

aliases: Q33b — an owner correction, not a plain answer

Owner, verbatim (2026-10-21):

> "Even that we aren't promising. We are constantly building and adapting to stop threats at the cutting edge,
> but how can we promise our filter is unhackable?"

He is right. It corrects two decisions made earlier the same night: D-20261021-04 said bypass was "impossible",
and D-20261021-32 called the mechanism "guaranteed".

What it decides, condensed:

1. The claim is effort, not impossibility: the strongest seal we can build, adapted as threats change — not a
 claim that a determined attacker cannot get through.
2. What stays absolute is smaller: no button, setting, shop override or support route removes the filter — the
 door, not the wall — and an unsealable phone is still not sold.
3. The brand keeps "owned and protected by the user himself" and loses "impossible to bypass": built so he
 cannot bypass it in the moment, and we keep closing what we find.
4. Classification and resistance to attack are both best effort; the guarantees we make are about what we do —
 no override, no covert mode, no sale of data, the anchor, the release floor.
5. "Impossible" and "guaranteed" leave the copy and the internal documents that claimed them.
6. A threat response process must exist: known techniques tracked, tested, closed, each closure recorded, or
 "constantly adapting" is "configured but never run".
7. The page and the counter say the same three lines: no promise it never misses, no promise it cannot be broken
 by someone determined, no easy route or quiet removal, and we fix what you report.
