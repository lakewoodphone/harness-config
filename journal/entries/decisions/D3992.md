**D3992 · 2026-10-20 · Owner rule: auto-close repair orders 50+ days in Out for pickup; the 12 active-state closures stand.**

Owner decision, asked and answered in the session of 2026-10-20.

**The rule:** auto-close a repair order that has sat in `READY_FOR_PICKUP` ("Out for pickup") for **50 days or more** with no update. He made this rule himself; it is not an agent's invention.

**Verified applied correctly where it was applied.** Across the 252 rows the assistant closed on 2026-10-20, time in status ran **52 to 300 days**, and **zero** rows were closed under 50. The threshold is right.

**He was also shown that a second writer closed 12 jobs from states the rule does not cover** — `AWAITING_PARTS`, `AWAITING_APPROVAL`, `AWAITING_DIAGNOSIS`, `AWAITING_PAYMENT` (e.g. `WO2602010001` awaiting payment since 1 Feb, `WO2604210130` awaiting parts since 21 Apr, and a cluster of 27 Aug orders). Asked whether to reopen them, his answer: **"i am happy they were closed."**

**So: do not reopen them.** A future session that finds those twelve orders in `CLOSED` from an active state must treat that as the owner's decision, not as corruption to repair. Replaying a status is a production write and must not be done on a hunch.

**Two facts that belong with the decision:**

1. The run **stopped early** — 92 orders that entered `READY_FOR_PICKUP` 50+ days ago are still open. The last history write was `16:10:03Z`, and from the first `CLOSED` write onward every order read in that process raised `Value 'CLOSED' not found in enum 'OrderStatus'`. The job was most likely killed by its own write hitting a client that only knew seven statuses.
2. The second writer, `personal-secretary-mvp/app/services/website_db.py::update_order()`, validates field allow-lists and a confidence score but **not the status value**, then runs `UPDATE orders SET "status" = %s`. That is how a status the codebase never declared reached live data.

**Consequence for the counter outage:** this mass close is what introduced `CLOSED` into live rows and broke the counter's ticket queue and four count tiles. The fix is to teach the code the eighth value, not to unclose anything.
