**D5930 · 2026-10-13 · Earbuds for Yitz's E4610: Soundcore P31i, with OnePlus Buds 4 named as the step-up (music requirement added).**

The requirement changed mid-task: Yitz also wants to listen to music on the same device, so the mono boom-mic headset was withdrawn in favour of **true-wireless earbuds**. Owner's words: *"he'll probably also want to be able to listen to music on it. It should probably be more like regular earbuds."*

## What the device forces on any earbud choice

- **SBC only.** Android 7.1.1 negotiates no AAC, no aptX, no LDAC. The codec, not the driver, is the ceiling on this phone.
- **No companion app can ever exist** — there is no Google Play Store on an E4610. Whatever the earbuds sound like out of the box is what they will always sound like. Any model whose default tuning needs its app's EQ is disqualified in practice.
- AVRCP on Android 7.1 is limited, so volume control from the buds may not sync with the phone; the phone's own volume keys still work.

## The decision

**Chosen: Anker Soundcore P31i — 34.99, sold by AnkerDirect, ships from Amazon, in stock** (`https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FNWCYLZN`, verified 2026-10-13). It is the only sub-50 bud where rtings calls the **microphone** *good* in so many words — *"renders your voice in a clearly understandable way… does a great job of separating your voice from its background, regardless of whether this is a busy schoolyard or a metro station with trains pulling in"* — and it also rates the **audio reproduction** *good*, with the mildest default tuning of the contenders (bass +4 dB). Bass-heavy defaults are the trap here: CMF Buds Pro 2 is +8 dB and rtings says it *"can muddy vocals"*, Liberty 4 NC is +7 dB with a *"mediocre microphone… dark and muddy"*, and both need app EQ that cannot be installed.

**Named step-up, not a menu: OnePlus Buds 4, about 109-129.99.** The only model where two independent labs both praise the call mic (rtings *"strong… especially good at separating your voice from any background noise"*; SoundGuys *"do a great job of blocking out background noise and focusing on the voice"*) and SoundGuys scores the out-of-box sound 4.8/5. Rejected because it is over budget and has **no first-party US listing today** — Best Buy shows it *Unavailable* at 129.99, OnePlus direct is list price, and the Amazon listings at 109/114 are third-party resellers with thin stock.

**Rejected with reasons:** Nothing Ear (a) — rtings *"sub-par for microphone use… thin and not very natural-sounding"*, which retired an earlier draft recommendation of it. CMF Buds Pro 2 — rtings' guide names it best-budget for calls but its own review says *"passable, but not good… not a substitute for a headset microphone"*. Galaxy Buds FE — rtings withdrew it over mic complaints. Jabra Elite 5 — no longer sold new (GN has left the consumer Elite line).

## The honest ceiling, recorded so it is not rediscovered

rtings opens its own calls guide by saying true-wireless buds *"usually don't perform as well for calls as dedicated Bluetooth headsets"*, and ranks the ~19 dollar wired Apple EarPods **above** the CMF Buds Pro 2, because an in-line mic sits near the mouth. Nothing on this shortlist beats a boom mic for the learning call. The proven fallback if the far end still cannot hear him is a wired boom-mic headset on the 3.5 mm jack — the E4610's audio HAL declares `AUDIO_DEVICE_IN_WIRED_HEADSET`.
