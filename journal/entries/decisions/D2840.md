**D2840 · 2026-09-30 · BC28 separation reason: voluntary quit, confirmed by the owner - and the seat may not take a decision whose cost is a false statement by him to a state agency.**

**The owner confirmed the NJ DOL BC28 separation reason: voluntary quit — Weinberg resigned. Asked 2026-09-30, answered the same session.**

He was shown three contradictory accounts of 2026-09-14 and chose the resignation:

1. **2026-09-14, his words that day** (journal D106, verbatim): *"today I fired Weinberg because I
 could not afford to pay him his salary any more."* A lack-of-work discharge — New Jersey charges the
 employer's account for it.
2. **2026-09-25, his words**: Weinberg came in very late, was there about three hours, and was told he
 did not have to come back. Also a discharge.
3. **2026-09-28, his words** (D2660, D2773): he resigned — asked for a raise the business could not
 offer and left for other employment. A voluntary quit — not charged.

**Decision: file it as a voluntary quit.** Recorded on queue row **#205**, answered and resolved.

**Why it was asked at all rather than taken from the record.** The 09-28 answer was already written into
the file, and re-asking a question the record answers is exactly the failure this system is supposed to
avoid. It was asked anyway because the three accounts differ on a **state form under the owner's
signature**, and the two answers move money in opposite directions: New Jersey charges the employer
account for a discharge or a lack of work, and does not for a voluntary quit without good cause
attributable to the employer. A voluntary quit is therefore both the true and the cheap answer **if it
is true** — and only the owner could say which. The rule this preserves: a question already answered by
the record is not worth the owner's attention; a question the record *contradicts itself on*, where the
answer is a legal posture carried on his signature, is.

**The line drawn, for next time:** the seat may take any decision whose cost is a rebuild. It may not
take a decision whose cost is a false statement by the owner to a government agency.

**Consequence recorded in the same pass.** Box 4 of the BC28 prints **Date of Claim 11/09/2025**, six
months before Weinberg was hired. Researched: N.J.A.C. 12:17-5.1 sets the base year as the first four of
the most recent five completed calendar quarters preceding the date of the claim, so a 11/09/2025 claim
has a base year of roughly 2024-10 to 2025-09 and **cannot contain LPT wages** (employment here was
2026-05-11 to 2026-09-14). The reading that fits both printed dates is a reopening of a benefit year
that began 11/09/2025. Whether LPT's account can be charged for benefits on that claim is a question
under N.J.S.A. 43:21-7 that has **not** been researched, and it is the highest-value question to put to
the claims examiner. Do not assert an answer to it.
