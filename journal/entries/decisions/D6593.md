# D: Autonomy is the default — a gate must name the vital asset it protects, or it goes

**Owner, 2026-10-18, deciding owner-queue #547 and stating the principle that governs every other gate:**

> *"we want autonomous action. We don't want to gate the agents and stop them at every point, but we do need to protect vital assets. But we kind of usually bend in the direction of autonomy over security and protection. We want autonomy more."*

**The rule this settles, and how to apply it to any future gate:**

1. **Autonomy is the default.** An agent may act — restart, deploy, change, fix — unless a rule stops it.
2. **A gate must name the asset it protects and say what failure it prevents.** "This might be dangerous" is not a gate; it is a preference for inaction, and it costs the owner attention every time it fires.
3. **Vital assets keep their protection, and they are few.** Tonight's list: the **frozen** office box (the rollback anchor), **read-only** hosts, and the single-writer rule on the company database. Everything else bends toward autonomy.
4. **When a gate has been stopping ordinary work, count the cost.** #547's gate was blocking **nine ledger items** — not by risk, not by uncertainty, purely by a rule that said a headless shift may never touch service state.
5. **Log instead of gate.** Where a real risk exists, prefer *recording the action* over *preventing it*: a named host, a named service, a named window, and a logged restart beats asking a human at 2am.

**The contract that replaces the blanket refusal (implemented the same day):** a headless shift MAY restart a service when the ledger item names the host, names the service, names the window, the host is **writable** in `settings/machines/host-posture.tsv`, and the restart is recorded on the item's attempt trail. It may not restart anything unnamed, anything on a frozen or read-only host, or anything outside the named window.

**Why this matters beyond restarts:** the same test applies to every other "should I ask him" I have been doing. The measured pattern across this session is that most questions reaching him were **not decisions at all** — a part tier that was already policy, a customer case that belonged in the business queue, a backup flag that needed one word. Each was a gate that should not have existed. This entry is the standing instruction to look for that shape first.
