**D2453 · 2026-09-23 · Shlock drive tows the pole ends, not the fabric; the 20 hitching rings leave the sliding path.**

**Decision (2026-09-23, Shlock sukkah rain cover).** Two changes, taken after the owner reported from the site that the
20 hitching rings (HD `322338.0`, delivered that day) "just fit" the rails and **friction-lock as soon as they are
pulled**, and that the design never named where the four ropes leaving the gather ring connect.

**1. The drive tows the POLE, not the fabric.** The four-rope bridle from a floating ring on the leading edge is
deleted. Reasons: all five points lie on the leading edge line, so the legs are collinear with the edge and carry
nothing in the pull direction; a bridle cannot equalise or straighten a skew; and each leg would terminate in an
unrated #4 grommet (150-200 lb tear-out) in vinyl. Instead: **both poles at 30 ft (10+10+10 ft = the six sticks in
hand), ~9 in protruding past each 342 in pocket end, the tow attached to bare pipe outside the fabric, two points,
both directions on one circuit** so the two ends are pulled by the same line and the edge cannot rack by construction.
This is the architecture the 2026-09-20 adversarial audit endorsed: a continuous line at 1:1 with a lock.

**2. The 20 rings leave the sliding path this season.** Hold the cover on the 8 leading-edge hangers + 8 house straps
(16 points, 129 lb/point at 40 mph, #4 grommet tear-out about 43 mph) and **drop the deploy rule to 25 mph** — the
trade §G of `2026-09-18-shlock-buy-now.md` already priced. If they are wanted as hold-downs they go back on **6 in
slit bushings** (2 in Sch 40 PVC tonight, UHMW 8705K23 next-day), where `theta_max` is 1.6-1.9 deg against a 7-17 deg
friction angle — geometrically unable to cock. **The 8 UHMW sleeves get the same hand test and become 6 in if they
cock.** Off-season: trolley sheaves replace sliders entirely.

**Fallbacks recorded, if the pocket ends turn out stitched closed or the poles stay 28.5 ft:** tow the fabric at x =
87 and 256.5 (two points), or at the package's own x = 87 / 171 / 255 as three independent lines with three cleats.

**Two owner-visible facts this rests on, each a ten-second check:** whether the tarp's pocket ends are open (the
fabrication spec says stitched+bartacked, the pole package and build sheet say open — both cannot be true, and closed
ends mean no pole can be fed in at all), and the calipered rail OD against the rings' 2.00 in bore.
