**D · 2026-10-14 · Trim the delivery, never the archive — and "same size" is not "same file".**

Owner's question, 2026-10-14, on the Raitzik Inspiron One 2205 job (296 GB of real family data):
*"are all 296 needed, can we trim it down to fit on 256 usb?"* **Answer: yes — 229 GB fits a 256 GB
stick (238 GiB usable), and no family material is dropped to do it.**

**The rule this establishes, in two halves:**

1. **The trim applies to the DELIVERY, never to the master.** The bench-archive copy keeps all 296 GB;
 the stick carries a pruned set. That makes the pruning decision cheap and reversible, which is what
 lets it be made at all — the alternative (deciding what to keep before anything is safe) is how a
 family loses the thing it wanted.
2. **Cut only what is replaceable or provably duplicated — never by size.** The four cuts, 66.6 GB
 total: a television series (52.6 GB, The West Wing), two of three **MD5-identical** copies of an
 Insanity workout set (12.2 GB, `f81bb70f…` in all three), two of three **MD5-identical** 72 MB PDFs
 (144 MB), and an antivirus definition cache (1.6 GB).

**The counterexample that makes the rule, and it is the valuable half.** The obvious "duplicate" on
this drive was a pair of 1.32 GB Outlook PSTs — `Documents/Outlook Files/OldComputer.pst` and the
`OldComputer (2017_04_04 …).pst` inside a Dropbox backup folder — **identical in size to the byte,
1,319,552,000 each. They are NOT the same file** (MD5 `d3e3178c…` vs `3a5711bd…`). A size-match trim
would have deleted one of two different mail archives and called it housekeeping. **Hash before you
drop, every time**, and remember that the shop has lost customer data twice.

**What was deliberately KEPT in the face of a "trim by size" instinct:** a 9.5 GB
`AppData/Roaming/Apple Computer/MobileSync` folder (**iPhone/iPad backups** — unrecoverable if dropped),
and a 42 GB `Favorites/From Old Computer/My Pictures` folder that is **bigger** than the live
`Pictures` folder, i.e. the archive rather than a copy of it.

**Also settled:** a 256 GB stick is 238 GiB usable; the next 89 GB of cuttable material is all
commercial media (59 GB of music in six places plus an 18 GB cloud-replicated Dropbox backup that
nonetheless holds one unique PST), and **that call is the customer's taste, not mine** — named, not made.
