**D261 · 2026-09-17 · Transfer-drive standard adopted: bare 2.5in SATA (Samsung 870 EVO) on the Sabrent dock - portables disqualified by the soldered USB-C port; MX500 and WD Red withdrawn on dead stock, bad batches and grey-market provenance.**

DECIDED 2026-09-17 (owner): the shop's transfer-drive standard is a BARE 2.5" SATA SSD — specifically the Samsung 870 EVO — read on the existing Sabrent EC-DFLT dock and cased in a replaceable 2.5" SATA-to-USB enclosure. Portables are out. Owner's framing that forced this: "These are long term shop products that will be used both for 300 dollar data recovery and for in shop data recovery. Many times they have to be very reliable, good products" and "focus more on actual good products rather then official length of warranty, we won't remember to actually use the warranty in 2 years."

WHY BARE SATA AND NOT A PORTABLE (the finding that decided it)
Portable SSDs concentrate two failure points onto the drive itself: a soldered USB-C connector and a USB bridge chip. MDRepairs, a recovery lab with several hundred portable-SSD cases, publishes the distribution: "USB-C Port on the Drive Gets Loose. The single most common T7 failure... the mechanical stress of repeated plug-and-unplug cycles eventually breaks the solder joints" and "External SSDs fail electronically: controller lockups, firmware corruption, or a dead USB bridge, and they often vanish instantly with zero warning." A bare 2.5" drive has neither - it mates with the dock, and if an enclosure dies you replace a ~25 dollar enclosure, not the drive or the data. 300DDR's own transfer-drive rules accept a bare SATA drive by name.

WHAT WAS DISQUALIFIED, WITH THE EVIDENCE
- SanDisk Extreme / Extreme Pro / My Passport portables: documented defect; WD acknowledged the firmware fault and shipped an updater, but "users continued reporting failures after applying the update, including journalists from The Verge and Ars Technica whose patched and replacement drives failed again" (Rossmann Group). Failure mode is sudden and total: "working one moment, blank the next. No warning, no gradual degradation." Consolidated as In Re: SanDisk SSDs Litigation, No. 5:23-cv-04152-RFL (N.D. Cal.); WD's motion to dismiss partially denied June 2024, discovery active. 453 reviews on the Amazon listing mention data loss.
- Samsung T7 / T9 portables: the loose-USB-C-port failure above, plus post-2025 reports of write performance degrading with age.
- Crucial MX500 2TB: WITHDRAWN TWICE. Best Buy's product page says "This item is no longer available in new condition" (SKU 6321004), and it is discontinued Dec 2024 - but the bigger reason is quality: r/sysadmin with 900 deployed reports "very high failure rates" on recent batches, TechPowerUp notes "bad batches of their 2 TB ones," MDRepairs calls the reputation "real but uneven" with endurance consumed by year 3-4 of heavy use.
- Seagate IronWolf 125 2TB: excellent paper (1,400 TBW, NAS-rated, 3 yrs Rescue) but Seagate lists it under its Vintage Product Support Policy - end of support. Judged as a product, finished.
- Budget class (Vansuny, fanxiang, Ediloca, Fikwot, Bestoss, KingSpec, TEAMGROUP Vulcan Z): DRAM-less or unknown, short life, and Vansuny has no warranty support past 30 days (two reviewers, one a 25-year tech, both documented being ignored). Vulcan Z also tested at 60-90 MB/s on mixed read/write vs 200 MB/s for a 7-year-old Samsung 850 Pro, and a reviewer reported Sabrent dock incompatibility.
- WD Red SA500 2TB: NOT adopted, on provenance rather than quality. The drive is good (1,300 TBW T2R0A / 2,500 TBW T1R0A, NAS-rated) but EVERY in-stock listing found was a marketplace seller shipping from Hong Kong in 9-19 days, with the page saying only "contact the Seller directly for warranty information" - and Best Buy's listing is a third-party seller at $604 against Newegg's $410. Its whole advantage was real warranty and vendor support, which grey-market import gives up. Revisit if an authorized US reseller stocks it.
- Enterprise (Samsung PM893, Micron 5400 PRO): technically right class (1 DWPD / 5 yrs, power-loss protection) but 2026 spot prices are $1,066-$3,477. The circulating "$179-229" price guides are stale.
