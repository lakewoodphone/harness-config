**D5951 · 2026-10-14 · The company's daily model budget stays at 37.50; efficiency is the lever, not spend.**

**2026-10-14, ZABZ-YOGA.** Decision taken by the owner in conversation, on a question I put to him
with the arithmetic.

**The question, and why it was his.** Post-deploy, 6 of 8 work sessions were pausing with
`[BUDGET-PAUSED: agent_soft_cap $10.0156/$10.00]` — not a loop failure. Measured read-only:

| | |
|---|---|
| today's company spend | **33.66** (3169 calls) against a daily cap of **37.50** |
| the agent that exhausted its share | `dept_engineering`, **10.0156** over 544 calls |
| daily cap derivation | daily 37.50 → CEO reserve 7.50 → dept pool 30.00 → agent soft cap **10.00** (`llm_budget.py`, `DEPT_AGENT_COUNT = 6`, `AGENT_SOFT_CAP_MULTIPLIER = 2.0`) |
| October to date | **228.15** against a monthly cap of **750.00** |
| last three days | 23.32 · 33.66 — rising |

**His answer: leave it at 37.50.** My recommendation had been 45, which projects to ~745 of the 750
monthly cap. He declined the increase.

**The consequence for the next round, which is the point of recording this.** The company must get more
out of the budget it has. And here the measurement points somewhere specific:

* ~**17–19 USD/day** of company spend is the steady state, of which the round-2/3 audits attributed
 **roughly a fifth to a quarter to work that failed** — 578 failed sessions in 7 days.
* So the available saving is not in a smaller cap; it is in **failed work that still gets paid for**.
 `STEP_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` and `TOOL_LOOP_DEADLOCK` are not just quality defects; they are line items.
* **Cost per *proven* outcome is the number to move, and it is the one this estate has never
 published for the company.** The cron wake loop does: **0.0900 USD per closed-with-proof item**,
 against the company's ~0.53 USD per "completed" work session of which most were not proven.

**Therefore, in priority order, and none of it needs more money:**
1. Do not spend on a session that cannot close — the convergence budget already stops some of this.
2. Make a retry inherit the previous attempt (measured: **238 tasks retried, 78 eventually
 completed**), because a retry that re-pays for the same reconnaissance is the most obvious waste.
3. Publish cost per proven outcome weekly, and treat a rise as a defect.

**What I must not do:** interpret "no more budget" as "spend more elsewhere", or quietly raise the
monthly cap. The monthly number is the one that bites, and it is his.
