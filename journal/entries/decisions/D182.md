**D182 · 2026-09-15 · Guard settings.getDefaults: anonymous callers get the shop's 30% markup percentage, and no frontend caller depends on it.**

FINDING (verified against production, 2026-09-15): settings.getDefaults is a publicProcedure (no
authentication) and is LIVE in production. Anonymous probe from lpt-apps against
http://localhost:3001/api/trpc returned 200 with:
 {"defaultLaborTierId":34,"defaultMarkupPercent":30,"taxRate":6.625}
Control in the same moment: auth.sessions -> 401. So an unauthenticated caller on the public internet
learns the shop's default markup percentage and tax rate.

WHY IT MATTERS: defaultMarkupPercent is not retail price. It is the multiplier applied to part COST to
derive the customer price - used in repair-part-cost-price.tool.ts:139, repair-part-tree.service.ts:354
(via getDefaultMarkupPercent, :209-228) and ai-assist.service.ts:1368-1412. That is the shop's margin
policy, i.e. competitive information, and the business does not publish it anywhere deliberately.

WHY IT WAS INVISIBLE: settings.router.ts is one of the 20 files whose procedures are declared
`publicProcedure` - an alias of `procedure`. Until the guard test was fixed the same day, all 59 of those
declarations were counted as GUARDED. This exposure was never a decision; it was a blind spot.

DECISION (dev, mine): guard settings.getDefaults. Safe to do - `Get-ChildItem frontend/src | Select-String
getDefaults` returns ZERO callers, so no public page depends on it and guarding it breaks nothing. Its
sibling settings.getLaborTiers returns retail tiers only (amount/name/description, no cost) and is
plausibly intended-public; PART-063 owns that judgement. defaultLaborTierId and taxRate are benign on
their own.

NOT DONE YET, DELIBERATELY: the edit is held until fleet batch 2 drains, because PART-063 is auditing
settings.router.ts right now and an edit would shift its line numbers under it. That is lesson L1620 (never
mutate the tree while a read-only fleet is reading it) applied rather than restated.

ALSO NOTE: production will keep exposing this until the next deploy, because a source fix needs the
rebuild - and the rebuild is itself blocked on the smsService/Dialpad migration (D179). Severity is Low
(one integer; no customer data, no credential), so a dist patch is not warranted; the source fix is.

INSTRUMENT FAULT, MINE, same probe: the bash script was written with Set-Content, which produces CRLF on
Windows, so bash reported `$'\r': command not found` and the LAST probe in the list printed a stale body
from the previous one (status 000). One of my two controls therefore did not run. Write bash scripts with
LF (\n) endings, and never read a probe's body without checking its status code first.
