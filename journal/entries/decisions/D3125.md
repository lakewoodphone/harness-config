**D3125 · 2026-10-12 · CORRECTION to D3124: the binding wake ceiling is a RELEASE COUNT (40/day), not the inert USD cap.**

CORRECTION TO D3124. That entry says the wake fleet is bounded by "WAKE_MAX_USD_PER_DAY=5". It is not.
The USD ceiling is INERT under the current cost instrument, and I only found that out after acting on it.
This entry exists because a future session must not inherit the belief that a USD cap is protecting the
balance.

WHAT I DID: installed the fan-out cron line with WAKE_MAX_USD_PER_DAY=5, believing it bounded spend to
5 per day.

MEASURED, secratary-cloud, 2026-10-13T01:29Z, by me:
 * /root/.sms-inbox/wake-cost.jsonl DID NOT EXIST before I ran wake-cost.py by hand.
 * After running it: 6 releases priced, and every one reports `cost=unsourced`
 ("unsourced (6 release(s) unsourced)"). The estate cannot currently price its own AI usage.
 * `wake.py stats --json` -> spend_today_usd = 0.0.
 * wake.py:733-742 branches on `spent is None`. The intended guard is a REFUSAL above
 UNMEASURED_REFUSE_ABOVE = 40 releases ("NO MEASUREMENT IS NOT ZERO") -- a good design.
 * BUT spend_today() returns 0.0, not None, once the cost file exists with unpriced rows: wake.py:695
 comments "No priced release yet today is a real (zero) reading for the autonomous budget."
 * So the elif branch runs with spent = 0.0, compares 0.0 >= 5.0, is False, and the cap never trips.
 The unmeasured-refusal guard at 40 is NOT consulted, because spent is not None.
 * Effective bound with my line as first written: max_per_day = 500 only. At the measured 0.036-0.068
 per release that is 18-34 of spend against a 30.55 balance -- it would have drained it.

FIXED: the installed cron line now also sets WAKE_MAX_PER_DAY=40. Verified:
 env override in force -> 40 (DEFAULT_MAX_PER_DAY 500, UNMEASURED_REFUSE_ABOVE 40)
40 releases/day at 0.036-0.068 is 1.44-2.72/day, i.e. 11-21 days of runway on the current balance, and
2x-10x the historic normal of 4-18 releases/day. It does not depend on the broken pricing path.

THE GENERAL LESSON, which is the reason this is a separate entry rather than an edit: a limiter and the
measurement it reads are ONE mechanism, and shipping the first without verifying the second produces a
guard that looks present, prints no error, and bounds nothing. Before trusting a ceiling, print the
value the ceiling READS -- `spend_today_usd` here was 0.0, and 0.0 is exactly what a working ceiling
looks like on a quiet day. Verify the reading can be non-zero: pick the number the guard compares, and
ask what would have to happen for it to become large.

ROOT CAUSE STILL OPEN: cost_usd is null/unsourced on every release because the pricing source is absent.
Until that is fixed, count ceilings are the only real ones. Tracked as cheap-capability work.
