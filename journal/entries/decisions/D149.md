**D149 · 2026-09-14 · lpt-sync write-back goes to references.websiteOrder; case identity may be established by caseId.**

Decided 2026-09-15 while fixing three lpt-sync defects. Both are schema-level and
will outlive the session.

1. THE WRITE-BACK KEY. The idempotency write-back records the production order
 under references.websiteOrder. references.productionOrder is only written
 where it is ABSENT, and each workflow key only where it is absent/empty/equal.
 WHY: references.productionOrder is not exclusively ours -- on weichbrud it
 held the eBay supplier PO {system: ebay, orderNumber: 09-15137-18879} and the
 in-place write-back destroyed it. alternatives considered and rejected:
 (a) overwrite productionOrder as before -- destroys other systems' links;
 (b) refuse the write-back when productionOrder is occupied -- loses
 idempotency, which is what caused duplicates WO26082712640/12644.
 The guard reads websiteOrder so a re-run still refuses correctly, and the
 legacy productionOrder/workflow.orderId reads stay for the records already on
 the corpus in that shape (266 of 368 records carry a production-order ref).
 A readable consequence: a re-run reaches create_order only through
 _known_production_order_id, which now prefers references.websiteOrder.

2. CASE IDENTITY FOR THE SIBLING RULE. Two sync records are the same case when
 they share a non-empty caseId OR a non-empty normalised phone, AND >=3 shared
 device-summary tokens (_GUARD_OVERLAP_THRESHOLD, the bar the rest of the guard
 already uses). WHY the token requirement survives even for a caseId match: it
 keeps the rule from blocking on a coincidental identifier collision, and it is
 the same device-identity bar used everywhere else. Measured effect: 3 false
 CREATEs become SKIP/sibling, and all 13 pre-existing sibling skips were found
 to match on caseId too, so the rule is now strictly more capable, never less.
 REJECTED: matching on caseId alone (would let one malformed record block
 another) and requiring the phone (which is the defect -- records with
 'unknown'/''/null phones are exactly the ones the rule silently ignored).

3. `linked_order` refusals are short-circuited BEFORE Step 1. A refusal must
 create nothing, including a customer row. This was a real live bug: the
 linked_order branch sat after the create_customer call.
