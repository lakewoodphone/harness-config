## Decision 2026-10-21 · The reset anchor: the chaver's account, ours by default, thirty days nobody may shorten (Q6)

Owner's answer to Q6, verbatim:

> "A wipe cannot give a clean phone, this is why we need a chaver to do it. When he sets his chaver the
> app sets his chaver's email address as the sign in, otherwise it defaults to us. I just realised
> something: if it defaults to us we warn that it takes 30 days to reset, we build it in that nobody can
> override it. That's why we recommend having a chaver, to do it faster, and you can set how fast it will
> be. That's an incentive to use a chaver not us, and that way we can always be a backup email but if
> someone comes in to demand we reset we explain it has a 30-day built-in wait."

Recorded as `kosher-filter-ai/docs/decisions/D-20261021-05-reset-anchor-and-thirty-days.md`
(commit `2dd0bd2`, gate 17/17).

1. **A wipe never yields a clean phone** — the reset-protection anchor is armed on every sealed phone.
 This is the Enhanced FRP layer that the research found coded and inert; it is now required.
2. **Anchor = the chaver's account when one exists**, written into the policy by the app at setup.
3. **Anchor = the shop's account when none exists**, so the shop is the guaranteed backup and the
 customer is told that in the same breath as the wait.
4. **The shop path carries a built-in thirty days that no staff member may override** — enforced by a
 dated request record and a dated release record, refused in code, not by policy.
5. **The wait is the incentive**: name a chaver, control your own speed; name nobody, and the shop is the
 slow path. The product recommends the chaver for exactly that reason.

Notes for whoever builds it (mine to decide, not questions for the owner):

- One bench assumption must be proven before this is sold: whether a Google account that has never been
 signed in on that device can satisfy the reset-protection prompt, or must first exist on the phone.
- Two details remain open and belong to later questions: on a child's phone, whether the anchor is the
 parent or the nominated chaver; and whether the thirty days run from the request or from proof of
 ownership.
- This contradicts my earlier recommendation in Q4/Q6 that the shop should never hold an anchor
 credential. The owner overruled it deliberately, with a control (the thirty-day record) that my
 recommendation did not have. The decision text records the override at the top.
