**D153 · 2026-09-15 · Sync records get a labEscalation block; Weissman SSD price is 800 not 500.**

Two record decisions made 2026-09-15 while bringing the Weissman SSD case current. Both
are dev/record decisions, not owner decisions; recorded so they are not relitigated.

1. SYNC RECORDS GET A `labEscalation` BLOCK IN THE SCHEMA.
 CUSTOMER_CASE_SYNC_RECORD.schema.json has additionalProperties:false at the root and
 never defined labEscalation, yet the 300DDR referral SOP (docs/customer-operations/
 300ddr-full-customer-referral-workflow.md) tells agents to write exactly that block,
 and the Weissman SSD record already carried one. So the contract and the practice
 disagreed and the contract was silently losing. Fix: labEscalation added as a
 documented root property (open object, labs differ). Chosen over "move it under
 pricing" because a lab referral is not only money — it has submission state, shipping
 state and a customer approval, and CASE-SYSTEM-AUDIT-2026-09-14 already names ad-hoc
 keys as the drift it is trying to kill. Verified: the rewritten Weissman record
 validates clean (jsonschema.validate -> SCHEMA VALID).

2. WEISSMAN SSD `price` IS 800, NOT 500 AND NOT 100.
 Two figures were in play and the old file flagged them as a discrepancy. They are not
 in conflict. 300DDR charges LPT a flat $500 for any SSD <=6TB ($0 if nothing
 recovered) — that is the lab side. $800 recovered / $160 not is LPT's price to the
 customer, quoted by Eliyahu on the 2026-09-14 call and accepted. Front-matter `price`
 is by definition the operative customer figure, so 800; the $100 bench figure the file
 opened with is superseded as the case price. payment_state quoted, because nothing is
 collectable until the lab reports.

Rejected: leaving the file at `status: new` with the old NEXT line because the correct
next step was arguably the owner's to authorise. The approval was already on record by
voice; the file's job was to say so.
