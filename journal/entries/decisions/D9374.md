**D9374 · 2026-10-27 · Uncollected devices: yellow bin at 50 days, and the inventory written down for the first time.**

The owner set the shop's uncollected-device rule on 2026-10-27, in six words of chat: "it goes in a yellow bin, and we take no resonsibility for it after 50 days" (spelling his).

WHAT IS NOW DECIDED. A device in a collectable state (READY_FOR_PICKUP or UNFIXABLE) that has been collectable for 50 days goes into the yellow bin, and from that day the shop accepts no responsibility for it. 50 days, not 30 and not 90. Moving to the bin is not destroying: nothing is scraped, wiped or reset on the day it enters, and a customer's data is pulled and kept before anything leaves the shop - this business has lost customer data twice.

WHERE IT IS WRITTEN. lpt-hub/docs/customer-operations/UNCOLLECTED-DEVICE-POLICY.md, committed 366c3f2d2 on branch work/price-ledger-20261025 and pushed, with a priority row and a maintenance-log entry in FLOWS-INDEX.md per AGENTS.md 11. Two reads preceded it: a search of all of docs/customer-operations/ for 30/60/90-day storage, abandonment and storage-fee rules (the only hit is the 300DDR lab's own $10/week, which is the lab's term, not the shop's), and the customer-facing templates. NO TEMPLATE STATES A HOLD PERIOD. The ready-for-pickup notice says "is completed and ready for pickup at Lakewood Phone & Tech. Thank you for your business!" and stops. So the 50 days is, today, unenforceable as a disclaimer: a customer told nothing about it was never bound by it. Two drafts are prepared for the owner's word - one for intake, one for the ready notice - and nothing sends until he gives it.

THE CLOCK, and this is the part that decides who goes in the bin. It starts at the NEWEST evidence that the device was collectable, never at intake, from five sources in order: pickup_notified_at; the newest READY_FOR_PICKUP/UNFIXABLE transition; the newest outbound "ready for pickup" message; the newest real status transition; createdAt only when nothing else exists. Two errors were measured getting this wrong on the day:

1. Including order_status_history rows whose toStatus is NULL as activity. A bookkeeping backfill on 2026-10-21 wrote 51 such rows reading "Backfill: order total set from its recorded successful payments". Counting them reset the clock and produced 12 devices past the line; excluding them produced 20. The 12 was wrong - it hid 8 devices, and those 8 included mrs gross (57 days, paid in full) and mrs meyer (50 days, paid in full).
2. Using pickup_notified_at as the primary source. It is null on all 60 collectable rows, so a query built on it returns "nobody is past the line" - a refusal that reads as good news.

THE STATE ON THE DAY THE RULE WAS SET. 60 rows in a collectable state. 20 at or past 50 days. 9 of the 20 are already PAID IN FULL - those customers have paid for property held 50 to 57 days and are the ones to call before anything moves. 560 is still uncollected across the 20. Next to cross: sarah levi WO2609030001 at 49 days.

WHAT THE RULE DOES NOT TOUCH, and this is a bound on it, not a detail: AWAITING_DIAGNOSIS, AWAITING_PARTS and AWAITING_APPROVAL are not collectable - the shop is holding the device for its own work, so the customer is not late. A repair that stalled is an engineering failure owned by the shop and must never be converted into a customer's abandonment by letting the calendar run. 34 rows sit in those states on 2026-10-27, the oldest an AWAITING_PARTS from 2026-07-16 (mrs klugman, 103 days).

WHAT IS STILL UNKNOWN AND IS MINE TO CLOSE, NOT HIS. The bin has two names in the record: one unclaimed device was noted on 2026-10-13 as moved to "the not-picked-up bin" (rosenbaum Q6, WO2608170005), and intake photographs of 2026-10-07 describe "a yellow bin on the counter" used for drop-offs. Section 8 of the policy records his words and does not equate them. And the database still cannot record a collection at all - pickupReleasedAt is null on 59 of 60 - so the policy cannot be automated into a batch close and every row it acts on is a row a human physically looked for.
