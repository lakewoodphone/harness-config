**D9297 · 2026-10-21 · Billing and autonomy are separate - the release never depends on payment, and payment buys time rather than control.**

aliases: Q26

Owner, verbatim (2026-10-21), asked whether the release wait can be shorter than the paid term:

> "For sure one. The payment he bought and his actual decisions have nothing to do with each other."

What it decides, condensed:

1. The release wait is the customer's own setting, a day included. Nothing ties it to what he paid, how long
 he paid for, or whether he is up to date.
2. Payment buys time on the service, never control of the phone. A prepaid year is fourteen months of
 service; it is not a lock, a hostage or a lever.
3. A customer who leaves early keeps his phone and loses his unused time — no pro-rata refund outside the
 fourteen-day window, said before he pays, because a surprise here costs more than the refund.
4. The two contracts are documented separately: the service agreement is about money and support, the filter
 is about the phone. A customer reading either is not asked to understand the other.
5. No route, flag or script may condition a release on billing state; the release path reads the customer's
 wait and nothing else, and dunning touches only the degraded state D-20261021-06 already defines.
6. Churn is a product signal, not a collections problem: a phone released in week one is a complaint to read.
