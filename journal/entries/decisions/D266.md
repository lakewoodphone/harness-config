**From the owner's own words, 2026-09-17, answering directly.** He was asked which member the bracket at t=79-81 s clamps to, and instead described the whole structure - which is better, because it resolves several long-standing ambiguities at once.

**His description, verbatim:** *"there are six thicker steel poles going up from the ground vertically. And across each three by the backyard side and the house side respectively, they're the same size pole. Well, actually two poles because it's longer, like 28 feet. On those three poles. And then there's 8 going from the backyard to the house horizontally on a slant that are not as thick. They're the same company and the same steel, but they're not as thick going across."*

**What that establishes, and it is a lot:**
1. **The 6 posts are the thick members - 2.375 in OD - vertical from the ground.** Confirmed by the builder.
2. **The beams are the SAME thickness as the posts and each is made of TWO pieces**, because 28 ft exceeds a stick. Two beams (house row and backyard row), four pieces total. This explains the Fazzio order exactly: ten heavy 21 ft sticks = 6 posts + 4 beam sections. The "two field-cut sections per beam" in the build documents is his, not a guess.
3. **The 8 rails are the thinner members - 1.660 in OD - and they run BACKYARD-TO-HOUSE, across the deck's depth, on a slant.** The slant is the fall. This confirms the fabrication spec's "tarp slides 12 ft on 8% slope over 8 fixed rails at 42 in" and kills the long-standing 28 ft rail-span contradiction in the corpus: the 28 ft members are the beams, in two pieces each.
4. **Therefore the rail span = the clear distance between the two beams = the deck depth** - the dimension that decides whether the deployed cover's limit is ~71 mph or ~58 mph. It is still unmeasured, but it is now unambiguous WHAT to measure: beam centre to beam centre.
