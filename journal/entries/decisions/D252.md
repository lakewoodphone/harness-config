**D252 · 2026-09-17 · Video8/Hi8 players: recommend transfer over repair (supersedes H467).**

**H467's recommendation is wrong and this entry corrects it** (H467 said take Levi's two Sony 8mm players as a diagnose-first repair).

WHAT I DECIDED: recommend **transfer, not repair** for Video8/Hi8 players. $30-40/tape with a ~$60 minimum, buy one known-good working deck (~$150-400 used) as shop equipment, and refuse repair on head/drum symptoms, no-power/dead-drum, or anything with no donor. Three owner options are recorded in the case file; this is recommendation A.

WHY (sourced, 2026-09-17):
- The **signature failure** on 1988-1998 Sony 8mm/Hi8 is surface-mount electrolytic leakage, and **it presents as a tape-deck fault** - so the customer's own description does not identify the subsystem. A tech who serviced these professionally: the leaked electrolyte destroys the surrounding area "comparable to leaked batteries" and "NO TECH wanted to mess with these, as results simply could not be guaranteed". 4-10 h/unit plus trace repair. `https://www.eevblog.com/forum/repair/capacitors-on-sony-camcorder-ccd-tr350e-(year-1994)-help-please/`
- The **good** failure (belts, idlers, pinch rollers) is cheap and buyable new - $10 kits, and that vendor machines custom rollers to measurement - but you cannot tell it from a cap fault until the unit is open. `https://webspareparts.com/products/belt-kit-for-camcorder-sony-ccd-v8af-video-8`
- Doing the mechanical job **properly** needs an alignment/calibration tape, an RF-envelope scope and a torque gauge; one practising tech's framing is "being a trained watchmaker will certainly help". Precision mechanical assembly is a different trade from board work and this bench has none of it documented.
- **Heads/drums are terminal** - no new stock, donor-only, and a working donor deck runs ~$500. `https://www.electrondepot.com/repair/pinch-rollers-hi8-video-heads-select-by-size-not-model-teac-35649-.htm`
- **Ceiling check:** a US mail-in specialist covering exactly these models quotes "most repairs average $150, this includes parts, labor, and shipping". $150 against 4-10 h is bad work. `https://tapeplayers.com/products/camcorder-repair-service-includes-shipping-costs-free-replacements-1-year-warranty`
- Transfer, by contrast, prices off a **published market**: $19.95-$28.50 per 2-hour tape (Legacy Digital, page updated Sept 2026), $25/tape at Save Your Tapes, and capture is 1:1 real-time.

CONSEQUENCE: my first pass was over-confident about the bench's microsoldering strength. The capability is real but it is aimed at the *worst-value* part of this job, and I had treated "we can solder it" as "we should quote it". The two things I did NOT have when I wrote H467 were the market price ceiling and the fact that the cap failure masquerades as the mechanical one. Both are cheap to obtain and both were decisive.
