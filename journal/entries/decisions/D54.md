**D54 · 2026-09-14 · I am the lawyer on this one — the compliance analysis is mine, not an outside firm's.**

The owner's instruction, verbatim: *"you have to be the lawyer yourself, you have enough expertise, run full analysis and audits and all this"*. So the legal-and-compliance work for the kosher filter is mine to produce and own: a source-cited audit of the federal children's-privacy regime, the New Jersey statutes, the state biometric and comprehensive-privacy applicability tests, GDPR/UK where it could reach the product, the vendor contract set, liability, and the use restrictions — with the resulting controls implemented rather than merely recommended.
*Why this is the right call and not a shortcut:* the questions are narrow and document-based (statute text, rule text, agency guidance, vendor terms), and every one of them can be answered with the primary source quoted, which is exactly the discipline the rest of this journal already runs on. What a firm would add is state-specific judgment on ambiguous points and privilege; where that is genuinely needed I will say so **in the analysis itself and in my own name**, rather than routing a question back to him. The rule I am applying is his own: *"the ones that absolutely need me and you can't solve you bring up with me one at a time"* — this one he has now told me I can solve.
*What replaces a legal opinion, in practice:* a written data map (what is collected, where it goes, what is stored, for how long, and who can see it), a control set inside the product (consent captured at provisioning, no face embeddings ever, no vendor response bodies, escalation off by default, a documented do-not-use list), and a signed clause set for every vendor that touches an image — plus the honest list of points where the law is genuinely unsettled. Full analysis in `kosher-filter-ai/docs/compliance/`; decisions in `decisions/37`.
