**D · 2026-10-14 · A waived-labour family job still charges for hardware: the media is hers to pay for.**

Owner, verbatim, when asked whether we hand his aunt a stick on a no-charge job:
*"if she wants a stick obviously she'll pay for it"*.

Applied to **Raitzik, Dell Inspiron One 2205 (W03B, Service Tag 1QL7NQ1)** — family/neighbour, owner
explicitly not charging:

- **The work is free.** `price: null`, `payment_state: waived`, no work order, no production order.
 That part is unchanged and it is the owner's call, already made.
- **The media is not free.** A stick we supply is charged to her; a stick she brings costs her nothing
 from us.
- **The number comes from the shop's own record, not from me.** The documented adder is the Greenfeld
 case (2026-09-08): *"data-only 75 (85 with our USB stick)"* → **our stick adds 10**. Do not invent a
 different figure.
- **Capacity is set by measured data, not by guessing.** If the pulled drive's user data will not fit a
 64 GB stick, the capacity goes up and the number moves with that stick's real cost — and **no
 capacity-based figure is quoted to anyone before the drive is read and the bytes are counted.**

**Why this is a decision worth writing down rather than leaving in a conversation:** "she's not paying"
was spoken about the *job*, and the natural shorthand is to extend it to everything attached to the job
— media, a cable, a donor part. The owner's answer draws the line at the labour only. This is the same
shape as the shop's existing split between a waived service and a sold part, and it means a no-charge
case is still a case with a money field to fill in correctly.
