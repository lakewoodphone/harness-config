## 2026-10-08 · The bank ledger is the authoritative record of money received. Do not repair the production payment table blind.

WHY THIS IS A DECISION AND NOT A FLAG: the measurement justifies a default, so it does not go to the
owner. The default is stated here so the next session does not have to rediscover it, and so it can
be overruled on evidence rather than relitigated on feeling.

THE FINDING: `operational` (the live AWS RDS store) holds **zero** `payment_transactions` rows dated
anywhere in September 2026; `app` (the frozen archive) holds 35; the bank/accounting ledger records
18 Stripe payouts to the shop's bank totalling 7,256.22 that month. Recorded in full as journal pain
P2928.

THE TRAP, and this is the part that decides it: this is **not** a clean subset gap that can be
patched by copying rows across. Measured:

- 25 ids (3243-3267) exist only in `app`; 12 ids (3273-3284) exist only in `operational`.
- **257 of the 337 shared payment ids carry a DIFFERENT STATUS in each store.**
- 13 shared ids hold what appear to be different payments entirely.
- The archive is itself short: the ledger's 61 September charges sum to 7,347.69 while `app` can only
 match 4,018.49 — 3,329.20 is in neither store.

So "copy the missing 25 rows into production" is not a repair; with id collisions of that size it is
a change to the shop's financial records that could as easily introduce a wrong status as a right
one, and the only thing it would buy is order-level payment visibility for a month of already-closed
jobs.

THE DECISION: **the bank/accounting ledger is the authoritative record of money actually received.**
The `operational` payment table is a counter record of card charges, not the bank record, and it is
incomplete for one month. Consequences, all already in force:

1. The reporting engine already reads money **landed** from the bank ledger and calls it the money
 source of truth — so no money number in any report changes because of this gap. That is why this
 needs no owner decision.
2. Money **recorded** (orders booked) and money **received** are reported as separate measures and
 are never added together, so a partial counter record cannot inflate a receipt figure.
3. The production payment table's incompleteness for September 2026 is written down here and in the
 audit, so no future session reads a zero from it and believes the shop took nothing that month.
 That failure — reading a stale/partial source and believing it — is journal pain P3, and it is the
 most expensive failure in this system's history.

WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT DONE: nothing was written to either production store. No repair script was
created. If the shop's own order record is ever to be the authoritative money record — a business
choice, not an engineering one — that is the owner's call and it comes with a proper reconciliation
first, not a bulk copy.

WHEN TO REVISIT: if staff need to see, on a work order, whether the customer paid — that is a real
operational need and would justify a careful reconciliation. It has not been asked for.
