**D142 · 2026-09-14 · Absorption and dedupe key on the logical entry, not on bytes.**

Measured before the change: the 789 shard entries in `log/**` were only 569 distinct entries - 220 redundant copies (196 lessons, 13 decisions, 9 handoffs, 1 pain, 1 win), 269,142 B, about 19% of the record, with groups as bad as `L154, L154a, L154b, L154ba`. The six flat files added 446 more candidates of which 194 were byte-identical to a shard entry and 184 were the same entry under a different heading id; only 68 were text the record did not already have.

Decided: identity is the LOGICAL entry - `(kind, normalised body)`, or the title for a heading-only entry - not the exact heading+body hash. A source entry whose identity the tree already holds is recorded as an ALIAS (`index/aliases.tsv`: alias_id, kind, canonical_id, reason, host, sha, source) and nothing is written. `dedupe --apply` applies the same rule inside the tree: canonical is the lowest number, every other copy is moved (never deleted) to `archive/duplicates/<kind>/<id>.md` and aliased.

Why the alias instead of a byte-identical copy: it preserves every reference (the old id still resolves, and `show` says where it now lives) while keeping one entry per fact. The count that matters is not how many files exist but how many distinct things the record knows.

Also decided: "unabsorbed" and "legacy drift" mean *running `import-legacy` would add something*, computed with the same identity rule. Before this, drift was counted by exact hash and reported 322-534 phantom gaps after the migration had closed them all, and `import-legacy` re-added one entry on every run which `dedupe` then collapsed - an import/dedupe loop that also left the always-read page crying wolf forever. Alias rows now count as absorbed evidence.
