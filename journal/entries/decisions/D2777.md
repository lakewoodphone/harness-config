## 2026-09-30 · CFO money plane: the decisions taken after the audit

Audit and plan: `lpt-books` `e459039`, `docs/CFO-PLAN-2026-09-30.md`. The eight source workstream reports are
committed beside it. Every decision below is a **development** decision — architecture, sequencing, store layout
— and is recorded here so it is not relitigated. The one decision that is the owner's is D4 and it is open.

**D1 — The bank-data provider stays Plaid.** Adopt its unused surface (transaction and item webhooks,
`/transactions/refresh`, live Balance polling, Statements, Enrich) and make Stripe primary for everything the
business charges. Rationale: Plaid already covers Chase, Bank of America and Amex via OAuth; no competitor
documents better swipe-to-posted latency because the issuers set the ceiling; Teller and Quiltt are cost plays,
not freshness plays; Yodlee/MX/Finicity/Akoya are enterprise sales motions; Flinks carries a $500/month floor and
a one-year term; Basiq/Powens/GoCardless are not US. A migration would cost 6–12 re-consents and buy latency that
is not for sale. **Re-open only if a measurement taken from this account contradicts it** — per-institution
refresh latency, the real per-request price on this contract, and whether webhook delivery alone closes the
"ask while he remembers" window. None of those three measurements has been taken.

**D2 — `books.db` is the accounting system of record; `live/accounting.db` is the operational store.**
`books.db` is the only store with a chart of accounts, a rules table, a double-entry journal with lines, periods,
reconciliations, obligations, owner-loan movements and an evidence table — i.e. the only one that can produce a
trial balance an accountant would accept. It is stale because nothing runs it, not because it is wrong. The
operational store keeps feeding it read-only, which is the migration path that already works. **No step in this
plan deletes a row from either store**, and the irreversible step, if it ever happens, is a separate named
owner-approved decision.

**D3 — The CFO runs on the existing work ledger and wake loop, not on a new scheduler or a new cron.** The carrier
is measured healthy: 45 releases today, 0.60 of a 25.00 USD ceiling, `caps_in_force: []`, no orphan attempts, no
expired leases held, 277 of 293 settled attempts carrying proof. The gap is that the `cfo` project has an empty
queue (`todo=0 done=7`), not that the machinery is missing. The recurring units become ledger items with a
definition-of-done that is a command, and the sensors that file them follow the existing
`comms-freshness.py`/`decision-queue-stale.py` pattern — because the ledger's own rule is that *a flag is raised
only by something that knows there IS work, and nothing on a timer may raise one*.

**D4 — The needs-attention badge carries the count and the headline; the text carries the question.** The badge is
a health-check channel by construction (`phone-gate.py:781` filters kernel findings on `needs_attention`), so it
gets one new kernel check — `cfo_money` — reporting how many purchases are waiting, the value at stake, the oldest
age, and the freshness of the pull. Making it carry free text would mean a new source, a new payload field and a
new contract on both sides: a health-check channel becoming a message channel. **Still open, and the owner's:**
the standing, bounded permission for the CFO to text him about unclear charges at all (see D5).

**D5 — The ask cap is worked out, not offered as a menu.** 263–323 transactions a month ≈ 10 a day; 65–74 %
classified leaves 2–3 new uncategorised charges a day; most are repeats and 512 of the uncategorised rows have no
merchant name at all and are not merchant-answerable. So the honest ask rate is **one to three genuinely new,
genuinely uncertain purchases a day**, and it decays because every answer makes that merchant permanent.
**Three questions a day, oldest and largest first, one per 5-minute tick, never about a merchant already asked
about**; everything above the cap goes to the tab and waits. **This is a change to the owner's own outbound hard
stop, which exists because it was broken twice with real customer damage — so it is his to grant, and it was
asked, not assumed.**

**D6 — Corrections are new rows; a clarification is resolved to transactions, never written onto them.** The
schema separates three things that are conflated today: a *categorisation* (well modelled, keep it), a
*clarification* (the human fact — what it was, who for, business or personal, deductible, reimbursement, owner
drawing — which has no home), and a *rule learned* (which exists but links to no human statement). Append-only
with a supersede chain, so a figure produced in January can still be explained in December, and a
receipt-derived 0.95 is distinguishable at the schema level from an agent's guess at 0.95.
