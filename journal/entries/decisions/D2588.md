**D2588 · 2026-09-24 · Shlock drive FINAL: no pole and no stay (stiffness is a dam, tension is a drive) - a limp rope tied to the pocket every 3 ft, bolted at both mouths, one line per direction with the bight in hand.**

**Decisions, 2026-09-24 evening, superseding D2587 after the owner's direction and the second fleet's findings.**

**1. The steel pole does NOT go back in.** It is a 1.375-in dam worth 17.7 in of backwater and the owner has ruled it out. D2587 (pole back in, drive its ends) is withdrawn.

**2. No rigid stay in the pocket** - not the pole, not PEX, not PVC, not fibreglass. The owner's argument, and it is correct: any member stiff enough to straighten a 28.5-ft edge is a dam. **Stiffness makes a dam; tension makes a drive.**

**3. The drive is a limp member, positively attached:** a 3/8-in rope through the empty leading-edge pocket, **tied to the pocket every 3 ft** (ten zip ties, or stitches), **bolted through at both mouths** (1/4-in bolt and shackle, 1 in in) so nothing can walk out, pulled from both ends on **one continuous line per direction with the bight in the operator's hand** (two independent lines rack a soft edge). Wheels at the house-end posts for IN and at the yard-end posts for OUT, 3 in outboard of the mouths; a redirect pad eye on the house beam so the drop does not steepen to 72 deg at full retract; cleats at one station; the 300 lb scale in line for the first pull (~40 lb dry expected). Nothing on the rails, nothing through the cloth. Water-neutral: the rope is a 0.375-in ridge sitting below the hanger stack that already dams 16.5 in.

**4. Why the loose rope alone was dead, for the record:** whole-length friction is 0.3-0.9 lb against ~17.5 lb per mouth, so it slides; the load then lands on stopper knots bearing on an unreinforced pocket mouth (two layers of 18 oz VCP, a stitched hem, no grommet, no webbing, no patch - weaker than the tarp's weakest rated point), and the knot is smaller than the mouth slot so it can walk out. The ties and the bolts remove both defects.

**5. The water fix that matters is the HANGER STACK, not the pole.** The stack is 1.29 in above the rail plane = 16.5 in of backwater; the record says the poles were never in the pockets when the 0.79-in pool was measured, so the measured failure state is the pole-out state. The hem's own sag (0.45-0.80 in) only shrinks the pool from ~50 lb to ~7-25 lb. **The fix: lower the tarp's front attachment - the eight sockets turned from 12 o'clock to 6 o'clock (a 4.07-in swing, no parts, two people, ~30 min) or a rope sling under each sleeve (3.2 in) - on a dry day.** Then the crest is below the water and the cover sheds.

**6. Guards, all cheap:** thin UHMW liners in the 8 sleeves (with no stiff edge, nothing else keeps the 8 stations in phase; a cocked sleeve that jams sends the pull into the fabric and tears a grommet - the liners drop the play from 0.170 in to 0.045 in); tie the two 24-in corner overhangs down with rope; close the two pocket mouths with cable ties so an empty pocket cannot scoop.

**7. The storm decision is separate and unchanged:** nor'easter, 35-50 mph gusts Friday to Sunday; 164 lb per point at 50 mph against a 150-200 lb grommet tear-out; the cover comes off the frame before Friday sunset, taken down and not stowed on the rails.
