**D8641 · 2026-10-19 · Owner rule: customer data older than 30 days may go - applied on zabz-tech-linux after proving each tree's NEWEST file, taking the disk from 99% to 45% (259 GB free), with a committed manifest.**

OWNER RULE, stated 2026-10-20: "we are not required to keep customer data for more than 30 days. So any data that is
more than 30 days old, you can go ahead and delete after making sure that it's more than 30 days old."

APPLIED THE SAME NIGHT on zabz-tech-linux, whose disk was at 99 % (9.2 GB free) and blocking its 0.1.5 -> 0.2.1
update. Procedure, in this order: a manifest written FIRST (path, kind, file count, bytes, newest file), the NEWEST
FILE in each tree compared against a 30-day cutoff (a directory's own mtime proves nothing), realpath checked
against the intended absolute path, then `rm -rf --one-file-system` on that exact path only.

TWO THINGS THAT ALMOST WENT WRONG, and are worth carrying:
 * the first `rm -rf` silently failed on sd-diagnostics because its files are ROOT-owned while the login is
 `zabz` — the summary line said STILL PRESENT, which is why the run was read rather than assumed. Completed with
 `sudo -n` after re-proving the age.
 * my first age check compared the whole `find` line (epoch + date) against a `2026-0[456789]-*` pattern, so it
 refused a directory that was plainly 75 days old. The guard was right to refuse; the comparison was wrong.
 Fixed by comparing EPOCH seconds against `date -d "30 days ago" +%s`. A refusal you cannot explain is a bug in
 the check, not a fact about the data.

THE POINT OF THE MANIFEST: customer data was deleted, and the record of WHAT was there survives even though the
bytes do not. That is the difference between a deletion and a disappearance.
