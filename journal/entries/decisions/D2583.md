**D2583 · 2026-09-24 · Shlock water decisions: never fit anything to the cover; re-clock the 8 hanger sockets 12 to 6 o'clock (zero parts); cover off the frame before Friday sunset.**

**Decisions, 2026-09-24, after a fifteen-angle water audit of the Shlock cover.**

1. **Nothing is ever fitted to the cover to manage water.** No through-hull, no grommet-and-spout, no hose, no drip
 flap, no drain sock. Thirteen audits converged: anything mounted on the cover either hangs into the swept path of
 the 8 hangers, or is folded into the gathering pack whose creases are undefined, or cannot be drilled and proven in
 the time available. The cover is left as sewn.

2. **The water problem is a geometry problem, and the fix is to re-clock the 8 hanger sockets from 12 o'clock to
 6 o'clock.** The socket may be parked at any clock position. At 6 o'clock the grommet moves from 1.29 in above the
 rail plane to 2.78 in below it - a 4.1 in swing- and the leading edge becomes the low line with a 51% grade from
 grommet to edge, so the pool leaves over the yard edge unaided. Cost: zero parts. Time: about 30 minutes with two
 people, on a dry day, working from outside the yard edge or with schach panels lifted.
 Conditions: guard the yard beam with a UHMW offcut because the pocket hangs 4-6 in lower and will reach the beam
 and its clamps; the socket and bolt become downward projections; and about 3 in comes off the retract envelope,
 which is re-set by hand anyway. **Equivalent and lower risk: a rope sling under each sleeve**, riding the sleeve
 rather than the rail, using rope already on site.

3. **The cover comes off the frame before Friday sunset.** A nor'easter is forecast Friday through Sunday gusting
 35-50 mph. On this frame's own numbers 50 mph is 2,627 lb = 164 lb/point against a #4 grommet's 150-200 lb
 tear-out, and the cover's failure point is about 43 mph. Even furled, the bundle takes 446 lb across 8 hangers.
 It is taken down, not stowed.

4. **The operating rules stand and one is added:** deploy below 25 mph; furl at 25 mph forecast gusts; **never furl
 when wet**; the default state is open; and the cover is not left deployed through a storm.

5. **The standard is re-specified.** What must be protected is the table and the people - not the schach. Schach is a
 permeable roof and rain on it is normal. The fabric itself does not leak (welded seams; the head of water is
 0.5-3.4% of the hydrostatic rating); the only water that can get in comes through the grommet holes, which sit
 over the sukkah.

6. **Rejected on arithmetic, and not to be re-proposed without new numbers:** a hole and hose at the dip; a
 through-hull fitting; a fixed gutter under the dip (the dip travels 9 ft with the cover); a rope or weight pulling
 the foot down (~2,400 lb of line tension, and it pulls inboard not down); lowering the 8 hanger attachment POINTS
 (it scallops the cover and deepens the dip fourfold); a deploy-stop change; stiffening the edge; a drip flap.

7. **Two halachic items are escalated to the owner and his rav, not decided here:** a waterproof cover over the
 schach, and the rule that a roll-out cover may be extended on Yom Tov only if it was already unrolled at least
 3 inches before Yom Tov.
