**D9300 · 2026-10-21 · A sealed phone does not transfer - except on written proof, after thirty days, and usually with the first owner present.**

aliases: Q29

Owner, verbatim (2026-10-21):

> "3 — we sold it to the first person. If the second person has clear proof of purchase from the first we can
> unlock it after 30 days, but that would usually need the first person to come in to prove it."

What it decides, condensed:

1. There is no transfer service. A sealed phone belongs to the household that bought it; the shop does not
 re-seal it to a stranger and offers no transfer fee.
2. One exception, a recovery rather than a resale channel: a second-hand buyer with clear proof of purchase
 from the first owner may have the phone unlocked — after thirty days, in person, with identity.
3. In the ordinary case the first owner comes in. The anchor is his account, so without him we may prove
 nothing; if the phone is anchored to him or his chaver the buyer is told to bring the seller or use
 Google's own recovery.
4. Proof means a document, not a story: a receipt or bill of sale naming phone, seller, buyer and date, plus
 the buyer's identity.
5. The thirty days is the same clock as the shop's own anchor path — one wait in the product, not two.
6. The buyer is told at the point of sale that the filter does not transfer; the release is recorded; the
 released phone is a normal phone, and re-sealing it for a new household is a fresh sale.
