**W · 2026-09-15 · Recovered a vendor reply that had been silently lost for three days, and fixed two dead MCP
write-path tools while doing it.**

*What happened:* the owner pasted a RattleByte support-ticket page and said "i think you missed this, log this
info". Nothing about the DMP-BD84 case existed in any system: `lptcase.py find BD84` → no match over 180 cases;
journal search for `RattleByte` / `BD84` / `Panasonic` → no match; the only trace was task `24658`, marked **done**.

*What was actually true:* the vendor had replied **six minutes** after opening the ticket, and that reply had never
entered the system, because it lived only on their portal. Task `24658` had been closed **nine minutes before the
reply was even posted** — 29 minutes after it was created.

*What was produced:*
- `lpt-hub/hardware-diagnostics/panasonic-dmp-bd84-rattlebyte-diagnostic-2026-09-12.md` — the case log, with the
 vendor's reply captured verbatim (the only copy in existence anywhere outside their portal), a ranked cause
 analysis, a ready-to-send portal reply, and a 10-point research brief.
- Task `24658` reopened (`status=open`, `assigned_to=dept_devices`, description 2,915 chars) — via a direct
 `PATCH /ceo/tasks/24658`, because the MCP tool for it was broken.
- `ps_mcp_server.py` fixed and committed on the authority (`8e8616e5`). Two tools had been returning **HTTP 404 on
 every call**: `ps_task_update` → `POST /api/v1/tasks/update` and `ps_memory_search` → `POST /api/v1/memory/search`,
 **neither route exists**. Repointed at `PATCH /ceo/tasks/{id}` and
 `GET /ceo/db/memories/search?q=&limit=`.
- A 10-source research brief on the device fault, which corrected the working assumption: **the install procedure
 was right all along** (USB at the Home Screen is the documented method; no service menu exists), and the
 ranked-likely cause is the **USB stick** — Panasonic specifies 256 MB–2 GB for this firmware family.

*Measurement:* `journal.py check` → **0 errors**. The MCP fix passed **17 deterministic checks** (with `_api`
stubbed: correct method/path/body, note appended to description, existing description preserved, no stray `note`
key, empty update refused without any HTTP call, unknown task refused, query encoding) and the underlying routes
were proven live — three `PATCH` 200s at 0.00–0.01 s plus a real reopen, and the memory-search route returning
`{"ok":true,...}`. Every claim in the case log carries its source; the vendor's words are quoted, not paraphrased.

*Why it is a win worth not "optimising" away:* the whole recovery cost one session because the *first* move was to
go and read the vendor's own portal instead of concluding "the vendor ignored us". The mailbox said silence; the
portal said a six-minute reply.
