**W60 · 2026-09-14 · 2026-09-14 - The evolution subsystems health was 611 successes and zero failures while doing nothing for ten weeks.**

**The evolution subsystem's health metric was a lie: 611 successes, zero failures, empty detail, ten weeks of doing nothing**

This is the precise residual P4 left behind after W59, and it is fixed.

**The evidence.** `data/autopilot_state.json` on the authority:

 evolution: successes 611 · failures 0 · consecutive_failures 0 ·
 last_status "ok" · last_detail ""

611 successes against zero failures, with an empty detail, while `evolution_log` shows the last applied
proposal on **2026-07-04** and 72 unapplied since. **The subsystem reported perfect health for ten weeks
across which it produced nothing** — which is why nobody noticed, and why the kernel's alarm was the only
signal, and it was aimed at the wrong thing.

**The cause was one call, and the outcome was known one line above it:**

 evolution_result = _evo_cycle(llm_tier=self._evolution_tier)
 evo_status = evolution_result.get("status", "unknown") # computed...
 proactive_actions.append(f"Evolution: {evo_status}") # ...into the tick's notes...
 self._record_subsystem("evolution", success=True) # ...and NOT into the health record

So the status and whether anything was applied went somewhere a human might read and never into the record
the rest of the system reads. **That is the honest-completion defect one layer up** — not a wrong status on
a work session, but a wrong *health* on the mechanism that is supposed to police everything else, and the
same shape as the work-loop, the briefing and the goal-progress defects: the outcome was known and not
recorded where it is believed.

**The fix.** `_evolution_health_from_result()` derives both fields from the cycle's own result:
`status` and `applied` go into the detail; `error` and `rolled_back` count as failures — a rollback means a
change was applied and the error-spike guard reverted it, which is a real problem, not a quiet no-op — and a
`reason` or `rollback_failed` is carried through and truncated. Ten tests, including a regression guard
that an empty detail can never be produced again.

**What it does not assert.** With `code_edit_auto_apply` off, not applying is the configured behaviour, so a
quiet cycle is still recorded as a success. The change is that the record now says *which* happened, so a
future reader — or a check — can tell "worked" from "ran". **The existing counters still read 611/0 until the
next evolution cycle overwrites the subsystem entry**; the correction appears in the record at that point,
not retroactively.

**Deployed and verified**: `success=_evo_ok` confirmed in the live file, 10 tests pass on the authority's own
tree, `/health` 200, backup at `handport-evo-health.py.pre`.

**A process slip of my own, caught this time.** My first attempt to run the new tests on the live tree used
`git show HEAD:...`, and the authority's `HEAD` (a080c228) does not contain my commit — so pytest reported
*"no tests ran"* and I had a `|| true` that let it pass silently. I noticed the line, fetched the commit
explicitly by hash, and got a real **10 passed**. **Reporting the first attempt as green would have been a
claim about a test that never executed** — the eighth instance this session of a verification that could not
see what it claimed to see, and the first I caught before writing it down.
