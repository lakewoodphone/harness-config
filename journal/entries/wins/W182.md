**Recorded 2026-09-16. What measurably worked in the LPT website-redo audit - keep it, do not "optimise" it away.**

**1. Fanning out six scoped workstreams found four defects that serial reading had missed for weeks.** The
decisive one - the customer path allowlist that blocks every sign-in call - was found only because one
workstream was scoped to "trace the sign-in surface" while another was scoped to "what does production actually
serve". Each alone produced a partial answer; neither would have produced the connexion.

**2. Requiring every subagent to state its NOT-VERIFIED items was worth more than requiring it to be thorough.**
The two most valuable statements in the whole fleet were admissions of a limit: "the deployed Prisma client's
generation state is unknown, so the column mismatch could break either way" and "I hold no customer token, so
this is code reading, not a live 403". Both told me exactly which single probe would settle the question.

**3. Reproducing the headline claim first-hand caught two real errors that had already been written into
documents.** The prior session's production-bundle probe had concluded the portal "ships in production", and a
subagent briefly reported the local checkout as older than the remote when it was newer. Both were caught by
re-reading the primary artefact rather than the report. **An agent's report is a claim; the command output is the
evidence.**

**4. Preserving the previous session's artefacts instead of overwriting them paid off immediately.** The prior
`_lpt-audit/prod-bundle.js` plus its probe scripts are what let me re-run the earlier probes and find that their
conclusion was invalid, instead of re-deriving the same wrong answer from scratch.
