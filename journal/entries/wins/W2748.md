**W2748 · 2026-10-13 · The tax tool said no payments were made; it had never opened the book.**

2026-10-13. Three separate faults stacked in one 200-line script, each hiding the next, and the
result was a tax tool that reported the owner had paid nothing while the bank showed 1,874.00 paid.

FAULT 1 - IT COULD NOT OPEN THE BOOK.
 candidates = [r"C:\Users\ezabz\accounting-data\live\accounting.db",
 r"/home/zabz/accounting-data/live/accounting.db"]
Neither path exists on the authority (the ledger is at /root/accounting-data/live/accounting.db), so
`get_db_path()` returned None and every caller behaved as though the ledger were empty. Now honours
`ACCOUNTING_DB_PATH` first and includes the cloud path.

FAULT 2 - IT LOOKED FOR THE WRONG WORDS. Even once the book opened, the payment query searched for
'ST-50', 'NJ SALES TAX' and 'Sales Tax Payment'. The bank writes "NJ GOV TAX PAYMENTS 856-238-5388"
and "Nj Gov Tax". Zero matches on a year with four filings.

FAULT 3 - AND THEN IT CRASHED, INVISIBLY. The print loop reads rows as mappings
(`p['transaction_date']`) on a connection with no `row_factory`, which raises "tuple indices must be
integers". The section therefore printed its heading and nothing else, and the traceback went to
stderr, where a `2>/dev/null` had been hiding it in my own first two attempts at this diagnosis.

AFTER ALL THREE, measured: "RECORDED TAX PAYMENTS: 9 found", including
 2026-07-01 NJ GOV TAX PAYMENTS 1144.25 / 393.54 / 302.52 / 274.90
 2026-10-06 NJ Division of Taxation 78.00 <- the hand-maintained tax-payments.json,
 last written 2026-07-14, does not know this one
The duplicates in that list (1,144.25 twice, and so on) are W2743's duplicate-transaction problem
surfacing again, not a new defect.

WHAT THE SCRIPT STILL CANNOT DO, and this is now established rather than suspected:
`print_tax_summary()` does not compute anything. It prints `KNOWN_TAX`, a hardcoded dict documented
as "Known amounts from the v9 FINAL P&L (verified 2026-06-26)", and skips every quarter not in it -
which is why `manage-taxes.py calculate --quarter 2026-Q3` accepts the flag, ignores it, and prints
the same fixed table. `calculate_from_db()` exists, is the only thing that reads transactions, and is
**called from nowhere**. Its basis does not reproduce the filed figures either: for 2025-Q3 it
returns sales 16,754.84 against a filed 3,051, and the current P&L returns 4,022.29 for the same
quarter. Three sources, three different numbers, and the FILED one is what the state was told.

SO: THE 2026-Q3 FIGURE IS NOT DERIVABLE FROM ANYTHING ON THIS BOX, and I am not inventing one. The
deadline is real - ST-50 Q3 2026 due 2026-10-20 - and the amount must come from the same basis the
prior four returns used, which is not the transactions table, not the current P&L, and not
`calculate_from_db`. Finding that basis is the next piece of work; guessing it would put a wrong
number on a state filing.

LESSON, and it is the third time this session: **a tool that cannot read its input reports an empty
input, and an empty input looks like a fact.** "NO SALES TAX PAYMENTS RECORDED" was not a finding
about the owner's payments - it was a finding about a Windows path in a list. When a report says
"none found", establish that it looked, and where.
