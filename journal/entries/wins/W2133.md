**W2133 · 2026-09-20 · W2143 - the monitor now says WHAT is about to be lost, not just how many messages are stuck.**

The objective's owner-message item, addressed at the point where it actually bites.

**The measurement.** `owner_message_queue` held 5 `held` and 4 `queued` rows. `held` is not a failure -
it is what happens while the owner's SMS kill switch exists (`data/OWNER_SMS_KILL_SWITCH`, created
2026-07-14 by the owner's own request after the company sent 28+ "URGENT" texts). **But a held row does
not sit there: `lifecycle_sweep` expires held/queued rows once they pass a per-reason horizon**
(24 h for reminders, 36 h for briefings, 168 h for `ask_owner` and the default). So a message can go
from `held` to `expired` without reaching the owner or anyone noticing.

One row sitting there was:

 #1795 [normal] reminder:193 age 8.4h horizon 24.0h -> 15.6h left
 "Reminder: Chase credit card (7542) statement due 09/22/2026 - min payment $232.25 ..."

A payment reminder that expires unseen is exactly the class of silent loss this monitor exists to
catch, and the old report **could not see it**: it printed `held_total` and the oldest age, which says
how many are stuck, never that one is about to disappear.

**What changed.** A new `held_ageing` section, deliberately NOT windowed (a backlog is not a rate -
the same distinction that produced a bug in this monitor earlier today). For each held/queued row it
reports the reason, the age, the horizon, the hours left, and an `at_risk` flag. The verdict gains a
WARN naming the three closest to expiry, and reports UNMEASURED - never a guess - if the horizon
function cannot be imported.

Live output:

 horizons_known = True
 waiting_total = 9 at_risk_total = 0
 {'id': 1795, 'reason': 'reminder:193', 'age_hours': 8.4, 'horizon_hours': 24.0,
 'hours_left': 15.6, 'at_risk': False}
 ...plus 4 google_voice_secretary_monitoring_failure rows (urgent, 45-118h old, 50-122h left)

**The horizons are read by CALLING `lifecycle_sweep._owner_message_horizon_hours`**, the same function
the expiry sweep itself uses, rather than re-implementing its table. A prediction that drifts from the
mechanism it predicts is worse than no prediction, and my first attempt at this patch guessed at a
table name that does not exist (`_HELD_TTL_HOURS`) - caught by checking before running, not after.

`at_risk_total = 0` is correct behaviour, not a failure to detect: nothing is within 6 h of its horizon
yet. The section exists to speak *before* the loss, not after.

**Honest scope note.** I did NOT re-enable owner SMS and did NOT delete the kill switch - both are the
owner's decisions, taken deliberately, and the switch's own file says the CEO was stuck sending 28+
"URGENT" texts when it was created. The defect was never "messages are held"; it was that nothing said
when they would vanish. Backup:
`~/code-backups/20260920T162023Z/company_outcome_monitor.py.before-heldage`.
