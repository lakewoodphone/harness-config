# Categorization: the writer existed and worked — nothing had ever run it

2026-10-09, on the live authority. The owner's ask was that the CFO "start categorizing
everything and do all the backlog for years". The blocker was not intelligence. It was that
**no clock, no caller and no cron entry had ever invoked the categorizer.**

## What was actually wrong

`app/services/auto_categorize.py` is the app's SANCTIONED writer into `transaction_categories`
(its own word for itself). It works. Its only entry point was its own `__main__`, hardcoded to
`run_auto_categorize(dry_run=True)` — so even running the module by hand categorizes nothing.
`grep` across `app/` and `scripts/` finds **no caller**. `crontab -l` finds **no entry**.

Meanwhile an AI CFO has been texting the owner one charge at a time asking what it was.

## What was done

1. **Ran it.** First dry run: 1,323 transactions categorizable, 0 errors. Applied.
2. **Taught it the difference between MOVEMENT and SPENDING** — the real defect in the data.
 Of the remaining backlog the largest families were not purchases:
 `zelle 414 / 114,766 | paypal 285 / 23,238 | stripe 199 / 49,753 | card autopay 26 / 37,029`
 A card autopay row reads `Chase Credit Card | Payment Thank You - Web | credit`. Booking a
 card repayment as an expense **double-counts** spending the card already recorded, and
 asking "what was this 52,449 Chase Credit Card charge?" is a question with no useful answer
 — he would be asked to explain his own bill payment. Added rules at the TOP of the table
 (so no merchant rule can capture a payment descriptor): card autopays → `Credit Card
 Payments` (personal) / `Internal Transfer` (lpt); Stripe and PayPal payouts → `Payment
 Processor Payout Clearing`.
 **Deliberately NOT guessed: Zelle.** 201 active Zelle rows carry about 115,000. A Zelle row
 can be a customer paying the business (revenue), the owner moving his own money (a
 transfer), or a payment to a contractor (a cost) — three different destinations, and the row
 does not say which. Booking a customer's payment as an internal transfer HIDES revenue;
 booking an internal move as revenue INFLATES it. That class must be asked, not guessed.
3. **Put it on a clock** so it never rots again: `scripts/auto-categorize.py` (`--apply` writes,
 default reports) and a wrapped cron entry `25 */6 * * *`. Verified through `cron-wrap.sh`:
 exit 0, run recorded in `~/.cron-status/runs.tsv`.

## Result — measured, active rows

| of which Zelle (genuinely needs him) | — | 201 |

`transactions` active total: 5,859. Entity-integrity check **unchanged at 133** mismatches
before and after — this work added none. API healthy, `journalctl -p err` clean.

## The check that mattered

I audited my own new rule for over-reach, because "Chase Credit Card" is the merchant on a
*purchase* as well as on a payment. It matched 56 CREDIT rows (78,503 — payments) and 5 DEBIT
rows (13,936). All five debits read `CHASE CREDIT CRD DES:AUTOPAYBUS` — genuine autopays. The
rule held. Had they been purchases, this would have filed real spending as a transfer, which is
the more expensive direction of error and I would have reverted the rule.

## Why this changes what he is asked

The owner's complaint was that alerts are too many and don't mean anything. A large share of the
CFO's questions were about **money movement** — autopays, processor payouts — which no answer
could have helped, because they are not expenses at all. Those are now categorized without
asking. What remains uncategorized is the set that genuinely needs him: Zelle, and charges whose
descriptor carries no merchant.

## Still open
1. **Zelle — 201 active rows / ~115,000.** Needs one question, not 201. The honest form is a
 single grouped question ("the Zelle payments totalling X — which were customers paying you,
 which were you moving money between your own accounts, and which were payments to someone?").
2. **905 rows with no merchant string** (205 active). Mostly bank plumbing; the memo is the only
 clue and some of it is `CHASE CREDIT CRD AUTOPAYBUS`, i.e. movement that the current rules
 miss because the merchant field is empty and the *description* is the bank's.
3. 367 "other" active rows with a real merchant string — rules can still be extended cheaply.
4. `books.db` (the parallel `lpt-books` ledger, 9,860 rows, 21 days stale) is untouched by this
 and has its own categorizer. Whether it should exist at all is a separate question.
