## 641 work orders reviewed against every source in about two hours, with the mistakes caught before they reached production

Measured 2026-10-09, first full run of `lpt-sweep`.

* **641 orders read, oldest first, 26 batches of 25**, each reviewed on the mesh by a read-only
 analyst with its own brief built from production, the frozen `lpt_prod` twin, the Dialpad message
 and call record, the intake records and the lpt-hub case files.
* **Verdicts: 414 clear, 141 proposed a change, 86 questions.** Production received **111 notes,
 8 status corrections and 50 prices**, on **115 orders**; 4 proposals were rejected and 26 held.
* **The verification step earned its cost.** Of the first 33 proposals read by hand against raw
 production, **two were wrong in the same way** — a note asserting a customer had been ignored, on
 orders whose logs hold the reply. Both would have been written to a customer's record by a naive
 apply. That single failure produced rule 9 of the analyst contract, the `hold` on ordering claims,
 and a lesson.
* **A second wrong proposal was caught by a shared fact, not by reading**: order 2580's own final
 note calls the order COMPLETED, and the proposal would have moved it to UNFIXABLE. The reviewer
 read the note; the policy would not have.
* **Nothing was destroyed.** No note edited, no message deleted, no order removed; corrections are
 new notes citing the record, and the 8 status changes each carry a reason on the order as well as
 in the ledger, because `lpt-write --reason` does not reliably reach `order_status_history`.
* **The bill-of-materials is on disk**: dossiers, briefs, the analysts' own verdicts, the reviewer's
 overrides, the exact actions, `applied.jsonl` with a read-back per write, and an append-only
 ledger on the authority. A future session can reproduce or audit any single change from those.
* **The tool is the artefact that lasts.** The run is one use; `bin/lpt-case`, `bin/lpt-review`,
 `bin/lpt-questions` and `bin/lpt-sweep` are what make the next one cost minutes instead of hours,
 and they work on a single work order as well as on 641.
