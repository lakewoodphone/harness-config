**W3137 · 2026-10-15 · The give-up ceiling change was measured, found to abandon work that finishes, and reverted the same hour - 5 of 13 paused tasks went on to complete.**

A change I deployed earlier today was measured, found harmful, and REVERTED -- all within the same hour. The give-up ceiling had been amended so a task reaches its 3-attempt limit on `status IN ('failed','paused')` instead of `status = 'failed'`. The reasoning was that `paused` was invisible to the ceiling, so a permanently-pausing task was re-attempted for ever.

The post-deploy check refutes it, 30 days per task on the authority:

 tasks that paused 3+ times : 13 of which 5 went on to COMPLETE (38%)
 tasks that failed 3+ times : 482 of which 9 went on to COMPLETE (1.9%)

and one case settled it. Task 28625, eight sessions in order:

 78754 paused 20:16:32 78763 paused 22:05:53 78778 failed 02:28:45
 78756 paused 20:24:16 78768 paused 22:34:21 78779 COMPLETED 02:44:44
 78759 paused 20:41:24 78771 paused 23:32:42

The amended predicate reaches 3 at 78759. It would have refused the task there and abandoned, four attempts early, work that FINISHED.

REVERTED: predicate back to `status = 'failed'`, occurrences of `status IN ('failed','paused')` in database.py now 0, `/opt/company` commit `ca9248eef`, `ExecMainStartTimestamp` 14:53:59 -> 15:13:48, /health ok, and the running interpreter reads the restored predicate.

KEPT, and this is the point: the OTHER half of the same change stays. `PRIOR_ATTEMPT_STATUSES` still includes `paused`, so a retry inherits its predecessor's context -- proven by observed sessions. "Should a retry know what happened?" and "should a task be given up on?" are different questions, and the first is yes while the second is no.

This is the first time in this system's record that a deploy was refuted by its own post-deploy check and rolled back the same hour, and it happened because the change was made to name an unmeasured risk at deploy time.
