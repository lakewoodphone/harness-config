A repeated mutating call no longer earns budget: the run length was the discriminator

MEASURED 2026-09-15 from every session that earned an extension: 75415 ran
[list_tasks, update_task] for NINE consecutive steps and collected four extensions before
failing at 15/15; 75419 and 75426 each ran [update_task] seven times to 11/11. The extension
test added earlier the same night -- whose entire purpose is to reward real work -- was
satisfied by one mutating call in a loop.

FIX: refuse the extension when the last _PROGRESS_REPEAT_RUN (3) step signatures are all
identical. Everything else unchanged; both conditions read the persisted markers. Committed
8a68ac4b, 56 tests across the budget suites, full suite unchanged at its 9 pre-existing
failures.

VERIFIED BY REPLAY, which is stronger than a unit test because it uses real logs: replaying
each session's extension decisions at the points where they were actually decided gives

 75415 15 steps 4 ext -> 11 steps 2 ext failed either way 4 steps saved
 75419 11 steps 2 ext -> 8 steps 1 ext failed either way 3 steps saved
 75426 11 steps 2 ext -> 8 steps 1 ext failed either way 3 steps saved
 75418 11 steps 2 ext -> 11 steps 2 ext COMPLETED preserved exactly
 75427 11 steps 2 ext -> 11 steps 2 ext COMPLETED preserved exactly

Ten LLM steps removed from three sessions that failed regardless, with both completions intact.

WHAT MEASURING FIRST PREVENTED: the obvious stronger design was "the task row must have
changed". That would have BROKEN 75418 -- its mid-session update_task calls may alter no task
field, so it would have lost the extension that let it finish -- and it would have looked like
a cleaner, more principled fix. The observed difference between completing and spinning is the
run, not the effect, and only the replay showed that.
