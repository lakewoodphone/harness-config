**W2604 · 2026-09-30 · The owner-text flood was already fixed, and the evidence now says so.**

The owner complained twice that the system sends him too much. The last flood event in the
ENTIRE history of the channel is 2026-09-28T23:06Z, and there have been ZERO repeats inside a
30-minute window in the last 24 hours, against 88 historical ones concentrated in the
2026-09-22 (191), 09-25 (53) and 09-27 (23) briefing retry loops.

WHY IT STOPPED, so nobody optimises it away: the 2026-09-27 morning briefing was 1,736
characters, rejected by Twilio 1,600-character limit, and RETRIED EVERY 30 SECONDS from
07:30:16 to 07:41:26 -- 25 submissions. A permanent failure became a loop because the day was
marked `sent` only on a success that could never come. The fix was not a bigger retry budget:
it was marking the day sent UNCONDITIONALLY and holding the briefing in the owner queue
instead of texting it. Briefings are documents, and documents are forbidden on his phone.

THE MEASUREMENT ITSELF HAD TO BE FIXED FIRST, and that is the transferable part.
`inbox.db.messages.date_sent` holds TWO formats -- RFC 2822 from textsend and ISO 8601 from the
mirror -- and my first check compared both against an ISO string with `>=`, which sorts them as
TEXT. It reported 258 rows where the truth was 118, and 49 duplicates that were really repeats
spread across weeks. A check that cannot tell two states apart is not a check. Second
self-inflicted miss in the same script: a phrase spanning adjacent string literals was not
found because the source keeps the quote characters, so a WORKING fix was reported broken.

READING THAT IS USABLE: 117 outbound rows over 7 days for 28 distinct bodies is the honest
picture and it is still too many messages for 14 replies from him -- but it is history, not a
live fault, and a check scoped to the last 24 hours says so.
