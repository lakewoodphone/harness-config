**Why `divergent-in-repo` mattered.** `~/.dsh/skills/<name>/SKILL.md` is a **single shared catalog** — one copy per skill, however many presets carry their own — so three presets with three different versions of the same skill means **whichever preset syncs last wins**. That is a rule that changes according to the order of an unattended job.

**My earlier conclusion on `lpt-production-case-work` was WRONG, and reading beat counting.** I had recorded that it "needs a hand-merge" because adopting the largest copy would lose 5 lines from cordis-bg and 15 from yocheved — a section headed *"There are TWO production databases. Name which one answered."* Counting lines, that looked load-bearing. **Reading the lines showed it was SUPERSEDED:** cordis-bg carries the **2026-10-06** measurement, while ZABZ-TECH's copy carries the same `L3351` measurement **plus** a **RE-MEASURED 2026-10-12** section with the commands and the two DSNs now resolving to the same host, and an updated table showing there is **one** store. So the newer copy is a strict improvement and nothing was lost. **A line-difference count identifies a difference; it does not identify a loss.** Both skills are now converged on the newer copy — `lpt-production-case-work` at 18,009 B and `parallel-agent-orchestration` at 13,700 B, byte-identical across zabz, cordis-bg, yocheved and the loaded catalog.

**Committed on both machines' HEADs**, which is the part that decides durability because `autosync.ps1` exports `git archive HEAD` (`L3807`): ZABZ-YOGA `d4e927f8` (`HEAD blob == worktree`, `53265f7e5682`) and ZABZ-TECH `a3021fb3` + `7bbda518`.

**And I made it worse before I made it better, which is worth recording.** Three mistakes in one pass: (1) a `scp` run from the wrong working directory, so two files silently did not copy and the machine ended up with cordis-bg/yocheved at 13,700 against zabz at 13,275 — **a new divergence I created**; (2) a commit that reported success while one of its paths was never staged, so the case-work convergence was not durable on ZABZ-TECH; (3) on ZABZ-YOGA the loaded catalog **reverted again** to its pre-fix hash (`5de3c07e2d72`) between my write and the detector — the `L3813` HEAD-snapshot reversion, observed for the third time in one day. Each was caught only by re-running the detector, and that is the point: **the detector is the only thing that told the truth, and it took four runs to get from clean to broken and back to clean.** A convergence claimed from the edits rather than from the detector would have been wrong three times.

**Method, worth reusing:** copy with **absolute** paths; stage **explicit** paths and then confirm the commit actually landed; re-run `preset-drift.py` **on the machine** after every one of those steps and require `drift=0` **and** `divergent-in-repo=0`.

**Not claimed:** `divergent-in-repo=0` means the repo and the loaded catalog agree on the 13 files the detector compares. It says nothing about the four lineages of the underlying checkouts, which is the real reason any of this needed doing by hand.
