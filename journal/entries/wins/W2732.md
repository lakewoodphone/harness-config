**W2732 · 2026-10-08 · Triangulating report numbers against independent lineages — and the live card takings line it produced.**

## Verifying a report means triangulating lineages, not re-reading the same database

MEASURED 2026-10-08, on secratary, when the owner challenged whether the shop's money/customer
report had been measured against anything other than itself.

WHAT WORKED, and is worth repeating deliberately:

1. **Name the lineage before calling something a cross-check.** `~/.fsearch/comms.db` agreed with the
 local Dialpad numbers and looked like corroboration — but its own `meta` table says
 `source=.../data/secretary.db`. Agreement between a store and an index built from it proves only
 that the index is not corrupt.
2. **Compare two stores that are filled by different pipelines.** Local Dialpad harvest (46 callers
 today) vs the website's communications push into production RDS (45) — and 20 vs 20 SMS senders.
 That is a real check, because a defect would have to occur identically in two unrelated code paths.
3. **Go to the vendor.** Stripe's own API is the only authority on what was charged. It matched the
 accounting ledger's `stripe-charges` source to the CENT on every day from 2026-09-29 to
 2026-10-07, and gave September as 61 charges / 7,347.69 — identical to the ledger. That single
 comparison validates or invalidates an entire ingestion path, and it is cheap.
4. **Compare a moving source at the same instant.** The engine's outreach figure read 52 at 22:40 and
 54 at 23:15, and a hand-recomputation gave 54. The "disagreement" was 35 minutes of live phone
 traffic. Chasing it as a bug would have wasted an hour and possibly "fixed" a correct number.

WHAT IT FOUND that the first pass had missed: the report's money lines were all late or wrong for the
CURRENT day. The bank figure is a daily sync (04:30), and the book figure is bookings — so the report
said "landed 0.00, recorded 712.00" on a day the shop had actually taken **960.64** by card. The
truthful number lived in exactly one place and was not being read. Fixed with
`lpt_analytics/stripe_takings.py`, surfaced as a `money TAKEN` line in the today view, deliberately
reported beside — never summed into — landed and recorded.

4-days-earlier corroboration that the fix was needed: not one of the 8 successful charges carried an
`orderId`, confirming journal H3335's counter-workflow defect straight from the vendor's metadata.

THE GENERAL RULE, which cost nothing to apply and would have caught the gap immediately: for every
number in a report, be able to say **which lineage produced it**, and make sure at least one number of
each kind is confirmed by a lineage that is not derived from the first.
