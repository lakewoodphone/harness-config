## What he said, and why it is the right response

*"you should have that on record already"* — he is correct that the vendor was inferable, and
the assistant's own verification found the receipts. But the ask was not only identification:
`My guess` was deliberately removed because inventing a category was wrong on all 11 earlier
asks, so the question was **how to file it**, and the answer he gave is what moved 20 charges
from uncategorised to categorised. That is the difference between "we could have guessed" and
"it is recorded, with his authority, and traceable."

## The gap that sits right next to it — found, not fixed

His answer named ONE vendor, but the ledger holds it under **several merchant strings**, and
the categories are split across them:

 data recovery categorised

 (and others)

25 data-recovery charges total, **5 still uncategorised**, and the categories that HAVE been
assigned are spread over six different accounts
(`Merchandise & Inventory`, `Cost of Goods Sold`, `Professional Services`,
`Business, Industrial, & Scientific Supplies Basic`, `Contractor Labor`, `Office & Shipping`).

So one owner answer identified a vendor whose ledger identity is fragmented across at least
four strings, and the fragmented ones keep generating new uncategorised charges that will be
asked about again — the same vendor, again, as if it were new. The obvious next step is a
**vendor alias table**: one canonical vendor, its merchant-string variants, and the answers the
owner has already given, so an answered vendor is recognised however its charges are spelled.

Left undone here on purpose: an alias table touches the categorisation path, and this session
is long. It is recorded with the measurement so the next session starts from the number
rather than from a hunch.
