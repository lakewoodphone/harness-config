**W2136 · 2026-09-20 · W2146 - the latch fix proved itself on a REAL timeout: run_once was abandoned and the loop kept going.**

Measured 2026-09-20 18:04:41Z, about 25 minutes after the fix was deployed. This is the acceptance test the
fix was written for, and it happened on its own.

 ERROR app.autopilot: run_once timed out after 600s â€” ABANDONING it (the thread keeps running;
 it cannot be killed) and applying backoff
 WARNING app.autopilot: run_once failed, backoff 300.0s (x1.0)

**And then the loop continued.** No `"run_once skipped â€” previous run still active"` followed the
timeout. Under the old code that is exactly what would have appeared, for ever: the abandoned thread
never cleared `_run_once_active`, so every subsequent iteration returned `already_running` and did
nothing. The company would have gone quiet, not broken - which is how it went unnoticed for weeks.

So the fix is verified against a genuine timeout rather than against my fixture or my structural
assertions:
 - the timeout fired (the mechanism it must survive),
 - the guard was released (the loop would have skipped otherwise),
 - the loop carried on.

The new log wording also paid off immediately: the old text said "killing", which was false and would
have made this look like a clean recovery from a killed run rather than a release of a latch.

**Honest context.** `run_once` exceeding 600 s is itself a problem - it means a tick takes over ten
minutes on this box. That is the slow-database / write-contention issue (P2132/P2127), not the latch,
and under the test-suite load I had running at that moment it is unsurprising. The latch fix makes such
a timeout survivable; it does not make the tick fast. Both facts belong on the record.
