**W2632 · 2026-10-04 · Autopilot email-catalog clock verified: fired by itself, persisted, and held its 1800s interval across six ticks.**

Correcting/confirming H3148's note that the autopilot clock "lags".

Measured 2026-10-05 after the fix went live (secretary-api restarted 03:16:10Z):

 * The autopilot produced `email_sync_runs` id 7 BY ITSELF at 03:17:36 - 4 accounts,
 121 messages upserted, finished 03:17:51, `errors_json []`. No human input.
 * The clock DID persist: `data/autopilot_state.json` carries
 `last_email_catalog_sync_at = 2026-10-05T03:17:34.477381+00:00`, written at 03:21:21Z.
 `_save_state()` runs on a cadence slower than the 300 s tick, which is why it is not visible
 immediately - it is not missing.
 * The interval gate then HELD across six consecutive tick checkpoints
 (03:20:38, 03:21:23, 03:22:08, 03:22:53, 03:23:38 ... 03:28:08): `max(id)` stayed 7 throughout.
 Next sync due 03:47:34, i.e. 1800 s after the stamped time - exactly `email_catalog_sync_interval_sec`.

So: the recurring trigger fires, persists, and does not hammer the 16.8 GB store every 5 minutes.
The only degradation left is that after a process restart the FIRST tick re-syncs (the in-memory
value starts at None). That is desirable rather than harmful - it is idempotent and never sends -
and it is worth keeping, because a fresh catalog after a restart is the point of the loop.
