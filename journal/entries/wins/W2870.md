**W2870 · 2026-10-14 · The email-receipt pipeline the owner asked for already exists and ran today (4053 purchases, reconciled to Plaid) - but Amazon is 8.8% parsed against eBay's 97.7%, and 99.8% of item prices are null.**

The owner asked for something that already exists, and measuring it found the real defect: the email-receipt pipeline is strong for eBay and nearly broken for Amazon.

**His words, 2026-10-14:** *"plaid doesnt help for exact ebay and amazon purchases and orders to know what was bought for the cfo to fully classify, but the cfo should be able to pull all that info from the emails which he has full access to and is probably eaiser than scraping amazon or ebay."*

**It exists and it ran today.** `_maybe_sync_receipts` (`autopilot.py:8922`) performs *"Nightly receipt sync (Amazon + eBay from Gmail)"* at `receipt_sync_time = 04:45`, importing `app.services.receipt_parser` → `sync_amazon_receipts` / `sync_ebay_receipts`. Log: `Oct 14 04:45:07 … autopilot: starting nightly receipt sync`. Destination is the **`purchases`** table (4,053 rows), whose columns are exactly the requirement: `source`, `order_id`, `order_date`, `total`, `vendor`, **`items_json`**, `raw_email_subject`, `raw_email_snippet`, `gmail_message_id`, `gmail_account`, **`matched_transaction_id`** — and rows carry real item names (`Lot of 5 Apple iPhon…`, `PNY 128GB Turbo Attaché 3 USB 3.0 Flash Drive 5-Pack`) with one showing `matched_transaction_id …|2|matched`, i.e. reconciled to a Plaid transaction.

**So the browser scrapers were correctly retired and NOTHING was lost by retiring them** — the capability lives on email, as the owner said, and it is running. **This also corrects the dead-weight child's claim** that `amazon_sync`/`ebay_sync` were *"a missing `npm install` of `tsx` → REPAIR not retire"*. There is no node scraper to repair; the mechanism that matters is a 69,695-byte Python parser, and the two `*_sync` names are legacy browser paths superseded in fact if not in name.

1. **Amazon is nearly unparsed: 10 of 113 rows carry items (8.8 %) against eBay's 97.7 %** — and Amazon is a major vendor for this shop. It is also suspiciously *thin*: 113 Amazon rows against 3,934 eBay rows over the same 2026-07-02 → 2026-10-14 window suggests the Amazon side may be failing to find the emails at all, not merely failing to parse them. **Which of the two it is has not been established and is the first thing to measure.**
