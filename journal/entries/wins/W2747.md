**W2747 · 2026-10-13 · 90 of the 671 orders were shipping notices, and the parser's docstring said they could not be.**

2026-10-13. The store reported "671 orders, 144 without a total". 90 of those 671 are not
purchases at all, and the parser's own docstring said they could not get in.

THE DISAGREEMENT. `parse_ebay_orders_from_email`'s docstring: "A record is returned only when at
least one eBay order number (NN-NNNNN-NNNNN) is present, so seller shipping notices and
member-to-member messages do not enter the store." Tested directly against the live mailbox:
 subject 'OUT FOR DELIVERY: The Apartment (USED,...' -> 1 record, total_cents=None, items=0
 subject 'ORDER DELIVERED: The Apartment (USED,...' -> 1 record, total_cents=None, items=0
A delivery notice carries the order number - that is what makes it useful to the recipient - so it
passes the order-number gate. The docstring described an intent, not the code.

WHY IT MATTERED. Those rows have no amount and no items, so they can never match a charge, and they
sat in the same table as real orders under the same word. Anyone reading "671 orders" was reading
671 rows, 90 of which only record that a parcel moved.

FIXED, non-destructively. A parse whose total, subtotal and items are ALL absent is now labelled
`<note>+notification-only`. The 90 existing rows were labelled the same way. Nothing was deleted:
the row still records that something shipped and when, which is worth keeping, it just no longer
claims to be a purchase.

THE NUMBERS THAT ARE NOW TRUE, and the ones to quote from here on:
 rows in the store 671
 notification-only 90
 PURCHASE RECORDS 581
 of those, with a total 546 (94%)
 with items but no total 35 (every one has at least one unpriced line item, so
 `_total_from_items` correctly refuses a partial sum)
`no_amount: 125` in the sync status file is the matcher counting 90 notifications plus 35 real
orders whose items are not fully priced. Both are honest; neither is a missing import.

LESSON, and it is the same shape as L3650 two entries back: **a docstring is a claim, and claims get
tested.** This one had been false long enough that I built two rounds of reasoning on top of it -
first concluding the parser mishandled multi-order emails (it does not), then that the totals were a
scraping problem (they are not). One direct call to the function with a real email settled in ten
seconds what two rounds of inference got wrong. Call the function on real input before reading what
it says about itself.
