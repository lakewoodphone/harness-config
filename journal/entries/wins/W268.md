**W268 · 2026-09-18 · Dry-run renders the whole artefact; test harness tripwires the network.**

## What worked
Two mechanisms, both cheap and both caught real defects that would otherwise have shipped.

1. DRY-RUN AS THE DEMO. `caller.js plan` prints the exact schedule without touching the
 network. Two real defects surfaced this way before a single call could be placed: messages
 collapsing to 15 distinct out of 30, and the run finishing at T+25:57 of a promised 45
 minutes. A dry run that renders the full artefact is worth far more than a summary.

2. A NETWORK TRIPWIRE IN TESTS. caller.test.js monkey-patches http/https `.request` for the
 whole process; ANY outbound request during the suite fails the test. Result: 38 assertions
 covering consent gating, caps and planning, with `network attempts: 0` printed every run.
 This makes "the tests cannot accidentally phone a stranger" a property of the harness
 rather than a hope. For anything that sends messages or places calls, do this.

## Also worked
Building the safety property INTO the data rather than into my judgement. The consent record
is a file with a sha256 of the signed document; both the runner and the webhook re-read and
re-verify it. Revoking consent = deleting or editing the file, and the system stops. That is
strictly stronger than a config flag, because it cannot be forgotten.

## Measured
verify.js 51 assertions / caller.test.js 38 assertions / planner: 30 calls filling 97% of a
45-minute window at 90s spacing with 30 distinct messages.
