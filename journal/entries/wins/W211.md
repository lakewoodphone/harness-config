**W · A fleet on the desktop costs the laptop nothing measurable; the same fleet on the laptop costs ~1.1 GiB and ~26 processes — and "does it feel slow" is STILL unmeasured**

MEASURED 2026-09-18 by workstream E, on `laptop-ts` (`ZABZ-YOGA`), 4 × 60 s windows at 58 one-second
samples each. Instruments are now in the repo: `scripts/mesh/Start-AgentFleet.ps1` and
`scripts/mesh/Get-NodeSample.ps1`. Full write-up `docs/mesh/113-laptop-under-fleet.md`.

THE NUMBERS THAT MATTER (mean-of-window, with the second control that makes them interpretable):
- **A vs A2**, both fleet-free and five minutes apart: commit differs **0.145 GiB**, processes **2.7**.
 That is the noise band, and anything inside it is not a signal.
- **Fleet on the DESKTOP − baseline: +0.046 GiB, +2.2 processes** — indistinguishable from idle.
- **Fleet on the LAPTOP − baseline: +1.081 GiB, +25.9 processes.**
- Process ranges **do not overlap**: A [324,334], B [325,336], C [347,361].
- **Per child ≈ 369–418 MiB**, bracketing the 403 MiB unit cost (CI 270–533) — and this is the first time
 that figure has been reproduced by an independent instrument.
- The DSH engine did **not** grow under load: RSS mean 381 MiB under load vs 488–493 at baseline.
- Available physical never fell below **15.24 GiB**; the 4 GiB abort floor never armed.

PLACEMENT WAS PROVED BY THE CHILDREN, NOT THE DISPATCHER: `C → MESH-HOST: zabz-yoga` ×3,
`B → MESH-HOST: zabz-tech` ×3, all `placement=CONFIRMED coverage=ALIVE_THROUGH_WINDOW_END`. Cleanup was
verified on both ends (`VERIFY_ALIVE=0` remote, `stillAliveAfterKill=0` local, and a post-hoc sweep found
only the pre-existing engine).

WHAT IT MEANS: offloading works and is now quantified. Moving work from the laptop to the desktop is worth
~1.1 GiB of commit and ~26 processes on the machine the owner is using, and that is exactly the trade
`prefer-remote` (W210) exists to make — now with a number behind it instead of an argument.

WHAT IT DOES NOT MEAN, AND THIS IS THE POINT: **this instrument measures memory and process count, and
nothing else.** It took no keystroke latency, no input-to-photon delay, no CPU time, no disk queue. So
"the fleet does not make ZABZ-YOGA feel slow" is **NOT** supported by this work. The owner's word was
*lagging*, and lagging is still unquantified. Reporting these numbers as an answer to *"is it speedy"*
would be the same error this programme has spent the day correcting: a real measurement of the wrong
quantity, presented as the answer to the question that was asked.

THE NEXT MEASUREMENT, NAMED SO IT IS NOT REDISCOVERED: a responsiveness probe on the laptop — the cost of
a fixed local operation (a shell round trip through the engine, or `Measure-Command` on a pinned command)
sampled at 1 Hz through the same four conditions — reported as p50/p95 against condition A. That is
buildable with the instrument that now exists; the generator already samples an arbitrary node at 1 Hz.

ALSO WORTH KEEPING: a headless turn prints ONLY its final assistant message, so **a child killed mid-turn
has empty stdout and can never prove where it ran**. The driver therefore waits for each child's
`CHILD_PID=` line before opening the sampling window and lets children finish their own turn before
killing them. Any future fleet that kills children on a timer will collect `COULD NOT ASK` where it
expected a hostname.
