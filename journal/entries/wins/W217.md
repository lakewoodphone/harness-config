**W · The authority's swap went from 0 free to 3,152 MB free, by deleting 3,770 stale tmpfs entries and touching no service**

EXECUTED 2026-09-18 on `secratary`, under the owner's instruction *"you are in charge figure it out and do
it"*. Reversible in effect and destructive in fact, so the discipline matters as much as the result.

THE MEASUREMENT BEFORE, and the two ways an earlier number was wrong:
- `/tmp` is a **12 G tmpfs holding 7.2 G across 5,216 entries**; swap is a 4 GiB `/swap.img` with **980 kB
 free** — and the holder is not a process (largest process owns 14 MiB; all processes together own 63 MiB
 of the 4,093 MiB used). It is **swapped-out tmpfs pages**, charged 3.69 GiB to `user-1000.slice`
 (user `zabz`).
- **My first dry run would have deleted 927 entries totalling 270 MB**, while `/tmp` held 7.2 G — so it was
 obviously not capturing the bulk, and **I did not delete on it.** Re-measured properly, the age buckets
 contradicted each other (an `--files0-from` sum said 5.14 GiB older than 2 days; the real delete list
 `du`'d at 820 MB), which is exactly why the before/after was taken with `df` and `free` rather than from
 the candidate list.

WHAT WAS DONE, in two passes, each with a manifest and hard assertions:
1. **Pass 1 — 1,984 entries deleted, 1,824 kept.** Rule: mtime older than 2 days; refuse any path outside
 `/tmp`; refuse if the candidate count was under 10 (a guard against `find` silently returning nothing);
 **keep any directory containing `.git`** (worktrees and repo clones — `/tmp/wt-green` among them) and any
 `*.db*`/`*.sqlite*`/`*.log`.
2. **Pass 2 — 1,786 stale SQLite temp files deleted, 0 kept as live.** The first pass's kept set turned out
 to be **4.4 G**, almost all of it `*.db`/`-wal`/`-shm` temp files from test runs. Those were deleted too,
 behind a **liveness guard**: a live SQLite database keeps its `-wal`/`-shm` fresh, so any candidate whose
 sibling had been touched within 2 days was **kept** — and zero were.

THE RESULT, measured, not inferred:

| quantity | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| swap used / free | **4,095 / 980 kB** | **943 / 3,152,000 kB** |
| `/tmp` used | **7.2 G of 12 G** | **2.3 G of 12 G** |
| Mem used / available | 8,896 / 14,525 MB | **6,589 / 16,832 MB** |
| `/tmp` entries | 5,216 | 3,241 → fewer after pass 2 |

So: **~4.9 GB of tmpfs and ~2.3 GB of RAM returned, and 3.15 GB of swap freed** — the node's named binding
constraint, which had *zero* headroom at rest.

AND NOTHING WAS DISTURBED, verified rather than assumed: `mesh-broker.service`, `secretary-api.service`,
`secretary-dashboard.service` and `vscode-tunnel.service` all `active running`; `curl http://127.0.0.1:8002/health`
→ **HTTP 200**; `curl http://127.0.0.1:3091/nodes` answers. **A corrupted service check earlier in this same
session reported `secretary-api` as inactive — the `\r` from a CRLF heredoc had been appended to the unit
name** — which is why the verification was re-run cleanly before anything was written down. A check that
cannot be trusted is not a check, and this one nearly recorded a false alarm about the company's API.

MANIFEST KEPT: `/home/zabz/tmp-prune-manifest-20260918.txt` on the authority lists every path as
`DELETE` / `KEEP(git)` / `KEEP(db)` / `KEEP(log)` / `KEEP(live)` / `DELETED2`, so what was removed is
auditable rather than merely asserted.

THE RULES: a candidate list whose total is obviously smaller than what you are trying to explain is not a
candidate list — **check the list against the thing it claims to cover before acting on it**; free a
tmpfs by deleting files, because a swapped-out page releases its swap slot the moment the file goes, which
is why this reclaimed swap *and* RAM and no reboot or fstab change was needed; and **a destructive pass must
carry a liveness guard, not just an age guard** — age alone would have deleted the companions of a live
SQLite database.
