Retry waste measured, and the threshold calibrated against real completions

MEASURED, secratary 2026-09-15, work_sessions since 21:00 the previous evening:
 13 failed sessions were a FIRST attempt on their task
 21 failed sessions were a REPEAT attempt <- 62% of all failures
 115 LLM steps were spent on repeat attempts alone
 worst offenders: task #24942 6 attempts, #25060 6, #25095 6, #25106 5, #25062 4

THE CALIBRATION QUESTION, and the answer changes the design: do repeat attempts ever
succeed? Since 12:00 the same day:
 first attempt : 2 completed of 44 (4.5%)
 repeat attempt : 2 completed of 61 (3.3%)
So retries are NOT pure waste -- they succeed at nearly the first-attempt rate, and the
successes sit in attempts two and three (a task that hits one transient fault and then
works). That rules out blocking on the first failure.

It also justifies the threshold exactly where it is: `_WORK_MAX_FAILED_ATTEMPTS = 3` lets
the two productive retries happen and cuts the tail, which is where the waste is -- three
tasks had reached SIX attempts, each burning its full budget to fail identically.

FIX: `count_recent_failed_work_sessions` (bounded to 24h so an old failure cannot block new
work) gates the claim path; at the threshold the task is marked blocked and its job is
closed with reason `not_retried` rather than failed, so the queue cannot re-enqueue and
loop. Committed 94647077.

NOTE FOR WHOEVER READS THIS NEXT: do not casually raise or lower the threshold. Two is not
enough to distinguish a transient fault from a task that cannot be done (and would foreclose
the 2 measured repeat successes); five is paid for five times over. If a threshold change
is proposed, measure completions by attempt number again.
