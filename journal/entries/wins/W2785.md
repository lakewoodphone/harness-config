**W2785 · 2026-10-13 · Clause 1 is already durable: finance-freshness is a wake source that alarms on what FEEDS each source.**

2026-10-13. Went to check whether the freshness check was itself on a clock. It is, and the estate had
already built the better version - including the failure mode I have spent all night finding one instance
at a time.

MY WORRY. Clause 1 says "every finance source pulling on a clock with measured freshness". `finance-freshness`
prints a verdict; nothing in the crontab named it. Sixth time tonight that a capability looked unwired.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY THERE, from `/root/bin/sources/finance-freshness.py` - a WAKE SOURCE, whose own docstring
records the failure it was built for:
 "A staleness number nobody computes is not a check. ... on 2026-10-13 NOTHING turned that number into a
 verdict - while three sources sat 68-210 days behind and four automated pulls (paypal, amazon, ebay,
 stripe) were dead on the cloud authority. The Stripe one had been dead since the 2026-10-11 cutover and
 by 2026-10-13 Stripe held charges worth over a thousand dollars that the ledger did not have, and no job
 on this host said a word."
and, critically, its alarm has TWO INDEPENDENT AXES:
 verdict STALE / NEVER / COULD NOT ASK - what we HOLD is too old, or cannot be judged at all
 pull_state failed / unscheduled - the thing that FEEDS the source is broken or was never put
 on a clock, EVEN WHILE the ledger row it produced is still
 inside its window
**That second axis is precisely the defect class I have been finding one case at a time all night** - the
marketplace sync with no crontab entry, the tax calendar writing into silence, the forward view never run.
The estate named it, built it, and put it on a clock today, and it says why in the same breath: "this is the
case the age axis cannot see: on 2026-10-13 `lpt_ebay` read OK at 4.9 days old while `ebay_sync` had failed
on every run for two days."

SO CLAUSE 1 IS NOT MERELY VERIFIED, IT IS DURABLE, and better than the thing I would have proposed: a check
that alarms on WHAT WE HOLD AND on WHAT FEEDS IT, separately, because the measured failure had the ledger
row healthy while the pipeline behind it was dead. A session that added its own freshness watcher would
have duplicated this and probably watched only the age.

THE LESSON THE `estate-systems` SKILL STATES AND I KEEP RE-LEARNING. It says to check BEFORE designing,
building or proposing anything, "because the fixed version may already exist". Six times tonight something
looked unwired; four were false alarms; and this one was already solved by someone who had measured the
same problem properly and written the reason down. **Reading the artefact's own docstring before proposing a
fix is worth more than any amount of pattern-matching against failures I happen to remember.**
