**The rule, written where a fleet-builder will read it** (`presets/*/skills/parallel-agent-orchestration/SKILL.md`, +2,248 bytes, all three presets byte-identical, committed `c13d6658` on ZABZ-YOGA and `2d01206f` on ZABZ-TECH, **`match=15 drift=0 divergent-in-repo=0`** on both):

- **Measure free disk on every node before a fleet and after it** — because a fleet's copies are measured in tens of GB: a `cp -a` of this estate's company repo is **23 GB** (`.git` alone 7.6 GB), and eight copy-making children took a host to 97 % with 4.9 GB free.
- The failure that produced was **not a slow agent, it was a wedged service** — with the chain written out, because **disk pressure looks exactly like a software defect and gets diagnosed as one.** That is what happened today: I diagnosed the wedge (`L3930`) as a control-flow defect, which it is, while the *trigger* was the disk, which nobody measured.
- **Prefer `git worktree` to `cp -a`** (it shares the object store); if a child only needs to read code, it does not need a copy at all.
- **A disk budget in every brief**, and one copy per child — two coexisting would have filled this box.
- **Never `git format-patch --all --stdout` on a large repo**: `--all` walks the whole history and wrote **4.78 GB** in one command, into `/tmp`, which is **tmpfs** and therefore RAM-backed.
- **Clean copies at teardown and read the host's disk after the fleet** — the skill already warned that *"a forgotten worktree is a slow disk leak"*, and that was not enough, because the leak was a **copy** and the pressure was on the **host**, which no part of the procedure measured.
- **Reclaiming is not deleting**: confirm artifacts exist outside the tree (`diff -q`), confirm the original is intact, and capture anything that exists only there — an unpushed branch, a report — as a small artefact. **And never remove a store you did not create** — the 26 GB pre-remediation database backup on this host is the owner's call, not mine.

**The durable part is not the deletion, it is that the fleet now checks the disk.** The admission governor watches RAM and nothing watched the disk, which is why a resource exhausted by eight well-behaved children presented as a control-flow bug in a service none of them touched.
