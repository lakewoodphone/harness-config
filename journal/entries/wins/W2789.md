**W2789 · 2026-10-13 · Amazon item parser fixed: two missing layouts, 28 purchases recovered, items sum to the total to the cent.**

2026-10-13. Fixed the Amazon item parser. 28 purchases got their line items, and the test order's items sum
to the order total to the cent.

THE CAUSE, and it is two missing layouts rather than one. `receipt_parser.AMAZON_ITEM_LINE_RE` requires a
leading `*` and `AMAZON_QTY_RE` requires a `Quantity: N` line:
 * PNY 128GB Attache X USB 3.2 Gen 1 Flash Drive 5-Pack
 Quantity: 1
 64.99 USD
The failing order has neither. Its body reads, consecutively:
 3 x Samsung FIT Plus USB 3.2 Flash Drive, 512GB ... MUF-512AB/AM
 $69.99
 Sold by: Amazon.com Services, Inc
 Condition: New
 Crucial X9 1TB Portable SSD, Up to 1050MB/s, ... CT1000X9SSD902
 $168.95
 Sold by: TEKTURN
**And only the FIRST item of an order carries the `N x` prefix** - so a rule that required the prefix
caught one item of two, which is exactly what the first attempt did.

WHAT I ADDED. Two forms. An `N x <title>` line. And a BARE TITLE LINE, trusted only when a `Sold by:`
appears in the window AND the price is the NEXT NON-EMPTY line - the `Sold by:` is what makes a bare title
safe, and the price adjacency is what keeps it honest.

AND I CAUGHT MY OWN FALSE POSITIVE IN THE SAME MINUTE. A window-based price test captured
**"Placed on today, May 20"** as an item, because a price appears a few lines below it. In this layout every
item is title / price / `Sold by:` consecutively, so requiring the price to be adjacent excludes it naturally
- and the exclusion list now names `placed on`, `order #`, `order details` and `your order` as well, so the
rule survives a layout change that moves a price up.

THE ARITHMETIC IS THE PROOF, and it is exact:
 114-7984660-4547428 total 378.92 items sum 378.92
with 3 x Samsung FIT Plus at 69.99 and 1 x Crucial X9 at 168.95. Those are TWO line items and FOUR units -
**the subject's "and 3 more items" counts UNITS, not lines** - which matters because the definition of done
I filed said "four line items" and a shift following it literally would have kept hunting for two that do
not exist. Closed item 76 done with that correction recorded in its attempt row.

PROPAGATED, measured before and after a full sync:
 item rows 739 -> 777
 purchases with items 549 -> 577
 still unitemised 80 -> 52
So 28 purchases that had no line items now have them, and every one of those emails was already in the
mailbox being read daily - the parser was dropping them at parse time.

A NOTE ON THE TWO DEFECTS THIS UNCOVERED, kept separate on purpose. This one is a PARSER gap: the mail
holds the items and the code did not extract them. The other (journal L3679) is Amazon's 2026-08 layout
change, where the mail genuinely carries a category and no products, and NO parser can recover it. **They
looked alike from the counts and need different fixes; merging them would have fixed neither.**
