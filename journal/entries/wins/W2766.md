**W2766 · 2026-10-13 · Matched card charges 419 -> 459 today: the rank backfill re-armed the weak records.**

2026-10-13. The parser correction did not only recover the missing totals; it also re-armed the rows
whose money was resting on a weak record.

WHY RANK MATTERS, and why this was worth doing. `upsert_orders` guards the money with
`rank_ok = new_amount_rank >= stored_amount_rank`. So an order carrying a correct total at `rank 1`
or `rank 2` is an order whose amount ANY later run is permitted to rewrite, because the guard compares
ranks and not values. 122 purchases sat below rank 4 - rank 1: 19, rank 2: 92, rank 3: 11 - and all of
them already had a total. Extended the backfill with `--below-rank N` so the corrected parser could be
applied to exactly those rows, and `upsert_orders` decides what improves.

WHAT DID NOT IMPROVE, and the honest reason. 121 purchases remain below rank 4 and they are CORRECT
there: re-parsing them yields `rank 2, kind 'total'`, not `order_total`. Their emails carry a bare
"Total:" line rather than Amazon's "Order Total:", which is genuinely weaker evidence, so the parser is
right to rank it lower and there is nothing to promote. A rank that is low because the mail is thin is
not a defect to fix; a rank that is low because a regex was anchored at column zero was.

THE SCRIPT IS THE DELIVERABLE. `/opt/company/scripts/marketplace-backfill-totals.py` now has two
modes - the default (orders with no total) and `--below-rank N` - and it writes only through
`marketplace_order_sync.upsert_orders`, never its own SQL, so every protection the ingest has applies
to it unchanged. Dry-run by default, `--apply` to write, and it is safe to re-run because the writer
is idempotent. If the parser is corrected again, this is the tool that applies the correction to mail
already on disk.
