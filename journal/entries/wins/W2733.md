## A fleet with a dedicated adversarial verifier produced a sourced profile of a living person with 4 real errors caught before the owner saw them

Measured 2026-10-11, ZABZ-YOGA. 26 workers, ~1.2 MB of sourced output, about 45 minutes wall clock.

What worked, in the order it mattered:

1. **A brief shaped like a contract.** Every worker got: the subject's identity with disambiguation
 cues, the exact input files and the host detection rule, one output file it alone owned, a required
 section structure, the honesty clause ("a confident unearned claim is worse than an honest gap"),
 a required `SEARCH LOG`, and a definition of done as commands. 26 of 26 workers produced a file;
 21 produced it complete; the four failures were transport, not the work.
2. **A worker whose only job was to disbelieve the others.** One worker took the 25 riskiest claims
 and re-checked them against primary sources: 33 claims checked, 4 contradicted, 2 partly correct,
 2 unverifiable. It caught a podcast episode list wrong on all nine values, a wrong zbMATH
 identifier, a wrong publication date, and a false "not found" in the biography. **The synthesis
 worker was then told the verifier wins**, and marked its four corrections in place. This cost one
 worker and saved the whole deliverable's credibility.
3. **Self-verification at the point of writing.** Each book worker machine-checked its own quotations
 against the extracted text before reporting; three reported "0 misses" out of dozens to hundreds
 of spans, and the manager's independent check confirmed the book summary at 150 of 150.
4. **Numbers reported as commands, not adjectives.** Every worker returned line count, byte size and
 sha256 from a real command. That made collection mechanical and caught mismatches — the manager
 compared each file on disk against the worker's report, and the disk won every time.
5. **Honest nulls.** The bibliography worker reported "no 2026 publication exists in any index I could
 reach" instead of padding; the talks worker reported whole sections empty and what it searched;
 the family worker stated exactly what it refused to publish for privacy. A fleet that reports
 nulls is a fleet whose positives mean something.

The numbers: 629 quoted spans across four synthesis documents, 595 found verbatim in our own sources
(94.6 %), 150 of 150 in the book summary; 33 independent claim checks; 24 papers downloaded and
verified as PDFs by magic bytes and size; zero fabrications found.
