**W171 · 2026-09-15 · Her agent found two real defects in my tooling, one of them destroying customer data.**

MEASURED 2026-09-15. This is the first time the loop has produced something I could not have found
myself, and it found a bug that was DESTROYING CUSTOMER DATA.

WHAT HAPPENED: I shipped ps-mesh.mjs (fleet access) and ps-case.mjs (case files) to her Mac. Her agent
used them on real shop work, found faults, and filed them as journal entries with evidence -- L1568,
L1569, P165, P166 -- from LAKEWOOECHSMINI. Those entries reached me and I acted on them.

THE FOUR FINDINGS, and what each turned out to be:

1. P166 / L1568 -- `ps-mesh file` WAS NOT BYTE-FAITHFUL. Her words: "on Windows hosts it silently
 mangles every non-ASCII character, so the natural workflow read a record over the mesh, edit it,
 write it back quietly degrades customer records."
 VERIFIED MYSELF rather than taking it on trust: a 360-byte record of the sort a customer file really
 contains (em dashes, e-acute, u-umlaut, pound, euro, Hebrew, emoji) came back from the office desktop
 as 340 BYTES with a different hash. Twenty bytes of customer content deleted, no error raised, by a
 tool whose entire purpose is moving customer records between machines.
 FIXED with base64 over the wire (pure ASCII, so no layer can mangle it) plus a matching `write` verb
 -- a faithful read with an unfaithful write would still have corrupted on the way back. Verified
 byte-identical through BOTH a Windows and a Unix host, from her Mac and from mine.

2. P165 -- ps-system did not retry a transient 5xx. Her words: "502 on the first call ... the very next
 call in the same minute succeeded ... should be retried once before concluding the archive is down."
 Right, and the direction of the error is what matters: a blip reported as failure teaches the reader
 that the archive is broken when it is fine. One quiet retry now; 4xx deliberately not retried.

3. L1569 -- THE CASE FILE COULD NOT ANSWER WHAT THE CUSTOMER OWES. The Gutterman file carried our cost
 and a status, and no customer price anywhere, so "how much does he owe for this" had no answer. The
 framework was documented all along (labor $30-$120 by complexity, parts at 1.4x cost, manager holds
 quoting authority) -- so this became `ps-case price`, which shows the arithmetic and leaves the
 number to her judgment. Verified on the real case: $65.84 panel -> $92.18 -> $122.18-$212.18.

4. (Found while acting on the above, not reported by her) MY SYNC RECORDS DID NOT FOLLOW THE REPO'S OWN
 CONTRACT. lpt-hub's AGENTS.md pointed at CROSS_REPO_CUSTOMER_SYNC_CONTRACT.md, and I had written
 `references.contactId`/`taskId` by copying one existing record instead of reading the contract. The
 contract requires workOrderId/orderNumber/ticketNumber/phone matchers and calls the structured record
 authoritative. Wrong keys are not a rejected record -- they are a record that silently never matches.

THE POINT: everything above was found by a non-developer manager's assistant, on live customer work,
because the tools reported what they did rather than succeeding quietly. Two of the four were defects I
introduced and could not have seen from my own machine.
