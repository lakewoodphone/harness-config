# Built: the US-Mobile-line ↔ Stripe mapping, and two of my own wrong answers corrected

**2026-10-16.** Source `harness-config/scripts/line-map.py` (commit **`d5147420`**), deployed
`/root/bin/line-map.py`, tests alongside, cron every 12 hours.

## What was asked

The owner pasted the US Mobile line inventory: *"audit all the info here to match it up with
stripe"*, then *"the 6 are the 6 heichal hatorah, doesn't heichal pay 66 a month, get this straigt.
figure this out fully"*.

## The two answers I got wrong, both from the same missing discipline

**1. `unit_amount` is not the subscription amount.** I said Heichal Hatorah pays **11.00** and had no
line; they pay **11.00 × 6 = 66.00** for **six** lines, with a paid 66.00 invoice on 2026-09-22. I
said total recurring was **89.00**; it is **144.00** — understated by exactly the quantity I never
multiplied in. **I was one step from reporting a 55.00/month revenue leak and asking him to
authorise a billing correction against a customer who was already billed correctly.**

**2. A phone number is not a join key.** I called Ahron Bodner a duplicate subscriber. He has **two
lines** — 845-558-1290 and 908-770-1707 — and two subscriptions, correctly. Only **2 of 8**
subscriptions matched a line by number; the rest only by name.

Both are the same shape as the rest of this session: **a field that looks like the answer is not the
answer until you check its multiplier or its scope.**

## What was built, and the rules that came only from breaking things

`line-map.py` — ingest a pasted US Mobile page, sync Stripe with quantity counted, assert links a
person confirms, audit, and check. Five rules, each one a defect found by running it on real data:

- **`line_key`, not `line_number`.** The owner's own **848-389-7895 carries two lines** — an
 Unlimited phone and an Apple Watch. A shared number is normal for a wearable, and keying on the
 number silently dropped one: the page's 20 lines read as 19.
- **A mapping is never invented.** Matching proposes; a person asserts. Every row records **how** —
 `PHONE`, `NAME`, `ASSERTED`, `NONE` — so a guess is never mistaken for a fact. Heichal's six lines
 cannot be derived from any data; they are the owner's assertion and are stored as one.
- **The alarm is a count mismatch** — a subscription's `quantity` must equal the lines mapped to it.
 That is what would have caught six lines billed as one.
- **A null never overwrites a stored value; a weaker source never overwrites a stronger one.** The
 first version broke both: a failed parse replaced nine good labels with nulls, and a re-seed reset
 `ASSERTED` links back to `NONE`.
- **It refuses rather than guesses** — `sync-usm` refuses when the parsed line count changes, because
 a changed count is either a real change or a broken parse and only a person can tell which. It
 fired on its first real run, correctly.

## Verified result

**20 lines, 13 billed, 7 unbilled, 144.00/month, `check` rc=0.** The seven unbilled are the owner's
own two and five shop lines — **no customer line is unbilled, nothing is leaking.** 11 tests pass.
The cron raises a wake flag on drift, because a mapping that is silently wrong is worse than none.

## Also fixed in the same row

The **filter Stripe webhook is re-enabled** — the row believed a Dashboard click was needed because a
prior attempt set `status`, which is read-only output; the writable field is `disabled`. And a GET
would have misled me: a POST-only route returns 404 on GET in some stacks and 405 in others, so I
POSTed an unsigned body and read the failure. `/root/bin/stripe-webhook-health.py` now runs every 6
hours and flags if Stripe disables an endpoint again — which is how this failed the first time: for
nine days, silently.

 line numbers appear in no store. The link between customer, lines and subscription exists only in
 Stripe and in the owner's head.
- **Hess and baruch ber svei carry the owner's own Google Voice number and the shop Gmail** as their
